## TOPIC A — EU bodies

### A1. European Commission
**Correction (material):** the "Drone Strategy 2.0" is **COM(2022) 652 final, 29 November 2022** — not COM(2023) 658 of 29 Nov 2023 `[S1][S3]`.
- **Drone Strategy 2.0 §5** `[A]` `[S1]`: *"no specific EU rules and guidelines on countering their unauthorised or even criminal use"*; malicious uses include *"smuggling of goods and migrants… attacks on public spaces, individuals and critical infrastructure"*; *"the threat is likely to grow"*; Member States face *"challenges with liberating the necessary budgets"*. **Flagship action 17** = adopt a C-UAS package; **Flagship action 18** = amend aviation security rules after Gatwick 2018 because EASA guidance's *"advisory nature makes them insufficient"*.
- **18 Oct 2023** `[S10]`: Communication on *"unlawful and dangerous use of drones… designed for civil use"*; six pillars, two JRC handbooks.
- **11 Feb 2026 — COM(2026) 81 final, "Action Plan on Drone and Counter Drone Security"** `[A]`/`[W]` `[S5]`: *"Drones have been used in violation of Member States' airspace, they have disrupted airport operations, and caused near-misses with civilian aircraft… Overflights by unidentified or non-cooperative drones increase security tension and act as a signalling tool, testing Union's preparedness."* Actors: *"state and state-linked hostile actors, terrorist organisations, organised crime networks and individuals."* Deliverables: Drone Security Package Q3 2026; EU counter-drone **centre of excellence Q1 2027**; **"EU trusted" drone label Q4 2026**; geofencing rules by 2027; *"Rapid Counter-drone emergency teams"* by Q4 2026; **National Drone Security Coordinators**. The Plan *"replaces… the drone strategy 2.0"* `[S7]`.
- **Costs:** BMVI **EUR 150 m** uncrewed-surveillance call; **EUR 250 m** border call (Dec 2025) *"including against drones"*; **EUR 1 bn** EDF drone R&D to date plus **EUR 200 m** over two years `[S5]`; **EDIP EUR 240 m** for common procurement *"such as counter drone, air and missile defence"*, grants **up to EUR 20 m per project** `[S12]`. Vehicles: **EDIS** (Mar 2024) and **EDIP** work programme **EUR 1.5 bn** (30 Mar 2026) `[S13][S23]`.
- **JOIN(2025) 27 final, 16 Oct 2025, "Defence Readiness Roadmap 2030"** `[W]` `[S11]`: *"Recent repeated violations of the airspace of EU Member States have shown the urgency of creating… European capability to counter unmanned aerial vehicles."* Flagships: **European Drone Defence Initiative** (renamed from "European Drone Wall"; *"detection, tracking, and neutralisation"*) and **Eastern Flank Watch**, both European Projects of Common Interest. Launched Q1 2026; initial capacity end-2026; Drone Defence Initiative functional end-2027; Eastern Flank Watch end-2028. **EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance** launched 17 Jul 2026 (18 founding members), implementing the **Drone Deal** of 15 Jul 2026 `[S15][S16]`.
- **Internal security:** EU Security Union Strategy 2020–2025 (COM(2020) 605, 24 Jul 2020); successor **ProtectEU**, 1 Apr 2025 `[S19][S20]`.

### A2. EASA `[S24]–[S32]`
Regulations **(EU) 2019/947 / 2019/945** create the **Open/Specific/Certified** categories. EASA: *"Neutralisation is NOT in the remit of EASA."* **U-space** = Regulations **(EU) 2021/664/665/666**; four mandatory services (flight authorisation, geo-awareness, network identification, traffic information); **applicability 26 January 2023**; direct remote ID mandatory in the Open category from 1 Jan 2023. **Counter-UAS Action Plan (2019)** folded into the European Plan for Aviation Safety (issue 3, 23 Sep 2020), five objectives — Obj 2 *"COMPLETED"*, Obj 5 *"Proposal being discussed with the commission"* (Nov 2024). Parts 2–3 are *"For Official Use Only - Need to know basis"*. **Costs** `[A]`: Gatwick 2018 *"up to EUR 64 million"*; ten largest EU airports, 30-minute runway closure *"EUR 325,000 to EUR 514,000"* (methodology undisclosed).

### A3. EUROCONTROL `[S33]–[S37]`
**7 Oct 2019** `[A]`: incursions *"increasing almost exponentially, with no reversal trend in sight"*. **4 Nov 2024** C-UAS workshop (160+ experts): *"the serious challenge posed by careless or malicious users"*; *"legislation is struggling to keep pace"*. **23 Jan 2026** 2025 Review: *"Some major European airports were disrupted (drone incidents, cyberattacks)."* **Thin evidence:** no airport, date, count or attribution — drones bundled with cyberattacks.

### A4. EU INTCEN — not public `[S38]–[S42]`
**No public INTCEN product exists.** INTCEN is *"the exclusive civilian intelligence unit of the EU"*, an EEAS directorate attached to the High Representative (INTCEN.1 analysis; INTCEN.2 open source), working with EUMS INT under the SIAC arrangement. All posts require clearance *"up to level EU Secret"*; across 108,108 EEAS sitemap URLs "intcen" appears **only in vacancy notices**. **INTCEN drone/hybrid assessments are EU-classified and not public; no inference about their content is made here.** Public substitutes come from other producers: the **EU Hybrid Toolbox** / **Hybrid Rapid Response Teams** (Council framework 22 May 2024) and EEAS StratCom **FIMI** reports (4th edition, 12 Mar 2026). `consilium.europa.eu` returned HTTP 403, so Council originals were read via EEAS reproductions `[NV]`.

### A5. Eurojust — a negative finding `[S43]–[S46]`
All 899 press items across 90 listing pages were enumerated: **no drone-specific press release exists**, and **Annual Reports 2023, 2024 and 2025 contain no drone/UAS content**. The only mention is OSINT evidence in core international crimes (*"drones, 3D modelling, satellite imagery"*). **No Eurojust–Europol joint drone case found `[NV]`.**

### A6. FRONTEX `[S47]–[S54]`
**Correction:** FRONTEX's series is **two-year** ("Risk Analysis for YYYY/YYYY"); there is no annual "Risk Analysis for 2024/2025/2026". Latest = **2026/2027 (17 June 2026)** `[S47]`. `[W]`: the use of *"fixed-wing drones flying at low altitude from the Moroccan coast toward the Cadiz area to evade radar is rising sharply and is expected to intensify"* (p.16) — **no incident counts, seizures or case numbers anywhere in the report**. Also *"the hostile use of drones and weather balloons are causing airport and airspace closures"* (p.7 — a Russian/Belarusian hybrid stream, not smuggling), and East Shield *"foresees… anti-drone deployments"* (p.10). SRA 2024 p.46: *"The use of drones and other unmanned craft is already a reality, but a lot of 'criminal potential' can still be discovered in the use of advanced AI devices."*
**Own capability is small:** CAAR 2025 reports **2 unmanned aerial vehicles** deployed (against 52 fixed-wing aircraft, 13 helicopters); the capability table targets **MALE RPAS "2 drones"**. Pilots: Bulgaria Tactical UAS (*"8,100 km2… independent red-teaming"*), Counter-UAS Prize Contest (Portugal), Italy VTOL trial (17 Jul 2026), and **"Establishment of a UAS and C-UAS cluster in the EU Innovation Hub (led by Frontex)"**. **No EUR drone cost figure exists — any FRONTEX drone cost is unverified `[NV]`.**

### A8. European Parliament research `[S60][S61]`
**EPRS PE 777.962, October 2025, "Eastern Flank Watch and European Drone Wall"** (Sebastian Clapp) `[A]`: *"at least 10 European countries (Belgium, Lithuania, Latvia, Denmark, Norway, Romania, Poland, Estonia, Germany and France) have reported drone sightings over airports and military installations, with officials suggesting in some cases that the drones may have been launched from vessels operating offshore."* *"…many of the devices recovered so far were unarmed and some merely decoys"*, yet governments *"regard these intrusions as a form of hybrid threat designed to test air-defence readiness and impose psychological and economic costs."* It records **disagreement**: frontline states see the Drone Wall as *"an urgent necessity"*, while France, Germany, Italy and Greece *"question its cost, technical feasibility and the risk of Brussels encroaching on national defence prerogatives"*; *"significant divisions remain over funding, scope and branding."* It quotes an **IISS expert** that air-to-air interception is *"operationally possible but fiscally unsustainable"*, and another expert that the wall's *"main purpose… is to reassure worried populations."*

## TOPIC B — NATO

### B1. Policy and official texts `[S62]–[S64][S76][S77]`
**"NATO's Drone Edge", official text, 7 July 2026** `[A]`/`[W]` — the key NATO document located: *"Drones have fundamentally altered the character of modern warfare… Drone incidents affecting Allies, in particular on the Eastern Flank, have increased in recent months."* Pledges: *"Investing over USD 40 Billion in counter-drone capabilities over the next five years"*, a **NATO counter-drone marketplace**, and *"Training five times as many drone operators… by the end of 2027"*. Rutte, same day: *"Allies themselves have seen repeated drone incursions… we are building robust counter-drone defences to detect, identify, and neutralise drones."*
**A silence worth noting:** the **Washington Summit Declaration (10 Jul 2024)** says only *"uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs)"* regarding DPRK/Iran; the **Hague Summit Declaration (25 Jun 2025) contains zero occurrences of "drone", "unmanned", "uncrewed" or "UAS"** (verified by string count on the official texts). The counter-drone push is therefore a **post-September-2025 development**, not summit-driven. **No published NATO C-UAS policy or doctrine document was found; it is not public `[NV]`.**

### B2. The September 2025 incursions and the response `[S65]–[S71]`
NATO (12 Sept 2025): Rutte and SACEUR Gen. Grynkewich outlined *"NATO's response to the violation of Polish airspace by Russian drones two days earlier"*; the **North Atlantic Council met on 10 September 2025 "in light of Poland's request for Article 4 consultations"**. Rutte: *"what happened on Wednesday was not an isolated incident. Russia's recklessness in the air along our eastern flank is increasing in frequency"* — *"the largest concentration of violations of NATO airspace"*. **Eastern Sentry** launched with *"novel technologies, including elements designed to address challenges associated with drones."* (SHAPE's release returned HTTP 403 `[NV]`.)
Follow-ons: **Baltic Sentry (14 Jan 2025)** — undersea-infrastructure protection with *"a small fleet of naval drones"* (NATO **operating** drones, not countering them) `[S67]`. **Ministers, 15 Oct 2025**: after *"recent airspace violations by drones and jets"*, agreed *"additional counter-drone measures… including testing integrated counter-drone systems under Eastern Sentry"*; *"nine Allies… along with Ukraine"* supported Denmark `[S68]`. **C-UAS Week, 28 Jan 2026**: *"Drones are here to stay. Growing in quantity, growing in quality"*; Vilnius airport *"had to suspend operations at least 10 times since October"* `[S69]`. **Arctic Sentry (11 Feb 2026)** `[S70]`. **Innovation Range, Latvia, 9–13 Mar 2026**: first UAS/C-UAS TEVV campaign, permitting *"high-speed and high-altitude interceptor flights"*; one of five pilot ranges under NATO's Rapid Adoption Action Plan `[S71]`.

### B3. DIANA and the NATO Innovation Fund `[S78]–[S86][S94]–[S97]`
DIANA's 2025 and 2026 challenge areas include **"Autonomy and Unmanned Systems"**; **no DIANA call is named "counter-drone"/"C-UAS" verbatim**. 2025 challenges carried **EUR 100,000** each; the 2026 cohort is **150 companies from 24 NATO nations** (10 Dec 2025), with 16 accelerator sites and 200+ test centres (distinct from the five RAAP Innovation Ranges). **NIF** = **"EUR 1 billion"**, 23 Allies as LPs. Portfolio: **ARX Robotics, Greenjets, Kelluu, Kraken, Stark, Tekever, Tytan** (Tytan builds *"AI-guided, low-cost interceptor drones"*). Disclosed rounds only — **NIF's own ticket is never stated**: Tytan **€30 m Series A** (NIF co-led); Kelluu **€15 m Series A** (NIF led); Greenjets **$40 m Series A**; Stark round **€500 m**; Expeditions Fund II **€197 m**. NIF/Dealroom: European defence/security/resilience startups raised **$8.7 bn in 2025** (+55% y/y). **No per-unit costs `[NV]`.**

### B4. Exercises and procurement cost figures `[S72]–[S75][S87]–[S93]`
**C-UAS Technical Interoperability Exercise, 10–20 Sept 2024, Netherlands**: 450+ participants, 19 Allies and 3 partners, **60+ systems** (*"sensors, drone-on-drone systems, jammers and cyber interceptors"*); **Ukraine's first participation**. Later: TIE26 at Marknesse (~300 participants, 40 companies including Ukraine and Australia); **Flytrap 4.5** (Putlos, 10–21 Nov 2025); **Flytrap 5.0** (Pabradė, 2026); Baltic Trust 25; REPMUS/Dynamic Messenger 2025.
**Costs.** The **USD 40 bn** pledge has **no baseline year, national breakdown or methodology**. **NATO's own documents conflict on currency:** the news article says NSPA awarded a contract *"in the hundreds of millions of dollars"* for surveillance drones; Rutte's same-day transcript says *"hundreds of millions of euros"*. The most specific published figure is the **Merops** interceptor demo (Nowa Dęba, filmed 18 Nov 2025, published 9 Dec 2025) `[S87]`: *"each drone costs roughly USD 14,500 - just about one tenth of the cost of the Shahed drones"*; it had *"taken down more than 1,000 kill Russian drones in Ukraine, inflicting more than USD 200 million in losses with only USD 15 million worth of interceptors"*; *"dozens"* were loaned to Poland and Romania under Eastern Sentry. **Attributed to "US officials" — not independently verifiable, and the source contains a textual error.** No NSPA tender notice or framework-contract value could be verified (portal 403) `[NV]`. NSPA will acquire **700 PAC-2 and 200 PAC-3 missiles** (air defence, not C-UAS-specific).

## Disagreements and thin evidence

1. **EASA vs EUROCONTROL on U-space timing** — EASA: *"entered into force"* January 2021; EUROCONTROL (18 Mar 2024): *"came into force at the start of 2023"*. The correct **applicability** date is 26 January 2023 `[S25][S37]`.
2. **FRONTEX "rising sharply" vs Eurojust's zero** — FRONTEX calls criminal drone use *"already a reality"* without counts, while Eurojust has published **no** drone prosecution or case example in 2023–2026. The criminal-use evidence base is assertion-heavy.
3. **Neutralisation authority is legally unsettled** — FRONTEX CAAR 2025: *"more work needed to address how to deal with neutralisation in terms of competences, both at national and European level"*; EASA: *"Neutralisation is NOT in the remit of EASA."* A legal, not merely technical, gap.
4. **EU capitals split on the Drone Wall** (EPRS) — cost, feasibility and sovereignty objections from France, Germany, Italy and Greece; an IISS expert calls air-to-air interception *"fiscally unsustainable"*; one expert calls the wall *"largely symbolic."*
5. **Attribution is asserted, not evidenced, in public documents** — EPRS says *"officials suggesting"* and that leaders *"assume"* Russian responsibility; NATO's political line is firmer (*"Russia's recklessness"*). **No public EU or NATO document publishes the underlying forensic evidence**, and no source found attributes any *airport* drone disruption to a state actor.
6. **NATO's internal inconsistencies** — dollars vs euros for the same NSPA contract; the Merops video dates the Polish incursion to *"9 September… more than 20 drones"* while NATO's own article says 10 September with no count; the USD 40 bn and *"more than 1,000 drones"* figures carry no methodology.
7. **Cost transparency is poor across the board** — the largest figures (USD 40 bn; EUR 240 m EDIP; EUR 250 m border call) are aggregate pledges; only EASA/EUROCONTROL publish per-incident estimates, and EASA's *"up to EUR 64 million"* methodology is undisclosed.
8. **Access limitation** — `eur-lex.europa.eu` (HTTP 202), `consilium.europa.eu`, `diana.nato.int`, `nspa.nato.int`, `act.nato.int`, `nso.nato.int` and SHAPE (403) were unreadable; re-verify those items against a working route.
