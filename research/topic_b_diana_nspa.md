# Topic B — NATO DIANA, NIF, counter-drone procurement/exercises, standards

Compiled 25 Sep 2026. **P** = primary (official NATO/NIF text/statement); **S** = secondary. `diana.nato.int`, `nspa.nato.int`, `nso.nato.int`: HTTP 403. No classified material used.

## B3 — DIANA and the NATO Innovation Fund

- **DIANA challenges (P).** The 2024 call (1 Jul 2024) covered "Sensing & Surveillance" among five areas [16]; the 2025 call (2 Jun 2025) listed ten including "**Autonomy and Unmanned Systems**" (€100,000 each) [17]; 2026 reuses those ten — 150 companies from 24 countries, 200+ test centres [18][19]. **No DIANA call is named "counter-drone"/"C-UAS" verbatim**; C-UAS work sits under Sensing & Surveillance / Autonomy.
- **Innovation range (P).** Latvia's uncrewed-systems Innovation Range ran its first UAS/C-UAS TEVV campaign 9–13 Mar 2026 at Sēlija (allowing "high-speed and high-altitude interceptor flights"), one of five pilot ranges under NATO's Rapid Adoption Action Plan [7].
- **NIF (P).** Closed at "EUR 1 billion", 23 Allies as LPs (1 Aug 2023) [23]. Autonomy/UAS/counter-drone portfolio: ARX Robotics, Greenjets, Kelluu, Kraken, Stark, Tekever, Tytan — Tytan builds "AI-guided, low-cost interceptor drones" [24]. Disclosed rounds (**NIF's own ticket is never stated**): NIF "co-led a €30m all-European Series A" for TYTAN [25]; NIF joined STARK's "€500 million" round [26]; NIF "leads €15mn Series A in Kelluu" [27]; Greenjets' "$40 million Series A" included NIF [28].

## B4 — Exercises and procurement with stated figures

- **USD 40bn (P).** NATO's official text "NATO's Drone Edge" (7 Jul 2026): Allies are "Investing over USD 40 Billion in counter-drone capabilities over the next five years" and "Training five times as many drone operators … by the end of 2027" [2]. **No baseline year, national breakdown or methodology is published** — an official announcement, not an auditable assessment. **Documents disagree on currency:** the news article says NSPA contracted "hundreds of millions of dollars" for surveillance drones; Rutte's same-day transcript says "hundreds of millions of euros" [1][3].
- **Rutte, C-UAS Industry Day, 28 Jan 2026 (P):** "Drones are here to stay. Growing in quantity, growing in quality"; a "400%, so five-fold" increase for "surface-based air and missile defence"; a "Counter-UAS Package of Measures" (Oct 2025) [4][5].
- **Low-cost C-UAS demo** (Nowa Dęba, filmed 18 Nov 2025; published 9 Dec 2025, P): the "Merops" interceptor, "roughly USD 14,500" each — "about one tenth of the cost of the Shahed drones"; it had "taken down more than 1,000 [sic: 'kill'] Russian drones … inflicting more than USD 200 million in losses with only USD 15 million worth of interceptors"; "dozens" were loaned to Poland and Romania under Eastern Sentry [6]. Attributed to "US officials" — **not independently verifiable**.
- **Exercises (P):** Flytrap 4.5 (Putlos, 10–21 Nov 2025) [9]; C-UAS TIE 26 (Marknesse, ~300 participants) [10]; TIE 24 (450+ participants, 19 Allies + 3 partners; Ukraine's first participation) [11]. Ministers agreed additional counter-drone measures "under Eastern Sentry" on 15 Oct 2025 [13]. **No NSPA tender notice or framework-contract value verified — NOT VERIFIED** (portal blocked) [33].

## B5 — Doctrine and standards (public status)

- NATO promulgates doctrine via the NATO Standardization Office, whose live site is not publicly reachable (403). The only public title-level evidence retrieved is an archived snapshot (19 Jun 2021) of NSO's "List of Current NATO Standards", showing "only the current (promulgated) documents, bearing security classification appropriate for this WEB Site" [34]: STANAG 3700 Ed 8 → "Allied Joint Doctrine for Air and Space Operations" (AJP-3.3 Ed B, 2016); STANAG 3880 Ed 6 → "Allied Joint Doctrine for Counter-Air Operations" (AJP-3.3.1(B), 2010); STANAG 4670 Ed 5 → "Minimum Training Requirements for UAS Operators and Pilots" (ATP-3.3.8.1 Ed B, 2019); STANAG 7232 Ed 1 → "UAS Tactics, Techniques and Procedures" (ATP-3.3.8.2 Ed A, 2020).
- **No C-UAS-specific STANAG title appears in that public list.** Document bodies are not public, and the snapshot predates 2024–2026, so a newer unlisted C-UAS standard cannot be excluded — **NOT VERIFIED**.
