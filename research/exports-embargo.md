# Iranian Drone Exports, Transfers and Smuggling Routes; UN Arms Embargo Status

**Compiled 25 September 2026 · defence research only — capability, cost, routes and legal status; no construction, payload or targeting detail.**
Labels: **DEMONSTRATED** (documented in primary/UN/government material or physical evidence) · **CLAIMED** (asserted by a government or belligerent, not independently verified) · **INFERRED** (analytical judgement). Confidence: high/medium/low.

---

## 1. UN arms embargo status — the key correction: sanctions were re-imposed in Sept 2025

- **UNSCR 2231 transition (2023).** The Arms Control Association states plainly: "Iran was prohibited from importing and exporting certain missiles, drones, and related technologies without prior UN Security Council approval under Resolution 2231… UN restrictions on Iran's missile program expired in October" — i.e. 18 Oct 2023. **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2023-11/news/un-missile-sanctions-iran-expire
- The 48-state PSI statement (18 Oct 2023) said Iran's "provision of missile and UAV technology to its partners and proxies endangers international stability and escalates regional tension," and committed states to "undertake effective measures to interdict the transfer to and from Iran of missile-related materials, including those related to UAVs." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/en/newsroom/news/resolution-2231-transition-day-2627650
- **UNSCR 2231 snapback (2025) — this reverses the 2023 position.** The E3 triggered snapback on 28 Aug 2025; the UK FCDO states: "We welcome the re-instatement since 20:00 EDT (00:00 GMT) on 27 September 2025 of Resolutions 1696 (2006), 1737 (2006), 1747 (2007), 1803 (2008), 1835 (2008), and 1929 (2010) after completion of the snapback process as provided for in UN Security Council Resolution 2231." Res. 1929 (2010) is the instrument containing the Iran **arms embargo** and ballistic-missile prohibitions. **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://www.gov.uk/government/news/e3-joint-statement-on-iran-activation-of-the-snapback
- Security Council Report confirms the mechanism and the failed 19 Sept 2025 vote (4 in favour, 9 against, 2 abstentions): "If the resolution fails to be adopted… UN sanctions on Iran will be imposed at the end of the 30-day window on 28 September," reinstating "extensive restrictions on arms, finance, and shipping." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/whatsinblue/2025/09/iran-vote-on-a-draft-resolution-regarding-the-snapback-of-un-sanctions.php
- US Treasury corroborates: OFAC acted "in support of the September 27, 2025, reimposition of United Nations sanctions on Iran" and expects "the international community to fully implement UN snapback sanctions on Iran." **DEMONSTRATED, high (US interpretation).** https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0313
- **UNSCR 2216 Yemen embargo remains separately in force.** The 2023 Panel of Experts worked "[p]ursuant to paragraphs 14 to 17 of Security Council resolution 2216 (2015)… the targeted arms embargo" and under "paragraph 16 of resolution 2624 (2022)." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://docs.un.org/en/s/2023/833
- The embargo is still operative in 2025: the latest Panel report (S/2025/650, 17 Oct 2025) was "prepared in accordance with paragraph 3 of resolution 2758 (2024)." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=s/2025/650&l=en&t=pdf

## 2. Non-state actors

**Houthis (Yemen).** The UN Panel of Experts' final report (S/2023/833, Nov 2023) records: "With regard to the targeted arms embargo, the Panel notes large-scale violations involving military material, such as ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, anti-ship missiles, attack uncrewed aerial vehicles, waterborne improvised explosive devices…"; and "From November 2022 to June 2023, 254 uncrewed aerial vehicles, 100 engines for uncrewed aerial vehicles, nearly 5 tons of explosive precursor chemicals, 2 diver propulsion vehicles and various pieces of military equipment were intercepted by the Government of Yemen." The Panel concluded these seizures "show that the Houthis are drastically reinforcing… their arsenal of missiles and uncrewed aerial vehicles, in violation of the targeted arms embargo." It documented Iranian-linked hardware, e.g. material whose "markings and packaging… are consistent with those manufactured in the Islamic Republic of Iran," 100 DLE-170 UAV engines seized at the Shahn border crossing (31 Dec 2022), and Quds cruise-missile debris. **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://docs.un.org/en/s/2023/833
- **Palestine-2 / Toufan missiles — CLAIMED/INFERRED.** A specialist reference work describes Palestine-2 (Falastin-2) as "an Iranian-derived, two-stage solid-propellant… missile fielded by Yemen's Houthi movement," noting Houthi claims of "hypersonic" performance (Mach 16, 2,150 km) that "independent assessments" reject, and that it "entered operational use in 2024." **CLAIMED/INFERRED, medium-low** (commercial outlet, partly paywalled). https://www.battlepolicy.com/palestine-2/
- **2025 UN finding (strongest single item).** S/2025/650: "In June 2025, the Yemeni armed forces seized more than 750 tons of ammunition and hardware intended for use by the Houthis. The seizure included several advanced cruise, anti-ship and anti-aircraft missiles that displayed characteristics similar to those of weapons produced in the Islamic Republic of Iran, such as the Ghadir anti-ship cruise missile, Shahed uncrewed aerial vehicle, AM-50 Sayyad anti-materiel rifle and the Misagh series man-portable air defence system. The shipment also included manuals in Farsi and Iranian police DNA kits." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=s/2025/650&l=en&t=pdf
- **Sammad/Wa'id.** The 2023 Panel annexes list "Attack UAV Wa'aed" and "Attack UAV Samad-2" as items previously documented (S/2019/83), and Samad-3 airframes carrying DLE-170 engines. **DEMONSTRATED (existence), high; Iranian origin INFERRED, medium.** https://docs.un.org/en/s/2023/833
- **Note on component sourcing:** much 2024–25 Houthi drone materiel seized originated in **China**, not Iran — e.g. the May 2025 Djibouti inspection (UNVIM) found "concealed military materiel in two out of four containers originating from China… drones, drone components, full drone kits, remote control units… GPS modules, propellers." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=s/2025/650&l=en&t=pdf

**Hezbollah (Lebanon).** UNIFIL/1701 reporting records IDF claims, not UN verification. S/2025/460 (11 July 2025): the IDF struck "Hezbollah infrastructure used to store UAVs"; on 5 June the Israeli Defence Minister "instructed the IDF to … destroy buildings used to manufacture and store drones by the terrorist organization Hezbollah"; and on 20 April the IDF said it killed a Hezbollah commander who "collaborated with Iranian operatives to facilitate the transfer of weapons and funds into Lebanon, including through the Beirut International Airport." The report also records Iran's formal denial (S/2025/242): "The Islamic Republic of Iran, once more, categorically rejects this unfounded accusation." **DEMONSTRATED that the claims were made; the underlying transfers are CLAIMED, medium.** https://unscol.unmissions.org/sites/default/files/s_2025_460-en.pdf
- **Hamas/PIJ and Iraqi militias: NOT VERIFIED in this file.** I did not retrieve a primary UN, OFAC or peer-reviewed source establishing specific Iranian drone transfers to Hamas/PIJ or to named Iraqi militias; I therefore make no claim.

## 3. State transfers

**Russia (Shahed-131/136 → Geran-1/Geran-2).** CSIS: Russia "received its first shipment of Iranian-made Shahed-136 and Shahed-131 attack drones" in mid-August 2022; "a delegation of Russian defense industry representatives traveled to Iran to finalize a $1.75 billion deal authorizing production of the drone on Russian soil—specifically, at the Alabuga Special Economic Zone (SEZ) in the Republic of Tatarstan." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://www.csis.org/analysis/shahed-geran-how-russia-continues-reinvent-one-way-attack-drone
- **Alabuga output and target.** ISIS (Nov 2023): internal documents show a plan to scale to "up to 226 drones per month, for a total of 6000 drones produced by September 2025," with "1400 Shahed-136 drones" supplied by end-October 2023. **DEMONSTRATED (leaked documents + satellite imagery), high.** https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/visible-progress-at-russias-shahed-drone-production-site
- ISIS (July 2025): Alabuga "produces far more Shahed 136, aka Geran-2, drones than originally planned for under its franchise agreement with Iran"; "Alabuga reportedly wants to reach a near-term production level of 25,000 Shahed 136 drones per year." It also flags a *possible* future export role and reported North Korean interest, explicitly unconfirmed. **DEMONSTRATED (production), high; export/North Korea CLAIMED, low.** https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/major-developments-at-alabuga-sez-point-to-significant-expansion-in-military-drone-production
- Parallel Russian site: Izhevsk (Kupol/Almaz-Antey), product "Garpiya"/variant К. **DEMONSTRATED, high.** (CSIS, above.)

**Venezuela.** OFAC (30 Dec 2025) designated Venezuela-based Empresa Aeronautica Nacional SA (EANSA): "Since 2006, Iran and Venezuela have coordinated Iran's provision of Qods Aviation Industries' (QAI) Mohajer-series UAVs for Venezuela, which are re-branded in Venezuela as ANSU-series UAVs," and EANSA "maintains and oversees the assembly of QAI's Mohajer-series UAVs in Venezuela," including "QAI's sale of millions of dollars' worth of Mohajer-6 UAVs." It names the "Iranian Mohajer-2, known locally as Arpia or ANSU-100." **DEMONSTRATED, high (US government).** https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0347

**Sudan.** US AFRICOM's ADF magazine: RSF-posted imagery and Planet Labs satellite imagery "show Mohajer-6 drones and a ground-control vehicle on the runway at Wadi Sayyidna air base about 22 kilometers north of the capital, Khartoum," concluding "Iran is supplying weaponized drones to the Sudanese Armed Forces." **DEMONSTRATED (imagery), high for Mohajer-6 presence; supplier intent CLAIMED, medium.** https://adf-magazine.com/2024/02/safs-use-of-iranian-drones-threatens-to-destabilize-region/

**Tajikistan.** The May 2022 Ababil-2 line is **contested**. RFE/RL (Mar 2026): two senior Dushanbe officials said privately the factory never opened ("I am 1,000 percent sure that such a factory does not exist in Tajikistan"); Dushanbe's MFA in Oct 2022 neither confirmed nor denied it, saying only that Tajikistan "does not export military equipment to third countries." **CLAIMED, low — treat the production line as unverified.** https://www.rferl.org/a/iran-tajikistan-drone-factory-mystery-/33719984.html

**Ethiopia and Belarus: NOT VERIFIED.** I did not fetch a primary or reputable source substantiating Ethiopian Mohajer-6/Shahed-136 deliveries or Belarusian Geran production/assembly. No claim is made.

## 4. Smuggling routes

**Caspian Sea (Iran → Russia).** The Caspian Policy Center documents the Amirabad/Anzali → Astrakhan corridor (and onward via the Volga-Don Canal), reporting that AIS gaps cluster "near Iran's Amirabad and Anzali ports, as well as Russia's Volga River and its Astrakhan port," with ~600 AIS gaps by Russian-flagged ships in three months, and that in June 2023 the US administration "officially acknowledged that Iran ships drones to Russia over the Caspian Sea." **DEMONSTRATED (shipping-pattern data + US acknowledgment), medium-high** (think-tank synthesis of Lloyd's List/Foreign Policy/WSJ). https://www.caspianpolicy.org/research/security-and-politics-program-spp/dark-deals-on-the-caspian-how-iran-ships-drones-to-russia
- **Yemen land route (2025):** "[L]and border crossings are reportedly the primary smuggling routes currently in use. The land border crossings of Shahn and Sarfayt, between Oman and Yemen, are regularly used to bring in items intended for military use… includ[ing] uncrewed aerial vehicle control devices, uncrewed aerial vehicle servo motors, Global Positioning System devices for uncrewed aerial vehicles." **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=s/2025/650&l=en&t=pdf
- Later reporting points to Makhachkala as a hub subject to Ukrainian strikes; I did **not** open those sources, so I make no claim about them.

## 5. Sanctions/procurement architecture (2025)
OFAC's 12 Nov 2025 action targeted "32 individuals and entities" in Iran, UAE, Türkiye, China, Hong Kong, India, Germany and Ukraine supporting Iran's ballistic-missile and UAV production, including the Mado UAV-engine network (Mado "produces UAV engines used in Iran's Shahed-131 and Shahed-136 UAVs") and HESA (producer of "Ababil-series UAVs"). **DEMONSTRATED, high.** https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0313
