# Ecuador — Criminal gang use of drones, 2023–2026 (evidence notes)

Scope: documented misuse of drones by criminal gangs in Ecuador, outside declared inter-state warfare. Emphasis 2024–2026. All dates/locations below are as reported by the cited source; nothing here is inferred beyond what the source states.

**Baseline finding on autonomy (see §6):** every retrieved incident describes **manually/remotely piloted** systems — commercial or agricultural airframes, adapted to carry or drop a payload, flown line-of-sight or via a preloaded map, with a **human operator activating or releasing the device**. No retrieved source reports autonomous flight, onboard machine vision, or automatic target recognition (ATR) in Ecuadorian gang drone use.

## 1. Prison contraband and contraband-attack drones

### 1.1 Monte Sinaí / Penitenciaría del Litoral plot — 15 August 2026 (Guayaquil)
- **Date:** operation 15 Aug 2026; reported 16–18 Aug 2026.
- **Location:** Monte Sinaí, northwest Guayaquil; target a printed plan of the **Penitenciaría del Litoral**.
- **Actor:** two detainees (a Dominican national, alias "Nica", and an Ecuadorian woman, alias "Sara"), attributed by the **Fuerzas Armadas** to **Los Choneros**. Infobae notes the evidentiary basis for the gang link had not been made public.
- **What it did / was for:** seizure of **one drone, two explosive charges, batteries, a remote activation system, drone accessories, and a printed floor plan of the Penitenciaría del Litoral**, in a home operating as a manicure shop. Official hypothesis: the device was rigged to carry explosives into the prison; a detonation would create conditions for an escape. No target pavilion or beneficiary inmates identified.
- **Control:** remote-controlled. The presence of a **remote activation system**, batteries and a **printed paper map** evidences a human operator and manual targeting; the reporting describes no autonomous navigation or target recognition.
- **Sources:** Infobae 18 Aug 2026 (`infobae.com/.../un-dron-con-explosivos-y-un-mapa-de-la-mayor-carcel...`); El Universo 16 Aug 2026 (`eluniverso.com/noticias/ecuador/dos-sospechosos-fueron-detenidos-en-monte-sinai-con-explosivos-adheridos-a-un-dron-nota/`).
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` for the seizure and detainee count (Fuerzas Armadas/Police); the Los Choneros attribution is a `unverified claim` as of publication (no public case file).

### 1.2 Machala (CPL El Oro No. 1) — 4 March 2026
- **Date:** Wednesday 4 March 2026.
- **Location:** Centro de Privación de Libertad El Oro No. 1, Machala.
- **Actor:** unidentified; police said the attack was **aimed at the prison director**. No armed group named.
- **What it did:** a drone carrying an explosive device was flown toward a **pabellón**; SNAI reported the device was **neutralised before reaching its target** and did not detonate; no injuries among prison population or staff. Perimeter and internal protocols activated; SNAI and National Police investigating who sent it.
- **Control:** remotely piloted (a drone flown toward a specific pavilion and intercepted). No autonomy reported.
- **Sources:** El Comercio 4 Mar 2026 (`elcomercio.com/actualidad/ecuador/alerta-en-la-carcel-de-machala-neutralizan-dron-con-explosivos-en-el-cpl-el-oro-no-1/`); related: El Universo 4 Mar 2026 (`...ataque-explosivo-con-dron-en-carcel-de-el-oro-seria-en-contra-del-director...`).
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` — SNAI communiqué plus police attribution of the target.

### 1.3 Machala (CPL El Oro No. 1) — 19 November 2025
- **Date:** night of Wednesday 19 November 2025.
- **Location:** CPL No. 1 El Oro, Machala.
- **Actor:** unidentified. Context: 9 Nov 2025 massacre at the same prison (31 dead per SNAI; separately 5 dead/42 wounded then 2 more deaths in another incident), and on 19–20 Nov a drone explosive was reported directed at the **Sao-Box pavilion**; 190 inmates were later transferred amid a band dispute.
- **What it did:** a drone overflew the prison's airspace; custodial staff carried out a **controlled detonation** of the device. SNAI confirmed no dead, no wounded, no structural damage. El Universo's preliminary report mentioned damage in the internal zone between pavilions, later contradicted by SNAI. Investigators said this was the **first time drones were observed at Machala prison**, though the modality was already known elsewhere.
- **Control:** remotely piloted; detected visually by custodians while overflying and neutralised — consistent with line-of-sight operation, not autonomy.
- **Sources:** El Comercio 19 Nov 2025 (`elcomercio.com/actualidad/seguridad/dron-sobrevolaba-carcel-machala-detonado/`); El Universo 19 Nov 2025 (`eluniverso.com/.../dron-que-sobrevolaba-carcel-de-machala-pudo-ser-detonado-de-forma-controlada...`); extra.ec 20 Nov 2025 (Sao-Box target); expreso.ec 20 Nov 2025 (190 inmates moved).
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)`.

### 1.4 La Roca (Guayaquil, vía a Daule) — 5 September 2024
- **Date:** drone landed in the early hours of Thursday 5 September 2024; controlled detonation same day.
- **Location:** CPL Zonal 8 / "La Roca", maximum-security prison, vía a Daule, Guayaquil (holds Jorge Glas and other political figures).
- **Actor:** unidentified; the **Bloque de Seguridad** said the action was intended to "inhabilitar" the prison.
- **What it did:** a drone carrying explosives **landed on the roof** of a pavilion. GIR and Armed Forces deployed; the device was destroyed by **controlled detonation**; road access restricted and a nearby school evacuated. Damage to the prison roof; Bloque de Seguridad called it a foiled attack. Infobae reported the objective was to **facilitate escapes**.
- **Control:** remotely piloted — a drone deliberately landed on a roof, then detonated under human control.
- **Sources:** El Universo 5 Sep 2024 (`eluniverso.com/noticias/seguridad/dron-que-aterrizo-en-carcel-la-roca-fue-detonado-nota/`); Infobae 6 Sep 2024; Primicias 6 Sep 2024; Digital Journal 5 Sep 2024.
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` (Bloque de Seguridad / SNAI / GIR); the escape objective is an official hypothesis.

### 1.5 La Roca — 12 September 2023
- **Date:** Tuesday 12 September 2023 (drone left device at dawn; controlled detonation ~11:30).
- **Location:** La Roca, vía a Daule, Guayaquil.
- **Actor:** unidentified; the article situates the practice with "las bandas."
- **What it did:** a **large agricultural drone (reported value over USD 20,000)** placed an explosive device on the roof of a pavilion; the controlled detonation caused **serious damage to the roof**. La Roca was left inoperative. El Universo records that this was not the first time: bands had already used drones to move prohibited objects and to **drop explosive devices onto rival pavilions**.
- **Control:** remotely piloted. Notably, the operator must fly an agricultural airframe to a specific roof — manual precision flying.
- **Source:** El Universo 13 Sep 2023 (`eluniverso.com/noticias/seguridad/instalar-inhibidores-de-senal-y-sistemas-antidrones...`); La Hora 12 Sep 2023; CNN en Español 12 Sep 2023.
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` for the event (Policía released detonation video); the >USD 20,000 valuation is journalistic.

### 1.6 Durán — 26 July 2023 (largest documented drone-contraband seizure)
- **Date:** 26 July 2023.
- **Location:** Durán, Guayas (a housing development).
- **Actor:** unidentified narcos; **one prison guard (guía penitenciario) detained**.
- **What it did:** police seized **three large agricultural drones, modified, with an estimated payload capacity of up to 30 kg and an operational range of ~5 km**, intended to supply **weapons and drugs** to the Guayaquil prison. Drug blocks and firearms found in the same house.
- **Control:** remotely piloted. Crucially, experts quoted in El Universo note that drones can fly where there is no internet/Wi-Fi using radio control, and that the operator must ensure **the map is loaded into the system before flight** — i.e. pre-planned human piloting, not autonomous operation or autonomous navigation.
- **Sources:** El Universo 27 Jul 2023 (`eluniverso.com/.../decomisan-en-duran-tres-drones-que-se-habrian-usado-para-proveer-armas-y-droga-en-carcel...`); Primicias 27 Jul 2023; Infobae 28 Jul 2023.
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` for the seizure and guard's detention. The 30 kg/5 km figures are police-sourced specifications.

### 1.7 Puerto Bolívar and Bajo Alto, El Oro — 24–25 June 2024 (first confirmed drone-dropped explosive against houses)
- **Dates/locations:** attack 1 in the early hours of **Monday 24 June 2024**, barrio 4 de Abril, **Puerto Bolívar** (Machala canton); attack 2 at **~02:30 on Tuesday 25 June 2024**, sector La Poza, **comuna Bajo Alto**, El Oro.
- **Actor:** **Los Lobos** — police hypothesis is a **dispute between rival cells of Los Lobos over territory for drug retailing**.
- **What the drones did:**
  - **Puerto Bolívar:** an explosive was flown by drone toward **a pier (muelle)**; it **detonated in mid-air before reaching the target**, blowing out windows and damaging structures in neighbouring homes. No injuries. Confirmed by **Colonel Hugo Silva, police chief of Zona 7**. `official statement (police)`.
  - **Bajo Alto:** armed men on vehicles and motorcycles first shot at a mixed-construction home, then **flew drones to drop an explosive**, starting a fire that spread and **completely destroyed four houses** belonging to the same family. `official statement (police)` for the method; `reputable journalism` for the sequence.
- **Significance:** police explicitly described this as "**el nuevo método que los grupos delincuenciales organizados están utilizando para atacarse entre sí**" — drone-delivered explosives used **against civilian dwellings in inter-gang territorial disputes**, outside prisons. This is the clearest pre-2025 evidence of drone weaponisation beyond prison walls.
- **Control:** remotely piloted, with a human operator flying to a **specific building**; the Puerto Bolívar device detonating short of its aim point is itself evidence of manual release, not terminal homing.
- **Source:** El Universo 25 Jun 2024 (`eluniverso.com/noticias/seguridad/explosivos-transportados-con-drones-destruyeron-cuatro-casas-en-bajo-alto-provincia-de-el-oro-nota/`); El Universo 23 Oct 2024 (El Oro governor linked explosive attacks to **gang war over territorial control**).
- **Evidence quality:** `official statement (SNAI/police/military)` for police confirmation of drone use; the Los Lobos attribution is an official **hypothesis** (`unverified claim` as to individual responsibility).

### 1.8 Machala (CPL El Oro No. 1) — 4 March 2026, police detail
The El Comercio report (see §1.2) is corroborated by El Universo the same day: **Colonel Remigio Albiño, police chief of subzona El Oro**, stated the device was "**lanzado desde una aeronave no tripulada**" against the Machala prison, detonating at **~14:00** and damaging a **window of the director's office**; no injuries. He noted prior threats against prison authorities, that the prison is a declared security zone, and that **drones represent "un reto adicional para los controles convencionales"**, with coordination with the Armed Forces to reinforce perimeter surveillance and evaluate detection/neutralisation mechanisms. `official statement (police)`.

### 1.9 Other documented contraband-drone incidents (pre-2023 baseline, context)
- **13 September 2021, Penitenciaría del Litoral / Regional, Guayaquil:** SNAI and Police reported a drone attack with explosions damaging pavilions; inmates climbed onto roofs and **shot at the drones**. Primicias reported SNAI and Police **disagreed** over the characterisation. `official statement` (contested between agencies).
- **20 May 2022, La Aurora (km 17, Av. León Febres-Cordero):** three men killed by what authorities described as mishandling of explosives; **drone remains and C4** were found with the bodies, and authorities said the material was destined for the prison across the river. `official statement`.
- **21 October 2021, near Penitenciaría del Litoral:** police found a hideout from which drones carrying **arms and ammunition** were allegedly flown to a prison band; drones, explosives and weapons seized. `official statement`.
- **25 December 2024, prison under construction:** El Comercio reported a "terrorist" attack at an Ecuadorian prison under construction leaving one dead (no drone specified). `reputable journalism`.
- **2–4 December 2024, Penitenciaría del Litoral:** three **grenades** were detonated inside the prison (pavilion 3 toward the transitory pavilion); 8 inmates and 2 prison officials prosecuted for terrorism; attributed to the band **'Los Duendes'**. This was a thrown-grenade attack, **not** a drone attack — included to avoid conflating grenade and drone incidents. `court record` (prosecution) / `reputable journalism`.

### 1.10 Machala riot of 7–8 December 2025 — reported "dron distractor" (PARTIALLY VERIFIED)
- **Lead:** Primicias headline of **8 December 2025**, "Un dron distractor y explosiones, esto se sabe del motín en la cárcel de Machala que dejó 13 muertos", indicates a **distraction drone** was used alongside explosions during a Machala prison riot in which **13 inmates died**.
- **Status:** the Primicias article URL could **not** be resolved (Google News wrapper unresolved; not returned by Bing News RSS). The **riot and 13 deaths are corroborated** by other outlets (CBS News 8 Dec 2025; Ecuavisa 7 Dec 2025 "Aumenta a 13 la cifra de reos fallecidos en la cárcel de Machala tras explosión"; La República EC 7 Dec 2025) and by El Telégrafo's report that **five inmates received 40-year sentences** for the Machala prison riot producing 16 deaths.

If used, it is a **remotely piloted distraction/reconnaissance flight**, not an autonomous system.
- **Context:** per El Universo (19 Nov 2025), Machala had a **9 November 2025 massacre (31 dead per SNAI, reported elsewhere as 32/27 in separate incidents)**, a 16 November attempted riot, transfers of 23 then 87 inmates, and 80+ alleged Los Lobos members removed to Guayas — a sustained gang contest over the prison.

## 2. Drones in gang violence against police, military and rivals

- **Prisons as the primary target set.** Foreign Policy (13 Aug 2026), citing ACLED's Tiziano Breda, reports that **Ecuadorian gangs have tried to attack rivals by dropping explosives from drones in prison yards and have used drones to deliver drugs and weapons into prisons through open windows**. ACLED recorded **eight incidents involving drones in Ecuador between September 2023 and 17 July 2026, with no fatalities directly caused by the aerial devices.** `reputable journalism` summarising an ACLED dataset.
- **Beyond prison walls — El Oro, June 2024 (see §1.7).** Police confirmed drone-dropped explosives against **civilian houses** in Puerto Bolívar and Bajo Alto, in an inter-cell Los Lobos territorial dispute, with **four homes destroyed**. This is the strongest documented case of Ecuadorian gang drones being used offensively outside a prison. `official statement (police)`.
- **Threat to officials — Machala, March 2026 (see §1.8).** A drone-lofted device detonated against the **prison director's office window**; police attributed it to prior threats against prison authorities. `official statement (police)`.
- **No confirmed gang drone attack on police or military personnel in Ecuador** was retrieved for 2023–2026. Attacks on police in Ecuador during the period were predominantly by firearms, car bombs and thrown explosives by other means.
- **Targeting shift in 2026.** Infobae (3 Sep 2026) reports ACLED's finding that Ecuador's eight armed-drone episodes since 2023 were directed mainly at **prison pavilions holding gang leaders**, but that **2026 events included attacks on a prison director and on a private home** — which ACLED reads as a possible change in target selection. `reputable journalism` reporting an ACLED assessment.
- **Armed Forces INTERPOL-scale operation, 15 Aug 2026:** the "Operativo" that produced the Monte Sinaí seizure (above) is the clearest 2026 example of a **pre-attack drone team** being interdicted before launch. `official statement`.
- **Cross-border context (not an Ecuadorian-gang incident):** on **20 April 2026**, three Colombian soldiers were killed and two wounded by explosive-laden drones in rural **Ipiales, Nariño**, near the Ecuadorian border; the **Comandos de la Frontera** (CNEB dissident structure) was blamed by Colombia's Third Division. This demonstrates armed-drone maturity in the Ecuador–Colombia borderlands but is attributed to a Colombian armed group, not to Ecuadorian prison gangs. `official statement (military)`.
- **No verified Ecuadorian gang drone attack on a police or military unit was retrieved.** Attacks on police in Ecuador during the period were predominantly firearms/explosives by other means. Where ACLED counts "attacks on officials," the specific 2026 case retrieved is the **prison director** (Machala, March 2026).

## 3. January 2024 "conflicto armado interno" and the 9 January 2024 TC Televisión attack

- **9 January 2024:** hooded, armed men entered the **TC Televisión** studio in Guayaquil during a live broadcast; the attack triggered road closures, evacuations and general uncertainty in the city. This was the symbolic trigger for the Noboa government's designation of an internal armed conflict. **No drone involvement is reported in any retrieved account of the TC assault.** `reputable journalism`.
- **January 2024 aftermath:** the internal-armed-conflict designation produced mass detentions — El Universo reported **1,534 detainees in the first six days**, exceeding the population of 32 prisons. **No drone incident during the January 2024 emergency was retrieved.**
- **Two-year retrospective and renewal:** La Hora (7 Jan 2026) reviewed two years of the internal armed conflict and called for urgent changes; El Comercio (18 Jun 2026) reported Noboa **decreed a new internal armed conflict**. Neither retrospective retrieved ties drones to the January 2024 episode.
- **Assessment:** drones were a **pre-existing (since at least 2021) prison-focused phenomenon** in Ecuador, not an innovation introduced during the January 2024 crisis. The retrieved spike in armed-drone activity is **2025–2026**, and ACLED's regional dataset (approx. **670 armed-drone events in Latin America since 2018, over 72% between 1 Jan 2025 and 21 Aug 2026**) supports that timing.

## 5. Drone attacks at sea / on fishing vessels (2026)

**Critical attribution caveat:** the 2026 attacks on Ecuadorian fishing boats in the eastern Pacific are **not attributed to Ecuadorian criminal gangs**. Survivors and the crew accounts describe **military drone strikes** by foreign (predominantly **US**) forces, or by an unidentified "ghost ship," amid the US counter-narcotics campaign. Reporting is contested between survivor testimony, Ecuadorian human-rights complaints, and US statements denying intent to kill fishermen. These events must not be folded into the "gang drone" picture.

- **17 March 2026, ~160 nautical miles off Manta:** the crew of a Manta-based fishing boat (16 crew) testified that a **drone struck the vessel at ~13:40, exploding near the cabin, causing fire, sinking and abandonment at sea**; the men were located alive on 24 March by a Salvadoran vessel. Crew also alleged detention and mistreatment by foreign military personnel. `witness` (crew testimony at a press conference, reported by El Universo 8 Apr 2026).
- **Barco "Don Maca":** crew reported being **"bombarded with drones"** and sunk in the eastern Pacific; 22 occupants, salvage and rescue discrepancies, with 16 survivors recovered by El Salvador. `witness` + `reputable journalism`, contested.
- **August 2026:** CNN en Español, El País, ADN Radio and others reported a **"ghost ship"** allegedly attacking Ecuadorian boats with **drones dropping bombs**, with survivors demanding answers; El País (5 May 2026) headlined "drones that shoot and a mysterious blue merchant ship." `reputable journalism` / `witness`, attribution unresolved.
- **Institutional response:** Human Rights Watch and UN experts questioned the legality of the strikes; Ecuadorian fishing organisations prepared litigation; congress members from Manabí requested information. `official statement` / `reputable journalism`.

**Bottom line for §5:** no retrieved source attributes these maritime drone attacks to Los Choneros, Los Lobos, Tren de Aragua, Los Tiguerones, Águilas, Fatales or Lagartos. The actors are external state/military or unidentified.

## 6. Autonomy / machine vision / ATR — explicit finding

**There is no evidence of autonomous operation, onboard machine vision, or automatic target recognition (ATR) in Ecuadorian gang drone use in any source retrieved (2023–2026).** The evidence supports a **remotely piloted, line-of-sight or pre-mapped, dropped/landed-payload** characterisation:

1. **Human release and human detonation are described in every case.** Monte Sinaí (Aug 2026) involved a **"sistema de activación remota"**, batteries and a **printed plan** of the target prison — a planned, manually executed attack, not a self-targeting one (El Universo / Infobae, 16–18 Aug 2026).
2. **Defeat mechanisms presuppose a radio link and an operator.** The 2023 expert commentary in El Universo — the need for **signal inhibitors**, RF "pistol" jammers that "inhibit the drone's control signal so it lands," radar-based counter-drone sets, and even systems that **seize control of the drone** — only makes sense against manually piloted aircraft. The same article notes operators must **load the map before flight** when no internet/Wi-Fi is available — pre-flight human mission planning, not autonomous navigation.
3. **Line-of-sight detection and intervention work.** At Machala (Nov 2025) custodians **saw the drone overflying** and executed a controlled detonation; at La Roca (Sep 2024) a drone **landed on a roof** and was destroyed in place; at Machala (Mar 2026) the device was **neutralised before reaching the pavilion**. These are consistent with slow, manually flown, payload-carrying commercial/agricultural airframes, not terminal-phase autonomous systems.
4. **The airframes are commercial/agricultural, modified "artesanalmente."** ACLED's assessment, via Infobae (3 Sep 2026), is explicit: "**Los aparatos comerciales, adaptados artesanalmente para transportar o arrojar explosivos**" — commercially available models **modified rudimentarily**, purchasable from about **USD 600**. The 2023 Durán seizure was of **agricultural drones** modified for payload, range ~5 km.
5. **Even the regional frontier described is not autonomy.** ACLED notes that *some Colombian* structures have drones with **night vision or fibre-optic guidance** — fibre-optic is a **jam-resistant control link**, i.e. still a piloted aircraft, not an autonomous one. The Infobae report attributes this to Colombian groups, not Ecuadorian gangs. CNN en Español (7 Sep 2026) quotes Crisis Group's David Mora on commercial drones adapted to carry explosives: "**aunque no tengan demasiada autonomía, eso también está cambiando**" — i.e. autonomy is currently *low* and only a forecast; the same article's framing is of **remote piloting and surveillance**, not ATR.
6. **No source records any Ecuadorian gang drone with onboard target recognition, terminal homing, or AI-enabled target selection.** Absence of reporting is not proof of impossibility, but for a defence threat assessment the honest position is: **as of the last retrieved evidence (September 2026), Ecuadorian gang drone capability is remote-controlled with human-in-the-loop payload release; autonomy is absent.** ACLED's own projection is only that criminal and insurgent drone use will **increase in scale and sophistication over the next 12–24 months** — it does not claim Ecuadorian autonomy exists now.

**Implication:** the near-term counter-threat problem in Ecuador is **detection, jamming and interdiction of short-range, manually flown payload carriers**, including around prison perimeters and no-fly zones — not counter-ATR.

Findings were gathered via the Google News RSS and **Bing News RSS** interfaces plus direct outlet retrieval (El Universo, El Comercio, El Telégrafo, Primicias, Infobae, Foreign Policy, InSight Crime, CNN en Español).
