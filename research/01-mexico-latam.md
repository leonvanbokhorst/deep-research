# Documented Misuse of Drones by Drug Cartels in Mexico and Latin America, 2020–2026

**Scope.** Non-state small-UAS use **outside declared inter-state warfare**; defence threat understanding only — no payload, build or counter-detection detail. Ratings: **[O]** official/court, **[J]** journalism, **[W]** witness/community, **[C]** unverified; bracketed numbers cite the source list. Compiled 25 Sept 2026. **All cases below are remotely piloted; nothing evidences autonomy, machine vision or AI targeting.**

## 1. The 26 June 2020 García Harfuch ambush — no drone

**26 June 2020** (not 30 June), Paseo de la Reforma/Lomas de Chapultepec, Mexico City. **CJNG**; ~28 gunmen blocked the convoy of police chief **Omar García Harfuch**, firing **Barrett .50-calibre rifles and fragmentation grenades** — **400+ impacts**; two bodyguards and a passer-by died. **No drone featured:** BBC Mundo and Milenio omit one, the FGJ-CDMX case rested on firearms and grenades, and the convictions (12 defendants, 316 years; two more, 86 years) contain **no drone element**. It reads as **conflation** with the 2020–23 CJNG drone-IED wave and the unrelated **Aug 2025 US overflight of Valle de Bravo**. **Rating: [C]; contradicted by the court record.** Autonomy: N/A. [J][1][2][34]

## 2. CJNG "Operadores Droneros"

Cell wearing a quadcopter-and-skull patch, active from ~**2020** in Jalisco/Michoacán, ~12 members, trained by foreign instructors (US explosives specialist; Colombian drone operator). Per **InSight Crime's 2025 investigation** and **Aug 2026** update it operated in **Michoacán, Guanajuato, Chihuahua, Jalisco and Baja California**, attacking police in vehicles and buildings. **CJNG does not build drones — it adapts commercial off-the-shelf models** (DJI Mini 3 ~US$419; DJI Agras T40 sprayer ~US$25,000). [J][3][4][5][32]

**Official counts.** SEDENA: **5 attacks (2020) → 107 (2021) → 233 (2022) → 260 in H1 2023**; **605 for 2020–mid-2023**. NCITE: **221 incidents 2021–2025**. ACLED: **~670 armed-drone events region-wide since 2018, >72% in 2025–26**. Seizures: **12 (2024) → 139 (2025) → 46 in H1 2026**; Sinaloa 63, Michoacán 53, Tamaulipas 36. [O][6][7][8]

## 3. Attacks on police and military; the chemical question

- **11 Feb 2025, Presa del Rosario, Apatzingán** — drone-borne explosives destroyed a municipal patrol car; the drone's own camera shows four drops, one direct hit; officers unharmed; attribution "presunto". Remotely piloted. **[J/W][9]**
- **27–28 Jan 2025, Apatzingán** — attempt to down a **SEDENA helicopter**; InSight Crime: the unit "hit an Army helicopter with a combination of gunfire and a drone-borne IED". Damage unconfirmed. **[J][6][33]**
- **15–16 Oct 2025, Playas de Tijuana** — drones dropped improvised devices on the **state prosecutor's anti-kidnapping offices**; three vehicles damaged, US Consulate alert; the FGE opened a **terrorism investigation**. Count disputed (El País one; Proceso/Infobae two to three). **[O/J][10][11]**
- **25 Aug 2026, Parácuaro** — FGE confirmed a drone attack but said it **was not aimed at police**. **[O]**
- **Control case (no drone):** Chihuahua SSPE helicopter hit by **gunfire only**, Coyame-Ojinaga, **12 Sep 2026**. **[O][12]**

**Chemical payloads — disputed.** **3–4 April 2024, Coahuayana:** the Observatorio de Seguridad Humana de Apatzingán alleged CJNG drones dropped "chemical/asphyxiating bombs" causing respiratory distress. On **10 April 2024** Michoacán Segob secretary **Elías Ibarra Torres** reported **no evidence** — the SSP hazmat unit and SEDENA found no trace and the state health ministry **no hospital admissions** at Aquila, Coahuayana or Coalcomán. **Rating: [C], officially denied**; InSight Crime still repeats it.[13][14]

## 4. Other Mexican groups

- **Familia Michoacana / La Nueva Familia Michoacana** — Guerrero; **May 2023** Heliodoro Castillo (2 killed, ~600 displaced); **8–9 July 2026** Guajes de Ayala/Coyuca de Catalán, prompting federal deployment. **[W/J][5][15]**
- **Los Ardillos** — **6–9 May 2026** attacks at Tula, Xicotlán, Acahuetlán and Alcozacán, **"drone bombardments for over eight hours"**, displacing **800+ families**; CNI/CIPOG-EZ and residents, not officially confirmed. **[W/J][16]**
- **Los Viagras** — **Sept 2023** eight Colombians detained over a clandestine workshop adapting drone-borne explosives; **Sept 2024** ex-Colombian soldier Elkin Pérez Loaiza "El Tigre" arrested training the group in IED manufacture. **[O][5]**
- **Cárteles Unidos** — named with CJNG and Santa Rosa among the heaviest drone users; fewer specific incidents. **[J][6][17]**

## 5. Beyond Mexico

- **Brazil (PCC, Comando Vermelho)** — drones first appear as **prison-contraband carriers from 2014**. In the **28 Oct 2025 "Operativo Contenção"** (Rio's Penha and Alemão complexes) CV **dropped bombs/grenades on police**; a detained Navy sailor had built grenade-release attachments and trained members. Main role remains **reconnaissance**. Remotely piloted. **[O/J][18][19][20]**
- **Colombia** (guerrillas, not cartels — context) — ELN/FARC dissidents fly **thermal-imaging surveillance drones**; ACLED places **97 of 182** LatAm armed-drone municipalities in Colombia. **[O/J][6][7]**
- **Peru** — **La Pampa, Madre de Dios:** detained ex-army member Jhon Berrú Rivadeneira testified rival gang "Los Malditos del Oro" used drones carrying explosives and grenades; police were unaware. **Single witness — [W], weak.**[21]
- **Chile** — no offensive attack found; analytical coverage plus an official prison-smuggling case. **[J/O][22]**
- **Argentina / Paraguay** — **no documented offensive cartel drone attack surfaced**; PCC has a Paraguayan border presence but no confirmed drone case. **An evidence gap, not a cleared negative.**

## 6. FTO designations and Mexican legal reform

**20 Feb 2025**: Cártel de Sinaloa, **CJNG**, Cártel del Noreste, **La Nueva Familia Michoacana**, Cártel del Golfo, **Cárteles Unidos**; **16 July 2026**: Cártel de Juárez (La Línea) and **Los Viagras** — **eight Mexican FTOs**. Mexico's **Senate approved** a 2024 reform criminalising illicit drone use (published in the **DOF**), penalties proposed from **10 years** (Michoacán) to **60 years** federally. On **17 Aug 2026** the US and Mexico signed a **declaration of intent** on drone/counter-drone cooperation; the US used an **Army laser to down three cartel-linked drones** near the Texas border in late Aug 2026. **[O/J][5][23][24][25][26]**

## 7. Autonomy and machine vision — absent

Experts told CNN (Sept 2026) there were **no regional reports of kamikaze drones by organised crime**; a Colombian military tally claims a first one-way attack on **24 Jan 2026** (Patía, Cauca) — one-way flight, **not machine-vision autonomy**. Crisis Group's David Mora: drones "don't have too much autonomy, but that is also changing". El País's "vuelo autónomo" denotes **GPS/waypoint flight**, not target-seeking AI; InSight Crime cites "AI-guided explosive drones" **only as a Ukraine/Russia precedent**; DroneXL's "AI" refers to **fraud and TikTok recruitment**. Human C2 persists via video downlink and manual release; jamming works. **Rating: absence of evidence — treat autonomy claims as [C].**[6][7][27]
