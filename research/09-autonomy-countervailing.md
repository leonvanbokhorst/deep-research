# SECTION A — Is there credible evidence of autonomy or machine vision in non-state drone misuse?

## A1. Headline finding: autonomy is absent; FPV and manual/remote control dominate

No verified seizure, court case, forensics report or official assessment located in this sweep substantiates machine-vision target recognition, facial recognition, geofencing defeat or autonomous engagement by a criminal or terrorist drone. Documented misuse is (i) hand-flown FPV with a human pilot watching a live video feed, (ii) manual radio/line-of-sight control for surveillance and payload drop, or (iii) pre-programmed GPS/waypoint flight. Every "swarm" label found refers to several drones flown simultaneously by several human pilots, not emergent autonomous coordination.

## A2. Latin America: FPV and video feeds, but pilots on the sticks

- **Brazil (Rio).** Civil Police state criminals control drones manually, mainly for surveillance of police and rivals. Seized devices were "adapted in an improvised way with plastic and ropes" — payloads lashed to commercial airframes; police imagery shows criminals holding controllers. *(G1/Globo, 22 Jul 2026 — MED)* https://g1.globo.com/rj/rio-de-janeiro/noticia/2026/07/22/investigacoes-drones-do-crime-no-rj.ghtml
- **Colombia.** FT reporting found FARC-dissident and ELN units using FPV "kamikaze" drones, but a Colombian FPV pilot said the cartels still **lack two critical skills**: advanced encrypted pilot–drone communications and correct physical payload installation. The capability frontier is piloting and payload engineering, not autonomy. *(Ynet citing FT, 6 Aug 2026 — MED)* https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1gs3xf8gg
- **"Swarm" claim unverified.** A Colombian Army commander described FARC-dissident "enjambres de drones" in Cajibío (Aug 2026) with no technical evidence of autonomous swarming; the same reporting describes repeated human-piloted attacks. *(El Tiempo, 14 Aug 2026 — LOW/MED)* https://www.eltiempo.com/justicia/conflicto-y-narcotrafico/enjambres-de-drones-12-militares-heridos-y-civiles-evacuados-la-ofensiva-que-sacude-a-cajibio-cauca-3578450
- **"Higher autonomy" is aspirational.** A US government assessment reported by the Washington Post says cartels "seek drones with greater autonomy and payload capacity" and study jamming evasion via fibre-optic control — a control-link upgrade, not machine autonomy. *(Informador/El Universal, 24 Sep 2026 — MED)* https://www.informador.mx/mexico/crimen-organizado-carteles-elevan-capacidad-en-el-uso-de-drones-20260924-0017.html
- **Analyst framing.** GI-TOC's Paddy Ginn and Crisis Group's David Mora describe "structured and repeatable operational use" of **commercial drones adapted** to carry explosives — supervised remote control throughout. *(CNN, 9 Sep 2026 — MED)* https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/drones-becoming-organized-crime-weapon-100029195.html
- **Scale, not sophistication.** ACLED: ~670 armed-drone events region-wide since 2018, >72% between Jan 2025 and Aug 2026; Colombia went from 38 attacks (2024) to 149 (2025) and 12 to 41 affected municipalities. Fibre-optic drones are used specifically to defeat jamming — by definition human-piloted over a tether. *(InSight Crime, 26 Aug 2026 — MED)* https://insightcrime.org/news/cheap-drones-are-a-costly-problem-for-governments-in-latin-america/

## A3. Islamic State and jihadist claims

IS set up a drone unit by ~2016–17 for reconnaissance, propaganda and grenade dropping; its January 2018 multi-drone attack on Russia's Hmeimim air base was defeated and used crude commercial-derived airframes. *(Small Wars Journal, 30 Aug 2026 — LOW/MED)* https://smallwarsjournal.com/2026/08/30/importing-warfare-the-rise-of-cartel-drone-operations/ The 2017 Pentagon SOLIC forecast that a non-state actor would fly a drone across the Atlantic within five years remains unmet. Hezbollah and Hamas are today's most advanced jihadist-adjacent users: **fibre-optic FPV**, human-piloted, jam-resistant. *(Defense News, 23 Jun 2026 — MED)* https://www.defensenews.com/global/mideast-africa/2026/06/23/frustrating-israel-fiber-optic-killer-drone-technology-has-arrived-in-southern-lebanon/ UNOCT runs UAS **digital-forensics** training — an official premise that recovered drones are examinable commercial systems. *(UNOCT, 2022 — HIGH)* The only credible autonomy claim found is Ukrainian and belongs to **inter-state warfare**: a one-off "Terminator mode" test, no video evidence, national ban on AI in final target interception. *(Ars Technica, 12 Jun 2026 — MED)*

## A4. Machine vision: demonstrated in the lab, not in misuse

- Andon Labs showed publicly available AI models can write code letting a ~US$100 consumer drone stalk a person using facial recognition. A **research evaluation**, with no criminal deployment, seizure or case following. *(NBC News, 5 Aug 2026 — MED)* https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-wrote-code-make-100-drone-stalk-someone-using-facial-recognition-rcna590642
- Europol's 2025 *The Unmanned Future(s)* characterises criminal exploitation as "opportunistic, remote-controlled, commercially available" UAS, with intelligence-gathering the more enduring threat. It documents open-source autonomy stacks (ROS 2) and swarm interest but evidences **no criminal autonomous deployment**. *(Europol, 2025 — HIGH)* `sources/europol-unmanned-futures.pdf`
- CSIS: fully autonomous weapons are "not yet a battlefield reality" even in Ukraine; drones increasingly have autonomous **navigation** while humans retain overall control. *(CSIS, 10 Jun 2026 — HIGH)* https://www.csis.org/analysis/defining-autonomy-why-software-not-drones-will-decide-next-war

## A5. The autonomy gap

1. **Control-link advantage beats autonomy.** FPV with fibre-optic or cellular control delivers precision without perception, classification or rules-of-engagement engineering.
2. **Cost.** FPV airframes cost ~US$600–1,200 in Colombia and Mexico; a defensible machine-vision stack costs far more and is fragile in cluttered urban terrain.
3. **Skill transfer is piloting, encryption and payload mounting — not AI engineering.** *(Informador 24 Sep 2026; Ynet/FT 6 Aug 2026)*
4. **Counter-UAS economics currently favour the defender:** USNORTHCOM reports 300+ cartel-linked drones stopped in 2026 and a ~75% fall in Rio Grande Valley incursions after laser employment. *(CBS News, 7 Sep 2026 — MED)* https://www.cbsnews.com/news/laser-weapon-us-military-drone-activity-us-mexico-border/

# SECTION B — Countervailing evidence: failure, marginality and disruption

## B1. Failed attacks, duds, no casualties

- **Tijuana, 15 Oct 2025.** A drone dropped three grenades on the Baja California prosecutor's anti-kidnapping unit; **one detonated**, damaging five private and two official vehicles. **No injuries**; Navy elements tried to down the drone and failed. *(Publimetro, 16 Oct 2025 — MED)* https://www.publimetro.com.mx/noticias/2025/10/16/terror-en-tijuana-atacan-con-drones-explosivos-la-fiscalia-de-baja-california/
- **Decapitation attempts failed.** Drones aimed at Nicolás Maduro (2018) and Sudan's Gen. al-Burhan (2024) left both unharmed; both are still mis-cited as proof of drone lethality. *(El Periódico, 6 Jun 2026 — MED)* https://www.elperiodico.com/es/internacional/20260606/drones-guerrillas-narcos-terroristas-america-latina-africa-130947721
- **Kurdistan PM's office, 17 Aug 2026:** two drones, **no casualties reported.** *(Reuters via Al Jazeera — MED)* https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/iraqi-kurdish-pm-barzanis-office-targeted-in-drone-attack

## B2. Marginality versus firearms, vehicle bombs and IEDs

Cartel explosive escalation is fastest in **car bombs and ground IEDs**, not drones: the September 2026 Zacatecas car bomb (~200 kg mining explosive, 11 injured) had more blast effect than any documented cartel drone attack. Cartel drone attacks are numerous and psychologically potent but individually low-casualty, and >60% target civilians rather than hard military targets. *(AP via Yahoo, 4 Sep 2026 — MED)* https://ca.news.yahoo.com/why-mexican-cartels-increasingly-using-172436793.html Drones changed *tactics* far more than *mass-casualty outcomes*.

## B3. Disruption and counter-drone success

- **US border, 2026:** 300+ cartel-linked drones stopped, ~100 in August alone, and a ~75% drop in Rio Grande Valley incursions after lasers (officials concede route changes may contribute). *(CBS News 7 Sep 2026; Fox News 3 Sep 2026 — MED)*
- **Colombia:** ~96% of attempted drone attacks claimed neutralised by RF jamming and physical destruction; a projected US$1.68bn *Escudo Nacional Antidrones* plus import restrictions at Cartagena and El Dorado. *(Ynet/FT 6 Aug 2026; InSight Crime 26 Aug 2026 — MED)*
- **Brazil:** Rio's Civil Police created a dedicated counter-UAS unit (COANT) in March 2026; São Paulo has localised analogues. *(G1 22 Jul 2026; InSight Crime 26 Aug 2026 — MED)*

## B4. Overstated claims

- **"Killer drone" and "swarm" framing** outruns forensics: the airframes are commercially unmodified or crudely modified, hand-flown, with plastic-and-rope payload attachments; Colombian "swarm attacks" are multi-pilot harassment, not autonomy.
- **Official downplaying:** Mexico's Defence Secretary attributed most border drone activity chiefly to **migrant smugglers** doing reconnaissance, not attack missions. *(Punch/AFP, 4 Sep 2026 — MED)*
- **Attribution caution:** the most dramatic 2026 European cases (Leipzig and other German airports) are attributed to **state-linked hybrid activity**; one reported drone was steered over the mobile phone network — remote control, not autonomy. *(Guardian, 5 Aug 2026 — MED)*

## B5. Criminological position

No located study shows drones becoming a mass-casualty terrorist weapon. The 2017 Pentagon SOLIC forecast of non-state drone reach remains unmet, and CSIS still places full autonomy outside battlefield reality for state armies. Europol, ACLED, InSight Crime, GI-TOC and Crisis Group converge on **capability diffusion without lethality escalation**: more attacks, more actors, more surveillance value, but casualties per attack low relative to firearms and vehicle-borne IEDs. Binding constraints are pilot skill, payload engineering, counter-UAS economics and — for autonomy — perception, target classification and operational/legal risk.
