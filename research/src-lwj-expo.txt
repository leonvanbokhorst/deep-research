Title: Iran displays missiles and drones at Baghdad military expo

Published Time: 2025-04-22T10:11:20-04:00

Markdown Content:
![Image 1: Iranian missile at Baghdad expo](https://www.longwarjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/iran-missile-baghdad-expo-800-1024x574.jpg)

An Iranian missile displayed at the 2025 International Defense Exhibition in Baghdad. (SSN)

An Iranian delegation led by the country’s Ministry of Defence and Armed Forces Logistics (MODAFL) attended the 13th International Defense Exhibition (IQDEX) in Baghdad on April 20, boasting of a range of military technologies. The event, attended by 150 companies from approximately 20 countries, featured some of Iran’s most advanced missile and unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) systems.

Among the [equipment](https://www.hamshahrionline.ir/news/939280/%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84%DB%8C%D9%86-%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4-%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C-%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D8%AE%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C) were the Mohajer and Shahed drone series. The Mohajer 10, with its 300-kilogram payload and 2,000-kilometer range, was presented alongside the less-advanced Mohajer 6. The Mohajer drones, which bear [similarities](https://www.businessinsider.com/americas-foes-unveil-drones-closely-resembling-us-military-reaper-2023-8) to the American MQ-9 Reaper, also have surveillance capabilities. In addition, Iran highlighted the Shahed 136 UAV, a loitering munition or “suicide drone” known by its Russian designation, the Geran-2. The Shahed 136 has a larger warhead and greater range [compared](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/russias-iranian-made-uavs-technical-profile#:~:text=The%20range%20of%20the%20smaller%20Shahed%20131,as%20anywhere%20between%201%2C000%20and%202%2C000%20km.) to its predecessor, the Shahed 131, which Russia has used against Ukrainian infrastructure. These drone models can [reportedly](https://t.me/voenacher/29532) be used in coordination, with the Mohajer 6 guiding the Shahed 136 to its targets.

Another notable [display](https://www.hamshahrionline.ir/news/939280/%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84%DB%8C%D9%86-%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4-%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C-%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D8%AE%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C) was the Rezvan loitering drone, first unveiled in January 2025. The Rezvan, [employed](https://www.tasnimnews.com/fa/news/1403/10/24/3237576/%D9%BE%D9%87%D9%BE%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D9%BE%D8%B1%D8%B3%D9%87-%D8%B2%D9%86-%D8%B1%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD%DB%8C-%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%A7%DB%8C-%D9%87%D9%85%D9%87-%D9%81%D8%B5%D9%88%D9%84) by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Ground Forces (IRGC-GF), is a suicide UAV with a range of 20–50 kilometers and a 4-kilogram warhead.

The exhibition also [featured](https://www.hamshahrionline.ir/news/939280/%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84%DB%8C%D9%86-%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4-%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C-%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D8%AE%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C) several ballistic missiles, including the [IRGC-developed](https://www.mashreghnews.ir/news/1421288/%D8%B2%D9%87%DB%8C%D8%B1-%D8%AC%D8%AF%DB%8C%D8%AF%D8%AA%D8%B1%DB%8C%D9%86-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%B4%DA%A9-%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%A7%D8%AA%DB%8C-%D8%B3%D9%BE%D8%A7%D9%87-%D8%A8%D8%A7-%D8%A8%D8%B1%D8%AF-%DB%B5%DB%B0%DB%B0-%DA%A9%DB%8C%D9%84%D9%88%D9%85%D8%AA%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AF%D8%B1%D9%86-%D8%AA%D8%B1%DB%8C%D9%86) Raad-500, also known as “Zohair,” which has a range of 500 kilometers. Iran showcased the solid-fuel Zolfaqar missile with a range of 700 kilometers, the same missile the Tehran-backed Houthis [reportedly](https://www.tasnimnews.com/en/news/2021/03/04/2463922/yemeni-missile-hits-saudi-aramco-oil-facility-spokesman) used to target Saudi Arabia’s Aramco site in 2021.

Additionally, Iran [touted](https://www.hamshahrionline.ir/news/939280/%D8%A7%D9%88%D9%84%DB%8C%D9%86-%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4-%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C-%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D8%AE%D8%A7%D8%B5-%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C) its air defense systems, such as the Sevom Khordad (3 Khordad), a road-mobile medium-range missile system, and the Noh-e Dey (9 Dey), a short-range system. The [Arman](https://jangaavaran.ir/9-dey-system/) system, a medium-range, high-altitude air defense system designed to protect sensitive areas from a variety of aerial threats, was also featured.

**Iran’s Ministry of Defence and Armed Forces Logistics**

MODAFL [oversees](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2431#:~:text=MODAFL%20is%20responsible,as%20the%20Houthis.) the development, production, financing, and logistics of Iran’s defense sector. The Iranian government allocates billions of dollars in oil revenue to support its military, which also supplements its budget through international arms sales. MODAFL’s subsidiaries produce advanced weapons, including ballistic missiles and drones, that are used by Iran’s military and the IRGC or exported abroad.

The ministry has been sanctioned by the US Treasury for its role in [missile development](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2510), [drone manufacturing](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2024/02/24/u-s-sanctions-iranian-entities-for-helping-russia-build-drones/), and [nuclear proliferation](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2194). MODAFL has [facilitated](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2024/02/24/u-s-sanctions-iranian-entities-for-helping-russia-build-drones/) the export of Shahed-131 and Shahed-136 drones to Russia and exports Iranian drone technology to [regime proxies](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sm639).

**Iran’s evolution from arms supplier to proliferator**

Tehran’s participation in Iraq’s defense exhibition reflects a pattern of Iran positioning itself as a regional arms exporter and military patron. Regime-affiliated media noted that Iraqi Prime Minister Mohammed Shia al Sudani, known for his close ties to Tehran, inaugurated the exhibition and welcomed the import of military technology to enhance Iraq’s defense capabilities. Iranian officials [hailed](https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/512091/Iran-showcases-defense-capabilities-at-major-Baghdad-military) the exhibition as a platform to deepen military cooperation between Iran and Iraq, with IRGC media [highlighting](https://www.tasnimnews.com/fa/news/1403/02/02/3072272/%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4-%D9%BE%D9%87%D9%BE%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%87%D8%A7-%D9%88-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%B4%DA%A9-%D9%87%D8%A7%DB%8C-%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86%DB%8C-%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D9%86%D9%85%D8%A7%DB%8C%D8%B4%DA%AF%D8%A7%D9%87-%D8%A8%D8%BA%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF) the potential to supply weapons to Iraq’s national army.

In addition to Hezbollah and the Houthis, Iran’s drones have been [used](https://nationalinterest.org/feature/iran-creeping-international-arms-market-212318) by state actors across various regions. Russia has relied heavily on the Shahed-136 drones, and Venezuela operates the Iranian Mohajer-2 drones (ANSU-100) and the ANSU-200, which resemble the Shahed-171. In Africa, Iran’s Mohajer-6 drones have been deployed by Ethiopia and Sudan in their internal conflicts. Armenia was also reportedly involved in a $500 million arms deal with Iran that includes drones.

Iran has also facilitated the establishment of local drone production abroad, including in Tajikistan, where it opened a factory for the Ababil-2 UAV. The Islamic Republic has previously attended defense expos in Malaysia, Qatar, Iraq, Saudi Arabia, Russia, and Serbia.

Janatan Sayeh is a research analyst at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies focused on Iranian domestic affairs and the Islamic Republic’s regional malign influence.
