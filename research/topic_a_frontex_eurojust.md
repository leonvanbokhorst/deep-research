# Topic A — FRONTEX / EUROJUST / CEPOL: drone & UAS primary-source evidence

All sources are **PRIMARY** (agency documents/statements). No journalistic/secondary source was reachable — public search engines were blocked — so **no independent corroboration exists in this note** (material limitation). Dates are the agency's own published dates.

## A6. FRONTEX

**Correction to the brief.** FRONTEX publishes a **two-year** "Risk Analysis for YYYY/YYYY" series; there is no annual "Risk Analysis for 2024/2025/2026". Dates read from `<span class="date">` on each publication page [36].

- **Risk Analysis for 2023/2024** — 2023-09-18 [26]
- **Risk Analysis for 2024/2025** — 2024-07-18 [27]
- **Risk Analysis for 2025/2026** — 2025-06-03 [28]
- **Risk Analysis for 2026/2027** — 2026-06-17 [29]
- **Strategic Risk Analysis 2024** — 2024-10-16 [30]
- **Consolidated Annual Activity Report (CAAR) for 2025** — 2026-08-11 [31]

**(i) Criminal drone use — OFFICIAL ASSESSMENT, largely forward-looking.**
- RA 2026/2027 p.16: *"The use of semi-submersibles, autonomous vessels and fixed-wing drones flying at low altitude from the Moroccan coast toward the Cadiz area to evade radar is rising sharply and is expected to intensify."* [29] — **EVIDENCE THIN:** no incident counts, seizures or case numbers appear anywhere in the report.
- RA 2026/2027 p.10: *"Methods of smuggling are expected to remain flexible, including… short distance drops and unmanned delivery tools."* [29]
- RA 2026/2027 p.7: *"the hostile use of drones and weather balloons are causing airport and airspace closures"* [29] — a **separate Russian/Belarusian hybrid stream**, not criminal smuggling.
- RA 2025/2026 p.9: *"Smugglers will continue to adapt… by using innovative methods such as drones, GPS-tracked balloons and rafts at river borders."* [28] — **OFFICIAL WARNING ABOUT THE FUTURE.**
- Earlier baseline: RA 2024/2025 p.9 — criminals "increasing usage of less traceable smuggling methods such as drones" [27]; RA 2023/2024 p.38 — drones in smugglers' "adjusted portfolio of modi operandi, in response to enhanced technical border surveillance means" [26]; SRA 2024 p.46 — *"The use of drones and other unmanned craft is already a reality…"* [30]

**(ii) FRONTEX's OWN UAS deployment — OFFICIAL, and small.** CAAR 2025 p.27: *"aerial means, 52 fixed wing aircraft were deployed, on average 2 per day, **2 unmanned aerial vehicles** and 13 helicopters."* p.161 inventory: **"Unmanned aerial vehicle 695"**; p.164 capability table targets **"MALE RPAS … 2 drones"** [31] — owned/contracted capacity far below stated ambition. Pilots: Tactical UAS (Bulgaria, *"Covering a surface area of 8,100 km2, and subject to independent red-teaming"*) and Counter-UAS Prize Contest (Portugal) [31]; PRs 2025-07-31 [32], 2025-10-31 [33] (four finalists named), 2026-07-17 [34]. EUROSUR/aerial strand: contracted *"aerial surveillance assets, including drones, covered a substantial share of operational requirements"*, CAAR 2025 p.14 [31].

**(iii) Cost — NOT VERIFIED.** No EUR figure for drones appears in the RA/SRA/CAAR set or PRs. A July 2026 ISR RFI lists cost *categories* (implementation, annual maintenance, customisation) with **no numbers** [35].

## A5. EUROJUST — largely a NEGATIVE finding

- **No drone-specific press release exists:** all 899 items across 90 listing pages enumerated; none concerns drones [7].
- **Annual Reports 2023/2024/2025** [8][9][10] contain **no drone/UAS content** on smuggling or organised crime.
- Only drone mention found: CAAR 2025 p.29 [11] — OSINT evidence in **core international crimes** (*"drones, 3D modelling, satellite imagery"*), unrelated to smuggling.
- **No Eurojust–Europol joint drone case found. NOT VERIFIED.**

**Flagged asymmetry:** FRONTEX calls criminal drone use "already a reality" and "rising sharply," yet the EU judicial-cooperation agency publishes **zero** drone prosecutions or case examples 2023–2026.

## A7. CEPOL / EU Innovation Hub / eu-LISA

**CEPOL — training confirmed (PRIMARY).** 3028/2023/WEB drones in counter-terrorism (30 Nov 2023) promises to *"List existing Counter Unmanned Aircraft Systems (C-UAS) technologies"* [12]; 3045/2025/WEB 'Drones: emerging threat to public spaces and critical infrastructures' (22 May 2025) [13]; 3042/2025/WEB '3D printing, drones, and AI' (10 Dec 2025) [14]; 81/2023/ONS UAV course, Poland, 9–12 May 2023, aimed at *"border control and police operations"* [15]; plus 92/2022, 78/2021, 59/2020, 100/2018 [16]. **No CEPOL report/OTNA dedicated to drones — training only.**

**EU Innovation Hub — drone work confirmed.** Europol main report **"The unmanned future(s): The impact of robotics and unmanned systems on law enforcement"**, sitemap lastmod **2025-12-08** [18], announced by newsroom item lastmod 2026-01-01 [19]. **CAVEAT: Europol pages are JavaScript-rendered; body text was NOT retrievable, so report contents are NOT VERIFIED — only title, URL, date.** Cross-link: CAAR 2025 p.50 records **"Establishment of a UAS and C-UAS cluster in the EU Innovation Hub (led by Frontex)"** [31].

**eu-LISA — UNREACHABLE.** `eulisa.europa.eu` and `www.eulisa.europa.eu` both fail DNS (`SERVFAIL`, no record — not merely down). **Nothing from eu-LISA could be verified.**

## Disagreement / competence gap flagged

CAAR 2025 p.42: *"operating small drones and operating in different configurations, including swarms, will require a re-think of rules of engagement. While detection, identification and tracking are in the remit of border and coast guard authorities, there is more work needed to address how to deal with neutralisation in terms of competences, both at national and European level."* [31] — official admission that **counter-UAS neutralisation authority is legally unsettled**; a legal, not merely technical, gap.
