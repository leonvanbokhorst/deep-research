# Airport and Critical-Infrastructure Disruption by Drones: Malicious, Careless, or Ambiguous?

**Compiled 25 September 2026 — defensive threat research only.** Evidence: **[O]** official/court record · **[T]** state or think-tank assessment · **[J]** journalism · **[U]** unresolved/contradicted.

**Framing:** of the IISS dataset of **144 UAV incidents over Europe, Aug 2024 – Feb 2026**, ~**48%** were over military sites, **18%** over civilian airports and **26%** over other critical infrastructure. Crucially, IISS states **no European government has publicly attributed a UAV sighting to Russia** — its "highly likely Kremlin campaign" finding is a think-tank judgement, not a state attribution. [T]

## 1. Gatwick, UK, 19–21 December 2018 — unresolved

First sighting **21:03 GMT 19 Dec**; the runway was **closed 33 hours**, reopened with a landing at **05:57 GMT 21 Dec**, and shut again **17:30–18:23 on 21 Dec**, affecting ~**1,000 flights and 140,000 passengers**. Police logged **170 reports, 115 credible**; Chief Constable **Giles York** cited **93 "credible" witnesses**. **No photograph or video of a drone ever emerged**, and two drones recovered nearby were ruled out. [O]

**Officials contradicted each other.** On 23 Dec DCS **Jason Tingley** said it was "a possibility there was never a drone" — later dismissed as a "miscommunication"; on 24 Dec York was "**absolutely certain**" a drone flew throughout, while conceding police drones "could have caused some level of confusion" and that after **26 sites searched** he did not think police had "found the drone responsible". [O]

**Arrests:** **Paul Gait and Elaine Kirk** were arrested on 21 Dec (12 armed officers, home searched), held **~36 hours** and **released without charge on 23 Dec**. Neither owned a drone and both were at work. York said he was "really sorry" but the grounds were "well-founded". [O]

**Correct a common conflation:** the reward was **£50,000**, *not* £200,000 — the latter is the **June 2020** out-of-court settlement (**£55,000 damages + £145,000 costs**) for wrongful arrest and false imprisonment. [O]

**The investigation closed 27 Sept 2019** — 18 months, **£790,000**, five forces, **nobody charged**. In April 2019 Gatwick's COO said the pilot "seemed to be able to see what was happening on the runway" and had picked a drone invisible to the airport's detector; Sussex Police called an "insider" a "credible line". [O]

## 2. UK aftermath, 2019–2026

- **Heathrow, 8 Jan 2019:** a drone sighting **halted departures** (~1 hour); detection radar followed. Gatwick installed a permanent counter-drone system **Nov 2019**, Heathrow **Jan 2020**. [O/J]
- **The independent review is still unpublished.** Sussex Police's review (**96 people "of interest"** eliminated) is withheld on "national security" grounds; PCC **Katy Bourne** endorsed this, and in **March 2026** The Register reported lawyers still mobilised to suppress it. [O/J]
- **Evidence decayed:** a **2024 FOI** showed the National Police Air Service logged **no drone sightings at Gatwick, 19–31 Dec 2018**. [O/J]
- **Verdict:** police and Gatwick **maintained "malicious attack"**; the Guardian's 2020 investigation argues **mass panic/misidentification**. No drone, no suspect, no court record — **unresolved and contested** [U].

## 3. Ireland, Germany and the "copycat" years

- **Dublin, 2023:** **4 Feb** — flights disrupted **45 min** after **two confirmed sightings**; **6 Feb** — ~**40 min**. **daa** demanded a Garda counter-drone system and longer sentences. **One man was charged in Feb 2023**; **by April 2024 two men were charged**, to be tried separately. [O] → **careless/recreational; the only strand with a court record.**
- **Frankfurt (FRA):** Germany's worst-affected airport — **35 disruptions by Aug 2025**, up from ~20 in each prior year. DFS logged **151 drone incidents at German airports in 2023, 161 in 2024 and 144 by Aug 2025**, with hobby pilots "frequently assumed" and perpetrators "usually unknown". Reports cluster on **May 2019, Feb 2020, Mar 2020, several times in 2023 and 11 Aug 2025**; **no individual July 2024 FRA closure could be substantiated**. [V/J]
- **Munich (MUC) — premise correction: the Bundeswehr deployment belongs to 3–4 Oct 2025, not May 2025.** Suspected drones on two consecutive evenings suspended operations twice, with **Amtshilfe** (military assistance) and thousands affected; a separate **2 May 2025** sighting was later given a **non-drone explanation**. On **30 May 2026** two pilots reported a drone at 09:03 — runways closed ~1 hour, **26 aircraft diverted**, nothing found. [V]
- **Berlin (BER):** closed ~**2 hours on 31 Oct 2025**, again **12 Mar 2026**, and ~**45 minutes on 23 Sept 2026** with nothing recovered; H1 2026 brought **28 sightings (seven suspending operations)** — the most of any German airport. **Bremen** closed 2–3 Nov 2025, where police explicitly **did not** assume espionage. [V]
- **Cologne/Bonn, 12 Aug 2026:** five sightings closed airspace ~30 min and diverted two Ryanair flights; **a drone pilot found near the airport became the subject of a criminal investigation** — the clearest single attribution in Germany. [V]
- **Leipzig/Halle, 4–5 Aug 2026 — the one confirmed malicious attack.** An FPV drone with **~800 g of explosive** struck an **An-124** near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft without detonating; a second drone was hit at ~400 m by a **Boeing 757 (flight 9LE)**, which diverted to **Hannover** with nose damage; a **third drone carrying RDX explosive** was later found. The drone had **two SIM cards and a 5G router** — readable as a "phone" by the counter-drone system — plus a relay antenna in a tree. The **Bundesanwaltschaft** took over; on **1 Sept 2026 Germany formally blamed Russia**, raised its threat level to "high", and closed the Russian House in Berlin and the Bonn consulate. [O] → **malicious, state-attributed, material evidence.**
- **Scale and response:** the BDL put drone disruption at **€60m in 2025 (€160m including follow-up)**. Dobrindt ordered the Bundespolizei drone-defence unit to grow from ~130 to **300 specialists at eight sites**, and interior ministers agreed to equip **eight airports** — Frankfurt, Munich, Berlin, Düsseldorf, Hamburg, Stuttgart, Leipzig and Cologne. [V]

## 4. Denmark and the Nordic wave, 22–30 September 2025

**22 Sept:** Copenhagen (CPH) suspended flights **~4 hours** after **2–3 large drones** were repeatedly seen in controlled airspace; **Oslo Gardermoen** closed briefly the same night. Copenhagen police chief **Jens Jespersen** called it a "**capable actor**" with "the capacity, the will and the tools", possibly operating "from a ship"; PM **Mette Frederiksen** called it "the most serious attack on Danish critical infrastructure to date". **NOST** escalated to highest readiness, and the National Police logged **500+ reports nationally in 24 hours (26 Sept)**. [O]

**Aalborg** (military airbase) closed from **21:44 CEST on 24 Sept** and again **25–26 Sept**; **Billund** closed an hour early on **25 Sept**; reports also at **Sønderborg, Esbjerg** and **Skrydstrup**. The **Danish Defence Command** confirmed drones over several military sites on **27 Sept**. In **Norway**, personnel at **Ørland** observed objects for **2.5 hours** on **27 Sept** and no-fly zones were widened from 5 to 10 km; at **Brønnøysund** on **28 Sept** police counted **five to six** drones but had **no suspects**. In **Sweden**, Gothenburg-**Landvetter** closed **6 Nov 2025**. [O/J]

**Russia suspected, not proven:** Danish authorities called it a likely **hybrid operation** and did not rule out launch from a vessel; **PET** chief Finn Borch Andersen said it "resemble[s] a model of hybrid warfare". [T]

## 5. The 2026 retraction — the key corrective

On **25 June 2026** Copenhagen Police Chief Inspector **Søren Thomassen** concluded police could **neither prove nor deny** there were drones over CPH on 22 Sept 2025 and, on the "capable actor" framing, said "**we were not clear enough with our reservations**"; Denmark's Defence Command found observations ranged from probable aircraft to confirmed drones, with **no validated baseline of normal drone activity**. [O] → **ambiguous; official framing retracted** [U].

By contrast **Øresund, 26 Feb 2026** is a rare *confirmed, attributed* case: the Swedish patrol boat **HSwMS Rapp** detected a drone launched from the Russian intelligence ship **Zhigulevsk** approaching Malmö (where the carrier *Charles de Gaulle* was anchored) and jammed it; Defence Minister **Pål Jonson** said technical data confirmed the launch. [O]

## 6. Spain — Madrid-Barajas

Barajas closed **3 Feb 2020** (>1 hour; Aena convened a committee with the Guardia Civil and Policía Nacional) and **6 Nov 2024** (≈1 hour, ~21 flights diverted). Decisively, on **7 Oct 2025 Telemadrid reported the Guardia Civil believes the drone that forced the Barajas closure never existed** — replicating Gatwick's evidentiary collapse. [O/J/U]

## 7. Wider Europe, the "drone wall" and NATO

**9–10 Sept 2025 (Poland):** 19–23 drones entered Polish airspace; airspace closed over **Warsaw Chopin, Warsaw Modlin, Rzeszów-Jasionka and Lublin**; the Lublin prosecutor called recovered drones unarmed "decoy drones". NATO launched **Operation Eastern Sentry on 12 Sept 2025**; **Baltic Sentry** has run since Jan 2025. The Commission's **"drone wall"**, promised by von der Leyen in 2025, was reported in **July 2026 as still not existing**. [O/T/J]

**Other European airports.** **Brussels-Zaventem closed 4–5 Nov 2025** after a drone sighting, with **Liège** also shut and Belgium rushing to buy counter-drone defences; **Eindhoven** closed ~2 hours on **22 Nov 2025**; **Luxembourg** suspended operations around **18–19 Sept 2026**, diverting 19 flights; **Rīga** diverted flights to **Tallinn** over drone suspicions on **10 June 2026**. Two cases are explicitly *not* drones: **Vilnius Airport closed on 13 Sept 2026 after birds were mistaken for a drone** (NATO fighters scrambled), and Munich's 6 Sept 2026 alarm, where the Bundespolizei **declined to classify it as a drone incident** at all. **No verified drone closure was found for Amsterdam Schiphol, Lisbon, Milan, Bucharest or Sofia** in 2024–26; Romania's incidents concern drones crashing on territory rather than airport closures. [J/U]

## 8. Critical infrastructure beyond airports

Incursions at Belgium's **Doel** nuclear plant (**3 drones, 9 Nov 2025**), nuclear-sharing bases **Kleine Brogel** (2 Nov 2025) and **Volkel** (Dutch military fired, **22 Nov 2025**, no wreckage), France's SSBN base **Île Longue** (5 UAVs, **4 Dec 2025**), **Brunsbüttel** LNG park from **Aug 2024** (prosecutors: "espionage for sabotage purposes"), and **Gösgen**/**Borssele** in **July 2026**. **No verified drone incident at a water utility or North Sea wind farm was found.** The widely cited "Swedish nuclear plant drones" date from **January 2022**, not 2024–26 — recent Swedish nuclear claims are **unverified**. A classified **BKA** report recorded several hundred drone incidents in Germany in 2025 and 165+ suspected sabotage cases. [O/T/J/U]

## 9. Attribution scoreboard

| Case | Official position | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| Gatwick 2018 | Malicious (police/airport) vs. mass panic | Unresolved; review withheld; no charge |
| Heathrow Jan 2019 | Unattributed | Official statement; no drone found |
| Dublin 2023 | Illegal/recreational flying | **Court record** — two charged |
| Frankfurt 2019–25 | Unattributed, recurrent | Journalism only |
| Munich/BER/Bremen/Hamburg 2025–26 | Unattributed | Official statements |
| **Cologne/Bonn Aug 2026** | Pilot identified near airport | **Criminal investigation opened** |
| **Leipzig Aug 2026** | **Russian state-directed** | Official attribution + explosives |
| Copenhagen Sept 2025 | "Capable actor" → **retracted Jun 2026** | Official retraction |
| Øresund Feb 2026 | **Russian, confirmed** | Official + technical data |
| Barajas 2024 | Drone "never existed" (Guardia Civil) | Official finding |
| Nuclear/energy sites | Mostly unresolved | IISS "highly likely"; **no state attribution** |

**Bottom line:** only **four** strands are evidentially solid — Dublin 2023 and Cologne/Bonn 2026 (prosecuted or investigated careless fliers), **Leipzig 2026** and **Øresund 2026** (confirmed, state-attributed). Two flagship cases — **Gatwick 2018** and **Copenhagen 2025** — collapsed into misidentification or unprovable claims, both after official positions were walked back. Two widely repeated premises also fail verification: Munich's army deployment was **Oct 2025, not May 2025**, and no **July 2024 Frankfurt** closure can be substantiated. Airport closures are a **weak standalone signal of hostile intent**; the reliable indicators are material evidence (recovered airframes, explosives, DNA, relay antennas), launch-platform correlation, and an identified pilot.
