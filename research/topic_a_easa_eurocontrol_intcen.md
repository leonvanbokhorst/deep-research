# Topic A — EASA, EUROCONTROL, EU INTCEN (primary sources, 25 Sep 2026)
*All items PRIMARY unless marked. Flags: **[A]**=official assessment; **[W]**=official forward warning; **[NV]**=NOT VERIFIED.*

## A2 — EASA / EU drone regulation
**Categories:** Reg. (EU) 2019/947 + 2019/945. EASA: *"open" … does not require a prior authorisation … nor a declaration*"; *"specific" … requires an authorisation … before the operation takes place*"; *"certified" … requires the certification of the UAS, a licensed remote pilot and an operator approved by the competent authority"*. Technical requirements cover *"remote identification"*, operational ones *"geo-awareness, a system that inform the remote pilot when a drone is entering a prohibited zone"* [1]. **[A]**

**U-space:** Reg. (EU) 2021/664/665/666. EASA: *"In January 2021, the U-space regulations entered into force"*; four mandatory services — *"UAS flight authorisation … Geo-awareness … Network identification … Traffic information"* [2]. EASA slide: *"Applicability date 26 January 2023"* [3]. **Disagreement:** EUROCONTROL (18 Mar 2024) mis-states *"the EASA U-space regulation, which came into force at the start of 2023"* [4] — conflating entry into force (2021) with applicability (2023).

**Remote ID / geo-zones:** direct remote ID mandatory open category *"from 1 Jan 2023"*, specific category below 120 m *"from 2 dec 2021"* [3]. CILs *"became applicable on 1 January 2024"*; since then *"only drones labeled with CILs are allowed to be operated under the standard scenarios of the Specific Category"* (3 Dec 2024) [5]. EASA geo-zone map is *"a preliminary version … not yet to be used for planning UAS operations"*, ED-318 format, Ireland/Spain [6]; Denmark's ED-318 data added *"for testing purposes"* (23 May 2025) [7]. **[A]**

**Counter-UAS:** *Drone Incident Management at Aerodromes Part 1* (8 Mar 2021): stakeholders *"adopted in 2019 a Counter-UAS Action Plan … included into the European Plan for Aviation Safety (EPAS)"*, five objectives, *"As amended by issue 3 … dated 23 September 2020"* [8]. Obj. 2 *"COMPLETED"*; Obj. 5 still *"Proposal being discussed with the commission"* (4 Nov 2024) [9]. Parts 2–3 are *"For Official Use Only - Need to know basis"* [8][9]. Remit boundary: *"Neutralisation is NOT in the remit of EASA … Law Enforcement Authorities and/or Military in charge"* [9]. **Costs (official estimates):** Gatwick 2018 *"up to EUR 64 million"*; for the ten largest European airports *"the delay cost of a 30 minutes runway closure is estimated to range from EUR 325,000 to EUR 514,000"* [8]; methodology not disclosed. **[A]**

**2025–26:** first USSP certificate (ANRA Technologies, 14 May 2025) covering the four mandatory services [10]; >2 m registered EU drone operators [7]; June 2026 Easy Access Rules revision incorporating ED Decision 2025/018/R (SORA 2.5, 29 Sep 2025) [11]. Commission **EU Action Plan on Drone and Counter Drone Security** (11 Feb 2026) *"replaces its midterm review and the drone strategy 2.0"*; includes *"€250 million call for land and maritime border surveillance"*, *"rapid counter drone emergency teams"*, a *"Drone Security Package in 2026"* and an *"EU trusted" drone label* [12][13]. **[W]**

**Gap:** across EASA's 21,717-URL sitemap the only "counter"-matching URL is the IATA GNSS-spoofing release; **[NV]** that EASA publishes a standalone counter-UAS page.

## A3 — EUROCONTROL
- **[A] 7 Oct 2019:** *"the number of drone incursions at and around airports has been increasing almost exponentially, with no reversal trend in sight"*; the threat *"is real and here to stay"*; actions include *"estimate budget required to pay for the heavy cost of counter drone measures"* [14].
- **[A]/[W] 4 Nov 2024** high-level C-UAS workshop (*"over 160 experts"*): *"the serious challenge posed by careless or malicious users"*; *"legislation is struggling to keep pace"*; mitigation risks (*"collateral damage, jamming, ground interference"*); a *"dedicated C-UAS Group"* was planned [15]. The 3 Jun 2025 *"Mastering the Skies"* workshop sought to *"effectively prepare for drone-related incidents and incursions at airports"*, EASA presenting *"How to approach illegal drones?"* [16].
- **[A] 23 Jan 2026** (*European Aviation Overview – 2025 Review*): *"Some major European airports were disrupted (drone incidents, cyberattacks)."* **Thin evidence: no airport, date, count or attribution**, and bundled with cyberattacks [17].
- *U-space ARA Vol. 1* (19 Apr 2023) [18]; latest U-space Implementation Monitoring Report found is Nov 2022 — **[NV]** a 2024–26 edition [19].
- **[NV]** any "Drone Zone": `/drone-zone`, `/drones`, `/utm` all HTTP 404.

## A4 — EU INTCEN: public vs classified
**Mandate (official):** *"the exclusive civilian intelligence unit of the EU, providing in-depth analysis for decision makers in all EU Institutions … a directorate of the EEAS … directly attached to the High Representative … It comprises an Intelligence Analysis Division (INTCEN.1) and a Support/Open Source Research Division (INTCEN.2)."* *"Under the SIAC agreement, it works hand in hand with its military counterpart, the EUMS INT."* [20][21] The EEAS org chart (16 Sep 2026) confirms INTCEN.1/INTCEN.2 [22].

**Classified, not public:** all INTCEN posts require *"a valid personal security clearance to access EU classified information up to level EU Secret"* [20][21]. Across 108,108 EEAS sitemap URLs "intcen" appears only in vacancy notices; no INTCEN product is published. **INTCEN assessments are EU-classified and not public; [NV] that any INTCEN drone/hybrid/Russia assessment exists publicly. I found none and do not infer its contents.**

**Public substitutes, other producers:** EEAS *Countering Hybrid Threats* documents the **EU Hybrid Toolbox** and **EU Hybrid Rapid Response Teams**, first mobilised *"in support of Moldova … ahead of the parliamentary elections of September 2025"* [23]; the Council approved the HRRT *"guiding framework"* on 22 May 2024 [24]. EEAS published the **4th Annual FIMI Threats Report** (12 Mar 2026) and a **2025 report on EEAS counter-FIMI activities** (24 Jun 2026) [25][26] — StratCom/FIMI products, **not INTCEN**. `consilium.europa.eu` returns HTTP 403 (browser check), so Council texts were read only via EEAS reproductions — **[NV]** against Council originals.

## Caveats
1. EASA vs EUROCONTROL disagreement on U-space force/applicability [2][3] vs [4].
2. Cost items differ in kind: two ex-post estimates [8], one forward funding commitment [13].
3. No source here attributes any European airport drone disruption to a state actor; the 2026 plan's *"hostile overflights"* [12][13] carries no public attribution — state-intent evidence **[NV]**.
4. Slide [3] cites "Regulation (EU) 1139/2018"; the correct citation is 2018/1139 (source typo).
