# Drones in the Hands of Armed Groups and Criminal Gangs: Colombia and Ecuador, 2024–2026

**Scope.** Non-state small-UAS use outside declared inter-state warfare; defence threat understanding only; no operational, payload or counter-detection detail. Bracketed numbers cite the source list. Ratings: **[O]** official statement/court record, **[J]** reputable journalism, **[W]** witness, **[C]** unverified claim. Compiled 25 September 2026.

## 1. Colombia — drone attacks on police and military

**Actors.** The ELN and the FARC-dissident Estado Mayor Central of "Iván Mordisco" (Martín Villa substructure; "Calarcá" fronts); Segunda Marquetalia is lesser. **Every case below was remotely piloted; no source claims autonomy** (§5).

- **8 Apr–13 Jun 2024**, Cauca/Valle — "suicide drone" reporting begins; 19 attacks against soldiers and police by mid-June 2024 [J].
- **13 May 2025, Teorama (Norte de Santander)** — ELN drones killed soldier Edison Guerrero; remotely piloted. [O][1]
- **12–13 Feb 2026, Saravena (Arauca)** — ELN drones wounded an Air Force officer; the Army downed a drone and **captured its operator**. [O]
- **18 Feb 2026, El Castillo (Meta)** — seized **190 grenades adapted for drone release**, 56 mortar rounds, 88 anti-personnel mines and 273 detonators (Mordisco). [O][3]
- **20 Apr 2026, Ipiales (Nariño)** — drones killed three soldiers near the Ecuadorian border; Comandos de la Frontera blamed. [O][30]
- **25 Apr and 16 Sep 2026** — drones disabled an **Aerocivil radar** at cerro Santana (Cauca) and hit a 3D radar at **Candelaria** (Valle), which the Defensoría called an IHL violation. [O][4][48]
- **7–13 May 2026, Jamundí (Valle)** — five drones in one attack on the Potrerito and Robles police substations; 17 devices, four wounded. [O/J]
- **8 Jun–7 Jul 2026, Chocó** — ELN drones hit a marine infantry battalion, then mounted **15 attacks in one week**, including the Tadó police station. [O/J][6][7]
- **19 Jun 2026, Tibú (Norte de Santander)** — a drone-released charge hit a church during EMC–ELN fighting; an 11-year-old killed. [O][5]
- **14 Aug–5 Sep 2026** — drones killed a soldier in the Catatumbo and three in an ELN "swarm" strike; nine police wounded at Norosí (Sur de Bolívar). [J]
- **25 Sep 2026, Aguachica (Cesar)** — drone attack on a police station; material damage. [O][9]

**Official counts.** A military intelligence report updated 7 Sep 2026 records **690 drone attacks since 21 July 2023 — 119 in 2024, 277 in 2025 and 292 in the first nine months of 2026**. Attribution for 2025–26: **382 to Mordisco's dissidents, 155 to the ELN, 30 to "Calarcá"**. **487 people were affected** (342 military and 59 police wounded; 22 soldiers, 3 police and 6 civilians killed). The first one-way "kamikaze" attack was on **24 Jan 2026 at Patía (Cauca)** — one-way, not autonomous. Mindefensa separately reported **8,395 *registered* incidents in 2025, only 4% effective (333 attacks)**: the lower figure counts attacks, the higher registered incidents. [O][2][14][45]

## 2. Pipelines, energy, illegal mining

**The pipeline campaign is not a drone campaign.** Mindefensa logged **8 pipeline bombings in Jan–Jun 2026 against 21 in Jan–Jun 2025 (−62%)**; the series reads 2019:35, 2021:14, 2023:28, 2024:3, 2025:21 — conventional *voladuras*. Arauca holds 92% of 2025 cases (Saravena 56.5%). [O][17][41][42]

Drone-specific oil events are few. On **22 Jul 2026** an improvised drone carrying a charge targeted a 100,000-barrel tank at **Caño Limón** (Arauca); it crashed en route and the charge failed, with ~200 workers present; Semana attributed it to the ELN. A second drone hit the **Banadía complex, Saravena**, the next night without detonating. Neither has official attribution. [J][15] **No drone attacks on Putumayo, the Oleoducto Transandino or Amerisur–Geopark were found for 2024–2026.**

PAres (3 Jul 2026) logged **11 Catatumbo drone events in Jan–Jun 2026**, 54.5% against the military, **24 victims**, 81.8% in Tibú and El Tarra [NGO][16]. Extortion and illegal-mining links are **thin** — one unverified Segovia mine attack [C]; **no verified Indepaz figures were found.**

## 3. Colombia's anti-drone policy and law

- **Escudo Nacional Antidrones** (announced 10 Jan 2026, MinDefensa-led) was billed at over **COP 6.3 billion** (~COP 1 bn for phase 1) but **failed**: only **1 of 119 bidders** passed trials, the Contraloría flagged "critical risks", it was still **unfunded at COP 800,000 million** on 16 Jul 2026, and the enabling directive was annulled on 8 Sep 2026. **Announced value ≠ executed capability.** [O/J][19][46][47]
- Also: a COP 3.7 bn supplementary defence request (Jan 2026) with COP 1 bn for the shield [O][2]; international counter-UAS trials (Apr 2026) [O]; an Army **UAV battalion** announced Aug 2025 [O][13].
- **No statute criminalises weaponised drones** — only civil-aviation rules apply (Aerocivil/UAEAC RAC 100/91; fines up to ~COP 20 million). Bills filed on 30 Jul 2025 (162 attacks cited) and 6 Aug 2026 (328 victims cited) are **not in force as a drone-terrorism offence**. [O][18][14]
- **Only Axon (US) is confirmed with counter-UAS deployed in-country** (Army and Air Force bases, Sep 2026); a reported USD 25 m "anti-drone" purchase is unverified. Gangs reportedly recruit Ukraine-experienced Colombians as pilots. [J][10][11]

## 4. Ecuador — prisons, gangs and the 2024 internal armed conflict

Ecuador declared an **internal armed conflict on 9 January 2024** after the TC Televisión assault (1,534 detained in six days), naming Los Choneros, Los Lobos, Tren de Aragua and others; the US designated several as terrorist organisations in September 2025. **No drone role is reported in the TC attack, and none was tied to the January 2024 emergency**; armed-drone activity is a pre-2021 prison phenomenon that spikes in 2025–2026. [J][33][34]

- **Prisons dominate.** **12 Sep 2023, La Roca** (Guayaquil): an agricultural drone left a device on a pavilion roof, destroyed by controlled detonation. **19 Nov 2025, Machala**: a drone overflew the prison and was destroyed by SNAI, with no casualties. **4 Mar 2026, Machala**: a drone-launched device targeted the **prison director** (SNAI reported neutralisation; police reported damage to the director's office window). All remotely piloted, visually detected and defeated; **no autonomy**. [O][25][26][27]
- **Contraband and escape plotting.** **26 Jul 2023, Durán**: three modified **agricultural** drones (to 30 kg payload, ~5 km range) seized, bound for Guayaquil prisons; a guard detained. **15 Aug 2026, Monte Sinaí**: a drone, two explosive charges, a **remote activation system** and a **printed plan of the Penitenciaría del Litoral** were seized — an official hypothesis of an escape-enabling attack. Interior Minister Reimberg said roughly **600 drones linked to organised-crime groups had attempted to reach the Cárcel del Encuentro**, all intercepted. [O][21][22]
- **Offensive use outside prisons is rare.** **24–25 Jun 2024, Puerto Bolívar and Bajo Alto (El Oro)**: drone-dropped explosives destroyed four homes in a **Los Lobos** inter-cell dispute — police called it the gangs' new method for attacking each other. [O][28]
- **ACLED counts only eight Ecuadorian drone incidents (Sept 2023–17 Jul 2026) with no drone-caused deaths**, and ~670 armed-drone events regionally since 2018, **over 72% in 2025–August 2026** [NGO][31]. **No confirmed gang drone attack on Ecuadorian police or military personnel was found for 2023–2026.**
- **The 2026 Pacific fishing-boat drone strikes are attributed to foreign state/military forces, not Ecuadorian gangs.** [W/J][36][37]
- **Capability gap:** in 2023 only **2 of Ecuador's 36 prisons had signal inhibitors**, and in 2024 the state fell back on **snipers**. A new civil aviation law (Sept 2026) would require drone registration. [O/J][27][29][32]

## 5. Autonomy and machine vision: no evidence

**No evidence of autonomous operation, onboard machine vision or automatic target recognition was found in either country.** Every case is human-in-the-loop: commercial airframes (~**USD 600**) modified artisanally; a **remote activation system** and **printed target map** recovered in Guayaquil; drones defeated by controlled detonation **after staff saw them**; an **operator captured** after his drone was downed. Counter-measures presuppose a radio-control link, and operators must preload maps before flight. "Swarm" headlines denote simultaneous coordinated launches, not autonomous swarming; the reported move to **fibre-optic tethering** is a jam-resistant control link — still piloted. Analysts describe autonomy as low today and only a forecast. **As of September 2026, autonomy is absent.** [O/J][21][22][31][35]

## 6. Evidence quality and gaps

Official material (Mindefensa, Ejército, Aerocivil, SNAI, Fuerzas Armadas) and materiel seizures give the strongest evidence **[O]**, corroborated by national press **[J]**. Weakest is actor attribution in multi-actor theatres (Catatumbo, Chocó), resting on military or ministerial statements alone: **no Colombian court convictions for weaponised-drone use were identified**, and the intelligence series itself notes under-registration of victims. The December 2025 Machala "dron distractor" is **only partially verified** — the riot and 13 deaths are corroborated, the drone element is not. Ecuador publishes no consistent series on prison incursions.
