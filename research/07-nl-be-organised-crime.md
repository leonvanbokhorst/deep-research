# Organised Crime Use of Drones — Netherlands and Belgium

**Research date:** 25 September 2026 · **Scope:** threat understanding for defence analysis; no operational, smuggling or evasion detail is recorded. Evidence quality is graded per entry (court record / official statement / academic report / journalism / claim).

**Headline judgement:** Criminal drone use in both countries is almost entirely *manually piloted* delivery and, secondarily, aerial observation. There is **no public evidence of autonomous or machine-vision-guided criminal drones in either country**; the only autonomy documented is state/port-side. Effects concentrate on prisons (contraband delivery) and port security (unidentified overflight). Drone weapons use against persons is rare and, where present, linked to terrorism rather than drug trafficking.

DJI Director-General Wim Saris called it an unwinnable "cat-and-mouse game". Notably, 243 of 298 detections in 2023 were non-suspicious — a low signal-to-noise ratio. Machine vision is used only on the **detection** side.

## 2. Netherlands — ports (Rotterdam, Amsterdam, Wadden)

- **Criminal use:** Platform Veilig Ondernemen reports criminals using drones, smart cameras and underwater robots around Rotterdam, alongside corrupt port workers ([PVO](https://pvo-nl.nl/nieuws/drugscriminelen-smokkelen-steeds-creatiever-in-de-haven/)). Evidence: claim/industry report. No public Dutch prosecution was located that turns specifically on a criminal drone flight over a container terminal.
- **State counter-use (context, not offending):** Rotterdam's "virtual fence" of cameras and drones; Douane drone teams in Rotterdam and the Wadden Sea; Amsterdam port surveillance with sonar, microphones and drones ([Rijnmond](https://www.rijnmond.nl/nieuws/1759281/drugssmokkelaars-steeds-creatiever-dit-doet-de-douane-ertegen)). A Douane manager called autonomous drones "the future" for intercepting drug extractors ([NPO Radio 1](https://www.nporadio1.nl/)). Evidence: official/journalism.
- **Effect:** Authorities partly attribute the fall in intercepted extractors (452 in 2023 → 266 in 2024) to tightened security including drones ([crime-nieuws.nl](https://crime-nieuws.nl/)). Rotterdam cocaine seizures fell again in 2025 to ~11,500 kg ([Douane](https://www.douane.nl/)). Evidence: official statistics.

## 3. Drone reconnaissance of rivals, police and officials

Public evidence in NL/BE is **thin and mostly indirect**. No Dutch case was located in which a criminal drone was documented watching a rival, a police station or an officer's home; Dutch "spotter" reporting concerns human lookouts. The clearest Belgian data points:

- **Oct 2025, Antwerp** — three suspects arrested for planning to attach an explosive to a drone to attack Belgian politicians. This is a terrorism case, not drug crime ([Dronewatch/NCTV](https://www.dronewatch.nl/2026/06/30/nctv-ziet-toenemende-terroristische-dreiging-van-drones-maar-geen-acuut-risico/)). Evidence: official (NCTV).
- **Aug 2026, Brussels** — a magistrate publicly argued for using *police* drones to watch drug "hotspots" and **police stations**, implying a perceived threat to police premises ([L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/)). Evidence: official statement.
- The Leiden review found criminal organisations played **almost no role** in drone attack incidents 2010–2025 (see §4).

## 4. Official Dutch assessments

- **NCTV** maintains a drone topic page and coordinates with Defence, Marechaussee and police; it treats drones primarily as a terrorism/counter-drone issue and stresses public incident reporting ([NCTV](https://www.nctv.nl/onderwerpen/d/drones)).
- **Dreigingsbeeld Terrorisme Nederland (DTN), June 2026** devotes a dedicated box to drones: no successful drone attack in Western Europe to date, several plots foiled, and effective drone attacks still require substantial technical knowledge; simple means remain more likely ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/06/30/), [Dronewatch](https://www.dronewatch.nl/2026/06/30/nctv-ziet-toenemende-terroristische-dreiging-van-drones-maar-geen-acuut-risico/)). Evidence: official.
- **Academic:** Stijn Willem van 't Land (Leiden/ISGA), *Tussen mogelijkheid en realiteit* (June 2026), commissioned by Kenniscentrum Bewaken en Beveiligen: only **34 drone-related attack incidents 2010–2025**, over half never executed; perpetrators were mainly lone actors and terrorist groups — **"criminal organisations and state actors play hardly any role"** ([Universiteit Leiden](https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/nieuws/2026/06/hoe-realistisch-is-een-drone-aanslag-in-nederland)). Evidence: academic report.
- **Policy:** a temporary national policy framework (March 2026) sets an escalating ladder — land, jam, take over, in extremis shoot down — for police and Defence, to overcome "handelingsverlegenheid" ([Dronewatch](https://www.dronewatch.nl/2026/03/17/nieuwe-richtlijn-politie-mag-drones-verstoren-overnemen-en-zelfs-neerschieten/), [Stcrt 2026-2035](https://www.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2026-2035.html), [Kamerbrief 2026Z05224](https://ikregeer.nl/tweede-kamer/zaak/2026Z05224)). No "Actieagenda Drones" exists under that name; relevant instruments are this framework and the EU "Actieplan beveiliging van en tegen drones" fiche ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2026/03/05/)).
- Politieacademie, NSCR, TNO and Het CCV: no dedicated public study on *organised-crime* drone use was located — a real evidence gap.

## 6. Prosecutions and sentences

- **Turnhout, Belgium (2026):** prosecutions of drone pilots for contraband delivery; one 18-year-old faced **10 months**; three suspects remanded. Court reporting, [GVA](https://www.gva.be/) / [VRT](https://www.vrt.be/).
- **Leuven, Belgium (Feb 2026):** a convict already serving time for a kidnapping-murder received **15 months** for a plan to smuggle drugs into Leuven prison — reported alongside drone-smuggling, though the drone element is not confirmed in the headline ([HBVL](https://www.hbvl.be/)). Evidence: journalism.
- **Netherlands:** despite regular DJI interventions and arrests (6 in 2023), no public Dutch judgment with a specific drone-smuggling sentence was located. This is a **documented evidence gap**, not evidence of impunity.

## 7. Autonomy and machine vision — explicit statement

**Absent on the criminal side.** Every documented case of criminal drone use in the Netherlands and Belgium is consistent with a human operator flying a commercial or FPV drone under direct manual control. No open source describes criminal use of autonomous waypoint flight, machine-vision target recognition, swarming, or AI-driven navigation in either country. Belgian FGP's "AI" warning concerns data analysis and targeting of port logistics staff — **not** drone autonomy.

**Present only on the state side:** Belgium's FPS Justice drone-in-a-box prison surveillance (from 2025) and Port of Antwerp-Bruges' D-Hive/YACOB network, plus Dutch ambitions for autonomous customs drones. Machine vision appears in detection and monitoring, not in any known criminal system.

**Limitations.** Several Belgian and Dutch items (Nieuwsblad, GVA/HLN, Knack, HBVL, Le Soir, Parool, Telegraaf) sit behind paywalls; where a headline/date/source is cited without an article-level URL, that item rests on news-aggregator metadata plus a publisher-domain link and should be treated as **journalism, not independently verified**. The Dutch aggregate detection figures come via a radio podcast, not a published DJI dataset. No judgement was located that squarely convicts a Dutch drone-smuggling offence.
