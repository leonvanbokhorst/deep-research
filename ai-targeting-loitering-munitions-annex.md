## Extended per-claim detail

### A1. Gaza AI — extended

- **Lavender.** Machine-learning system scoring every Gazan 1–100 for likelihood of being a Hamas/PIJ operative, built on mass surveillance of ~2.3 million residents; fed "features" such as WhatsApp group membership with known militants, frequent phone and address changes, and social-media connections. Six Israeli intelligence officers (Unit 8200-linked) described it first-hand. At peak, **37,000** individuals marked. The check before bombing a junior target's family home was, per source "B.", about **20 seconds** and consisted essentially of confirming the target was male. Sources described a **~10% error rate** and a "no zero-error policy… mistakes were treated statistically." Collateral-damage thresholds: **15–20** civilians authorised per junior target early in the war; **100+** for senior commanders. One source authorised bombing "hundreds" of private homes. Sources also said junior targets were struck with **unguided ("dumb") bombs** to save precision munitions. The IDF Spokesperson denied the existence of such a kill list and stated AI tools are "auxiliary tools that assist officers in the process of incrimination", with independent analyst verification required.
- **Where's Daddy?** Tracking software that flagged when a Lavender-marked individual entered a family home, enabling the strike. One source said he personally added ~1,200 names to the tracking system during a lull in strikes; these decisions were "not made at high levels".
- **Gospel/Habsora.** Generates structures rather than people; described by a former intelligence officer as enabling a "mass assassination factory" where the "emphasis is on quantity and not on quality". A human "will go over the targets before each attack, but it need not spend a lot of time on them". By 10 November 2023 (35 days) the IDF said it had struck 15,000 targets, versus 1,500 in 11 days in 2021 and 5,266–6,231 in 51 days in 2014.
- **2026 disclosure.** Elbit's Tzayad ("Hunter") digital army programme: 850,000 real-time intel targets across all theatres, 7 Oct 2023–end 2025 (~1,000/day); >20,000 battle plans; >46,000 "joint strikes and closing fire on real-time intel" (~50 strikes/day); external fire-support time cut from 40–50 minutes to 1–7 minutes. Elbit denied the 850,000 figure referred to enemy targets. Former Pentagon targeting adviser **Wes Bryant**: "there is no way each and every one of the 1,000 targets a day… are thoroughly and effectively characterized in terms of collateral damage analysis"; "even characterising 50 a day is hard enough." Cambridge researcher **Sophia Goodfriend**: no military could vet 1,000 daily targets without outsourcing verification to other automated systems, "which raises questions of accountability and concern about shrinking amounts of human oversight." NATO's **Air Chief Marshal Sir Johnny Stringer** and a British Army brigadier were on the panel; Edelstein had been listed only as "speaker to be announced" until the session began.

### A2. Hamas/Hezbollah — extended

- **Hamas 7 Oct.** INSS: "From the very first minutes of the Hamas attack… drones have played a key role… from attacks on the observation stations and communication and energy centers on the border to intelligence collection prior to the attack, as well as during combat and subsequent attacks on IDF forces." Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists: UAS "constituted the first wave of attacks to eliminate Israeli observation towers, cameras, and communications"; munitions dropped on tanks, soldiers and emergency responders; drones also used against naval vessels and energy infrastructure. Al-Qassam claimed **35 Al-Zawari** attack drones. Nahal Oz probe: 53 soldiers killed, 10 captured, 2 Merkava tanks and 1 Namer disabled; drones plus social-media intelligence used to map the base.
- **Hezbollah inventory.** **Mirsad-1** = Iranian **Ababil-T**, ~120 km assault range, ~370 km/h, up to 40 kg payload (Israeli reporting). **Hudhud-1** ~1.20 × 1.90 m, negligible thermal signature, small radar cross-section, advertised 30 km range; Hudhud-1 assessed as **Yemeni-manufactured**. "Unit 127" responsible for UAV production, maintenance and operation. Alma (Israeli research centre) describes conversion of cheap Chinese civilian drones in workshops across Lebanon at **$300–400 (small) to ~$4,000 (large)**; claims of a June 2024 seizure of 5,000 drones and "dozens" of shipments are **low confidence**.
- **Binyamina, 13 Oct 2024.** 4 killed (Sgt. Alon Amitay, Omri Tamari, Yosef Hieb, Yoav Agmon), 58 wounded (7 serious, 14 moderate); MDA reported 67 injured. Target: Golani Brigade training base (Zar'it barracks) dining hall near Binyamina, ~18:45–19:15. Reports range from two to three drones; one downed by the Navy off Haifa, one by Iron Dome. The survivor dropped off radar 48 km NE of Acre and was assessed to have flown very low. Hezbollah claimed a "swarm"/"squadron", with missile attacks on Nahariya/Acre keeping defences busy. Israeli probe: IAF responded by assuming a vanished drone is still flying and by broadening warning areas.
- **Scale.** IAF/ToI (14 Oct 2024): "some 1,200 drones have been fired at Israel [during the war], and 221 have gotten through Israel's defences."

### A3. Houthi/Red Sea — extended

- **Navy expenditure (McLane, Jan 2025):** 111 reported Red Sea attacks/hijackings since 19 Nov 2023; ~400 drones/missiles engaged; 380 defeated; 2 vessels sunk; 4 mariners killed; 120 SM-2, 80 SM-6, 20 ESSM/SM-3 and 160 5-inch rounds fired.
- **Interceptor unit costs** (MDAA/Congressional data via reputable outlets; procurement estimates, not marginal cost): SM-2 ≈$2.1M; SM-6 ≈$3.9–4.3M; ESSM ≈$1.7M; RAM ≈$0.905M; SM-3 $9.6M–27.9M; Patriot ≈$4M (one Patriot ≈ 115 Shahed-class drones at ~$35k).
- **LaPlante quote (May 2024):** "If we're shooting down a $50,000 one-way drone with a $3 million missile, that's not a good cost equation."
- **Types.** Samad-2/3, Qasef-1/2K (Ababil-T/Ababil-2 derivatives; ~40 kg warhead, ~2 h endurance, ~370 km/h), Shahed-136 analogues; the 19 Jul 2024 Tel Aviv strike used a Houthi-claimed "Yafa" drone. Voskuijl (*Science & Global Security*, 2020): Samad-2 likely cannot reach strategic Saudi targets from Houthi territory; Samad-3 can exceed 1,800 km.
- **USVs.** MV *Tutor* (12 Jun 2024): converted 5–7 m fishing skiff with mannequins, hit the stern, immobilising the ship; sank 18 Jun. *Chios Lion* (15 Jul 2024): struck ~97 nm NW of Al Hudaydah. "Toofan"/"Blowfish": Toofan-1 ~150 kg, Toofan-2 ~400 kg, Toofan-3 ~500 kg warhead (claims to 1,000–1,500 kg); 35–52 kt; require a pilot to the start line plus mothership C2. First-generation WBIEDs used a 454 kg P-15 warhead; the 2017 attack on the Saudi frigate *Al Madinah* killed two. Three Houthi USVs destroyed 21–22 Jun 2024 (CENTCOM).
- **Israel strikes.** 19 Jul 2024 Tel Aviv: struck a building near the US Embassy branch; 1 killed, ≥10 injured; IAF tracked it ~6 minutes and judged it harmless; an indirect route used. 4 May 2025 Ben Gurion: "Palestine-2" ballistic missile hit the perimeter after Arrow and US THAAD intercepts failed; 8 injured.

### A4. Iran/Iraq — extended

- **Shahed costs.** Common $20–50k figure is [C] and, per Defence Express, unrealistic: leaked 2022 Russian buys were **$370k/unit (2,000 order), $290k, then $193k for the 6,000-unit order**; Russian domestic ~$70k by 2025; Alabuga modelled at $48k rising to ~$80k by Apr 2024. Shahed-238 (Geran-3) jet: 300–600 km/h, 50–90 kg, 1,000–2,500 km, GNSS/INS plus EO/IR and radar-homing (anti-radiation) variants; reported export interest **$900k–$1.4M** [C].
- **Recipients.** Russia (licensed Geran production); **Sudan** — AFRICOM/Planet Labs imagery shows Mohajer-6 and a ground-control vehicle at Wadi Sayyidna; **Venezuela** — OFAC (30 Dec 2025): QAI Mohajer-series assembled by EANSA and re-branded ANSU-series. Armenia (~$500M deal incl. drones) [C]; Ethiopia [C, L]; Belarus "Nomad" Shahed-136 shown at the July 2024 Minsk parade [C, L]; **Tajikistan's 2022 Ababil-2 plant contested** — RFE/RL (Mar 2026) quotes officials saying it does not exist [L]; Algeria unverified.
- **Alabuga.** Opened July 2023 under a reported **$1.75bn franchise** for 6,000 Shahed-136s by Sept 2025, sited on the Kama for Caspian transport; fully localised by 2025; ISIS reports a near-term target of **25,000/year** plus a parallel Izhevsk (Kupol/Almaz-Antey) line.
- **Tower 22.** Deputy Pentagon Press Secretary Sabrina Singh: "footprints of Kataib Hezbollah… We know that Iran is behind it", declining a final assessment. The type was indicated as a **Shahed** by a US official, never formally released. Command investigation: the base defence operations centre was distracted by a **Scan Eagle recovery**, dismissed southern radar tracks as "birds or trash", and assumed the southern approach safe. US retaliation 2 Feb 2024: 85 targets at seven sites, 125 precision munitions, B-1Bs.
- **Iraq PM attack (7 Nov 2021).** Iraqi Armed Forces said one drone attacked and **two were shot down**; some reports cited two armed drones [contested]. Iraqi agencies called them quadcopters. **No group claimed responsibility.** Anonymous Reuters sources blamed Kata'ib Hezbollah or Asaib Ahl al-Haq with Iran-made weapons [ALLEGED, L]; US officials doubted Tehran directly ordered it. Militias circulated a false-flag claim built on a **forged image**.
- **Proxy strike statistics.** US bases in Iraq/Syria attacked **150–170+ times** (Oct 2023–Jan 2024) [contested count]. Islamic Resistance in Iraq vs Israel: 312 claimed attacks (Nov 2023–Nov 2024), only **89 reached Israeli territory, 4 struck**. April 2024 "True Promise": ~170 drones plus 30+ cruise and 120+ ballistic; Israel claimed **99% intercepted**, Maariv reported **84%**. October 2024 "True Promise II": ~180–200 ballistic missiles with demonstrated Nevatim/Tel Nof impacts. June 2025 Twelve-Day War opened with 100+ Shaheds on 13 June. 2026 war from 28 Feb: CSIS — UAE absorbed 1,422 drones + 246 missiles (1–8 Mar); drones ~71% of strikes; opening wave 867 drones/339 missiles; daily launches reportedly fell 720→60 by 9 Mar. US reportedly used ~65% of its 2,330 Patriot interceptors Feb–Jul 2026 [C, M].
- **Europe.** 31 July 2025 **14-state statement** (state.gov): Iranian intelligence attempts to "kill, kidnap, and harass" journalists, dissidents, Jewish citizens and officials, "increasingly collaborating with international criminal organizations" — **no mention of drones**. German 2026 prosecutions charged suspects with plotting to kill Jewish leaders for Iran (arson/surveillance), not drones. September 2025 Danish/Norwegian airport incursions attributed to **Russia**. Washington Institute mapping of Iranian external operations records assassinations, abductions and surveillance via criminal proxies with no established drone pattern. **No public European case found of police seizing Iranian drone components in a plot context.**
- **Embargo.** UNSCR 2231 restrictions **lapsed 18 Oct 2023**; E3 triggered snapback **28 Aug 2025**; UK FCDO confirms reinstatement on **27 Sep 2025** of Resolutions including 1929 (2010), the arms-embargo instrument; US Treasury corroborates. UNSCR 2216 (Yemen) remains in force. Panel S/2023/833: 254 UAVs, 100 UAV engines and ~5 tons of precursors interdicted (Nov 2022–Jun 2023), debris "consistent with… the Islamic Republic of Iran". Panel S/2025/650: June 2025 seizure of >750 tons including a Shahed UAV, Ghadir anti-ship missile and Misagh MANPADS, plus "manuals in Farsi" and Iranian police DNA kits; much materiel originated in **China**; Shahn/Sarfayt (Oman–Yemen) land crossings now primary. OFAC 12 Nov 2025 designated **32 entities** across Iran, UAE, Türkiye, China, Hong Kong, India, Germany and Ukraine. Caspian route Amirabad/Anzali→Astrakhan (~600 AIS gaps by Russian-flagged ships in three months).

### A5. Kargu-2 — extended

The full para. 63 quotation and the four contestation points are given in the core brief. Additional context: the UN report describes the deployment in the context of Government of National Accord forces (with Turkish support) attacking Haftar-aligned forces in what appears to be a non-international armed conflict. Turkey has marketed the Kargu-2 aggressively for export.

Hero: US Army/Marines, **Argentina** (Janes, Dec 2022); Rheinmetall licensed production for NATO (Nov 2025). Warmate: Poland, Ukraine, **UAE**, **Georgia** (licensed plant). Shahed-136: Russia (Geran-2/Alabuga), Ethiopia, Sudan, Venezuela, plus non-state recipients. - **Non-state acquisition.** State-supplied purpose-built loitering munitions documented by Oryx: **Hamas** — Ababil-2T "Shihab" (2021), "Al-Zawari" (2022); **Hezbollah** — Ababil-2T "Mirsad-1" (used Oct 2024); **Houthis** — Samad-2/3, Shihab, Shahed-136 "Wa'aed", Khatif-1/2; **Iraqi militias** — Shahed-131 "Murad-6" (2021), Murad-5 (2020). ### A7. Non-state actors — extended

- **ISIS.** CAR field teams documented an IS drone workshop in Ramadi, with armed-drone experimentation from Feb 2016. Peak spring 2017: **60 to >100 aerial drone bombing attacks per month**; first lethal IED-drone Oct 2016 (two peshmerga killed); by Sept 2017 sightings fell to ~7/month; believed to have killed more than a dozen and injured dozens. Supply: a documented network used PayPal purchases from US/Canada vendors (Oct 2014–Aug 2015), shipped via Şanlıurfa, Turkey, under aliases. Coalition response: a **$700M crash counter-UAS programme**, EW/jamming, a "red no-fly zone" over Mosul, drone guns/nets, strikes on workshops. Fleet size unknown — evidence absent.
- **Two-tier baseline.** Tier 1: Houthi **Qasef** (Ababil-T analogue, ~120 km, ~30 kg warhead), **Samad** series, Chinese-derived **Rujum/Rased** (Skywalker X-8 analogues). All known Houthi UAVs used piston engines until a Feb 2025 seizure of **JetCat P500/P550 jet engines** (~$7,890 vs $69,500 for a Quds cruise-missile engine) and DJI Mavic 3 FPV kits. Tier 2: modified commercial/FPV. Hezbollah manufactures **fibre-optic FPV at ~$300–400 each**, 3D-printed with COTS electronics, after the Syrian land corridor was disrupted post-Assad; explicitly **operator-piloted from the video feed and detonated on impact**. Iraqi militias used the same fibre-optic FPV pattern against US bases (Victory Base, Mar 2026).
- **Somalia.** Baseline to 2023: al-Shabaab used commercial UAVs mainly for surveillance/propaganda. Aug 2024: Puntland seized **five FPV UAVs plus an explosives cache hidden in speakers** in Galkayo; 7 detained; press linked them to "kamikaze drones from Yemen intended for al-Shabaab". UN Panel recorded **no confirmation of weaponised UAV use** by Somali non-state groups, adding that the capability "do[es] not require… technology transfer from… the Houthis". A Jul 2025 policy paper records **no confirmed al-Shabaab strikes in 2024–25** (recon drones intercepted pre-impact, Galgaduud, Feb 2025); CAR has not seen al-Shabaab FPV. **ISIS-Somalia:** by late 2024 the Puntland faction fielded **>1,000 fighters (≥60% foreign) and over 100 UAVs**; on 31 Dec 2024 it attacked Puntland forces with 12 suicide bombers followed by a wave of explosive-laden UAVs. Houthi–Shabaab nexus: UN monitors report al-Shabaab leaders requesting "advanced weapons and training" from Ansar Allah in 2024; two Houthi engineers reportedly sent to Jilib for drone-modification training [C].
- **Sahel.** JNIM from first drone activity (Bandiagara, Sep 2023) to **>two dozen confirmed incidents, 82% since March 2025**, including FPV kamikaze strikes in Togo (Apr 2025, 5 soldiers) and Djibo/Timbuktu/Kabankoura; FLA used VTOL/FPV from Feb 2025; IS-Sahel used an FPV in Tillabéri (May 2025). Libya Panel identified two primary Libya→RSF supply routes (air bridges and land routes) active to June 2024, with LAAF facilitating movement — but **drone-specific Libya→Sahel transfers are not itemised**, an evidence gap.
- **Black markets.** 52% of surveyed states reported illicit trafficking (postal shipments, smuggling routes); UAS and components are "widely available both online and in retail stores". A European civil aviation authority built a **5 kg-payload UAV purely from open online sources**, identifying the **electric engine** as the key chokepoint. Evidence for encrypted-app drone-component marketplaces is **absent** from retrieved primary reporting.
- **Gaza smuggling.** With tunnels degraded, drone-based weapons smuggling emerged on the Egypt–Israel border: **10+ confirmed interceptions (Oct 2024–May 2025)**, mostly **DJI FlyCart 30 / Matrice 300 / Agras T30**, carrying **9–10 M16-type rifles per flight (~31–35 kg)**. Attribution to Palestinian militant groups is **CLAIMED, unverified**; the tunnel-substitution thesis is explicitly not established. Lebanon resupply now runs through **local 3D-printed manufacture** rather than Iranian land transfer.
- **Autonomy — negative finding.** Across a 600+ incident ICCT dataset and the UNOCT/CAR survey of 21 states, **no non-state machine-vision target selection was identified**; systems are remote-piloted, FPV, or GPS-waypoint. UNOCT/CAR found 3D printing for munitions release but "little current evidence" it is widespread. JNIM "offline AI"-enabled autonomous flight is speculative and prescriptive, not evidenced.
- **Anthropic September 2026 report.** Case **GTG-27005**: likely freelance Russia-based actors building a "full-stack autonomous FPV kamikaze drone swarm" — shared swarm memory, an onboard small language model governing attack/observe/return behaviours, camera-based terminal guidance issuing the detonation call, target classes including "person", "without a human in the loop"; a CV classifier trained on scraped Ukrainian combat footage using hardware-in-the-loop testing. Anthropic assesses a **small freelance team, not a state entity** (ties to Russia's Advanced Research Foundation/MoD claimed, unverified). **Design DEMONSTRATED; fielding or use NOT.** Case **GTG-87001**: a **Yemen-based** guided-weapons cell used Claude Code to write **GNC software, flight-control firmware and terminal guidance** for a rocket on a "commodity phone-class flight computer", ran a **live field test** (which appears to have failed), and modelled a >2,000 km ballistic missile — **AI-assisted weapons development, not onboard autonomy**.

### A8. Defensive/legal picture

Hardest to counter: **fibre-optic FPV** (unjammable, hard to detect — Hezbollah versus Iron Dome); **low/slow small UAS exploiting coverage seams**; **cheap platforms versus expensive interceptors**. CTC notes C-UAS guns are only as good as operator situational awareness and fixed systems leave gaps; the US spent **$700M** against an IS programme costing "thousands of dollars a month". Legal/regulatory instruments: Wassenaar Arrangement, MTCR, UNSCR 2370 (2017); states describe a permanent "cat and mouse" game. Caution: Houthi/Hezbollah/Iranian "AI", "autonomous swarm" and "self-targeting" claims are typically vendor or partisan — treat as **CLAIMED** until independently verified.

## Method and limitations

- `web_search` was intermittently non-functional in this environment; research used Google News RSS, DuckDuckGo HTML (and the r.jina.ai proxy when rate-limited or CAPTCHA-blocked), the Internet Archive for paywalled/blocked articles, and direct retrieval of UN/OFAC/state.gov primary documents. PDFs were retrieved with curl and read via pdftotext.
- Some wire services (Reuters, AP) and CENTCOM/Europol/INTERPOL endpoints blocked automated retrieval; figures from those sources reached this brief via citation lists and are flagged where relevant.
- All interception percentages and battle-damage claims are belligerent assertions without independent assessment and should be treated as such.
- The two most important **negative findings** are stated explicitly rather than smoothed over: (a) no demonstrated autonomous lethal engagement by an AI system in any of these theatres; (b) no demonstrated autonomous target selection by any non-state armed group.
