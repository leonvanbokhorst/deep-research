# Source Register and Bibliography

**Companion to** `AI-Autonomy-Threat-Assessment-2026.md` · Research cut-off 25 September 2026

**Grading:** **A** primary/official · **B** independent research with transparent method · **C** reputable journalism · **D** single-source or trade press · **E** vendor/advocacy/anonymous.
**Confidence:** High / Medium / Low, reflecting quality *and* convergence of evidence.

> **Note on retrieval.** During the research window the primary web-search facility failed repeatedly with an upstream error. Discovery therefore relied on news-RSS search, publisher site search, sitemaps, publisher REST APIs and direct document retrieval. This biases coverage toward English-language and easily indexed sources. Non-English primary material and paywalled investigative journalism (NYT, FT, Bloomberg, The Atlantic) are the main recall gaps; where such material is cited, it is cited through corroborating secondary reporting and graded accordingly.

## 7. Weak evidence — explicitly flagged (grade D/E)

- Vendor range, endurance and hit-rate claims; "95% kill rate"; "one operator controls hundreds"; laser "$5 per shot"; "$5/shot" and "20:1 advantage" figures describing marginal energy cost only.
- The "**$4,000 Shahed**" figure attributed to a US official in 2026 — inconsistent with every other source; treat as an outlier.
- The **Lancet-on-Kyiv "AI swarm"** claim (Mar 2026), inferred largely from coloured markings on debris and disputed by Ukrainian officials.
- Retrospective "**first AI drone swarm**" framings of Israel/Gaza 2021.
- The **Kargu-2 autonomous kill** claim as commonly stated.
- The reported **~$40 bn NATO counter-drone figure** and **~340 UK incidents** figure — secondary and unconfirmed.
- Counter-drone deployments and costs at the Paris 2024 Olympics, Euro 2024, NATO summits and Eurovision — not verifiable to primary-source standard.
- Any "first fully robotic assault", "first robot-only capture" or similar framing originating with a belligerent unit rather than an independent assessment.

## 8. Research gaps identified (and their effect on confidence)

1. **No harmonised Europe-wide series** of drone incidents, arrests or prosecutions. Europol and Eurojust publish no central counts. National series exist only for the UK.
2. **Attribution gap.** Many incidents cannot be attributed; the AIVD explicitly cautions that suspected Russian involvement is often unconfirmable and that detected drones are not always threats. This limits any claim about the *rate* of hostile activity.
3. **Civilian-site protection costs are almost entirely unpublished.** Gatwick 2018 is the only well-documented cost stack.
4. **Non-English primary sources are under-represented** because the search facility failed during the research window; Chinese, Russian, Turkish, Farsi and Arabic primary material is the largest recall gap, and assessments of Chinese and Turkish systems rely on English-language secondary reporting.
5. **Paywalled investigative journalism** (NYT, FT, Bloomberg, The Atlantic) was accessed through corroborating secondary coverage, which weakens the evidential chain for the most important single case (the AI-guided Zaporizhzhia strike).
6. **State-classified material** — EU INTCEN assessments, national service reporting — is by design unavailable; any open-source claim to cite it should be treated as unverifiable.
