# Proliferation of Drones and New Technologies in the Sahel

* * *

Do the operational benefits of drones outweigh the risks in today’s Sahelian settings?

 Africa -  Technology & Innovation - **Format**: PDF - **Size**: 381.73 KB - **Date**: Mar, 2026 - **Pages**: - **Copyright**: NATO Strategic Direction South HUB - **Tags**: drones, sahel, africa 

State forces across the central Sahel are deploying ISR and strike-capable drones to operate over difficult terrain, while non-state actors are adapting commercial drones for reconnaissance, attacks, and propaganda. Since late 2023, violent extremist organisations have increasingly used armed drones across borders, expanding conflict into low-altitude airspace and complicating base defence, convoy protection, and urban security.

Drones have become symbols of military modernisation and geopolitical alignment. Procurement choices from foreign suppliers often include training, financing, and rapid delivery, but create new dependencies in maintenance, software, and data governance. Although regional production initiatives, such as Morocco’s “Atlas Istar,” signal ambitions for greater autonomy.

As drone-related incidents and civilian casualties have risen since 2021, civilian protection has become a key legitimacy test. Ensuring targeting discipline, proportionality, coordination with humanitarian actors, and systematic after-action review is now both a legal and operational imperative.

Overall, drone proliferation in the Sahel represents not only a tactical shift on the battlefield but also a broader strategic and diplomatic realignment.

## Background

The Sahel is an increasingly unstable region marked by insurgency, terrorism, intercommunal violence, and organised crime. In this volatile context, drones have rapidly evolved from niche tools into central instruments for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance (ISR), and targeted strikes. This “dronisation” reflects both growing militarisation of airspace and Sahelian governments’ efforts to assert technological sovereignty, reduce reliance on foreign forces, and rebalance power vis-à-vis armed groups and external partners.

## Key insights

### Trends

*   Drones are now integral to conflicts in the Sahel.
*   They give national forces greater reach and faster operational tempo.
*   They provide non-state actors with low-cost, asymmetric capabilities.
*   Their proliferation has:

    *   Accelerated the pace of military operations
    *   Lowered thresholds for engagement
    *   Created new vulnerabilities for governments and civilians

*   Drone use involves a three-way trade-off between:

    *   Tactical effectiveness
    *   Technological independence
    *   Political sovereignty and legitimacy

*   Effective mastery depends not only on technology, but also on:

    *   Legal frameworks
    *   Public consent
    *   Clear procedures and accountability

Opportunities vs Challenges  

*   Transparent and rule-based drone employment can strengthen national and regional cohesion in perception-driven conflict environments.
*   Sahelian states face a dual challenge:

    *   Exploit the operational advantages of dronisation
    *   Prevent drones from increasing instability or mistrust

*   Meeting this challenge requires:

    *   Clear operational and legal standards
    *   Sustainable, nationally owned budget policies
    *   Routine after-action reviews and experience-sharing
    *   Practical regional cooperation

*   Ultimately, drones reflect political choices:

    *   Without safeguards, they can deepen fear and fragmentation
    *   Used responsibly, they can enhance protection, resilience, and modern state capability
