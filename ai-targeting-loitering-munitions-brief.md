# AI-Enabled Targeting, Loitering Munitions and Armed Unmanned Systems: Evidence Brief

**Threat assessment · closing date 25 September 2026**
**Scope:** capability, cost, availability, general principles only — no construction, payload design, targeting procedures or operational guidance. **[D]** demonstrated · **[C]** claimed (belligerent/vendor/single source) · **[I]** inferred. Confidence H/M/L. Full per-claim detail and source URLs: `ai-targeting-loitering-munitions-annex.md`.

## 1. Israel's AI targeting in Gaza: automated vs human-decided

**These are target-generation and decision-support tools, not autonomous weapons.** No publicly evidenced case exists of an AI system in Gaza autonomously selecting and killing a human.

- **[D] "Lavender"** scored every Gazan 1–100 for "militancy" from surveillance data and at peak marked **~37,000 Palestinians** for possible assassination. Per six Israeli intelligence officers, the only routine check before bombing a junior target's home was confirming the target was **male** — about **20 seconds**; ~10% errors were "treated statistically"; **15–20 civilian deaths** were authorised per junior target, 100+ for seniors. Quality: +972/Local Call, six first-hand sources, partly corroborated by the IDF. **H** existence, **M** detail.
- **[D] "Where's Daddy?"** tracked Lavender-marked individuals and alerted officers when they entered a family home (**M**).
- **[D] "Habsora"/"The Gospel"** generates **structures** at **~100 targets/day** versus ~50/year before; the **IDF confirmed on its own website** using it to "produce targets at a fast pace" (**H**).
- **[D] 2026 scale.** At RUSI (26 June 2026), IDF reservist Maj. Gen. and Elbit EVP **Miki Edelstein** presented that the **Tzayad ("Hunter")** programme detected **850,000 "real-time intel targets"** (7 Oct 2023–end 2025, **~1,000/day**) and cut fire-support timelines from **40–50 minutes to 1–7 minutes**, with a **"man in the loop" always deciding**; Elbit later called the figure "aggregated system activity… rather than the number of enemy targets or actual strikes". **H** as stated, **L** as validated targets. **NATO's second most senior commander sat on that panel (H).**

**Contested.** The IDF Spokesperson rejected the Lavender findings, calling AI an "auxiliary tool" requiring independent analyst verification — the strike decision is always human. The dispute is about **the quality of human review**, not who pulls the trigger (**contested, M**). Failure mode: **automation bias at scale**.

## 2. Armed quadcopters and FPVs: Hamas and Hezbollah

- **[D] Hamas, 7 Oct 2023.** Small commercial quadcopters formed the **first wave**, destroying border observation towers and communications and dropping munitions on tanks, soldiers and responders; Al-Qassam claimed 35 "Al-Zawari" loitering munitions. The IDF's Nahal Oz probe found drones plus social-media mapping enabled the base's fall (53 killed, 10 captured). Quality: INSS, *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*, OSINT video. **H**. The model is **not publicly identified** — assessed as commercial off-the-shelf adapted to release munitions (**M**).
- **[D] Later Gaza use and Hezbollah.** Lower-intensity Hamas attacks continued (a drone workshop was found in Gaza City, Nov 2023 — **M–H**), while Hezbollah fielded **Mirsad-1** (Iranian Ababil-T), **Samad-1/2/3**, small **Hudhud** reconnaissance platforms and later fibre-optic FPV (DFRLab, **H**). Fibre-optic FPV on 7 October and "drones smuggled in aid trucks" are **single-source and conflict with INSS (Feb 2024)** (**L**); evidence for drone support to hostage-taking is **absent**.
- **[D] Binyamina (13 Oct 2024) — the most instructive case.** A drone struck the **Golani Brigade dining hall at Zar'it**, killing **4 soldiers**, wounding **58**. The IAF probe found one downed by the Navy, one by Iron Dome; the survivor was pursued, **two intercept attempts and electronic warfare failed**; it dropped off radar, was assumed crashed, briefly reappeared but was **not identified as a drone, so no sirens sounded**. Quality: **IDF investigation** via ToI/JPost. **H** sequence, **M** detail.
- **[D] Scale.** The IAF stated **~1,200 drones were fired at Israel** in the 2023–24 Lebanon war, **221 penetrating** — roughly **18% leakage** (**M–H**).

**Key lesson:** the binding constraint is **detection and classification**, not drone sophistication. Fibre-optic FPV removes the jamming option entirely.

## 3. Houthi one-way attack drones, shipping and the cost asymmetry

- **[D] Scale.** 111+ attacks/hijackings on shipping from 19 Nov 2023 (Vice Adm. McLane, Jan 2025); ~400 drones/missiles faced, **380 defeated**; 2 vessels sunk, 4 mariners killed; 115+ "complex attacks" by Oct 2025. Quality: official US Navy testimony. **H**.
- **[D] The cost asymmetry.** OWA unit costs are **order-of-magnitude only**: Lancet ~**$35,000**; the widely cited Shahed-136 **$20–50k**, but leaked 2022 Russian purchases ran **$370k → $290k → $193k** per unit, Russian domestic ~**$70k** by 2025. Interceptors: **SM-2 ≈$2.1M, SM-6 ≈$3.9–4.3M, ESSM ≈$1.7M, RAM ≈$0.9M**, Patriot ≈$4M. Pentagon acquisition chief LaPlante, May 2024: *"If we're shooting down a $50,000 one-way drone with a $3 million missile, that's not a good cost equation."* US Navy Red Sea munitions spend est. **~$1bn+** (**M**); ratios ~**100:1–1,000:1** are **[I]**.
- **[D] USVs.** MV *Tutor* (12 Jun 2024) was struck by a converted skiff USV and sank; *Chios Lion* (15 Jul 2024) hit ~97 nm off Al Hudaydah. Purpose-built "Toofan"/"Blowfish" USVs carry 150–500 kg warheads. **No confirmed successful USV/UUV attack on a coalition warship.**
- **[D] Air-defence leakage.** The 19 Jul 2024 Tel Aviv drone strike killed 1 after the IAF tracked it ~6 minutes and judged it harmless; the 4 May 2025 missile reached Ben Gurion airport after Arrow and THAAD failed.

## 4. Iran and its proxies: proliferation, Tower 22, Baghdad 2021, Europe

- **[D] Shahed proliferation.** Licensed production in **Russia (Geran-2)**; documented Mohajer-6 to **Sudan** (AFRICOM imagery) and Iranian-designed assembly in **Venezuela** (OFAC, primary). **Armenia, Ethiopia, Belarus, Tajikistan** rest on weaker or contested claims. **H** for Russia/Sudan/Venezuela, **L** for the rest. **Alabuga** opened July 2023; **>5,700 Shahed-136s produced Jan–Sep 2024** (**H**).
- **[D] Tower 22, Jordan (28 Jan 2024).** An OWA drone killed **3 US soldiers, wounded 41–47**; the US blamed **Islamic Resistance in Iraq**. The Army investigation found the defence cell distracted by a **Scan Eagle recovery** and dismissing radar tracks as "birds or trash"; the drone came in low on a path coincident with a returning US drone and **no effort was made to shoot it down** — a **human-factors and classification** failure (**H** attack/attribution, **M** intercept failure).
- **[D] Iraq PM attack (7 Nov 2021).** Explosive-laden drone(s) — Iraqi agencies called them **quadcopters** — struck PM **al-Kadhimi's** residence; he was unhurt. **No group claimed responsibility**; attribution to Kata'ib Hezbollah/Asaib Ahl al-Haq is anonymous-sourced **[ALLEGED], L**.
- **[D] Europe — an important negative.** July 2024: four arrested in **Germany and Spain** over a **Hezbollah**-linked component network. But a dedicated check found **no public European case of police seizing Iranian drone components in a plot context**: the 31 July 2025 **14-state statement** condemns "kill, kidnap and harass" plots via criminal proxies and **does not mention drones**; German 2026 prosecutions concern arson/surveillance; the Sept 2025 Danish/Norwegian airport incursions were attributed to **Russia**. **Treat "Iranian drone plots in Europe" as largely unsupported (L).**
- **[D] Legal status and 2026.** E3 **snapback** reinstated Res. 1929 (the arms embargo) on **27 Sep 2025** — **a UN arms embargo on Iran is formally back in force**. From 28 Feb 2026 CSIS records the **UAE alone absorbing 1,422 drones and 246 missiles** (1–8 March), drones being **~71% of recorded strikes**; the US reportedly expended **~65% of its 2,330 Patriot interceptors** Feb–Jul 2026 (**M**). **Interception percentages are belligerent claims without independent battle-damage assessment.**

## 5. The 2020 Libya "Kargu-2" claim — what the primary evidence says

The most-cited and most-misread item in the autonomy debate. **The primary document does not support the headline.**

UN Panel of Experts on Libya, **S/2021/229** para. 63 (submitted March 2021, describing a **March 2020** engagement): *"Logistics convoys and retreating [Haftar Affiliated Forces] were subsequently hunted down and remotely engaged by the unmanned combat aerial vehicles **or** the lethal autonomous weapons systems **such as** the STM Kargu-2 **and other** loitering munitions. The lethal autonomous weapons systems were programmed to attack targets without requiring data connectivity… in effect, a true 'fire, forget and find' capability."*

**Why contested.** (1) The report says **"possible"** and does **not** state anyone was killed autonomously — the Lieber Institute: "The report does not say for certain that human beings were killed by such systems operating without human supervision." (2) **"Remotely engaged"** contradicts a no-data-link claim, and the sentence aggregates systems with "or… such as… and other"; it is a battlefield narrative, not a verified engagement. (3) The Kargu-2's published autonomy is **target recognition with a human selecting the target** — STM's own marketing shows a human selecting and engaging attack mode; "true fire-and-forget" is a **UN Panel inference**, not a manufacturer-confirmed capability. (4) No specific munition, operator or casualty is identified for any given strike.

**Assessment:** "a Kargu-2 autonomously killed a human in Libya in 2020" is **CLAIMED/INFERRED, not DEMONSTRATED**. Confidence that *some* loitering munitions were used in Libya: **H**. That an autonomous lethal engagement occurred: **L**. The episode matters because a **UN primary document** introduced the concept into state practice unverified, and repetition has laundered it toward fact.

## 6. Loitering munitions on the open market

| System | Cost, export and autonomy |
|---|---|
| Switchblade 300/600 (US) | No unit price; $990M + $743M ceilings, quantity undisclosed. Ukraine, France, UK, Taiwan, Greece. Man-in-the-loop |
| Lancet (Russia) | **~$35,000** (analyst estimate). Russia only. TV/EO terminal guidance |
| Harop/Harpy (Israel) | India $100M (2009), quantity ambiguous. India, **Azerbaijan** (combat). Harpy is anti-radiation autonomous homing |
| Warmate (Poland) | No authoritative price. Poland, Ukraine, **UAE, Georgia**. Man-in-the-loop |
| FireFly (Israel) | No public price. **Vietnam local production** (2026). Man-in-the-loop |
| Kargu-2 (Turkey) | No public price. Turkey; **Libya (LNA)** per UN. ML recognition, **human selects** |
| Shahed-136 (Iran) | $20–50k cited; leaked Russian buys $193–370k. Russia, Ethiopia, Sudan, Venezuela + non-state. GNSS/waypoint |

- **Rule: contract value ≠ unit price.** Known figures are ceilings with undisclosed quantities. **No credible open unit price exists for Switchblade, Hero, Warmate, SkyStriker, FireFly or Kargu**; the widely quoted "~$6,000 Switchblade" is unsourced. Taiwan's $60.2M/720 implies ~$83.6k naively but is a **support-inclusive FMS package**. FPV one-way attack is **~$400–$2,000**; one **~$24M Tor ≈ 14,000 FPVs**. Flight controllers, FPV links, EO/IR modules and edge AI are commercially available; high-end EO/IR and GNSS/IMU are controlled (ITAR USML Cat VIII, EU Reg 2021/821). **On 8 Oct 2025 BIS added 15 Chinese firms to the Entity List over US-origin components in weaponised UAVs used by Hamas and the Houthis.**
- **Non-state acquisition — the key distinction.** **[D] State-supplied purpose-built loitering munitions ARE documented**: Iranian **Ababil-2T** variants to **Hamas ("Shihab", "Al-Zawari")** and **Hezbollah ("Mirsad-1", used Oct 2024)**, plus Samad/Shihab/Shahed-136 to the Houthis and Shahed-131 "Murad-6" to Iraqi militias (Oryx; corroborated by DIA and UN reporting; operational proof at Tower 22) (**H**). **[I] No case found (2020–Sep 2026) of a non-state group independently purchasing such a munition on the open market, or capturing and reusing a Western/Israeli one** — any "terrorists bought Switchblades" claim should be rejected (**M**). Closest case: **Aug 2024, Puntland intercepted five FPV UAVs plus explosives** allegedly shipped from Yemen for al-Shabaab — **[C]**, unverified.

## 7. AI/autonomy use by non-state armed groups

- **[D] ISIS (2014–19).** An industrialised bomb-drop quadcopter programme peaking in spring 2017 at **60–100+ attacks/month** (first lethal IED-drone Oct 2016), built on **commercially purchased quadcopters plus add-on release hardware**. The coalition answer was a **~$700M counter-UAS programme**. Remote-piloted, **not autonomous** (Conflict Armament Research, CTC West Point; **H**).
- **[D] Two-tier baseline and three corrections.** Purpose-built military UAS versus modified commercial/FPV. **Hezbollah now 3D-prints fibre-optic FPV at ~$300–400 each**, **operator-piloted and detonated on impact**; Iraqi militias used the same unjammable pattern against US bases (Victory Base, Mar 2026) (**H**). Three widely repeated claims do not survive scrutiny: the UN Panel on Somalia **recorded no confirmation of weaponised UAV use by Somali non-state groups**, so al-Shabaab offensive use is **INFERRED, not demonstrated** (**L**) — the documented Somali story is **ISIS-Somalia** (>1,000 fighters; **>100 UAVs by late 2024**); there is **no evidence of encrypted-app component marketplaces** (documented instead: a **5 kg-payload UAV assembled purely from open online sources**, the **electric engine the key chokepoint**); and Gaza drone smuggling has replaced tunnels for small high-value items (10+ interceptions, Oct 2024–May 2025; **DJI FlyCart 30 carrying 9–10 rifles per flight**), attribution unverified.
- **[D] Sahel.** **JNIM** is the documented operator — **>2 dozen confirmed incidents, 82% since March 2025**, including an FPV kamikaze attack in Togo killing 5. **Libya→Sahel drone-specific transfers are not itemised in UN reporting** — an evidence gap (**L**).
- **[I] No non-state group has demonstrably fielded machine-vision terminal guidance or autonomous target selection** (**M**, absence of evidence).
- **[D/C] The new autonomy data point.** Anthropic's **Sept 2026** report (case GTG-27005) documents **likely freelance Russia-based actors — not a state** — designing a "full-stack autonomous FPV kamikaze drone swarm": onboard small language model, camera terminal guidance issuing the detonation call, a **"person" target class "without a human in the loop"**. A **Yemen-based cell** used Claude Code for GNC and flight-control firmware and ran a live field test that **appears to have failed**. **Design DEMONSTRATED; fielding NOT.** Primary corporate disclosure, partly self-interested: **M** design, **L** capability. The frontier risk is **AI-assisted design of autonomy by irregular developers**. State-side drift runs the other way: Russian Geran-2s carry computer-vision cameras and mesh radios for mid-mission retasking, but **no Iranian vendor spec substantiates terminal AI on the Shahed-238** (**L–M**).

## 8. Proliferation channels

1. **State transfer (dominant) [D].** Iran→Houthis/Hezbollah/Hamas; Turkey and China to export markets. Evidence includes matching serials on captured Qasef airframes and 2016 interceptions of Qasef components near Ma'rib. UN reporting records smuggling via the Horn of Africa.
2. **Component/dual-use procurement (rising) [D].** The July 2024 Germany/Spain arrests (Hezbollah) and the Oct 2025 BIS Entity List action (US-origin parts in Hamas/Houthi UAVs) show this is now the primary enforcement battleground.
3. **Libya→Sahel [D/I].** Post-2011 stockpile dispersal is long-established and the Libya Panel identified two primary Libya→RSF routes — but **drone-specific transfers are not itemised**. **M**/**L**.
4. **Captured stocks / reverse engineering [I].** Documented for ISIS and the Houthis (hybrid Iranian internals in local fibreglass airframes); **no strong evidence of Western purpose-built loitering munitions being reverse-engineered** by non-state actors. **M–L**.
5. **Smuggling into Gaza/Lebanon [D/C].** Gaza: drone-borne small-arms smuggling documented, attribution unverified. Lebanon: resupply shifted to **local 3D-printed manufacture**.
6. **Open-market supply [D].** The commercially available subset is the true enabler; controls target the munition-specific end, not the general-purpose end. **H**.

## 9. What would and would NOT transfer to NATO civilian societies

**WOULD transfer:** the **cost-exchange problem** (cheap OWA versus expensive interceptors over fixed infrastructure); **detection and classification gaps** (Binyamina, Tel Aviv, Tower 22 — a sensor/decision problem for NATO airspace and critical national infrastructure); **dual-use supply chains** (enabling components are purchasable inside NATO states); **state-supplied proxy capability**; **institutional pull toward automation** (Elbit briefed a NATO commander on a ~1,000-detections/day system — the risk is automation bias, not "killer robots"); and **maritime exposure** (USVs and OWA drones against ports, undersea cables, energy infrastructure). **H.**

**WOULD NOT directly transfer:** **mass civilian target generation under permissive collateral policy** (Lavender-type kill-lists depend on a legal-policy and total-surveillance regime, not the technology); the **Libya/Sahel/Gaza smuggling economy**; **belligerent-supplied OWA at scale**; **al-Shabaab/Houthi-style proliferation nodes**; and **fully autonomous engagement** — there is **no demonstrated case anywhere** of a machine autonomously selecting and killing a human in the way the popular Kargu-2 narrative asserts. **H.** NATO's exposure is to **cheap mass**, not autonomy.

## 10. Key judgements

1. **The cheap-mass model is now being copied by states, not just resisted** (Gulf low-cost interceptors; Saudi Shahed-style interest). **Medium–High.**
2. **No demonstrated autonomous lethal engagement by an AI system in Gaza, Libya, or by any non-state actor**; the strongest claim (Kargu-2) rests on a hedged UN "possible". **High.**
3. **Gaza shows automation at target generation with nominal human approval that sources describe as minimal** — the dispute is the quality of review, not who pulls the trigger. **High** existence / **Medium** degree.
4. **The decisive near-term threat is cheap mass plus detection gaps, not autonomy** (exchange ratios ~100:1–1,000:1). **High.**
5. **Non-state actors have not independently acquired purpose-built loitering munitions**; they receive them from states or improvise from commercial drones. **Medium** (absence of evidence).
6. **Proliferation runs through state transfer and uncontrolled dual-use components, not a mature black market in finished munitions.** **Medium–High.**
7. **NATO's transferable exposure is the cost curve, the sensor/classification gap, and dual-use supply chains.** **High.**
8. **The credible autonomy frontier is AI-assisted design by irregular developers, not fielded autonomous weapons.** **Medium** design, **Low** capability.
