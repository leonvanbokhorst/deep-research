# Platform Propaganda: CIB, Computational Propaganda, and Generative-AI Influence Operations

**Purpose.** Evidence base for auditing the *"agentic threat amplification"* hypothesis — that AI agents could deliberately amplify ambiguous **physical** threats (drone sightings, sabotage reports, infrastructure incidents) so that communities, media and institutions generate the disruptive effect themselves. September 2026. Confidence: **H** = primary source read this session; **M** = secondary/quoted, URL verified; **L** = weak/single-source; **UNVERIFIED** = not confirmed.

---

## A. Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour (CIB)

**Origin.** The term enters public use in Meta/Facebook's 2018 takedown announcements and is explained in Nathaniel Gleicher, *"Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Explained," Meta Newsroom, 6 Dec 2018* — https://about.fb.com/2018/12/inside-feed-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior/ [H].

**First formal definition** — *"Inauthentic Behavior Policy Update," Meta, 21 Oct 2019* — https://about.fb.com/2019/10/inauthentic-behavior-policy-update/ [H]: *"groups of accounts and Pages working together to mislead people about who they are and what they're doing. When we find domestic, non-government campaigns in which the use of fake accounts is central to the operation, we will remove all inauthentic and authentic accounts, Pages and groups directly involved."* That post created the separate **Foreign or Government Interference** category and launched the *CIB Report*.

**Current definition** (Community Standard, change log through 12 Dec 2025) — https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/ [H]: CIB is *"particularly sophisticated forms of Inauthentic Behavior where false identities are central to the operation and operators use adversarial methods to evade detection or appear authentic."* Meta's operational gloss: *"coordinated efforts to manipulate public debate for a strategic goal, in which fake accounts are central to the operation… we focus on **behavior, not content** — no matter what they post or whether they're foreign or domestic"* (Q1 2025 Adversarial Threat Report, p.3) — https://transparency.meta.com/sr/Q1-2025-Adversarial-threat-report/ [H].

**Rename/retirement.** Meta did **not** retire "CIB" as policy — it survives in the Community Standards. What changed is the **reporting brand**: monthly *CIB Report(s)* (2019 – Jan 2022) → *Meta's Adversarial Threat Report* (Dec 2021; quarterly from Q1 2022) → **semiannual from 2025**. Index: https://transparency.meta.com/metasecurity/threat-reporting/ [H].

**Covers / explicitly excludes.** CIB covers fake-account networks whose deception about identity or origin is central; a residual *"Inauthentic Behavior"* category covers spam, fake engagement and *"inauthentic amplification."* Enforcement is *"agnostic of content or ideology"*: it targets the network, not the message. It therefore deliberately does **not** cover authentic users organically amplifying a claim — however false or disruptive. "Authentic" assets are removed only when part of an operation's infrastructure. [H]

**Equivalents.**
- **X** — state-affiliated-media and automated-account labels; platform-manipulation policy covers fake accounts/coordination. X largely withdrew from this architecture after 2023 (Civic Integrity team disbanded; transparency reporting suspended). URL verified reachable but Cloudflare-gated: https://help.x.com/en/using-x/state-affiliated-media-labels [M].
- **TikTok** — dedicated **Covert Influence Operations** report, *"Strengthening our approach to countering influence attempts," 23 May 2024* — https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/strengthening-our-approach-to-countering-influence-attempts [H]: **15 influence operations disrupted and 3,001 accounts removed in the first four months of 2024**. Hub: https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/covert-influence-operations.
- **Google** — GTIG/TAG defines IO as activity that *"attempt[s] to influence online audiences in a deceptive, coordinated manner… sockpuppet accounts and comment brigading"*; *"Adversarial Misuse of Generative AI," Jan 2025* — https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/adversarial-misuse-generative-ai [H]: *"while AI can be a useful tool for threat actors, it is not yet the game-changer it is sometimes portrayed to be… we do not see indications of them developing novel capabilities."*

**Critiques.** The critical literature argues CIB is (i) **vague/under-theorised** — its boundaries ("coordination", "inauthenticity") are set by the enforcing platform; (ii) **legitimising of platform policing**, expanding private power over political speech under a security rubric; and (iii) unable in principle to **distinguish authenticity from inauthenticity**, since "authentic" is an identity claim, not a measurable property. The standard citations include Kate Starbird, *"Disinformation's spread: bots, trolls and all of us," Nature* (2019) — https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-02235-x [H] — plus the platform-governance literature (Gillespie, Gorwa); fuller verified bibliography in `amplification/notes/cib-critiques.md` [M].

**Verdict — Partial (contrast vocabulary only).** *Deliberate* (engineered fake-account networks). Supplies the governance vocabulary and the closest thing to an official success metric (Breakout Scale, "authentic audiences"). **Does NOT fit:** no concept of an ambiguous **physical incident**; it **explicitly excludes organic amplification**, so a hypothesis whose payload is real communities/media/institutions reacting falls outside CIB by construction; and it presumes **fake identities**, whereas agent-crafted narratives can spread through entirely real accounts.

## B. Computational Propaganda

**Origin/definition.** Samuel C. Woolley & Philip N. Howard (eds.), *Computational Propaganda: Political Parties, Politicians, and Political Manipulation on Social Media* (Oxford UP, 2018) — https://global.oup.com/academic/product/computational-propaganda-9780190931407 [M]. The **Oxford Computational Propaganda Project (COMPROP)** is now the *Programme on Democracy and Technology* at the OII — https://comprop.oii.ox.ac.uk/ [H]. The most-quoted definition — *"the use of algorithms, automation, and human curation to purposefully distribute misleading information over social media networks"* — is from the book's introduction; exact wording is **M** (widely cited; canonical URL = the book). The companion article, Woolley & Howard, *"Political Communication, Computational Propaganda, and Autonomous Agents,"* IJoC 10 (2016) — https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/6298 [H] — frames political bots as *"technological proxies… in subtle attempts to manipulate public opinion"* and notes their operation *"can be unpredictable, even to those who build them."* Follow-up: Woolley, *The Reality Game* (2020) [M].

**Claims:** automation, scale and anonymity let actors manufacture the *appearance* of consensus. *Mostly deliberate*, but the 2016 paper explicitly flags **emergent unpredictability**. **Fit: Partial** — the "computational" layer maps onto novelty claim (i). **Does NOT fit:** the tradition is about **beliefs and elections**, not physical incidents, and its core unit ("bots") is now a discredited measurement category.

**Bot-detection accuracy.** The canonical critique is **Rauchfleisch & Kaiser, "The False Positive Problem of Automatic Bot Detection in Social Science Research"** (2020) — which showed high false-positive rates and systematic misclassification of activists, non-English and Global South users. This underpins the **scholarly backlash against over-claiming "Russian bots"** in 2016–2018: reach and influence were routinely overstated, and better-measured later work found far smaller effects. Verified bibliography in `amplification/notes/cib-critiques.md` [M; volume/page details pending]. **Implication:** any claim that "AI agents drove the amplification" must survive the standard that "bots" failed — detection-based attribution is weak evidence of agency or intent.

**Do automated campaigns work? Minimal-effects evidence.** The strongest constraint on the hypothesis.
- **Eady et al. (2023),** *Nature Communications* 14:62 — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-35576-9 [H, Crossref-verified]. Exposure was concentrated in a small slice of users; **no meaningful relationship** with changes in attitudes, polarisation or voting behaviour.
- **Bail et al. (2019/2020),** *PNAS* 117(1):243–250 — https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116 [H, Crossref-verified]. At most small effects; IRA direct reach was a tiny fraction of the platform.
- **Meta's own reports:** operations removed *"before the operators were able to build authentic audiences"*; in Romania *"the majority of these comments received no engagement from authentic audiences"* (Q1 2025) [H]. **Simon, Altay & Mercier (2023)**, *"…Fears about the impact of generative AI on misinformation are overblown,"* HKS Misinformation Review — https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/misinformation-reloaded-fears-about-the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-misinformation-are-overblown/ [M].
- **Counterpoint (lab effect sizes):** Goldstein et al., *PNAS Nexus* 2024 — GPT-3 propaganda **+19.1pp** agreement vs **+23pp** human; edited+curated **52.7% vs 47.4%** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae034. Salvi et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* 2025 — GPT-4 **+81.2% relative odds**, but **only with personalisation** (otherwise n.s.) https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6. Hackenburg et al., *Science* 2025 (N=76,977) — gains from post-training/prompting, traded against accuracy https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea3884 [all M]. **Reading:** persuasion is demonstrable **individually, in controlled settings**; there is **no campaign-level causal estimate** of population-scale opinion or behaviour change. Exaggerating AI amplification is a *known* failure mode.
- **Fit:** the single most important corrective. It does not falsify the hypothesis — which relocates the effect from *persuasion* to *institutional reaction* — but it puts the burden of proof on demonstrating real-world disruption. **Does NOT fit:** it measures **elections/beliefs**, not emergency response or infrastructure disruption. That outcome variable is largely unmeasured — the hypothesis's opportunity *and* its evidential hole.

## C. Generative-AI Influence Operations (c. 2024–2026)

**OpenAI** is the most explicit vendor source, and it runs *against* the strong hypothesis:
- **May 2024** — https://openai.com/index/disrupting-deceptive-uses-of-ai-by-covert-influence-operations/ [H]: *"these campaigns do not appear to have meaningfully increased their audience engagement or reach as a result of our services."* None of five exceeded **Breakout Scale Category 2**; none managed *"authentic engagement."*
- **October 2024** — https://cdn.openai.com/threat-intelligence-reports/influence-and-cyber-operations-an-update_October-2024.pdf [H, PDF read]: *"we have not observed any cases of election-related influence operations attracting viral engagement or building sustained audiences through their use of our models."* All election IO scored **Category 2**; and *"the deceptive activity that achieved the greatest social media reach and media interest was a hoax **about** the use of AI, not the use of AI itself."*
- **February 2026** — https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/df438d70-e3fe-4a6c-a403-ff632def8f79/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai.pdf [H, read by research stream]: seven tweets from **one prompt** reached **150,000 vs 57 views**, tracking follower counts (600,000 vs 827) — **followers, not AI authorship, drove reach**. China's "Cyber Special Operations": **50,000+ posts across 200+ platforms, under 150 clearing 300 shares/comments (~0.3%)**.
- **Anthropic, September 2026** — https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026 [M]: *"Influence operations often fail to reach a genuine audience"*; a 70-site/8,913-article network still scored **Category Two**; the only Category Four case reached people via **FM radio**. March 2025: an LLM-orchestrated network (Claude deciding when 100+ bots engaged) saw *"no content achieve viral status"* [M].

*Caveat:* vendors report only operations touching their own models — evidence about *reach achieved*, not prevalence [H as caveat].

**Demonstrated vs speculative.** Demonstrated: AI lowers content-production cost; AI-assisted IO exists; LLMs can orchestrate bot networks (cost model: Musser 2023, arXiv:2308.03740, ~70% reduction) [M]. Simulation only: autonomous agent-to-agent propaganda coordination (USC, WWW 2026, arXiv:2510.25003) — **not observed in the wild** [M]. **UNVERIFIED/speculative:** any fully autonomous agentic campaign achieving sustained real-world disruption; any campaign-level causal effect.

**Physical incidents.** The best-documented ambiguous-incident disruptions involve **little to no verified AI role**:
- **Gatwick, 19–21 Dec 2018** — a 33-hour closure triggered by reported drone sightings: **~1,000 flights diverted/cancelled and ~140,000 passengers affected**; two people arrested and **both released without charge** (later compensated), and *"no culprit or evidence of drones was found"*; a 2024 FOI showed the police air service recorded **no drone sightings at Gatwick** in the period. The investigation ran 18 months, cost £800,000 and involved five police forces (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport_drone_incident [M]). Disruption came from *institutional response to an ambiguous report* — the hypothesised mechanism — but **without AI**.
- **US Northeast "drone panic," Nov 2024 – Jan 2025** — the DHS/FBI/FAA/DoD joint statement (17 Dec 2024, https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/dhs-fbi-faa-dod-joint-statement-ongoing-response-reported-drone-sightings [H]) recorded **5,000+ sightings, ~100 leads**, assessed as lawful drones, hobbyist drones, manned aircraft, helicopters and **stars**, with *"nothing anomalous."* A verified AI-content role in *driving* the panic is **UNVERIFIED**; AI-generated drone imagery did circulate [M, pending].

**Verdict — mechanism fits, agent does not.** *Emergent*, not deliberate: the disruptive effect came from official precaution, media amplification and public alarm. The hypothesis's novelty claim (i) — AI agents as engine — is **capability-plausible but empirically unobserved**; claim (ii), physical-incident targeting, is essentially **absent from the CIB/computational-propaganda/LLM-IO literature** — the strongest argument for genuine novelty, and the strongest warning that there is no baseline to measure against.

## Fit audit

| Hypothesis component | Closest literature | Fit | Principal gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) AI agents as amplification engine | Computational propaganda; LLM-IO reports | **Partial** | Automation demonstrated, autonomy not; agentic coordination simulated only; reach driven by followers not AI |
| (ii) Ambiguous *physical* incidents | Gatwick; NJ drones | **Partial (mechanism), Poor (evidence)** | Mechanism documented without AI; no AI–physical-incident literature |
| (iii) Population supplies disruption | Minimal-effects literature (inverted) | **Poor as persuasion; High as reaction** | Persuasion effects small/contested; reaction effects real but unmeasured |
| CIB as governance frame | Meta CIB | **Poor** | Excludes authentic/organic amplification — the core payload |

**Bottom line.** These three concepts are strong on *vocabulary and mechanism*, weak on *demonstrated effect*. The decisive gap is measurement: no campaign-level evidence that inauthentic amplification moves populations, and **no case of AI agents engineering disruption through ambiguous physical incidents**. Present the hypothesis as a plausible, novel, currently unobserved threat class — not as an extrapolation from demonstrated effects.
