# Reach and persuasive effect of covert influence operations and coordinated inauthentic behaviour

**Cut-off 25 Sep 2026.** Defensive/analytic only. Grades: **A** primary/official · **B** peer-reviewed · **C** reputable news · **D** vendor/think-tank/preprint · **E** unverified. Confidence = my judgement that the specific *number* survives scrutiny. **[DEM]** measured · **[CLM]** asserted but unmeasured · **[INF]** inference.

**Bottom line.** Foreign IO reach was real but heavily concentrated and small relative to domestic political content; its measured relationship to attitudes, turnout and vote choice is null-to-negligible. The strongest causal effects come from targeted voter-*suppression* advertising and from laboratory AI-persuasion experiments — neither shown to scale to elections. A Dec 2025 meta-analysis finds **no significant LLM-vs-human persuasion difference**, and the headline "AI microtargeting is uniquely dangerous" result was **corrected to non-significance in Sep 2026**.

## 1. IRA measurement literature

**Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker**, *Nature Communications*, 9 Jan 2023, <https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9> · **B/high** · **[DEM]**. Panel of 1,496 US Twitter users linked to full timelines (1.2bn posts): **1% of users = 70%** of exposures (10% = 98%); **1% of Russian accounts = 89%** of IRA content in timelines; ~**4** IRA posts/user/day vs **106** news + **35** politicians (**25×**/**9×**); median weekly exposure in the final month = **0**; "Strong Republicans" ~**9×** Democrats/Independents; 63% potentially exposed → **~32m Americans** **[INF]**; vote choice **−0.18 pp** (90% CI −1.15, 0.78); no meaningful attitude or polarisation relationship. Caveats: "exposure" = *potential* exposure, not attention; observational; Twitter-only. The paper's own candidate for the campaign's largest effect is **second-order** — convincing Americans the interference worked — which it does not measure.

**Bail et al.**, *PNAS* 117(1):243–250, 25 Nov 2019, <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116> · **B/high** · **[DEM]**. 1,239 partisan Twitter users; only **76 (6.1%)** had interacted with IRA accounts (44 treated vs 1,106 control); Bayesian causal forest finds **no significant effect on any of 6 outcomes**; interactions were **0.1%** of those users' engagement and concentrated among high-echo-chamber heavy users — the least persuadable.

**Freelon & Lokot**, *HKS Misinformation Review*, 16 Feb 2023, <https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/russian-disinformation-campaigns-on-twitter/> · **B/med-high** · **[DEM]**: 2,057,747 authentic replies across **10 communities** — the IRA reached conservative, progressive *and* Black audiences, **qualifying** the partisan-concentration finding above (engagement, not timeline exposure). **Linvill & Warren**, same venue, 14 Jan 2020: the IRA's final phase mostly *retweeted* real users, amplifying **>100k** external accounts · **B/medium**. Extreme concentration is general, not an IRA quirk (Baribi-Bartov et al., *Science*, 30 May 2024, <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adl4435>: **2,107** voters, 0.3% of a 664,391-voter panel, produced **80%** of fake-news sharing; **B/high**).

**Official figures and provenance.** **SSCI Vol. 2**, *Russia's Use of Social Media*, 8 Oct 2019, p. 77 · **A/high**: **81** Facebook pages / 61,500 posts / 3,393 ads; **133** Instagram accounts / 116,000 posts; **3,841** Twitter accounts / **10.4m** tweets; ~**1,100** YouTube videos (43h) / 17 channels. SSCI says the data are "almost certainly not the entirety" — **every figure is a floor**.
- **Facebook's "126 million" is *reach*, not impressions.** Facebook QFR (Stretch, 8 Jan 2018): 29m served in News Feed × 3 share multiplier → ~126m "may have been served" (**0.004%** of News Feed); ad reach **11.4m**, ~$100k, ~3,000 ads · **A/medium** · **[CLM]**.
- **Twitter**, 19 Jan 2018: 3,814 accounts, 175,993 tweets, **677,775** US users emailed, ~1.4m notified (**A**); **288m** views 1–15 Nov 2016 with **1.4m** direct interactions (**A/medium**) — ~0.5% of views became interactions.
- **Instagram** (187m engagements vs Facebook's claimed 20m reach) is **disputed and unresolved** (**low**); the widely cited Google bundle (1,108 videos / 309,000 views) **conflicts with SSCI and is untraceable to a primary source** → **C/low**. The vendor analyses (New Knowledge; Oxford Computational Propaganda, Dec 2018) are **D**; OII finds the **top 20 pages = 99% of engagement**. **No official document after 2019 revises these numbers**, so the official quantitative record **stops at SSCI Vol. 2**.

## 2. Follow-up, 2023–2026: attitudes, turnout, vote choice

- **Kalla & Broockman**, *APSR* 112(1):148–166, 2018, <https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055417000363> · **B/high** · **[DEM]**: across 40 meta-analysed + 9 original field experiments the "best estimate … is **zero**"; the authors' 2023–26 updates find no rebuttal.
- **Coppock, Hill & Vavreck**, *Science Advances* 6(36):eabc4046, 2 Sep 2020, <https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046> · **B/high** · **[DEM]**: 59 experiments, 49 ads, ~34,000 people; favorability ATE **0.049**, vote-choice ATE **0.7 pp (SD 2), not significant**. Persuasion decays fast: half-life **~4 days** (Hill et al. 2013), persistence **≤1 week** (Baum et al., *PSRM* 2026, <https://doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2026.10095>) · **B/medium**.
- **Allcott et al.**, *Nature Human Behaviour* 10(5):884–895, 2026, <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02328-w> · **B/high** · **[DEM]** — the hardest recent null: **>60,000** users; removing political ads from Facebook/Instagram produced **no detectable effect** on knowledge, polarisation, turnout or participation.
- **Kim, Dahlke, Song & Heinrich**, *PNAS*, 26 Jan 2026, <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2519944123> · **B/medium** · **[DEM]** — the main *positive* result: user-level ad tracking merged with verified turnout records; clear geo-racial targeting in 2016 and **small** turnout decreases, sharpest among non-Whites in minority counties of battleground states. This is **demobilisation via paid targeting**, not attitude change via organic IO.

**[INF]** Not "interference never works" but "**the population-level persuasion channel is weak; the suppression channel is real but small and narrow**."

## 3. AI-generated persuasion

- **Salvi, Horta Ribeiro, Gallotti & West**, arXiv **2403.14380** (Mar 2024), published as "On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4," *Nature Human Behaviour* 9(8):1645–1653, 19 May 2025, <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6> · **B/high (lab only)**: N=900; personalised GPT-4 won **64.4%** of untied debates, **+81.2% relative odds** (95% CI +26.0% to +160.7%, P<0.01). **CRITICAL UPDATE — Author Correction, 3 Sep 2026** (<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02588-0>): a calculation error means personalised vs *non*-personalised GPT-4 is now **+48.7%, 95% CI −2.9% to +127.6%, P=0.068 (n.s.)**; the paper now says it "**cannot conclusively establish … that personalization confers an additional advantage**." The microtargeting headline is effectively retracted.
- **Hölbling, Maier & Feuerriegel**, *Scientific Reports* 15:43818, 12 Dec 2025, <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-30783-y> · **B/high** · **[DEM]**: 7 studies, **17,422** participants — **no significant overall LLM-vs-human persuasion difference (g = 0.02, p = .530; I² = 76%)**. The "AI persuasion is not special" anchor.
- **Hackenburg & Margetts**, *PNAS* 2024, <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2403116121> · **B/high**: N=8,587; messages moved opinion up to **+12 pp**, but **microtargeted 4.83 pp vs non-targeted 6.20 pp (P=0.226)** — targeting did **not** beat a generic message.
- **Hackenburg et al.**, *Science* 390(6777):eaea3884, 4 Dec 2025, <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea3884> · **B/high**: N=76,977; 19 LLMs; 707 issues. Post-training **+51%**, prompting **+27%**; personalisation and scale smaller. **Levers that raised persuasion systematically lowered factual accuracy** — effects run on information, not style, and trade off against truth. **Goldstein et al.**, *PNAS Nexus* 2024, <https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae034> · **B/high**: N=8,221; control 24.4% → original propaganda **47.4%**, GPT-3 **43.5%** (+19.1 pp); curated/edited n.s.; **no behaviour measured**.
- **No robust "AI penalty."** **Gallegos et al.**, *PNAS Nexus* 2026, <https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag008> · **B/high**: N=1,601; messages moved policy views **+9.74 pp**, yet labelling them as AI had **no significant effect** on attitudes, accuracy judgements or sharing — so detection-based countermeasures are weakly supported.
- **Inoculation works, then fades.** Roozenbeek et al., *Science Advances* 2022 (n=6,464 + 22,632 field) · **B/high**. Stable **≥3 months only with repeated testing**, decay at 2 months without (Maertens et al. 2021, *JEP:Applied*); text/video ≈**1 month**, games decay faster, **memory boosters restore** (Maertens et al., *Nature Communications* 2025, N=11,759); **without an immediate post-test the durable effect is ≈0 (d=.010, p=.854)** (Capewell et al. 2023, preprint). Counterpoint: Wang, Phillips, Carley, Lin & Pennycook 2024 (OSF preprint) — the effect "**appeared to nullify**" in realistic feeds.

## 4. Sceptical assessments

- **Breakout Scale** — Ben **Nimmo** (Graphika), Brookings, **Sep 2020** (not 2019; not François), <https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-breakout-scale-measuring-the-impact-of-influence-operations/> · **D/high**. **Six categories** (not A–F): (1) one community/one platform → (3) multi-platform/multi-community → (4) mainstream-media breakout → (5) celebrity amplification → (6) policy response or call to violence. Its own caveat: breakout "**does not necessarily equate to actual impact**." Worked example: the IRA's 2017 "March Against Sharia" drew "**under a dozen anti-Muslim protesters … and only an estimated 60 counter-protesters**." The bottleneck is *legitimate* amplifiers. **[CLM]** as an impact measure.
- **Takedown arithmetic ≠ reach.** **Google's Influence Operations Bulletin**, Q1 2026 (8 May 2026) and Q2 2026 (31 Jul 2026), <https://blog.google/security/influence-operations-bulletin-q2-2026/> · **A/high**: **15,727** then **18,514** YouTube channels terminated. **Neither bulletin publishes any reach, view or engagement figure** — verified absence. **Meta**: >**200** covert operations removed since 2017 across **68 countries**, **two-thirds wholly or partly domestic**; Q1 2023: "we removed the majority … before they were able to build authentic audiences." Best-quantified case — **Moldova, 11 Oct 2024**: ~**4,200** page followers, **0** group joins, ~$4,000 ad spend. Meta's 2025–26 CIB figures are **unverified** (endpoint blocks automated retrieval).
- **Backfire is rare; debunking helps but decays, and pooled estimates disagree sharply.** Guess & Coppock, *BJPS* 2019: "**no evidence of backlash**" · **B/high**. Walter & Tukachinsky 2019 (32 studies, N=6,527): residual continued influence after correction **r = −.05**. **But** Chan, Jones, Jamieson & Albarracín 2017 (k=52, N=6,878) report debunking **ds = 1.14–1.33**, whereas **Chan & Albarracín**, *Nature Human Behaviour* 2023 (k=245, N=53,320) report **d = 0.11, p=.142 (n.s.)**. **[INF]** A ten-fold spread means correction effect sizes are design- and outcome-dependent, and decay over time.
- **Implied truth effect.** Pennycook, Bear, Collins & Rand, *Management Science* 2020, <https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3478> · **B/high**: warnings produced only a "modest reduction" in false-headline accuracy while **untagged** headlines were judged *more* accurate than control. **Carnegie Endowment** (Bateman & Jackson, 31 Jan 2024, <https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/01/countering-disinformation-effectively-an-evidence-based-policy-guide>) · **D/high**: "**None of the interventions considered in this report were simultaneously well-studied, very effective, and easy to scale**." **Does interference backfire for the intervener? Not verified** — an open hypothesis.

## 5. AI-generated content reach and prevalence, 2025–2026

Prevalence numbers are large, but nearly all come from **vendors with a commercial interest** or **unaudited classifiers**. Reach for *state-linked AI* content is the opposite story.

- **NewsGuard** (for-profit → **D**), AI Content Farm tracker, updated 23 Jun 2026, <https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/ai-tracking-center>: **3,749** AI content-farm sites in 16 languages; classification is **analyst judgement**, and the tracker publishes **no per-site traffic figures** — so the widely repeated "these sites get tiny audiences" claim is **not documented there**. Its *Storm-1516* report (27 Feb 2026) counts 175,000 posts/articles and **274m X views** for 34 false claims (impressions, not unique reach); its 9 Dec 2025 TikTok study counts **6,042** AI-audio deepfake videos, only **22%** of a 100-video sample labelled, **all** creator-applied. Both are **CLAIMED**, not audited.
- **Kapwing** (Jun 2026, **D**; manual classification, no codebook, vendor sells video tooling) found **294 of the first 500 videos (59%)** served to a new TikTok account were AI-generated; YouTube Shorts ~**21%**. **Pangram Labs** (9 Jul 2026, **D**) found a mean AI rate of **13.8%** across 1,002,627 posts, with LinkedIn 62% of flagged content; its 0.01% false-positive rate is **self-published**. The peer-reviewed figure is far smaller — **~0.2% of Community Notes** in Corsi, Marino & Wong's sample of 556 synthetic-media tweets (**1.5bn views**, 3 Jun 2024, *HKS Misinformation Review*) — but that is **a floor, not a prevalence estimate**. DiResta, Goldstein et al. (15 Aug 2024) found one **unlabelled** AI image among Facebook's **top-20 most-viewed posts of Q3 2023 (40m views)**.
- **State-linked LLM operations — the clearest reach datum is small.** **OpenAI**, 25 Aug 2026, <https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-new-covert-influence-campaign-from-russia/> · **A/high** for the takedown, **medium** for reach: a banned Russian cluster reached "**relatively small audiences**," Telegram channels ~**10,000–20,000 followers each**; "the significance of the operation lies less in the audience it reached … than in the infrastructure it had built." **Anthropic** (10 Sep 2026) documents influence-for-hire operations on six continents with **no reach figures**.
- **Hungary, 2026** (Global Voices/Lakmusz, 15 May 2026, <https://globalvoices.org/2026/05/15/why-were-russian-disinformation-government-propaganda-and-ai-generated-campaigning-ineffective-in-hungarian-elections-2026/>) · **C/medium**: Fidesz's AI-generated attack videos went viral but provoked visible disapproval; voters "**strongly objected**," the opposition's top posts drew **≥2×** the interactions of Orbán's, and the autumn-2025 Meta/Google ad ban removed paid amplification — **backlash, not persuasion**. Platform label counts (TikTok: >1.3bn AI videos labelled by Nov 2025) measure **labelling uptake, not prevalence**. **Gap:** no Graphika/DFRLab or Meta Content Library measurement of CIB *reach* was locatable.

## 6. Where the evidence is thin (explicit)

1. **No field experiment has ever randomly assigned exposure to foreign IO.** Every IRA estimate is observational and measures *potential* exposure, not attention — **upper bounds at best**. "1% received 70%" is a concentration statistic about potential exposure, not attention or belief, and is routinely miscited as an impact finding.
2. **Attitudinal effects are asserted far more than measured.** "Sowed discord"/"radicalised" framings are **[CLM]**; the two best-identified individual-level studies find nothing. Persuasion estimates are also bounded, not precise: Eady excludes >0.7 pp in 95% of simulations but not 0.3 pp.
3. **AI-specific impact is the weakest link.** No population-level measurement shows LLM-generated IO changed attitudes, turnout or votes. Lab effects are short-horizon, self-reported, low-dosage; the LLM advantage is context-dependent and **wanes across repeated interactions** (Liu et al., arXiv 2505.09662), averaging to **no advantage** (Hölbling et al.).
4. **"AI qualitatively changed the mechanism" is [CLM].** Demonstrated changes are in **production cost, volume and speed** (takedowns) and **conversational adaptivity** (lab); no study shows a changed population-level effect size.
5. **Prevalence numbers rest on weak instruments** — unaudited detector error, opt-in panels, incompatible definitions ("AI-generated" vs "AI-assisted" vs "brainrot"). **Second-order/legitimacy effects are unmeasured**, **official quantitative reach data stops in 2019**, and pooled correction/inoculation effect sizes disagree by an order of magnitude (§4).

## 7. Citation-integrity corrections to the brief

- **Eady et al. 2023's authors are Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker** — **no "Poteat" and no "Trexler"** (verified). Treat as a **fabricated/garbled citation**.
- **"Like, Post, and Mistrust" (attributed to Guess, Nyhan et al.) could not be located** in Crossref, arXiv, Europe PMC, the HKS Misinformation Review index, or web/news search — independently confirmed twice. **Do not cite without a DOI.**
- **"Costello/Pennycook/Rand, Durability of the effects of gamified psychological inoculation, Science Advances 2024" is not verifiable.** Closest real work: Costello, Pennycook & Rand, *Science* 385, 2024 (<https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814>), N=2,190, ≈−20%, still present at 2 months. **"Durability of the accuracy nudge (Nature Communications 2024)" and "Persistence in the Effects of Advertising" are also unverifiable.**
- **Attributions:** the Breakout Scale is **Nimmo, Sep 2020** (not François, not 2019), and its levels are **Category One–Six**; the **ABC** framework is the one credited to François. Salvi et al.'s arXiv ID is **2403.14380** (2410.12743 is a physics paper), and the **Google/YouTube IRA bundle is C-grade**, not A.
