# Stream E — 2025–2026 measurement of AI-generated content prevalence and reach

Cut-off 25 Sept 2026. Defensive/analytic framing. **D**=demonstrated, **C**=claimed, **I**=inferred.
Grades: A primary/official, B peer-reviewed, C reputable news, D vendor/think-tank, E unverified.

## 0. Bottom line

Prevalence is measured in the tens of percent on several surfaces, but nearly every large number comes from a
**vendor with a commercial interest in the finding** (NewsGuard, Kapwing, Pangram, Originality.ai) or a
**detector-based classifier with no public benchmark**. Reach for *state-linked* AI content is the opposite
story: the best-documented 2025–26 cases report **small audiences**, and the best-studied 2026 national
election (Hungary) suggests AI campaigning **backfired**.

## 1. NewsGuard (for-profit vendor — grade D)

**AI Content Farm tracker [D/C; high confidence on the count, low on traffic].**
https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/ai-tracking-center — **3,749** "AI Content Farm" sites across
**16 languages**, page "last updated **23 June 2026**". Classification requires substantial AI-produced content,
publication without significant human oversight, and no AI disclosure — **analyst judgement, not detector
output**. The tracker publishes **no per-site traffic figures**, so the widely repeated claim that these sites
draw tiny audiences is **not documented on the current tracker**; NewsGuard's historical audience work used
third-party panels (Similarweb; an archived NewsGuard/Comscore co-report), which undercount app/in-app traffic.
**No 2025–26 panel figure verified.**

**Storm-1516 (27 Feb 2026) [D, medium-high].** https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/russian-influence-campaign-storm-1516-targets-france-germany
34 false claims targeting France/Germany since Jan 2025, in **175,000 posts and articles**, drawing **274
million views on X alone** (2024: 12 claims). H2 2025: 18 claims/**147M views** vs H1: 12 claims/97M. Most viral
single item (Macron/Epstein hoax, 4 Feb 2026): **38,000 X posts / 20.4M views**. These are impressions on posts
repeating a claim, not unique reach.

**TikTok anti-Starmer network (9 Dec 2025) [D, high on counts].** https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/tiktok-deepfakes-attack-uk-prime-minister-government
73 accounts, **~1M followers combined**, **6,042** AI-audio deepfake videos since May 2025; **197 videos in
1–8 Dec 2025 → 943,000 views**; some videos **>500,000 views**. Only **22%** of a 100-video sample carried an AI
label, and **all** labels were creator-applied — none auto-applied by TikTok.

Also in the register: Iran-war tracker — **151 false claims**, "hundreds of millions of views"; Taiwan — **294**
phony Threads accounts; Hungary — **34** TikTok accounts posting AI content against Péter Magyar.

## 2. Video prevalence — Kapwing (vendor, grade D)

TikTok "AI Slop Report" (21–22 Jun 2026; https://startupfortune.com/tiktoks-algorithm-is-surfacing-ai-slop-to-new-users-at-three-times-youtubes-rate-and-advertisers-should-be-paying-attention/):
**294 of the first 500 videos (59%)** served to a new TikTok account were AI-generated or AI compilations;
**10,742 videos across 20 categories**. YouTube Shorts comparator **~21%**; the earlier YouTube figure
(Guardian, 27 Dec 2025) was **104 of the first 500 recommendations (20.8%)**. Kids: **57%** of 2,000 videos;
**#CartoonKids 97/100**; #cartoons and #babysong 83%; #forkids 79%. Limits are severe — manual classification,
no published codebook or inter-rater statistic, and Kapwing sells video-creation tooling. Other coverage puts
Shorts at **21% AI + 33% "brainrot"**. **Direction: medium; exact percentages: low.**

## 3. Text platforms — Pangram / Originality.ai (grade D)

Pangram Labs, 9 Jul 2026 (https://ppc.land/linkedin-carries-62-of-flagged-ai-content-pangram-data-shows/):
**1,002,627 posts**, five platforms, opt-in Chrome-extension panel, posts <50 words excluded. Mean AI rate
**13.8%**; **LinkedIn = 62% of all flagged AI content while ~1/3 of items scanned**; **>40%** of longform
LinkedIn posts fully AI; all longform **25.72%**; X articles **23.9% fully + 22.9% mixed**; Substack longform
21.9%; Reddit **4.4%** (replies 98.1% human). Pangram's **0.01% false-positive rate** is its own unaudited
benchmark. Originality.ai (Jan 2026): **53.7%** of longform LinkedIn posts "likely AI" (n=3,368, 99 profiles) —
different model/sample, **not comparable**. **Magnitude: low–medium; direction: medium.**

## 4. Academic and think-tank studies

- **HKS Misinformation Review, Corsi/Marino/Wong, 3 Jun 2024 [B]** (https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/the-spread-of-synthetic-media-on-x/):
  **556** tweets with synthetic media, **1.5 billion views** Dec 2022–Sep 2023; prevalence stabilised at
  **~0.2% of Community Notes**; 59.3% non-political; 76.4% images; **58.2% of videos political**; 57% of
  sharers verified. **The 0.2% is a floor, not a prevalence estimate** — a keyword search over voluntary
  Community Notes cannot see unflagged synthetic content.
- **HKS, DiResta/Goldstein et al., 15 Aug 2024 [B]** (https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/how-spammers-and-scammers-leverage-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-for-audience-growth/):
  125 Facebook Pages posting ≥50 AI images each; **mean 146,681 followers, median 81,000**; "hundreds of
  millions of exposures"; **one unlabeled AI image was among Facebook's top-20 most-viewed posts of Q3 2023 —
  40M views, 1.9M interactions**; algorithm-recommended "unconnected" content rose from **8% (Q2 2021) to 24%
  (Q3 2023) of Feed views**. CrowdTangle-based, non-random.
- **Graphika (2025) [D]** found propaganda campaigns using AI for content creation and **inauthentic influencer
  personas** — fake profiles, synthetic "news reporters" in video, AI-generated fake news sites. Chain verified:
  Lowy Institute/RSIS, 22 Sep 2026 (https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/hostile-influence-peddling-slopaganda-style)
  → NBC News, 19 Nov 2025 (https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/online-propaganda-campaigns-are-using-ai-slop-researchers-say-rcna244618).
  **No prevalence or engagement numbers** in either — qualitative only. The same piece documents an Australian
  network of **hundreds of Facebook pages** running AI deepfakes of politicians, **10% of them monetised by
  Facebook** (ABC News, 9 Sep 2026), and a Singapore campaign of **hundreds of YouTube videos**.
- **Knight First Amendment Institute (Jul 2025)** and **Brennan Center (Aug 2026)** are sceptical syntheses —
  counterweight, not numbers (both rate-limited, unretrieved).

## 5. Platform-reported figures (grade A for policy, low for meaning)

- **TikTok: >1.3 billion AI-generated videos labelled by Nov 2025**, plus invisible watermarks and feed controls
  (via The Guardian, Nov 2025; **URL unverifiable here — medium confidence**). Contrast NewsGuard's **22%**
  labelled in one deepfake sample. Label counts measure *labelling uptake on platform-tooled content*, not
  prevalence, and C2PA metadata is strippable.
- **YouTube, 27 May 2026** — automatic AI labels for realistic AI video without creator disclosure
  (https://blog.youtube/). **Meta** signed the EU AI Act transparency Code of Practice (28 Jul 2026), expanded
  AI ad-disclosure tags (Jul 2026) and limited undisclosed AI profiles on Instagram (31 Aug 2026); Instagram
  labels were reported malfunctioning Sep 2026. **EU AI Act Art. 50** duties took effect **2 Aug 2026**
  (Dec 2026 deadline for pre-existing systems). No platform publishes a prevalence percentage.

## 6. Reach of state-linked LLM-run accounts

- **OpenAI, 25 Aug 2026 [A primary; read via https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-bans-russian-chatgpt-accounts.html].**
  Banned a Russian cluster promoting the "International Burke Institute"; OpenAI assesses the campaign reached
  **"relatively small audiences," with Telegram channels at ~10,000–20,000 followers each**. AI was a
  *supporting* tool to manufacture institutional authority — "the significance of the operation lies less in the
  audience it reached… than in the infrastructure it had built." **The best reach datum in the stream, and it is
  small.**
- **Anthropic, 10 Sep 2026 [A primary, read via https://fintechnews.sg/117000/ai/threat-actors-use-claude/].**
  Influence operations from Russia, Iran, Turkey, the Gulf, South Asia, Africa and Europe on six continents;
  actors include governments, state-aligned propaganda institutions and **influence-for-hire firms**.
  Election-timed: Russian state media fabricating claims about Moldova's president before the Sep 2025 vote; a
  **pro-government Kenyan operator preparing fake grassroots posts ahead of Kenya's 2027 general election**.
  **No reach or engagement figures published.** Adjacent: Citizen Lab, *BlackCore's Influence Operations for
  Hire*, 17 Sep 2026; OpenAI "Data Center Bandwagon", 1 Jun 2026.

## 7. 2026 national-election effect — Hungary (grade C)

Global Voices/Lakmusz, 15 May 2026 (https://globalvoices.org/2026/05/15/why-were-russian-disinformation-government-propaganda-and-ai-generated-campaigning-ineffective-in-hungarian-elections-2026/)
— interviews with Ágnes Urbán (Mérték Media Monitor) and Péter Krekó (Political Capital). **D/C, medium.**
Fidesz's AI war/attack videos went viral, but Urbán: Fidesz "started using AI too early and in too cheap a way"
and users "strongly objected to being fed this type of content"; Krekó: "the majority of voters encountered
AI-generated content and disapproved of its use quite strongly," making it a source of the government's
credibility crisis. Magyar's most popular posts drew **≥2× the interactions** of Orbán's. Meta/Google's
autumn-2025 political-ad ban removed the paid amplification layer. **The strongest 2026 election-reach evidence
— and it points to backlash, not persuasion.**

## 8. Explicit evidence gaps

1. No panel-independent estimate of **audience/reach** for AI content-farm sites in 2026.
2. Every large prevalence percentage (59%, 40%, 25.7%, 13.8%) rests on **vendor classifiers or manual review
   with undisclosed error rates**; the one peer-reviewed X figure (~0.2%) is a biased-sample floor.
3. Definitions are incompatible — "AI-generated" vs "AI-assisted" vs "low-quality" vs "brainrot" (Kapwing's
   21%+33% split shows how far a number moves with definition).
4. Detector error runs both ways: NewsGuard found leading AI image detectors "often declar[e] authentic content
   fake", and three leading chatbots failed to spot Sora videos in **78–95%** of prompts; Pangram's 0.01% FPR
   is unaudited.
5. **Graphika's 2025 slopaganda work is confirmed to exist but cited only qualitatively** — the underlying
   report and any numbers were not retrieved. Treat any Graphika/DFRLab/Alethea prevalence figure as
   **unverified, confidence low**, until sourced directly.
6. Guardian URLs cited via secondary coverage (Kapwing/YouTube; TikTok's 1.3bn labels) were not retrievable here.
