## 1. Minimal-effects baseline (B) — DEMONSTRATED

**Kalla & Broockman, *APSR* 2018, 112(1):148–166**, DOI [10.1017/S0003055417000363](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055417000363) (online 28 Sep 2017; resolves 200). **[D]** "The best estimate of the effects of campaign contact and advertising on Americans' candidate choices in general elections is **zero**" — meta-analysis of **40** field experiments plus **9** original ones (~10× prior evidence). Only two exceptions: unusually unpopular candidate positions + unusually heavy persuadable-voter targeting; and contact long before election day with immediate measurement — "this early persuasion **decays**." Preprint: [SSRN 3042867](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3042867).

**2023–2026 updates [D]** (from [joshuakalla.com/research](https://joshuakalla.com/research), 200 OK): Broockman & Kalla, *AJPS* 2023 67(4):833–849, DOI [10.1111/ajps.12724](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12724) (no OA; **specific effects not retrieved, low confidence**); Kalla & Broockman, *Journal of Politics* 2025 87(1):246–261, DOI [10.1086/730725](https://doi.org/10.1086/730725); Kalla et al., *JEPS* 2026, [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-experimental-political-science/article/framework-to-assess-the-persuasion-risks-large-language-model-chatbots-pose-to-democratic-societies/AB0627A88FEBF023094B8A373F64ECA2). **No published rebuttal overturning the 2018 result was found.**

## 2. Persuasion decay / advertising (B) — DEMONSTRATED

**Hill, Lo, Vavreck & Zaller, *Political Communication* 2013, 30(4):521–547**, DOI [10.1080/10584609.2013.828143](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2013.828143) (OA: [escholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6g9321sd/qt6g9321sd.pdf)). **[D]** "The half-life of persuasion effects in the 2000 election is about **four days**"; sub-national races decay faster (**1–2 day half-lives**, "no discernible long-term survival"); a small residue survives ~6 weeks.

**Coppock, Hill & Vavreck, *Science Advances* 2020, 6(36):eabc4046**, DOI [10.1126/sciadv.abc4046](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046) (PMC7467695, full text read). **[D]** 49 ads, **59** experiments, ~34,000 people. Average favorability effect **0.049 scale points** (5-point scale; SD across experiments 0.07). Average **vote-choice effect 0.7 pp (SD 2 pp), not significant**. "The distribution of advertising effects… excludes large persuasive effects."

**Baum, Berinsky, de Benedictis-Kessner, Jeong, Markovich & Yamamoto, *PSRM* 2026**, DOI [10.1017/psrm.2026.10095](https://doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2026.10095) (OA PDF read). **[D]** One short article/video persists **up to one week** (Fox treatment 0.133 SD at wave 1, attenuated but detectable at wave 2); testing accumulation from repeated doses would need "an unrealistic number of respondents."

Related: Murphy, Coppock, Hill & Vavreck, *Political Communication* 2026, DOI [10.1080/10584609.2026.2726266](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2726266) (**9 Sep 2026; findings not retrieved, low confidence**); Coppock, Green & Porter, *Research & Politics* 2022, DOI [10.1177/20531680221076901](https://doi.org/10.1177/20531680221076901).

> **UNVERIFIED — "Persistence in the Effects of Advertising" (Coppock/Hill/Vavreck).** No Crossref record (title/author/bibliographic), Google News or Bing hit. **Do not cite without a source document. Confidence: low.** Nearest verified: Hill et al. 2013; Baum et al. 2026.

## 3. Foreign influence exposure and attitudes (B) — DEMONSTRATED nulls

**Bail et al., *PNAS* 2020, 117(1):243–250**, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1906420116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116) (full text: Europe PMC PMC6955293). **[D]** n = **1,239** Twitter users, two waves Oct–Nov 2017, merged with nonpublic IRA data. Only **76 (6.1%)** interacted with IRA accounts; those interactions averaged **0.1%** of their likes/mentions/retweets. Bayesian CART: **no significant ATT on any of 6** attitude/behaviour outcomes; dosage models also null; polarization ATTs if anything small and negative.

**Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker, *Nature Communications* 2023, 14**, DOI [10.1038/s41467-022-35576-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-35576-9) (OA PDF read). **[D]** n = 1,496 linked survey–Twitter respondents. **1% of users produced 70% of exposures**; exposure skewed to strong Republicans. Change in attitudes: **0.05 SD** (issue positions), **0.06 SD** (perceived polarization); simulated vote-choice change for Trump **−0.18 pp (90% CI −1.15, 0.78)**. "No evidence of a meaningful relationship… the limits of election interference campaigns."

**Guess, Nyhan & Reifler, *Nature Human Behaviour* 2020**, DOI [10.1038/s41562-020-0833-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0833-x) (PMC7239673, read). **[D]** Untrustworthy sites ≈ **6% of the online news diet (95% CI 5.1–6.7%)**; **62%** of visits came from the most-conservative **20%**; **56.7%** of Trump supporters read ≥1 untrustworthy conservative article vs **27.7%** of Clinton supporters. Equivalence tests rule out only effects **≥10 pp on Trump support / ≥9 pp on turnout** — a *bounded*, not precise, null.

**Guess, Lockett, Lyons, Montgomery, Nyhan & Reifler, *HKS Misinformation Review* 2020**, DOI [10.37016/mr-2020-004](https://doi.org/10.37016/mr-2020-004) (200 OK) — "Fake news may have limited effects on political participation beyond increasing beliefs in false claims." **[D]** title-level; coefficients not retrieved (medium confidence).

> **UNVERIFIED — "Like, Post, and Mistrust? Exposure to Russian Troll Tweets and Americans' Political Attitudes" (Guess/Nyhan et al.).** No Crossref record (title, author=Nyhan, Guess+Nyhan co-authorship), no Google News or Bing hit; venue/year unconfirmed; does not appear to be a published article. **Confidence: low. Do not cite.** Substitutes: Bail 2020; Eady 2023.

## 4. Randomized exposure experiments 2023–2026 (B) — DEMONSTRATED nulls

Meta/Facebook–Instagram 2020 election suite — strongest recent evidence that large real *exposure* changes leave attitudes/turnout unmoved:

- **Guess et al., *Science* 2023, 381(6656):398–404**, DOI [10.1126/science.abp9364](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abp9364). **[D]** Chronological feeds changed exposure substantially; **no significant change in issue/affective polarization or political knowledge** over 3 months. Errata: [10.1126/science.adu8261](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu8261) (2024); [10.1126/science.aeh2575](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeh2575) (2026).
- **Guess et al., *Science* 2023, 381(6656):404–408**, DOI [10.1126/science.add8424](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.add8424). **[D]** Removing reshares cut political news exposure and news knowledge; **no significant effect on polarization or attitudes**.
- **Nyhan et al., *Nature* 2023, 620:137–144**, DOI [10.1038/s41586-023-06297-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06297-w). **[D]** n = 23,377; cutting like-minded content ~⅓ **no measurable effect on 8 preregistered attitudinal measures**. Correction DOI [10.1038/s41586-023-06795-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06795-x).
- **Allcott et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* 2026, 10(5):884–895**, DOI [10.1038/s41562-025-02328-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02328-w). **[D]** **>60,000** participants; removing political ads "did not have a detectable effect on political knowledge, polarization, turnout or political participation." Summary DOI [10.1038/s41562-025-02329-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02329-9).

**Linegar, Sinclair, van der Linden & Alvarez, *Royal Society Open Science* 2026, 13(8)**, DOI [10.1098/rsos.252226](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.252226). **[D]** Preregistered two-wave US experiment, n = **4,293**: LLM-generated prebunking **reduced belief in election rumours** (persisting, attenuated, at 1 week), modestly offset confidence declines, **no partisan backlash**. Foreign-propaganda label null: Betzer et al., *HKS Misinformation Review* 2025, DOI [10.37016/mr-2020-173](https://doi.org/10.37016/mr-2020-173).

## 6. Exposure-measurement limits: reach ≠ effect

**[D]** Concentration findings (Eady: 1% → 70%; Guess/Nyhan/Reifler: ~6% of diet; Bail: 0.1% of engagement) show impression counts overstate the exposed population and its dose. **[D]** Vavreck, *QJPS* 2008 2(4):325–343, DOI [10.1561/100.00006005](https://doi.org/10.1561/100.00006005) — self-reported exposure inflates effects. **[D]** Mosleh & Rand, *Nature Communications* 2022, DOI [10.1038/s41467-022-34769-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-34769-6). **[I]** Attention-weighting should shrink estimates further, but I found **no 2023–2026 study quantifying impression-vs-attention scaling for foreign-influence content.**

## 7. Election-level Russian interference: reanalyses/critiques

**[D]** The peer-reviewed reanalyses most cited as rebutting strong 2016 vote-switching claims are **Eady et al. 2023** and **Bail et al. 2020**; both limit scope to their samples and disclaim any 2016 aggregate estimate. **[C]** News framing, from Google News RSS metadata only (9–20 Jan 2023): *Washington Post* "Russian trolls on Twitter had little influence on 2016 voters"; *Vox* "A new study says Russian trolls didn't sway the 2016 election"; *CJR* "A new study reignites the debate over Twitter, bots, and 2016"; *The Intercept*; NYU press release. The RSS redirect URLs do not resolve, so **canonical URLs and article bodies are unverified**. **No 2023–2026 peer-reviewed study was found credibly attributing a measurable 2016 vote-choice or turnout shift to IRA activity.**

## 8. Bottom line

**[D]** Across 40–49 contact experiments, 59 ad experiments, four >20k-user platform field experiments and four meta-analyses (k up to 245), average effects on vote choice, turnout, polarization and beliefs are ≈0 or very small; where non-null they decay in days (half-life ~4 days, ≤1 week). **[D]** The largest pooled positive effects are on *belief accuracy after correction/inoculation* (d ≈ 0.11–0.36) and *belief change from real misinformation exposure* (g = 0.24, health) — belief, not behaviour. **[C]** Two titles in the brief (**"Like, Post, and Mistrust?"**, **"Persistence in the Effects of Advertising"**) could **not** be verified; do not cite without a source document.
