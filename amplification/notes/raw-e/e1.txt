Court u-turn: Finland does have jurisdiction in Eagle S cable damage case | Yle News | Yle

The Helsinki Court of Appeal on Thursday overturned a district court ruling about the Eagle S oil tanker, which is suspected of involvement in severing subsea cables in the Gulf of Finland over Christmas 2024.
The appeal court has ordered the case be reopened at district court.
Prosecutors have alleged that crewmembers allowed the tanker's massive anchor to drag across the sea floor and damage five undersea cables, including a key electricity link between Finland and Estonia, on Christmas Day in 2024.
A criminal trial began at the district court last August, and hinged on whether the crew should have noticed that the ship's anchor had been dragged behind the vessel for 90 kilometres.
In early October 2025, the district court ruled that Finland did not have jurisdiction and dismissed the case.
However, in a reversal on Thursday, the appeal court ruled that Finland does have jurisdiction, as the events directly impacted the country.
The Court of Appeal also determined that the central events outlined in the indictment fall outside the definition of a maritime accident under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. It said this means the Convention does not restrict Finland's ability to pursue criminal charges in the case.
The appeal court's decision is not final and can still be taken to the Supreme Court, if it grants leave to appeal.
Eagle S is an oil tanker flagged in the Cook Islands, while its crew were citizens of Georgia and India. In its decision last October, the district court said the suspected crimes should be handled in courts of the crewmembers' home countries or in the country where the Eagle S is flagged.
The district court's decision was appealed by the prosecutor as well as Finnish electricity grid company Fingrid, Estonian grid company Elering, and the Peninsular Maritime India company, which operated the Eagle S.
