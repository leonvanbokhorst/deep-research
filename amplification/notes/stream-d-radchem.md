# Stream D — Radiological and Chemical Scare Campaigns and Hoaxes, 2002–2026

Research cut-off: 25 September 2026. Analytic notes on amplification dynamics. All figures below were
checked against the cited document unless explicitly flagged in **UNSOURCED/UNCERTAIN**. Evidence
grades: **A** primary/official/court/peer-reviewed · **B** high-quality secondary journalism ·
**C** single-source/weaker secondary · **D** Wikipedia/aggregator · **E** advocacy/vendor/anonymous.

## 1. Litvinenko / polonium-210, London 2006 — the reference case

**Chain (adversarial → mediated → institutional).** A covert state act with no initial public claim:
Litvinenko ingested Po-210 in the Pine Bar of the Millennium Hotel on 1 November 2006 and died at
University College Hospital on 23 November 2006. Ambiguity was clinical and forensic, not rhetorical.
The discovery of Po-210 on the day of death converted a suspected poisoning into an unprecedented
public-health event; the police/HPA response generated the visible "polonium trail", which became the
evidentiary substance of the case. Institutional validation was unusually strong and unusually slow:
the Home Secretary charged Lugovoy and Kovtun in 2007/2011 (both remain in Russia), and the Owen
inquiry, established July 2014, reported on 21 January 2016 that Lugovoy and Kovtun killed
Litvinenko and that the FSB operation was "probably approved" by FSB head Patrushev and by President
Putin. The ECHR's *Carter v. Russia* (no. 20914/07, 21 September 2021) subsequently held Russia
responsible. Attention here was mediated and diplomatic rather than a mass-report cascade, and the
inquiry explicitly noted the FSB motive. Persistence factors: a physical radiological trace, a named
victim, and a legal chain that outlived the event by a decade. Collapse factors: Russian
non-cooperation, no extradition, and — until 2021 — no binding international finding.

**Figures (HPA, primary).** Urine samples from **753 people** processed (about 500 in the first month,
another 250 to end-May 2007, three more to August 2007); **139** above the 30 mBq d⁻¹ Reporting
Level; doses for **36** in the 1–6 mSv band and **17** at ≥6 mSv, highest ≈**100 mSv**. **664 persons
from 52 countries and territories** were identified for overseas follow-up. The police examined
**more than 60 scenes** and made **more than 40 mutual legal assistance requests to more than 15
countries**; **over 200 police officers** were involved. The inquiry sat 34 days, took evidence on 30,
and heard 62 oral witnesses. The widely repeated "40+ contaminated locations" is therefore the
*lower-bound* of the police figure (**60+ scenes**); treat "40 locations" as imprecise.

**Copycat afterlife (organic → mediated).** No verifiable London "polonium" copycat wave exists in
the record; the strongest documented later polonium-adjacent claims are (a) the **February 2026
Punjab** case, where an email threatened to poison Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann "with polonium"
alongside a bomb threat at a Mohali hospital — Indian police traced the mail to Dhaka and the poison
claim was exposed as false; and (b) **Vladimir Kara-Murza**, poisoned in 2015 and 2017, whose FBI
records and Bellingcat/FSB-squad work establish an intentional-poisoning investigation but **not**
polonium. Navalny (August 2020) was an OPCW-confirmed cholinesterase inhibitor, not radiological.
Persistence factor: the polonium brand is reusable as a *threat* trope; collapse factor: no confirmed
copycat *event* in 20 years.

## 2. Padilla and the 2002–2005 US dirty-bomb apparatus

**Chain (political → media → institutional).** Ambiguity was manufactured rather than reduced. Padilla
was arrested at Chicago O'Hare on **8 May 2002** on a material-witness warrant; on **10 June 2002**
Attorney General Ashcroft announced, from Moscow, an al-Qaeda "dirty bomb" plot and a possible
Washington target. Official statements supplied the *name* of a weapon that was never charged. The
grand jury instead returned an **11-count superseding indictment on 22 November 2005** for conspiracy
to murder, kidnap and maim overseas and material support to terrorists — no radiological count. He
was convicted on **16 August 2007** and sentenced to **17 years 4 months on 22 January 2008**; an
appeals court found the sentence too lenient and he was resentenced to **21 years on 9 September
2014**. Validation was inverted: the trial, not the announcement, disconfirmed the amplification. By
June 2004 the Justice Department itself was describing the device as **uranium** wrapped in
explosives — which physicists told CBS "cannot" be a radiological dispersal device. This is the
cleanest documented case of an official claim being downgraded by later official briefing.
Persistence factors: the phrase "dirty bomb" and a named suspect; collapse factors: no charged
radiological offence, expert refutation, and 2014 resentencing coverage that foregrounded the
absence. The parallel **2002–2003 detection build-out** (portal monitors, Securing the Cities,
TOPOFF 2, 12–16 May 2003, Chicago and Seattle) institutionalised vigilance that outlived the plot
claim; I could not verify hard participant/cost figures from a primary source (GAO and DoD are
IP-blocked — see UNSOURCED).

## 3. Hoax radiological incidents with evacuations

Documented, dated, resolvable exemplars of *deliberate* radiological hoaxing are sparser than the
literature implies. Verified:

- **14–15 June 2017, Port of Charleston / Wando terminal, South Carolina.** A caller claimed a
  container aboard the *Maersk Memphis* was a "dirty bomb"; the terminal was evacuated, the FBI and
  Coast Guard searched, nothing was found, and a person was detained. The hoax was traced to a
  conspiracy-theory caller. (Mediated → political.)
- **February–March 2010, Middleboro, Massachusetts.** "Radioactive" canisters reported to authorities
  were declared a hoax by police (local press only — grade C).
- **February 2026, Mohali, Punjab.** Bomb-threat email to Fortis Hospital plus a false polonium
  poisoning claim about the Chief Minister; hospital on high alert, claim exposed.
- **11 June 2026, the Pentagon.** An anthrax/hazmat *sensor* triggered a multi-hour lockdown and
  partial evacuation; air testing confirmed no hazard. This is organic false-positive, not a threat,
  but it shows the same evacuation cascade.

Consequence profile is consistent: short evacuation, multi-agency search, no injury, rare
prosecution, minimal aggregate reporting. **No government or peer-reviewed aggregate count of
radiological hoaxes was found**; the closest quantitative series is the IAEA Incident and Trafficking
Database (ITDB), which counts unauthorised possession/trafficking, not hoaxes. Treat any "wave"
claim as unsupported.

## 4. The 2022 Russian "Ukrainian dirty bomb" narrative

**Chain (adversarial → political → mediated → institutional counter).** Trigger: Russian battlefield
setbacks in Kharkiv and Kherson in September–October 2022. On **23 October 2022** Russian state media
asserted Ukraine was near completing a dirty bomb; Defence Minister **Shoigu** called US, UK, French
and Turkish counterparts the same day. **24 October:** Gerasimov called US and UK counterparts;
Russia's UN ambassador wrote to the UN Secretary-General calling Ukrainian use "nuclear terrorism";
the head of Russia's NBC defence forces briefed that forces were preparing for a radioactive
environment; Lavrov and Peskov insisted the threat was real. **26 October:** Putin repeated the claim
at a CIS security meeting while overseeing the Grom nuclear exercise; Shoigu called Indian and
Chinese counterparts. **28 October:** Polyansky issued the last explicit warning, after which the
narrative stopped abruptly. Western response was collective and rapid: the P3 foreign ministers
rejected the claims as a pretext (24 October); NATO's Stoltenberg called them "absurd"; the US State
Department called them a pretext for escalation. The institutional counter was decisive: on the
request of Ukraine's government, IAEA inspectors visited the **Institute for Nuclear Research (Kyiv),
Eastern Mining and Processing Plant (Zhovti Kody) and P.A. Pivdennyi Machine-Building Plant (Dnipro)**
and on **3 November 2022** reported "no indications of undeclared nuclear activities and materials at
the locations", with unfettered access. CSIS (2024) records at least five non-exclusive plausible
motives and notes US officials admitted uncertainty about the purpose. Persistence factors:
integration with real nuclear rhetoric and the Grom exercise. Collapse factors: Kherson withdrawal
completed, IAEA access, and the narrative's unexplained stop.

## 5. Zaporizhzhia NPP narratives, 2022–2026

**Chain (adversarial ↔ adversarial, mediated, institutional).** The IAEA Support and Assistance
Mission arrived at ZNPP on **1 September 2022** and has maintained a continuous presence since — the
single strongest institutional anchor. All six units are in cold shutdown (IAEA welcomed this on
13 April 2024). The dominant recurring incident class is **loss of off-site power** (diesel-generator
operation), driven by damage to the 330 kV line; UN News reported renewed shelling risk as early as
28 August 2022 and a further power loss on 1 October 2025. Competing claims are structural and
mutual: Russia attributes strikes to Ukraine (e.g. a turbine-building drone strike asserted 31 May
2026), Ukraine denies and attributes endangerment to Russian occupation and militarisation. Both
sides' claims are reported by state-aligned media and picked up by wire services — the definitional
case of *competing state claims sustaining ambiguity*. Institutional responses that worked:
IAEA-brokered local ceasefires for line repairs, with off-site power restored in early September 2026
(ANS, 9 September 2026). Figures I could verify: six units cold shutdown; a 330 kV line damaged and
repaired under an IAEA-brokered ceasefire; existence of an IAEA mission since 1 September 2022. A
headline figure of a **"24th off-site power loss"** appears in an Anadolu Agency headline (4 August
2026) but I could not verify the count from the underlying IAEA update — flag as unverified.

## 6. Syria: Ghouta 2013, the JIM, and the Douma attribution fight

**Chain (adversarial → political → mediated → institutional validation).** Ghouta, **21 August 2013**:
the UN Mission (Sellström) report **S/2013/553** (13 September 2013) found Sarin in surface-to-surface
rockets at Ein Tarma, Moadamiyah and Zamalka — "clear and convincing evidence" of use, but its mandate
excluded attribution. That mandate gap is the structural source of the sustained ambiguity. The US
Government Assessment of **30 August 2013** asserted government responsibility and a death toll above
**1,400**; Russia advanced an alternative assessment attributing the attack to the opposition. Competing
national assessments, not evidence, drove the 2013 political crisis. Institutional attribution came
later and in stages: OPCW Fact-Finding Mission findings on chlorine (2014–2015); the OPCW-UN Joint
Investigative Mechanism (established by UNSC resolution 2235 (2015)) attributed **Khan Sheikhoun
(4 April 2017) to the Syrian government** in S/2017/904, and **Ltamenah (March 2017)** in S/2018/202.
Russia vetoed JIM renewal in November 2017; its final report closed the mechanism. The attribution
function then moved to the OPCW's Investigation and Identification Team (IIT), whose first report
attributed Douma to the Syrian Arab Air Force; a fifth IIT report was released in January 2026.
Douma 2018 added a second-order information operation — leaks, a whistleblower dispute and a
"staged attack" narrative — which corroded the OPCW's own credibility even as IIT attributions
accumulated. The OPCW Conference suspended Syria's rights in **2021**. After Assad's fall
(December 2024), the OPCW Director-General visited Damascus in **February 2025**, the Secretariat
deployed repeatedly, and on **9 July 2026** the Executive Council reinstated Syria's rights and
privileges (co-sponsored by 67 States Parties, adopted by consensus), with agreed destruction plans
for Al Qutayfah and Homs. Ambiguity collapsed only when the state that sustained it fell.

## 7. 2023–2026 state information operations around nuclear/chemical incidents

The pattern since 2023 is *pre-emptive attribution of an attack not yet occurred*. Verified currents:
(1) Russian messaging that Ukraine or its allies will stage a radiological/nuclear provocation,
amplified through RT and Lavrov statements (DisinfoWatch, September 2026); (2) 2026 warnings by
Lithuania (8 July 2026) and by Poland and the Baltics (Reuters, 10 August 2026) that Russia may stage
a drone "false flag" against NATO infrastructure using captured Ukrainian drones; (3) 2026 reporting
that Moscow claimed Ukraine planned to start a Russia–UK war; (4) continued nuclear-rhetoric
signalling catalogued by ISW. The ZNPP drone-strike exchanges of May–June 2026 are the live
radiological version of this pattern. Structural feature: the claim is calibrated to be
unfalsifiable in the short window, so the burden falls on third-party inspection (IAEA access) — the
same mechanism that defeated the October 2022 dirty-bomb narrative.

## UNSOURCED / UNCERTAIN

- **"40+ contaminated locations"** — the popular figure. The Owen report's verified text says **more than 60 scenes** and the HPA paper does not give a single location count. Use 60+, not 40.
- **Post-2006 "polonium copycat wave"** — **not established.** No aggregate study or verified cluster found. Individual claims (Punjab 2026; various "polonium" letters) are single-source/unverified.
- **Kara-Murza and polonium** — **contested.** Poisoning is well evidenced (FBI records, Bellingcat, UK sanctions); the **agent is not publicly confirmed as polonium**. Do not merge with Litvinenko.
- **Padilla's 10 June 2002 Ashcroft text** — the direct DOJ release could not be retrieved (archive numbering 404s); the June 2002 announcement is corroborated by CBS/AP (2004) and PBS, not by a primary transcript I fetched.
- **Padilla 2007 conviction / 2008 and 2014 sentence dates** — corroborated through aggregated headlines (NYT 17 Aug 2007; CBS 22 Jan 2008; NPR 9 Sept 2014); the DOJ 2007/2008 releases and the 11th Circuit opinions were not retrieved.
- **TOPOFF 2 participation and cost figures** (commonly "8,000+ participants") — **unverified**: gao.gov, defense.gov and hsdl.org were IP-blocked or 404. Do not cite numbers.
- **Radiation portal monitor / Securing the Cities deployment counts** — **unverified**; GAO reports gao-04-652, gao-06-665 and gao-22-105094 all returned 403 (or no Wayback snapshot). GAO is the required source; this is a genuine recall gap.
- **Charleston 2017** — verified as an event (NYT, WCIV, gCaptain, WorkBoat, Post and Courier headlines); the specific VOA and WCIV article URLs no longer resolve. No prosecution outcome verified.
- **ZNPP "24th off-site power loss"** — **unverified.** A 2026 Anadolu headline asserts it; cross-checking 2026 items also give "15th this year", and the IAEA update page is Cloudflare-blocked. The existence, not the count, of repeated off-site power losses is solid.
