# Case B — Mpox clade Ib PHEIC (2024–2026)

## Causal chain

**Ambiguity (2023–mid 2024).** A novel clade Ib MPXV lineage emerged ~September 2023 in Kamituga, South Kivu (DRC), spreading person-to-person, largely sexually. Conflict, displacement and thin laboratory coverage (nine DRC testing sites) left true size and severity unknown. WHO's first IHR Emergency Committee (EC) report (19 Aug 2024) noted "insufficient information" on clade Ib severity and warned against over-interpreting crude case-fatality rates (CFRs).

**Initial reports → amplification (Aug 2024).** DRC reported >15,600 cases and 537 deaths in 2024; over 100 laboratory-confirmed clade Ib cases appeared in four previously mpox-free neighbours (Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda). Africa CDC declared a **Public Health Emergency of Continental Security (PHECS) on 13 August 2024** — its first, using its 2022-revised statutory mandate. WHO followed on **14 August 2024** with a PHEIC; Committee Chair Prof. Dimie Ogoina framed it as "an emergency, not only for Africa, but for the entire globe."

**Attention.** Media framing outran official assessment: ECDC (16 Aug 2024) rated EU/EEA general-population risk "low," imported cases merely "highly likely." Sweden reported the first clade Ib case outside Africa on 15 Aug 2024; Thailand (22 Aug), Germany, India and the UK followed, with one UK household transmission. No European or US border, entry/exit screening or travel restrictions were imposed (per WHO temporary recommendation 16).

**Institutional response.** A joint Africa CDC–WHO continental plan (6 Sept 2024) was budgeted at **close to US$600 million** (Sept 2024–Feb 2025); Africa CDC's own **US$245 million** ask was ~10% funded in late Aug 2024. WHO prequalified MVA-BN on 13 Sept 2024 and EUL-listed LC16m8 on 19 Nov 2024. First doses reached DRC on 5 Sept 2024; Gavi's "largest shipment yet" — **200,000 doses** — arrived in May 2025.

**Validation/refutation.** Clade Ib's CFR settled near 0.2–0.5% — close to clade IIb, far below clade Ia (2.5–3%; historically ~10%). Declines in DRC, Burundi and Uganda followed. WHO **terminated the PHEIC on 5 September 2025**, extending standing recommendations to 20 Aug 2026 and then **August 2027**; Africa CDC **lifted its PHECS only on 22 January 2026**. The feared Western wave never materialised, but clade Ib resurged in Madagascar (Dec 2025) and Angola (Apr 2026).

## Persistence vs collapse factors

**Durable:** standing recommendations to Aug 2027; Africa CDC's PHECS precedent and "4-Ones" model; >US$1 billion mobilised and >5 million doses deployed across 16 countries; DRC testing sites 9→28; the global ICG mpox vaccine stockpile launched 27 Aug 2026; a transition roadmap integrating mpox into HIV/STI services.

**Fragile:** WHO's Sept 2024 US$87.4m appeal raised only US$65.5m by Feb 2025; the April 2025 plan's US$145m requirement drew no new commitments by June 2025; of 2.9m doses distributed by June 2025, ~724,000 were administered. The US funded one-third of the response; its 2025 freeze stranded US$7.5m and cut DRC testing, and it completed WHO withdrawal on 22 Jan 2026. Falling cases then ended the emergency — the 2026 Madagascar/Angola surges show how fast vigilance decays.

## COUNTEREXAMPLES

- **No Western disruption:** despite "new pandemic" framing, EU/EEA general-population risk stayed low; clade Ib produced only sporadic imported cases and small clusters. Ambiguity drove institutional disruption in global-health and African bodies, not Western social disruption.
- **Threat overstated:** clade Ib CFR (~0.2%) proved comparable to clade IIb and ~15× lower than clade Ia; "more deadly strain" framing was not borne out.
- **Burundi:** controlled its outbreak largely with non-pharmaceutical interventions — sensitive surveillance, contact tracing, decentralised district action — despite minimal resources.
