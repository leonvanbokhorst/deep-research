## A4. Documented corroboration: pseudo-news used to make a contested claim look independently confirmed

### A4.1 The strongest 2026 evidence — a primary threat-intelligence document

Anthropic's **"Detecting and countering misuse of AI: September 2026"** (published 10 September 2026; covers activity disrupted **December 2025 – August 2026** across seven harm areas, including a dedicated **influence operations** section covering **nine cases** originating in Russia, Iran, Turkey, the Gulf, South Asia, Africa and Europe, targeting audiences on six continents) describes exactly the synthetic-corroboration mechanism:

|---|---|---|
| **Laundering of attribution, sourcing and certainty** | "Actors used Claude to engineer content so that state or commissioned narratives appeared to come from independent voices… Actors prompted Claude to intentionally **strip state attribution from republished material, passing claims through chains of outlets so they read as independently confirmed.**" | A |
| **Caveat stripping** | In one case tied to a Russian state media operation: "an actor produced claims the model flagged as unverified, then instructed it to **drop those caveats and present everything as confirmed**, so the material would read as established fact." | A |
| **Think-tank laundering** | During a 2026 US–Israel–Iran war campaign, an Iranian network (Bina Cultural Observatory) **"attributed false claims to Western research institutions (including CSIS, Brookings, and RAND)"** to make state-backed messaging appear credible. | A |
| **Doctrine reused almost verbatim across sessions** | "Markdown files containing doctrine were reused almost verbatim across hundreds of sessions… Actors kept lists of banned words… maintained shared files of approved sources and evasion rules." | A |

**Analytic point for the brief:** this is the clearest published 2026 evidence that the *bottleneck is distribution and credibility, not generation*. The corroboration technique (washing a claim through a chain of outlets) is documented; the audience payoff is documented as weak.

# DERIVED COST ESTIMATES — **MY OWN ARITHMETIC (GRADE D)**

> Everything in this section is **grade D**. It is arithmetic performed by me on **grade-A source prices** from Part B. It is an attacker-cost *envelope*, not a verified figure, and it excludes staff, legal, FOIA fees, domain ageing, SEO, paid amplification and distribution costs.

## D1. Assumptions for a "1,000-word article"

I state these explicitly so the estimate can be re-run:

- **Output**: 1,000 words ≈ **1,300 output tokens** (English, ~1.3 tokens/word for the GPT/Claude class of tokenizer).
- **Input**: ~**2,000 tokens** for prompt + source material on a first pass; I allow **4,000 input tokens** for a generate-then-revise two-pass workflow.
- **Total per article**: ~4,000 input + ~1,300 output = ~**5,300 tokens**.
- **Excluded**: image generation, embeddings, search grounding ($14/1,000 queries), human editing, and retries. Each of these materially raises real cost.

## D5. The analytically important comparison (D, but the inputs are A)

| Quantity | Figure | Basis |
|---|---|---|

| **Reported PAC/nonprofit spend on just the 2021–22 cycle** | **$14,000,000** | A (CJR/Tow) |

**Interpretation for the brief.** The ratio is on the order of **1,000:1**. Whatever $10–14M buys in the Metric Media ecosystem, it is **not** text generation. It buys **people, legal capacity, FOIA operations, print and mail distribution, political access, domain ageing and the reputational surface that makes a synthetic network look like a real one.** The technology cost of synthetic corroboration has collapsed toward zero; the **credibility and distribution cost has not.** This is the single most useful defensive conclusion available from this strand: counter-measures aimed at the *cost of generation* (model safeguards, watermarking, API monitoring) are aimed at the cheapest part of the problem, whereas counter-measures aimed at **distribution, funding transparency and provenance of the publishing surface** address the expensive part.

It also aligns with Anthropic's own 2026 finding that most AI influence content "drew little or no authentic engagement," with the widest authentic reach achieved where **traditional state media** provided distribution.
