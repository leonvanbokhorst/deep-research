# Stream C — AI/LLM Political Persuasion & Inoculation Durability

Cut-off 25 Sep 2026. Defensive/analytic only. Grade: A = primary/official; B = peer-reviewed/high-quality; C = reputable news; D = vendor/think-tank; E = unverified. Tags: **DEM** (measured/replicated), **CLM** (asserted, not measured), **INF** (inference). Effect sizes as reported.

## 1. LLM persuasion — core experiments

- **Hackenburg & Margetts (2024), "Evaluating the persuasive influence of political microtargeting with large language models," PNAS 121(24):e2403116121.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2403116121 (PMC11181035). Preregistered RCT, **N=8,587**, GPT-4, 4 issues. **DEM:** broadly persuasive, up to **+12 pp**; microtargeted **4.83 pp vs non-targeted 6.20 pp, P=0.226** — microtargeting did **not** beat generic messages. Grade A, conf high.
- **Hackenburg, Tappin, Röttger, Hale, Bright, Margetts (2025), "Scaling language model size yields diminishing returns for single-message political persuasion," PNAS.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413443122. 720 messages, 24 models, **N=25,982**. **DEM:** sharply diminishing returns; size–persuasion link shrinks to ~0 and turns non-significant once task completion (coherence/on-topic) is controlled. Grade A, conf high.
- **Argyle, Busby, Gubler, Lyman, Olcott, Pond, Wingate (2025), "Testing theories of political persuasion using AI," PNAS.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2412815122. Two preregistered experiments. **DEM:** all four AI strategies shifted attitudes vs control and vote support, **but customization/microtargeting and interactive elaboration added little over one generic message**; attitude moderation did not reliably extend to democratic tolerance. Grade A, conf high.
- **Hackenburg, Tappin, Hewitt, Saunders, Black, Lin, Fist, Margetts, Rand, Summerfield (2025), "The levers of political persuasion with conversational artificial intelligence," Science 390(6777):eaea3884, 4 Dec 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea3884. **N=76,977; 19 LLMs; 707 issues; 466,769 claims fact-checked.** **DEM:** post-training raised persuasiveness up to **51%**, prompting up to **27%**; personalization and scale had smaller effects; **levers that raised persuasion systematically lowered factual accuracy** — effects run on information, not style, and trade off against truth. Commentary: Argyle, Science 390(6777):983–984, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec9293. Grade A, conf high.
- **Lin, Czarnek, Lewis, White, Berinsky, Costello, Pennycook, Rand (2025), "Persuading voters using human–artificial intelligence dialogues," Nature, 3 Dec 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09771-9. Preregistered; 2024 US, 2025 Canadian/Polish elections + MA psychedelics ballot. **DEM:** significant candidate-preference effects, **larger than typical traditional video ads** (numbers not in abstract); models used facts/evidence, not sophisticated psychological tactics; right-advocating models made more inaccurate claims. Grade A, conf high.

## 2. Salvi et al. — publication, effect size, 2026 correction

- Preprint **arXiv:2403.14380**, 21 Mar 2024 (N=820; "81.7% higher odds"). *The brief's ID 2410.12743 is a physics paper; correct ID is 2403.14380.*
- Published as **"On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4," Nature Human Behaviour 9(8):1645–1653, online 19 May 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6 (PMC12367540). 2×2×3 design, **N=900**. **DEM:** where not equally persuasive, **GPT-4 + personalization won 64.4% of the time; +81.2% relative odds** of higher post-debate agreement (95% CI **[+26.0%, +160.7%]**, P<0.01). Without personalization, GPT-4's edge was **non-significant** (arXiv p=0.31). Grade A, conf high.
- **Author Correction, Nat Hum Behav, 3 Sep 2026.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02588-0. **DEM:** a calculation error; personalized-vs-**non**-personalized GPT-4 is now **P=0.0678 (~0.07), +48.7%, 95% CI [−2.9%, +127.6%]** — non-significant. Paper now states it **"cannot conclusively establish … that personalization confers an additional advantage over non-personalized GPT-4."** This undercuts the headline microtargeting claim. Conf high (primary correction text retrieved).
- **"AI persuasion is not special": Hölbling, Maier, Feuerriegel (2025), "A meta-analysis of the persuasive power of large language models," Scientific Reports 15:43818, 12 Dec 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-30783-y. 7 studies, **17,422 participants, 12 effect sizes**: **no significant overall LLM-vs-human difference** (Hedges' g n.s.); large heterogeneity; Egger's test flagged possible small-study effects but trim-and-fill imputed none. Grade B, conf medium-high (k=7 limits power). **DEM** null on average; moderators exploratory.

## 3. AI-generated propaganda (Goldstein et al.)

- **Goldstein, Chao, Grossman, Stamos, Tomz (2024), "How persuasive is AI-generated propaganda?" PNAS Nexus 3(2):pgae034.** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae034 (PMC10878360). **N=8,221**; 6 foreign-propaganda topics; GPT-3 davinci. **DEM:** control agreement **24.4%**; original propaganda **47.4% (+23 pp)**; GPT-3 **43.5% (+19.1 pp)**, 3.9 pp below original; curated (2/18 dropped) **45.6%**, n.s.; edited prompt **46.4%** vs 47.4%, n.s.; edited + best-of-3 **52.7%**, above original. Only **38.7%** found the original propaganda trustworthy; GPT-3 matched/exceeded it on credibility and style. Grade A/B, conf high. Caveats: one legacy model, 6 issues, short exposure, thesis-agreement DV, no behaviour or durability measured.

## 4. Inoculation / prebunking — effect sizes, decay, weak replications

- **Roozenbeek, van der Linden, Goldberg, Rathje, Lewandowsky (2022), Sci Adv 8(34):eabo6254.** https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abo6254. 6 RCTs (**n=6,464**) + YouTube field study (**n=22,632**); videos improved manipulation-technique recognition and sharing discernment, robust across the political spectrum. Grade A, conf high (no single pooled d in abstract).
- **Maertens, Roozenbeek, Basol, van der Linden (2021), J Exp Psychol Appl.** https://doi.org/10.1037/xap0000315. N=151/194/170. **DEM:** effect stable **≥3 months with regular re-testing**; **significant decay over 2 months without re-testing, long-term effect no longer significant**. Grade B, conf high.
- **Maertens, Roozenbeek, Simons, Lewandowsky, Maturo, Goldberg, Xu, van der Linden (2025), "Psychological booster shots targeting memory increase long-term resistance against misinformation," Nature Communications.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57205-x. **N=11,759**, five preregistered longitudinal experiments. **DEM:** text/video inoculation effective ~**1 month**; **game-based decayed faster**; memory boosters restored waning effects. Grade A, conf high.
- **Capewell, Maertens, van der Linden, Remshard, Compton, Lewandowsky, Roozenbeek (2023), "Misinformation interventions decay rapidly without an immediate post-test," PsyArXiv.** https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/93ujx. N=503+673. **DEM:** at 48 h, with immediate post-test **d=.416 (p=.007)**; **without it d=.010 (p=.854)** — durable effect ≈ zero. Grade B (preprint), conf medium.
- **Roozenbeek, Freeman, van der Linden (2021), "How Accurate Are Accuracy-Nudge Interventions? A Preregistered Direct Replication," Psychological Science.** https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211024535. **Stage 1 failed (p=.67)**; pooled N=1,583 gave a small interaction (treatment **d=0.14** vs control **d=0.10**). Grade A, conf high. *The brief's "Durability of the accuracy nudge (Nature Communications 2024)"* **NOT VERIFIED** — no such title in Crossref/Europe PMC/Google News (conf low).
- **Cheetham, Yee, van der Linden (2026), "Psychological inoculation against deepfakes," PsyArXiv.** https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/zqf38_v1. N=297. **DEM (preprint):** inoculation video improved deepfake detection **d=0.625, 95% CI [0.39, 0.86]** without shifting response bias — this **contradicts** the "inoculation fails on deepfakes" framing (prior *detection-training* mostly failed; inoculation worked here). Not peer-reviewed; conf medium.
- Brief's **"Costello, Pennycook, Rand — Durability of the effects of gamified psychological inoculation (Science Advances 2024)"** — **NOT VERIFIED** (no Crossref/Europe PMC/PubMed/Google News record); likely a conflation. Closest real work: **Costello, Pennycook, Rand (2024), "Durably reducing conspiracy beliefs through dialogues with AI," Science 385**, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814 — **N=2,190**, conspiracy belief **−~20%**, **still present at 2 months**, generalizing to unrelated conspiracies. Grade A, conf high.

## 5. Detectability / "AI penalty" — does AI attribution discount the message?

- **Gallegos, Shani, Shi, Bianchi, Gainsburg, Jurafsky, Willer (2026), "Labeling messages as AI-generated does not reduce their persuasive effects," PNAS Nexus.** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag008. N=1,601. Messages moved policy views **+9.74 pp**; **92.0%** believed the label; labels had **no significant effect** on attitude change, accuracy judgments or sharing intentions across party, education, age, prior AI experience. Grade A, conf high — strong evidence **against** a general AI-persuasion penalty.
- **Boissin, Costello, Spinoza-Martín, Rand, Pennycook (2025), "Dialogues with large language models reduce conspiracy beliefs even when the AI is perceived as human," PNAS Nexus.** https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf325. N=955; **no significant difference** between AI- and human-framed debunking: **"AI persuasion is not reliant on the messenger being an AI model."** Grade A, conf high.
- **Ballot, van de Sande, Schraffenberger, Schmid (2026), "Chatbots reduce health-related conspiracy beliefs not because of but despite being perceived as AI," Scientific Reports.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-66242-5. N=554. AI-labelled **−7.88 pp** confidence (**d=0.63, p<.001**); human-labelled **−13.76 pp** more than control (**d=0.87, p<.001**), mediated by perceived neutrality. AI framing if anything **diluted** the effect. Grade A, conf high.
- **Yin, Jia, Wakslak (2024), "AI can help people feel heard, but an AI label diminishes this impact," PNAS.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2319112121. Grade A — an AI discount, but on *feeling heard*, not persuasion. **INF:** no robust, replicated audience "AI discount" on persuasiveness exists to date.
- Preprint **Schilke & Reimann (2026), "The transparency dilemma: how AI disclosure erodes trust,"** https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/vsw6m_v1 — trust-erosion claim, unrefereed (Grade B, conf low-medium). News context (Grade C): Time, "Why AI Watermarks and Detectors Could Backfire," Aug 2026; The Debrief, "AI Labels Can Backfire…," Mar 2026.

## 6. Adjacent null / framing

- **Velez, Green, Sevi (2025), "Chatbot Voting Advice Applications inform but seldom sway young unaligned voters," PNAS.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2515516122. **DEM:** improved knowledge of party stances, but **weak effects** on vote preference/party evaluation. Grade A. Framing context: Summerfield/Argyle et al., "The impact of advanced AI systems on democracy," Nat Hum Behav 2025, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02309-z (Grade B).

## 7. Bottom line

**DEM:** LLMs persuade at scale, but persuasion runs on **information/facts plus post-training/prompting**, not microtargeting or scale (Hackenburg 2024/2025; Argyle 2025). Salvi's personalization effect was **corrected to non-significance in Sep 2026**, so the strongest "AI microtargeting is uniquely dangerous" claim is unsupported. On average LLMs are **not** more persuasive than humans (Hölbling meta-analysis). AI-labelling does **not** blunt persuasion (Gallegos 2026), weakening detection-based countermeasures. Inoculation works immediately but **decays within ~1 month (faster for games) without boosters or immediate post-tests** (Maertens 2021/2025; Capewell 2023). **CLM/unverified:** "inoculation fails on deepfakes"; the Costello/Pennycook/Rand gamified-inoculation citation. **INF:** durable, AI-discount-based countermeasures are not evidenced; evidence favours accuracy/information-focused and memory-booster designs, since persuasion gains carry **accuracy costs** (Science 2025).
