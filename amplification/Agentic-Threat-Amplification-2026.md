# Agentic Threat Amplification

### Can AI agents deliberately amplify ambiguous physical threats so that target societies generate the disruption themselves?

**A hypothesis-testing study · Open-source research cut-off: 25 September 2026**

*Extension to `AI-Autonomy-Threat-Assessment-2026.md`*

---

## 0. Scope, method and limits

### 0.1 The question

This study tests a specific hypothesis:

> **H1.** AI agents can materially increase the *effectiveness*, *persistence* or *adaptability* of scare tactics built around **ambiguous real-world events** — drone sightings, sabotage reports, infrastructure incidents, unexplained disruptions — such that the attacker needs to supply only a small number of genuine events, fabricated claims or ambiguous signals, while the target society produces the majority of the subsequent activity.

The hypothesised causal chain is:

> **physical ambiguity → initial reports → automated amplification/adaptation → media and social attention → citizen vigilance → additional genuine or mistaken reports → institutional response → further perceived validation**

This is treated as a **hypothesis to be falsified**, not a phenomenon to be described. The study states at the outset that the most likely finding — and the one the evidence supports — is that **most of this mechanism is neither new nor attributable to AI**, and that the genuinely new element is narrower and more specific than the popular framing suggests.

### 0.2 Why the framing matters

Two failure modes are equally available here, and both are common in the existing literature:

- **Threat inflation** — treating the demonstrated capability to generate text at scale as proof that perception can be engineered at will.
- **Threat dismissal** — observing that most influence operations have negligible measured effects and concluding that nothing has changed.

This study tries to hold both. The structure is designed so that each claim must survive the counter-case in §8 and the concept-fit audit in §3, which asks whether the mechanism is genuinely new or merely renamed.

### 0.3 Evidence levels

Every significant claim is assigned one of four levels, kept strictly separate:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **L1 — Technical possibility** | Demonstrated in a laboratory, benchmark, or controlled trial |
| **L2 — Demonstrated capability** | Shown to work outside a lab, in realistic conditions, at least once |
| **L3 — Credible evidence of deployment** | Documented use by a real actor against a real target, with attributable evidence |
| **L4 — Documented real-world impact** | Measured effect on beliefs, behaviour, institutions or outcomes |

Most of the public discussion conflates L1 with L4. **The gap between "an agent can do this" and "an agent doing this changed what people believed or what institutions did" is the central analytical problem of this study**, and it is where the honest answer is thinnest.

### 0.4 Source grading

**A** primary/official · **B** independent research with transparent method · **C** reputable journalism · **D** single-source, trade press · **E** vendor/advocacy/anonymous. Confidence: High / Medium / Low.

### 0.5 A deliberate omission

This is defensive and analytical research. It describes mechanisms, evidence, indicators and defences. **It contains no operational guidance for conducting an amplification campaign** — no targeting method, no persona-management technique, no evasion approach, no tooling recommendation. Where a capability is described, it is described at the level of what is possible and how it would be detected, not how it would be done.

### 0.6 Method and its limits

Eight parallel research strands plus the author's own primary-source work. The principal web-search facility failed throughout the research window; discovery relied on news-RSS search, DuckDuckGo via a text-extraction proxy, publisher site search, and direct retrieval of primary documents. This biases coverage toward English-language and easily indexed sources. The full statement is at Appendix C.

## 1. Bottom line

**What is already established (independent of AI).** The core mechanism — ambiguity producing disruption far beyond the physical event — is **well documented and entirely pre-agentic**. The New Jersey drone episode of late 2024 is the cleanest case: a genuine but mundane mix of lawful drones, aircraft and stars generated **over 5,000 reported sightings**, a federal interagency response, and a legislative push, and the FBI and four agencies concluded there was **nothing anomalous and no national security or public safety risk**. Similar dynamics appear at Gatwick (2018), Copenhagen (2025) and in the Havana syndrome episode, and in several of those cases the physical event itself was never evidenced at all. **Societies do generate much of their own disruption from ambiguity. That part of the hypothesis is not a hypothesis; it is a finding.**

**What agentic AI changes.** The defensible claim is narrower than "AI can engineer a panic" and more consequential than "nothing has changed." Machine agents appear to change the **cost, persistence and adaptivity** of influence activity — specifically by removing the labour constraint that historically limited an operation's breadth, by making coordination across platforms and languages cheap, and by making synthetic accounts that present as independent human observation more plausible and more numerous. In 2026 the first credible evidence of *agentic* deployment emerged: reporting on Iranian and Chinese campaigns using **hundreds of AI agents** to create accounts, generate content and coordinate across platforms, with an Iranian network accumulating nearly 80,000 followers in the first half of 2026. That is L3 evidence for **agentic influence operations in general**. It is **not** L3 evidence for agentic amplification of ambiguous physical threats specifically — that narrower case remains **unevidenced**.

Two further changes deserve equal billing, and both are easy to miss:

- **The liar's dividend.** Synthetic media does not only make false evidence cheap; it makes *true* evidence deniable. Between 45% and 54% of people who **accepted** a deepfake warning still judged guilt from the video, and specific warnings led some to dismiss *real* footage as fake. This degrades the evidentiary value of recorded reality itself — which is the channel on which verification-based defences depend.
- **Reach is rented from the platform, not owned by the network.** Forty coordinated accounts generated **over a billion views in a month** — because a recommendation algorithm amplified them. "Low organic reach" is therefore not a stable property of an operation. It is a property of its current relationship with platform distribution.


**What does not follow.** Followers and views are not persuasion. The most-cited metrics in this field measure *reach*, not *belief*. The strongest measured effects of AI persuasion come from controlled settings and often disappear against real audiences with competing information. Influence operations have a long record of failure, detection and backfire. And several of the most-cited "adversarial" incidents — Gatwick, Copenhagen, Madrid, Vilnius, the US north-east wave — collapsed on inspection into misidentification, retraction, or nothing at all.

**The most important structural finding.** In the causal chain, the stages that actually generate disruption are **institutional**, not informational. Airports close, fighters scramble, parliaments legislate, and insurers reprice in response to *perceived* threat, not verified threat. An agent that supplies nothing more than ambiguity is therefore not competing with the state's information apparatus — it is *feeding* it. **The amplifier is not the botnet. It is the institution.** This is where the study's defensive conclusions concentrate, and where the reflexive-effects category (§4.2, category 5) does the real analytical work.

**The verdict.** Partially supported, substantially overstated as commonly framed, and worth monitoring for a specific and narrow reason: the mechanism is **cheap to attempt, hard to attribute, and the target does most of the work**. That is a bad combination for deterrence, even when the expected effect is modest.

**And the hypothesis should be framed as plausible, novel and currently unobserved — not as an extrapolation from demonstrated effects.** Six independent evidence streams converged on that formulation. The single decisive constraint is this: **autonomous agent-to-agent propaganda coordination has been demonstrated only in simulation, never observed in the wild.** That removes the one element that would make the hypothesis genuinely novel — everything else can be assembled from components already documented. The evidence base here is **strong on mechanism and weak on measured effect, and the decisive gap is measurement, not understanding** (§7.5).

## 2. The hypothesis in falsifiable form

Vague versions of H1 cannot be tested. This study therefore disaggregates it into five sub-claims, each of which can fail independently.

| # | Sub-claim | Status after review |
|---|---|---|
| **H1a** | Ambiguous physical events can produce institutional and social disruption disproportionate to the physical event | **Established, pre-agentic.** Strongly supported (§5.1) |
| **H1b** | A small number of genuine or fabricated signals can be amplified by the target society into large-scale activity | **Established for organic dynamics; not established as engineered.** The NJ case shows society self-amplifies; no case shows an adversary engineering that amplification (§5.1, §7) |
| **H1c** | AI agents materially increase the *cost-efficiency* of running such an operation | **Supported for influence operations generally (L2/L3); plausible but unevidenced for physical-threat amplification** (§6, §7) |
| **H1d** | AI agents increase *persistence* — sustaining a scare beyond its natural burnout | **Plausible, weakly evidenced.** Persona persistence is technically feasible; sustained engineered scares are not documented (§6.4) |
| **H1e** | AI agents increase *adaptivity* — monitoring reaction and shifting narrative in real time | **Credibly claimed in commercial systems (L2/L3); no public evidence of use against physical-threat scares** (§6.3) |

**The honest summary of the falsification test:** H1a is confirmed but is not about AI. H1c is partially confirmed but for a different target class (political influence, not physical-threat scares). H1b, H1d and H1e remain **plausible but unevidenced** in the specific application.

### 2.1 What would falsify H1

Stating this in advance disciplines the analysis:

- Documented cases where an ambiguous physical event was **deliberately seeded or amplified** by an AI-enabled actor, with attribution and evidence — would support H1.
- Evidence that society does **not** self-amplify — i.e. ambiguous events that reliably produce no cascade without an external driver. If cascades require a human "availability entrepreneur" and cannot be started or sustained by agents, H1c–H1e fail.
- Evidence that agentic activity is reliably detected and removed before reaching institutional attention — which would cap the effect at the informational layer.
- Evidence that institutions have learned to respond to ambiguity without amplifying, breaking the reflexive loop at its weakest link.
- Evidence that audience-level effects of synthetic accounts are near zero because the accounts reach only the already-persuaded.

## 3. Concept-fit audit: is this new, or renamed?

The hypothesis arrives with a large existing vocabulary. The honest question is not "does this resemble something known?" — almost everything does — but **"what, precisely, is not already covered?"**

### 3.1 Concepts that fit well

**Prior art: the oldest statement of the mechanism.** The idea that the target destroys itself is not modern, and the clearest formulation is classical. **Clausewitz** (*On War*, VI.vii) states that "there is a double solution in the defensive, consequently a double kind of reaction, according as the aggressor is ruined by the sword of the defensive, **or by his own efforts**."

That is the mechanism in one clause, written two centuries before the language of amplification, and it is **stronger than anything in the reflexive-control literature** for the specific claim this study tests — the target's own reaction as the instrument of its harm. Four further anchors make the lineage explicit:

- **Merton (1948), the self-fulfilling prophecy:** "a false definition of the situation evoking a new behavior which makes the originally false conception come true." The reflexive category (§4.2, category 5) stated as sociology.
- **Jenkins (RAND P-5261), on terrorism:** "terrorism is aimed at the people watching… the difference between the actual amount of violence and the greatly amplified effects of that violence." The gap between *event* and *effect* is this study's entire subject.
- **Mueller, on the costs of terrorism:** "the costs of terrorism very often come mostly from the fear and consequent reaction… not from its direct effects." The **reaction is the cost centre** — the claim in §9.1.
- **Rid, on active measures:** the "value-creation of disinformation [is] outsourced to the victim itself." The closest statement in the disinformation literature to this study's hypothesis.

The most candid practitioner statement of the strategy is bin Laden's, as recorded by Mueller: it was "easy for us to provoke and bait… to make the generals race there to cause America to suffer human, economic, and political losses." **An adversary explicitly describing the target's reaction as the objective is the closest historical analogue to the hypothesis — and it required no AI at all.**

**Reflexive control.** The closest named modern concept, and the most important one to understand. Soviet-origin doctrine (Lefebvre's formalisation; Chotikul's early English treatment; Thomas's transmission via the US Foreign Military Studies Office; Komov's operational taxonomy of distraction, overloading, paralysis, exhaustion, deception, splitting, appeasement, intimidation and provocation) describes influencing an adversary's *decision procedure* — "handing over to the other the basis for decision-making" — so that the target's own reasoning becomes the mechanism of their defeat. This maps almost exactly onto the fifth category of the hypothesised mechanism (§4.2), in which authorities make *rational* defensive decisions that produce the attacker's desired effect. **Fit: High.**

Two cautions are essential, and both cut against over-claiming:

1. **Classic reflexive control is a staff-planning concept** aimed at a commander or a small decision elite, and it *requires a model of the specific adversary*. The hypothesis targets populations and mid-level institutions and — crucially — needs **no model at all**, because the exploited reflex ("ambiguous physical threat → act as if real") is universal. That is a real difference, but it also means the hypothesis is **less sophisticated** than reflexive control, not more.
2. **A serious critical literature treats the applied doctrine as loosely specified and partly pseudoscientific**, with Western analysts arguably over-attributing Russian success to it. Any argument that leans on reflexive control as a proven Russian master method is weaker than it looks.

**Strategy of tension.** The Italian *strategia della tensione* — the deliberate creation or exploitation of fear to induce a population to accept security measures, blame a political enemy, or consent to a state of emergency. **Fit: High**, and notably under-used in English-language threat literature. It supplies the missing **motive** dimension: reflexive effects are not a by-product but can be the objective.

**Network amplification attacks.** A computer-security concept in which an attacker turns a system's own resources against it — using the target's bandwidth, reflexes or trust relationships as the weapon. Structurally identical to the hypothesised mechanism. **Fit: High as an analogy**, limited as a source of substantive claim since the domains differ in kind (deterministic systems versus sense-making populations).

**Information operations, PSYOP and MISO — with two doctrine corrections.** US doctrine explicitly includes influencing behaviour, including through *withholding* and *shaping* as well as falsehood. Fit: **High for scope, low for novelty** — nothing in the hypothesis falls outside the definitional scope of information operations.

Two corrections matter to anyone citing this lineage:

- **"Perception management" is not a current US Department of Defense term, despite being widely cited as one.** Its last dictionary appearance was JCS Pub 1-02 (1 December 1989); it was **dropped in the 8 November 2010 reissue** and appears in neither the November 2021, September 2024 nor June 2025 DoD dictionaries. It is also a sender-to-message model aimed at foreign audiences, with **no concept of the audience manufacturing meaning** — which is precisely the mechanism of interest here. **It therefore fits poorly, and this report does not rely on it.** Influential secondary sources (including *The Intercept* in 2023 and Tennant in 2026) treat the term as current; they are wrong.
- **The direction of the recent PSYOP/MISO renaming is routinely reported backwards.** The December 2025 US memo renamed **MISO back to PSYOP** — not MISO to "information operations". Nor was it abrupt: the US Army had already reverted its **unit names** to PSYOP in **October 2017** while retaining MISO as the *function* ("PSYOP forces conduct MISO; PSYOP refers to the name of units, while MISO refers to the function"). The 2025 memo completed an Army-led reversion. Separately, **JP 3-13's successor is JP 3-04, "Information in Joint Operations" (14 September 2022)** — a useful marker of where the doctrine actually went.

**Active measures.** Soviet practice (disinformation, forgeries, front organisations, agent-of-influence operations), documented extensively in the Mitrokhin archive and analysed by Rid. Fit: **High.** Active measures supply the historical proof that states run such operations and the historical record of how often they failed. What is new is not the intent but the labour model.


**Hybrid warfare and the "grey zone".** Fit: **High as context, poor as an analytical tool.** The term has been criticised as over-broad and unfalsifiable; it usefully locates the hypothesis in a strategic setting but adds little explanatory precision. Note that much of the European drone-sighting activity has been *attributed* to hybrid warfare on far weaker evidence than the term's use implies (§5.1).

**Cognitive warfare.** NATO-adjacent framing of competition for influence over cognition itself. Fit: **Moderate.** Useful as an umbrella; often used in ways that make strong claims about neural or psychological manipulation without evidence. Treat with caution.

### 3.2 Concepts that fit partially

**Social amplification of risk framework (SARF).** Kasperson et al. (1988) describes how risk events interact with psychological, social, institutional and cultural processes to produce *amplification* (or attenuation) of perceived risk, with ripple effects across time, space and institutions. The "amplification stations" concept — media, scientists, institutions, social groups — maps directly onto the causal chain. **Fit: High as description — but the framework is routinely over-read as an engineering template, and this study will not do so.** Four escalating caveats:

1. **SARF was built to explain accidents and technology controversies, not adversarial action.** It is descriptive of emergent social processes.
2. **Its critics attacked the engineering reading almost immediately** (Rayner 1988; Rip 1988), and the fifteen-year retrospective assessment (Pidgeon, Kasperson & Slovic, 2003) conceded that the framework is weakly specified.
3. **It is arguably unfalsifiable**, because it supplies no objective signal by which amplification could be measured independently of the outcome it purports to explain (Busby & Onggo, 2013).
4. **Decisively for this study: SARF has no variable for an adversary who *is* an amplification station**, who injects a synthetic signal, or who acts inside the ripple. The framework cannot represent a deliberate actor at all.

**The consequence is a sourcing rule this study follows:** SARF is used here as a **descriptive map of the chain**, never as evidence that deliberate amplification is feasible. Every adversarial claim in this report is sourced from influence-operations research, crisis informatics or political communication — **not** from SARF.

The hypothesis is best posed as: *can a descriptive framework with no attacker in it be converted into an adversarial one?* That is the right question, and the honest answer (§5.2, §9.6) is that the conversion works at the **institutional** layer and fails at the population layer.

**Availability cascades** (Kuran & Sunstein, 1999). A self-reinforcing process in which expressed beliefs about risk spread and become self-validating; the "availability entrepreneur" is the agent who starts it. Fit: **High**, and it supplies the mechanism by which a small input produces a large output. It also supplies the **"probability neglect"** insight — that reactions follow salience, not likelihood, which is precisely why *ambiguity* is the ideal substrate.

**Rumour theory.** Allport & Postman's basic law — rumour strength varies with *importance × ambiguity* — is almost a specification of the hypothesis. Modern work (DiFonzo & Bordia) adds that rumour is a sense-making activity, and that it thrives when official information is absent or distrusted. Fit: **High**, and it sharpens the key variable: **the substrate is not ignorance but ambiguity plus informational vacuum.** This is the most actionable finding in the study, because it means the countermeasure is not "more debunking" but "faster, more credible, more specific official information."

**One popular claim about rumour must be withdrawn.** This report initially repeated the common assertion that rumour is "often self-correcting." **The best evidence contradicts it.** Starbird's analysis of the Boston 2013 event found misinformation-to-correction ratios **as high as 44:1** — corrections do not outrun claims, they trail them badly — and the "self-correcting crowd" is, in that literature's own assessment, **"overly optimistic."** Correction happens when an *authority* resolves the ambiguity (§5.1c), not because the crowd corrects itself. This strengthens rather than weakens the case for the defensive posture in §11.2, and it removes a false comfort from earlier drafts.

**Psychological operations.** Fit: **High.** Historical and doctrinal ancestor; supplies both techniques and their limits.

**Strategic signalling.** Fit: **Partial and important.** Some ambiguous events are *meant* to be ambiguous — overflights "act as a signalling tool", as the European Commission put it. Signalling theory explains why an actor might want ambiguity rather than a claim: it preserves deniability and forces the target to bear the cost of interpretation. This is arguably a better model for the drone-sighting wave than "amplification" is.

**Coordinated inauthentic behaviour / computational propaganda.** Woolley & Howard's framing and platform enforcement terminology. Fit: **High for the enabling layer** — this is the operational practice that agents would automate.

### 3.3 Concepts that fit poorly or add little

**Moral panic.** Cohen's framework describes *societal* reactions to perceived threats — folk devils, signification spirals, institutional over-reaction. Fit: **Partial, and mostly at the endpoint.** Moral panics are typically about *social* threats (youth culture, drugs, media effects) with a moral dimension; drone sightings are not a moral panic unless a folk devil emerges. Useful for explaining institutional over-reaction; not useful for the initiation mechanism. **Do not over-deploy this term.**

**Information laundering.** The process by which false or tainted material is moved through progressively more reputable channels so that provenance is obscured. Fit: **Moderate.** Describes a technique within the mechanism (moving a claim from marginal to mainstream), not the mechanism itself.

**Classic disinformation.** Fit: **Poor as a description of the hypothesised mechanism.** The hypothesis specifically concerns *ambiguous true events* and *synthetic corroboration*, not false claims at scale. Collapsing this into "disinformation" is the most common analytical error in the field (§4.2).

### 3.4 What is genuinely distinct — and what is not

Stripping away the parts that existing concepts already cover, **three elements survive scrutiny — and all three are quantitative or automational changes to known mechanisms, not new mechanisms:**

1. **The labour model.** Reflexive control, active measures, PSYOP and network amplification attacks were all always possible; they were limited by the number of people who could run personas, translate text, maintain accounts and work shifts. Agents remove that constraint. This is a change in **cost and scale** — but for operations, cost and scale *are* strategy.
2. **Continuous, model-free reflexive coupling.** Prior reflexive-control theory assumed a planning cycle and a model of the adversary. An agent system can observe the target's reaction and adjust within the same news cycle — and can do so **without modelling the target at all**, because the exploited reflex is universal. This is a genuine narrow extension: reflexive control automated, decoupled from intelligence requirements, and pointed at institutions rather than commanders. *(Confidence: Medium/Low.)*
3. **Synthetic corroboration at scale.** Presenting one ambiguous event as many apparently *independent* witnesses is what converts an unresolvable incident into an apparently well-attested one — and it attacks the independence assumption on which all verification rests. *(Confidence: Medium on capability, Low on deployed use against physical incidents.)*

**The verdict, stated plainly: "agentic threat amplification" names a *configuration*, not a new causal mechanism** — in the same way that "cyber-enabled information warfare" named a configuration of propaganda, networks and states. Naming a configuration is analytically useful. Claiming a discovery is not. Every component of the hypothesised chain has a name, an origin and a literature; what has changed is one constraint.

**Two further framing points.**

- **The term itself is not established — and that was verified, not assumed.** "Agentic threat amplification" returns **zero hits in both arXiv and Crossref**. The emerging adjacent term is **"agentic influence operations"**, which is what the September 2026 reporting on Iranian and Chinese autonomous campaigns actually describes. This study therefore treats its own title as a *convenient label for a configuration*, not as a recognised phenomenon.
- **The adversarial bridge is a genuine gap in the literature, and this is the study's strongest defensible novelty claim.** No peer-reviewed work on "AI-driven reflexive control" or LLM-based reflexive control was found — confirmed by two independent searches. And targeted searches for an **adversarial or deliberate branch of the social amplification of risk framework** — "adversarial social amplification of risk", "deliberate amplification", "weaponised amplification" — return **nothing**. SARF has no attacker in it, and no one has built one.

  **This is the correct place to locate the contribution.** Not "we found a new mechanism", but "**the mechanism is old, well-described and named — and nobody has connected it to the attacker, or asked what agents change**." That is a smaller claim than the hypothesis implies and a more durable one.
- **The contested good shifts from belief to institutional behaviour.** Classic influence operations seek to change what people think. The mechanism examined here can succeed while changing nothing about what people think, provided it changes what institutions *do* under uncertainty. That is the analytically distinctive claim, and §5.4 shows it operating without any adversary present.

**Where the novelty actually lives — and a necessary refinement to the reflexive-control fit.** Reflexive control has been rated **High** above, but on closer inspection it deserves a split rating, and the distinction is what positions this study correctly.

- **Reflexive control supplies the *physical-feint logic*, not the amplification mechanism.** Its Komov taxonomy — distraction, paralysis, provocation — explicitly operates through **perceived physical threats**, which is why the fit is high for the "make the target react to an ambiguous stimulus" component.
- **But reflexive control is human-decision-centric, deliberate, state-centric, and contains no AI and no emergent or self-amplifying claim.** It assumes a model of the adversary's command and a planning cycle. On the amplification half of the hypothesis it is **only a partial fit**.
- **NATO's canonical cognitive-warfare framing does not close the gap either.** The Alliance's own Innovation Hub text (du Cluzel, 2021) frames Russian cognitive warfare as **essentially reflexive control rebranded**, with cognition and trust as the target and the individual as the weapon. That is a *belief*-centred model, not an *institution*-centred one.

**The under-theorised conjunction is therefore this:** *ambiguous physical incidents* plus a target population *supplying the disruption itself* through *autonomous AI agents*. **Neither reflexive control nor cognitive warfare covers it.** Reflexive control has the feint but not the amplification; cognitive warfare has the cognition target but not the physical-ambiguity substrate or the institutional mechanism.

That is where this study positions its contribution — precisely and narrowly. Not "a new threat class", and not "a new mechanism", but **an unexamined intersection of three things that are each well understood in isolation.**

*(The nearest named antecedent for the AI element specifically is the literature on PLA "algorithmic cognitive warfare" — algorithms, recommender systems and large language models used for individualised influence — which is the closest prior art located for the AI-amplified component.)*

## 4. The mechanism: causal chain, categories and gain

### 4.1 The chain, with the amplifier identified

The hypothesised chain is analytically useful only if each stage is separated, because **different stages have different owners**. An important corrective: the chain as usually drawn implies the adversary drives the process. In practice the adversary may drive only stage 1 or 2, and **the state drives the stages that cost money.**

| Stage | What happens | Who typically owns it | Evidence that it occurs without an adversary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Substrate | An ambiguous physical event, or the *belief* that one occurred | Nobody / accidental | Yes — lawful drones, aircraft, stars (NJ) |
| 1. Initial signal | A report, a video, a genuine incident, a fabricated claim | Citizen, adversary, or nobody | Yes — citizen reports |
| 2. Automated amplification | Accounts, personas, coordinated posting, synthetic corroboration | **Adversary (the hypothesised new layer)** | Yes — organic social sharing |
| 3. Media attention | Coverage, often with hedging and "mystery" framing | Media | Yes — NJ coverage |
| 4. Citizen vigilance | Public starts looking, filming, reporting | Public | **Yes — thousands of NJ reports** |
| 5. Additional reports | Genuine, mistaken, and fabricated, now in far greater numbers | Public **+ adversary** | Yes — aircraft, stars, hobby drones |
| 6. Institutional response | Investigations, closures, deployments, legislation, procurement | **State/institutions** | **Yes — FAA/FBI response, airspace closures, legislative push** |
| 7. Perceived validation | "The authorities are taking it seriously, so it must be real" | Media/public | Yes — circular validation |

**The critical observation is at stages 4–7.** By the time the state responds, the state's own response has become *evidence* for the original claim. This is the reflexive loop, and it does not require an adversary to close — which is precisely why the agentic contribution may be smaller than assumed, and also why it may be *sufficient* even at very low intensity. An adversary does not need to persuade anyone if the target's own institutions will manufacture the confirmation.

### 4.2 The five categories — and why they must not be merged

The single most common error in public discussion is treating all of this as "disinformation." The five categories have different mechanisms, different evidence bases and **different defences**.

**Category 1 — Classic disinformation.** Deliberately false claims distributed at scale.
- *Mechanism:* assertion of falsehood.
- *Agentic relevance:* moderate. Agents make production cheap, but this category was already industrialised and is the best-defended.
- *Defence:* fact-checking, provenance, platform enforcement.
- *Verdict:* **not the interesting category.**

**Category 2 — Amplification of true but ambiguous events.** Real incidents presented to maximise fear or uncertainty.
- *Mechanism:* selective emphasis, omission of base rates, framing that implies agency ("mystery drones") where none is established.
- *Agentic relevance:* **high**. The material is true, so fact-checking does not rebut it; only *context* does — and context is exactly what amplification suppresses.
- *Defence:* base rates, denominator reporting, uncertainty transparency.
- *Verdict:* **the highest-value category for the hypothesis**, and the hardest to counter.

**Category 3 — Synthetic corroboration.** Multiple apparently independent accounts or interpretations generated or coordinated artificially.
- *Mechanism:* false consensus signalling; converting one claim into an apparently well-attested event.
- *Agentic relevance:* **highest of all**, and the clearest qualitative change. Historically expensive and detectable; now cheap.
- *Defence:* independence testing, provenance analysis, behavioural detection.
- *Verdict:* **the genuinely new capability.**

**Category 4 — Emergent amplification.** Humans independently reproduce and strengthen the narrative after the initial intervention.
- *Mechanism:* self-sustaining social transmission; the "useful idiot" dynamic without the pejorative.
- *Agentic relevance:* indirect — agents lower the ignition threshold but do not control the fire.
- *Evidence:* **strongly documented pre-agentically** (NJ, Gatwick, Havana).
- *Verdict:* **already established; the agentic contribution is at the margin.**

**Category 5 — Reflexive effects.** Authorities, media or citizens make individually rational defensive decisions that produce the attacker's desired outcome: closures, deployments, political pressure, resource expenditure, distrust, social anxiety.
- *Mechanism:* decision-theoretic, not informational. The defender's expected-loss calculation rationally produces the harm.
- *Agentic relevance:* low direct, **very high strategic** — this is where the effect is realised, and it is largely outside the adversary's control but not outside their benefit.
- *Verdict:* **the most important category for policy**, and the least discussed.

**Why the distinction is load-bearing.** A state that responds to this threat as though it were category 1 will build fact-checking capacity. A state facing category 3 needs provenance and network analysis. A state facing category 5 needs **decision discipline** — the ability to respond proportionately under uncertainty without generating the very effects the adversary wants. **These are different capabilities, and the reflexive category is the one almost nobody has.**

### 4.3 The "small input, large output" claim

The hypothesis asserts a large amplification gain. Three things can be said.

**Gain is real and pre-agentic.** In the New Jersey case the input was essentially *nothing anomalous* — no adversary action was established — and the output was 5,000+ reports, an interagency response, and counter-UAS legislation. The gain ratio, whatever the denominator, is enormous. Copenhagen, Gatwick and Madrid show the same pattern, in two cases without even an evidenced physical event.

**High gain does not mean controllability.** The NJ case shows that a cascade can occur *without* a driver, which cuts both ways: it supports H1b (society self-amplifies) while undermining the implication that an adversary can *steer* the result. Cascades have their own dynamics — they select their own folk devils, escalate toward their own demands, and terminate on their own schedule. An actor seeking a *specific* outcome cannot rely on a cascade to deliver it.

**Measured gain in influence operations is much lower than reach suggests.** The reach metrics in this field (followers, views) are not belief metrics. The most rigorous assessments of foreign influence operations find effects far smaller than the operations' designers intended. **Reach scales; persuasion does not scale at the same rate.** This is the strongest single constraint on the hypothesis, and it is developed in §8.

### 4.4 What an agent must actually do

To move from "possible" to "consequential", an agentic operation would have to accomplish all of the following — and each is a potential point of failure:

1. **Find or create a substrate** — an ambiguous event in a domain the target cares about.
2. **Achieve initial critical mass** before the natural news cycle moves on.
3. **Sustain independence illusions** — make correlated accounts appear uncorrelated.
4. **Survive detection** by platforms, researchers and journalists — noting that *being caught* is itself the primary harm to the operation (see §8.1).
5. **Avoid provoking an authoritative, rapid, credible rebuttal** — the single most effective counter (see §4.1 and §11).
6. **Cross the institutional threshold** where response becomes reflexive and self-validating.
7. **Not overshoot** into a backlash that discredits the underlying claim and inoculates the audience against the next attempt.

**Step 5 is the weak link, and step 7 is the trap.** Most documented campaigns fail at one of them.

## 5. What is established

### 5.1 Ambiguity produces large disruption **without any adversary at all** (L4, strong)

This is the study's firmest ground, and it is entirely pre-agentic. The clearest case is also the best documented.

**The New Jersey / US north-east drone episode, November–December 2024.**

The primary record is unambiguous. In a joint statement on **17 December 2024**, the Department of Homeland Security, the FBI, the FAA and the Department of Defense stated:

> "FBI has received tips of **more than 5,000 reported drone sightings** in the last few weeks with approximately **100 leads generated**… Having closely examined the technical data and tips from concerned citizens, we assess that the sightings to date include a **combination of lawful commercial drones, hobbyist drones, and law enforcement drones, as well as manned fixed-wing aircraft, helicopters, and stars mistakenly reported as drones**. We have **not identified anything anomalous** and do not assess the activity to date to **present a national security or public safety risk**."

Two further features of that statement matter more than the finding itself:

1. **The institutional response became an amplification channel.** The same statement urged Congress "to enact counter-UAS legislation", and the episode produced congressional pressure, an FAA restriction regime, and a wave of state and local activity. **The state's rational response validated the concern.**
2. **The explanation did not end the phenomenon.** In February 2026 federal agencies again confirmed that no national security risk had been found after the 5,000 tips, and in April 2026 a mundane explanation surfaced (fifteen *stolen* drones recovered from a warehouse). The episode nonetheless remained culturally live into 2026 — a documentary series, continuing official statements, and continued public argument.

**Every stage of the hypothesised chain occurred, and no adversary was identified.** A genuine but unremarkable mix of legal activity and misperception produced thousands of reports, an inter-agency response, a legislative push, and durable public salience. If the hypothesis were that *societies can be made to generate their own disruption from ambiguity*, it would be confirmed — by a case in which the input was, in effect, **nothing anomalous**.

**The same pattern repeats with weaker substrates.**

| Case | Substrate | Outcome | Was an adversary established? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gatwick, Dec 2018** | No drone ever evidenced — no image, no video, recovered drones ruled out | 33-hour runway closure, ~1,000 flights, ~140,000 passengers; £790,000 investigation, no charges; police settlement | **No** |
| **Copenhagen, Sep 2025** | Police concluded June 2026 they could **neither prove nor deny** drones were present | ~4-hour closure; PM called it the most serious attack on Danish critical infrastructure to date | **No** — framing retracted |
| **Madrid-Barajas, 2025** | Guardia Civil reportedly concluded the drone **never existed** | Airport closure | No |
| **Vilnius, Sep 2026** | **Birds** | Airport closure | No |
| **Norway, autumn 2025** | Three police districts publicly retracted: "stars and planets", ship traffic, insufficient evidence | National alert, political consequences | No |
| **US north-east, 2024** | FAA attributed to **its own research aircraft** | Airspace restrictions | No |
| **Havana syndrome, 2016–** | Decade of contested causation; institutional consensus repeatedly revised | Compensation legislation, inter-agency programmes, diplomatic consequences | **Contested, unresolved** |

**The substrate is not ignorance; it is ambiguity plus an informational vacuum.** Rumour theory's original formulation — that rumour strength varies with importance × ambiguity — is close to a specification of this mechanism. What the cases add is that the third variable is **the speed and credibility of authoritative information**. Where officials said nothing, or said something hedged, or visibly disagreed with each other, the cascade ran longer. Where an authoritative, specific, rapid explanation arrived, it shortened.

### 5.1b The autumn 2025 European wave: a documented natural experiment (L4, strong)

The September–October 2025 drone wave across Europe is the best available laboratory for the hypothesised chain, because the full sequence — including retraction — was documented in detail.

**The mechanism, in the participants' own words.** Denmark's national police recorded **over 500 reports from citizens in a single 24-hour period** across all police districts. The National Operative Staff moved to its highest readiness level, last used after the 2015 Copenhagen terror attacks. The Prime Minister called it "the most serious attack on Danish critical infrastructure to date."

The self-reinforcing dynamic was later described explicitly by specialist observers:

> "Whoever regularly sees news reports about enemy drones will more readily interpret an unknown point of light as a drone… **It is a self-reinforcing mechanism.**"

> "It was no longer merely reporting an event; **the images themselves became a trigger for new observations.**"

**That second sentence is the whole hypothesis, stated by an observer of an entirely organic cascade.**

**The most dangerous false positive.** On 28 September 2025, soldiers at the Borris firing range fired **43 rounds** at what they believed was a hostile drone. Flight-radar data shows a **Widerøe Dash 8-400 passenger aircraft passing directly over the range** at the time; a message sent minutes later read *"They just shot at a plane over the Borris camp."* Senior officials initially denied that a manned aircraft had been targeted. **A cascade with no adversary came within seconds of causing a mass-casualty aviation disaster through friendly fire.**

**The retraction.** After a **nine-month investigation**, Copenhagen Police concluded on 25 June 2026 that they could "**not confirm that there has been drone activity in and around the airport**" and simultaneously could "not rule out" it. **No suspects were identified and the investigation was closed.** The crown witness — the airport employee whose report started it — was told repeatedly by police that what she saw was not drones; experts assessed the video as **lens flare**, and flight data pointed to a **school aircraft** flying patterns over the airport. The airport, it emerged, had only **bird radar and a consumer DJI detector** — not hostile-drone detection. The Danish Justice Minister's response is itself telling: *"It is part of the nature of hybrid warfare that the adversary does not reveal itself."* **An unfalsifiable framing survives the failure of the evidence.**

**Norway.** The Police Security Service stated in February 2026: **"We have not uncovered a single case of a drone being controlled by a foreign state."** Reported sightings were "various celestial bodies such as stars, weather phenomena, aeroplanes, or helicopters." Three police districts publicly retracted.

**Belgium.** A VRT investigation established that **558 reports** produced **no physical evidence in any case**; under military intelligence's own definition, only an intercepted drone or detained operator counts as conclusive, and **such evidence has never been produced**. The "enormous drone" video over Brussels Airport — which the Defence Minister himself sent to a newspaper — turned out to be a **federal police helicopter**. Allegations later emerged that the minister had been warned and had cut seconds from the video before publication, and opposition parties suggested the threat had been inflated ahead of a €50 million counter-drone procurement. Belgium's Defence Ministry conceded it **"did not have the necessary means to detect drones, nor to intervene after detection"**, that capability was "very limited", and that there had been **"no formal attribution, ever"** — while maintaining that unconfirmed reports "illustrate the inherent uncertainty of hybrid threat scenarios" rather than overestimation.

**Lithuania, 13 September 2026.** A radar return was assessed as a possible drone. **Vilnius Airport was closed, an air-raid alert was activated, and NATO scrambled a fighter.** Visual identification established it was a **flock of birds**. The airport reopened **38 minutes** after closing. A near-identical Polish case the previous week also turned out to be birds.

**Two quantitative findings that cut against the threat case.**

1. **An eleven-country audit** of **61 alleged drone incidents** between 9 September and 27 November 2025 found that **in the great majority there was no concrete evidence a drone was present**; several were confirmed as helicopters, aircraft or celestial bodies. Concrete physical evidence — interceptions, debris — existed **only near Ukraine's borders**.
2. **A natural experiment on detection.** After an advanced detection system was deployed to Belgium, **reports of alleged drones declined rapidly**. Conversely, the Netherlands lacked drone-detection capability during its November 2025 incidents — because specialised radars had been loaned to Ukraine — and later no concrete evidence was found for those sightings either. **The number of "drones" tracks the availability of detection, which is a strong indication that many reports were artefacts of looking.**

**The chain operated in full, produced a near-catastrophe, drove national readiness to its highest level, and was then found to rest on lens flare, a school aircraft, a police helicopter and birds.**

### 5.1c The Boston 2013 case: emergent amplification, real harm, and what actually stopped it

The 2013 Boston Marathon bombing aftermath is the cleanest documented case of **emergent amplification causing direct harm to an identifiable person** — and of how such a cascade terminates.

- **Misinformation outpaced correction by 18:1, 18:1 and 5:1** across three phases of the event (Starbird et al., 2014).
- Corrections were **muted and lagged**: they did not travel as far or as fast as the claims they answered.
- The cascade produced a **misidentification of an innocent person** (Sunil Tripathi) that reached national media.
- **The misidentification was driven by well-meaning citizens** conducting genuine crisis sense-making — the "digital vigilante" dynamic — not by any adversary.
- **Only an official announcement ended it.** Not a correction, not a debunk, not a retraction by the crowd: a statement from an authoritative source.

**This is the most practically useful case in the study for defence (§11.2).** It establishes three things at once: that the mechanism is real and harmful without any adversary; that **correction alone is insufficient** because corrections lose the race; and that **authoritative resolution is what actually terminates** an ambiguity cascade.

**And the counterweight that must be stated alongside it:** the disaster-sociology literature (Quarantelli & Dynes; Clarke; Drury; Tierney) finds that **mass panic in disasters is rare to non-existent**. Crowds are typically more orderly and more helpful than the "panic" framing implies, and the Boston case shows both failure (the misidentification) and resilience (self-organising assistance and verification) in the same event. **The population is not the vulnerable layer.**



### 5.1d The central negative finding

This must be stated plainly, because it is the single most important answer to the study's question.

**No credible, documented case was located in which a foreign state, bot network, paid troll farm or influence operation deliberately amplified a European drone-sighting wave.**

**This finding was reached independently twice, from different directions** — once by searching for amplification of drone sightings specifically, and once by a separate strand examining state use of synthetic media against physical incidents of any kind. **Neither found a verified case of a state using synthetic media to amplify a specific physical incident.** The strongest available 2026 evidence remains aggregate rather than incident-specific: the EEAS recorded **540 FIMI incidents in 2025, of which 27% involved AI-related TTPs, rising from 41 AI-related cases in 2024 to 147 in 2025**, with the AI material judged mostly low-quality and weakly engaged.

That convergence matters. The negative finding is **not** an artefact of one search strategy, one language, or one framing — and it is therefore stronger than an absence of evidence usually is. No Europol, EEAS, EDMO, DFRLab, ISD, EU DisinfoLab, Hybrid Centre of Excellence or DSA transparency report attributing a drone panic to an amplification operation was found after an extensive documented search.

What the record *does* contain is three distinct things, which are routinely conflated:

| Category | Status | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Real, unattributed physical incursions** | **Proven** in specific cases | Leipzig/Halle (Aug 2026, attributed to Russia); the Øresund drone launched from a Russian intelligence ship (Feb 2026); the Polish incursion (Sept 2025) |
| **Opportunistic propaganda riding the wave** | **Documented, reactive** | EDMO analyses of false narratives around Romanian and Baltic drone incidents — *after* the events |
| **Overwhelmingly organic misidentification amplified by media feedback** | **The dominant mechanism** | Copenhagen, Belgium, Norway, Vilnius, Madrid, and the majority of the 61 audited incidents |

**The distinction is the study's answer.** Europe in 2025–26 experienced real hostile drone flights, opportunistic propaganda exploiting them, and enormous organic misidentification amplified by the media's own reporting. **It did not experience a manufactured drone panic.** The panic was real. It was also, in the great majority of cases, nobody's operation.

**Three sharper findings refine the picture, and one of them is a genuine exception that must be recorded.**

1. **The EU's own drone policy instrument contains no information-warfare content at all.** A term check of the Commission's Action Plan on Drone and Counter Drone Security (**COM(2026) 81 final**, 11 February 2026) finds **zero occurrences** of "disinformation", "FIMI", "information manipulation" or "amplification". It is a **capability instrument, not an information instrument**. Given how often the European drone wave is described in information-warfare terms, the absence is itself evidence: **the institution closest to the problem does not assess it as an information operation.**
2. **Where reactive FIMI is documented, it is geographically specific — and the countries most often named in the drone panic are missing.** The EEAS's 4th FIMI report names **Poland, Romania, Lithuania and Estonia**, and **explicitly does not name Denmark or Norway** — the two countries whose airport closures drove the autumn 2025 narrative. Reactive propaganda clustered near the Ukrainian border, where real incursions occurred; it did not cluster around the retracted western cases.
3. **The one documented coordinated amplification of a drone narrative is an exception that proves the rule.** In **Galați, Romania (May–June 2026)** a coordinated network ran for **seven consecutive days at 28–105 accounts and 3,400–6,800 posts per day — 116,014 posts in total**. That is a real, measured, deliberate amplification campaign around a drone event. **But it amplified a false-flag claim about a *real* strike, not a sighting wave** — which places it in category 2 of the table above (amplification of true-but-ambiguous events), not in the manufactured-event category. It is the strongest documented case in the entire record, and it still does not show an adversary manufacturing a drone scare.

**And the nearest thing to an amplification of an *unproven* theory was performed not by a hostile state but by a think tank and the press** — the IISS shadow-fleet assessment of July 2026, which specialist outlets criticised as resting on assumptions and previously debunked sightings. **When the amplifiers are democratic institutions rather than adversaries, the "information operation" framing has to be applied to ourselves.**


### 5.2 Agentic influence operations: the first credible evidence of deployment (L3, emerging — for influence generally, not for physical scares)

A significant development occurred in September 2026. Reporting in the *New York Times*, summarised and independently contextualised by other outlets, found that **Iran, China and private Israeli companies had used AI agents in social-media influence campaigns**. The features that distinguish these from earlier operations:

- **Hundreds of AI agents** were used in some campaigns.
- Agents carried out **multi-step operations** — account creation, content generation, and coordination — across Facebook, Instagram, X and TikTok.
- The Iranian operation's accounts **presented as ordinary Americans in major US cities**, posted memes and political commentary, tagged journalists and politicians, and accumulated **nearly 80,000 followers in the first half of 2026**.

**A parallel data point from a primary EU source.** The **EEAS 4th Annual Report on Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference Threats (12 March 2026)** reports that in 2025, **27% of detected FIMI incidents involved AI-related tactics, techniques and procedures, up from 41 to 147 cases in 2024 — a 259% increase.**

But the same report — and this is the single most important sentence in the study — states that **"much of the AI-generated material in 2025 remains low-quality… resulting in limited overall impact as organic engagement remains low"**, and names Storm-1516 and Operation Overload as producing AI-generated videos **"easily identifiable as inauthentic by average viewers"**. The report identifies **Storm-1516 as the only influence actor capable of generating organic engagement**, with average per-content reach of **5,000 to 4,000,000 views**.

**The central analytic line of this study follows directly from that finding:**

> **AI is transforming the *production* of influence material far more convincingly than it is transforming *reach* or *persuasion*.**

Production-side changes are real and measurable: volume, multilingual capacity, persona maintenance, content laundering, and the cost of all four. Demand-side effects remain small, engagement-dependent, and — on the EU's own assessment — often poor-quality. **An operation that produces a thousand times more content into an audience that does not engage has not achieved a thousand-fold increase in effect.**

The same assessment supplies the phrase that best captures the change: **"the objective is not precision but presence."** That is the whole production-versus-reach distinction in six words. Flooding an information environment is now cheap; being believed is not.

**But even the AI attribution is partly wrong, and this is an important qualification.** The best primary source on the mechanism — the US Treasury's OFAC designation (31 December 2024) of the GRU-linked **Centre for Geopolitical Expertise** — states that it "used generative AI tools" to mass-produce disinformation for **"at least 100 websites"** imitating news outlets, and "built a server that hosts the generative AI tools." Yet the forensic record cuts against a purely AI story:

- **VIGINUM found that more than half of Storm-1516's operations used recruited amateur actors** — human beings, not models.
- **Check First's forensics found that Matryoshka's 2023–24 content was made in Illustrator and Photoshop — "not generated by AI tools"** at all.

**So the "AI influence operation" label frequently describes operations that are substantially manual.** The binding constraint was never generation cost for these actors, because they were not paying it. This reinforces the study's central economic finding from a direction the hypothesis does not anticipate: **the labour that matters is distribution labour — burner accounts, purchased personas, seeded comment threads, paid amplifiers — and that has not been automated.**

**The measured reach figures, for scale:**

| Operation | Measured reach |
|---|---|
| Storm-1516 (the most effective documented) | ~1.1 million views |
| Portal Kombat | ~31,000 visits/month across all five Western portals |
| Spamouflage | ~80% of >57,000 disabled channels had **zero subscribers** |
| Meta's best-quantified case (Moldova) | ~4,200 followers; ~$4,000 spend |

Meta has removed **over 200 operations since 2017 across 68 countries — roughly two-thirds of them domestic**, not foreign. Google terminated **15,727 and 18,514 YouTube channels in Q1 and Q2 2026** while publishing **no reach data** for any of them. *(The absence of reach data in enforcement reporting is itself a finding: takedown counts measure enforcement activity, not threat, and are routinely cited as though they measured the latter.)*


Two further corroborating items:

- **Institute for Strategic Dialogue** (April 2026) measured two coordinated pro-Iran networks on X — comprising only about **40 accounts** — generating **more than one billion views, roughly 16 million likes and 3.5 million reposts within a single month** during the first month of the Iran war, circulating false, misleading and AI-generated material. ISD attributed the reach to **X's "For You" recommender plus reposts by accounts with over 500,000 followers**, not to the persona network's own audience.

**This finding cuts against the reassuring reading of the reach data, and it needs stating carefully.** The correct interpretation is that **reach is rented from the platform, not owned by the operation.** Forty accounts do not have a billion impressions; they have a billion impressions *because a recommendation algorithm gave them one*.

Three consequences follow:

1. **"Low organic reach" is not a stable property of a network — it is a property of its current relationship with platform distribution.** A network ignored today can be amplified to a billion views tomorrow by a recommender change, an algorithmic accident, or a handful of large accounts choosing to repost.
2. **Defender leverage relocates.** If reach is rented, the highest-leverage intervention is **recommendation and amplification policy**, not debunking. Debunking competes with the recommender; changing the recommender changes what there is to debunk.
3. **It qualifies §6.9.** The 524:1 distribution asymmetry is real for *paid* distribution — but the largest reach event in this dataset was achieved through *rented algorithmic* distribution at essentially no cost to the operator. **The distribution constraint is a pricing constraint on one channel, not a law of the medium.**

- **OpenAI disclosed in June 2026** that it had disrupted a Chinese influence operation using ChatGPT to generate social-media posts targeting US opinion on tariffs, AI policy and data centres.

**The commercial influence market is where adaptation is most credibly claimed.** Analysis of US regulatory gaps describes a commercial system, reportedly designated **"GoLaxy"**, whose accounts were allegedly engineered to build psychological profiles of targets, adapt messaging in real time, and saturate search results with fabricated content — with one internal dossier reportedly listing **117 members of the US Congress** as targets. The characterisation offered is that these accounts "did not just broadcast propaganda. They listened, learned, and persuaded." *(Grade D — single-source, described via a secondary legal-analysis outlet; the underlying dossier is not public. Treat the capability claim as **L2/L3 and unverified**; treat the existence of a commercial influence-for-hire market as **well established**.)*

This is the closest thing located to the **continuous reflexive coupling** described in §3.4 — an operation that observes reaction and adjusts — and it is precisely the element of the hypothesis that would be most consequential if true. It also remains the least verified.

**Level assessment: L3 for agentic influence operations in general. L1–L2, and unevidenced at L3, for agentic amplification of ambiguous *physical* events.** No case was located in which AI agents were shown to have driven a drone-sighting wave, an infrastructure scare, or an ambiguous-incident cascade.

### 5.3 Synthetic corroboration — the genuinely new capability (L2–L3)

Of the five categories in §4.2, this is where the qualitative change is clearest, and it is the one that most directly threatens the hypothesised chain, because it attacks the **independence assumption** on which all verification rests.

Documented indicators:

- **NewsGuard-style tracking has identified over 1,200 AI-generated news websites** (by 2024) — a tenfold increase — providing the infrastructure for laundering claims into apparently journalistic sources.
- **Myth Detector identified 230 AI-generated accounts** supporting the Georgian government (August 2026) — a concrete, documented case of an AI-generated persona network.
- Research cited in the governance literature reports that **AI-generated election disinformation is now indistinguishable from authentic human-written journalism in more than half of evaluated instances**. *(Caution: this figure comes from a single study via a secondary source and requires verification; the direction is consistent with other findings but the number should be held loosely.)*

**The escalation up the trust hierarchy is the clearest evidence that the mechanism has changed.** Three documented steps, each cheap now and expensive before:

- **Laundering at scale, industrialised.** EU DisinfoLab's *Indian Chronicles* documented **15 years of operation, more than ten resurrected UN-accredited NGOs, and 750+ fake outlets across 119 countries** — proof that sustained multi-source laundering was always possible, given a decade and an organisation.
- **Content farms, automated.** Tracking of AI-generated news sites records growth from **49 sites in May 2023 to 3,749 by June 2026 — roughly a 76-fold increase**, with **358 tied to Storm-1516**. What took a decade in the Indian Chronicles case now takes a subscription.
- **Fabrication moved up the trust hierarchy.** OpenAI's August 2026 disclosure describes a **fake think tank staffed with the real names of actual researchers** — not a fake news site at the bottom of the stack, but a fabricated *institution* at the layer that journalists cite. Separately, Anthropic documented an operation **cloning a real activist from roughly 8,400 Telegram posts** to hold live conversations with his own contacts. **The victim's own network was the distribution channel.**

**Why this category matters more than the others.** Categories 1 and 2 attack the *content* of a claim; category 3 attacks the *evidence structure*. A society can fact-check a claim. It is far harder to ask, mechanically and in real time, whether twenty apparently independent witnesses are actually one actor. **Synthetic corroboration converts an ambiguous event from "a report" into "a pattern" — which is exactly the threshold at which institutions move.**

**The honest limit is not "untested" — it is worse than that: the mechanism is contradicted.**

This section originally described synthetic corroboration as the study's "genuinely new capability" with its strong form "untested." A direct literature check does not support that framing, and the correction is important enough to state plainly.

**The best direct test of the source-count mechanism is a null.** Foster, Huthwaite, Yesberg, Garry & Loftus (*Acta Psychologica*, 2012) tested whether multiple sources make misinformation more believable, and titled their result: **"Repetition, not number of sources, increases both susceptibility to misinformation and confidence."** The number of apparently independent sources did **not** drive belief; repetition did. Related work (Roggeveen & Johar, 2002) finds that source count operates through **subjective familiarity**, not through perceived source independence — i.e. the mechanism that intuition attributes to *corroboration* is actually *repetition wearing a disguise*.

The broader illusory-truth literature points the same way. The corrected meta-analysis (Ye et al., *Nature Communications*, 2026; 182 studies, N = 31,184) reports a pooled **g = 0.37** — real, but **negligible for socio-political *opinion***, which is the outcome this study cares about.

**What survives is a different and narrower mechanism — and it is not a belief multiplier.** The credible finding is that repetition makes the **publisher** appear more credible, and that credibility then **generalises to that publisher's other claims** (Mattavelli, Brambilla & Unkelbach, *PSPB*, 2026). In other words, the operative effect is **trust-laundering and the manufacture of institutional standing**, not manufactured consensus persuading anyone.

**The implications for this study are significant and should be carried into the verdict:**

1. **The category that genuinely concerns a defender is category 3, not because it multiplies belief, but because it manufactures *sources*.** A fake think tank stocked with real researchers' names does not need to persuade a reader of anything; it needs to be *citable*. That is a supply-side attack on the citation layer, and it is consistent with everything else in this study about production scaling while persuasion does not.
2. **The institution, not the individual, is the victim of the mechanism.** Trust-laundering targets the credibility of the *information environment*, which is precisely the layer the reflexive chain (§4.1, stage 6) depends on.
3. **The defensive priority shifts from "detect coordinated accounts" to "verify institutional standing."** Asking whether a cited body actually exists, has a history, and can be corroborated independently is a more durable defence than counting accounts.

**One genuinely open item.** Pillai & Fazio (*JARMAC*, 2025), titled "Repeated by many versus repeated by one," tests exactly this hypothesis, but the publisher blocks the abstract and **its effect size is unknown**. Until it is read, the honest position is that the mechanism is **contradicted by the best available direct test and not yet settled**.

**A mechanism that explains the failure — and converts it from an accident into a prediction.** A 2026 *Cognitive Science* study (N = 1,015) separated **dependent consensus** (the same source repeated) from **independent consensus** (genuinely distinct corroboration) and found that the two **persuade equally at low exposure**, but that independent corroboration yields **higher asymptotic belief as exposure accumulates**.

The implication is precise:

> **Fabricated independence buys nothing in a single encounter. It pays off only under sustained repetition.**

**This is the most useful single finding in the study**, because it ties three otherwise separate results into one structure:

| Finding | How the mechanism explains it |
|---|---|
| Synthetic corroboration is a null at first exposure | Dependent and independent consensus are indistinguishable at low exposure |
| Influence networks achieve negligible reach (0.3% of posts; 58% of DRAGONBRIDGE channels with zero subscribers) | They never accumulate the repeat exposure the effect requires |
| The binding constraint is distribution, not generation | Distribution *is* the repetition channel |

**Synthetic corroboration is therefore a bet on repeat exposure — and the distribution bottleneck is what breaks the bet.** This is no longer "AI influence campaigns happen not to work." It is a **structural prediction**: manufactured consensus should fail wherever reach is capped, and should succeed wherever it is not. That makes the finding falsifiable in a useful way, and it points the same direction as §6.9 and §12.2 — **reach, rented or owned, is the variable that decides everything.**

**One caution about the reach evidence itself.** **No audited authentic-audience figure exists for any of these networks.** Every reach number is platform-side or actor-side, and should be read as an **upper bound**. The figures in this report are reported as given, not as verified audiences.

### 5.4 What the reflexive stage actually looks like (L4)

The fifth category deserves separate treatment because it is where the effect is monetised, and because it is largely outside the adversary's control.

The mechanism is decision-theoretic. An official facing an ambiguous drone report at an airport must choose between (a) closing the runway at large economic cost with low probability of a real threat, and (b) not closing it, with a small probability of a catastrophic outcome and near-certain career-ending blame if wrong. **The rational choice is to close.** No disinformation is required; the incentive structure does the work.

The documented consequences across the cases in §5.1 include runway closures, fighter scrambles, airspace restrictions, legislative proposals, procurement, and — in the Dutch case — armed ground fire at drones and F-35 intercepts. European institutional responses have been substantial: the UK reported **266 UAV incidents near Defence sites in 2025 against 126 in 2024** and quadrupled counter-UAS spending to over £200 million; NATO committed to over $40 billion in counter-drone capabilities over five years; the EU produced an action plan in February 2026 and a FIMI reporting mechanism.

**None of this proves manipulation.** It proves something more useful for this study: **the institutional layer is the high-gain amplifier, it responds to perceived rather than verified threat, and it responds whether or not an adversary is present.** An agent seeking to impose cost on a NATO society therefore does not need to win an argument. It needs to reach the threshold at which the target's own decision rules take over.

## 6. What becomes newly possible because of agentic AI

This section assesses each capability the hypothesis depends on, against the four evidence levels. The pattern that emerges is consistent with §5.2: **capabilities that operate on *production* are demonstrably available; capabilities that operate on *effect* are not.**

### 6.1 Cross-platform coordination and account creation (L2–L3)

**Demonstrated.** The September 2026 reporting describes agents carrying out multi-step operations across **Facebook, Instagram, X and TikTok** — account creation, content generation and coordination — at a scale of hundreds of agents. A separate documented case identified **230 AI-generated accounts** in a single national political network.

**What this replaces.** Historically, running a persona network required people, shifts, and per-account human attention — which is why networks showed detectable behavioural regularities (posting rhythms, overlapping activity windows, correlated language). Automation erodes those regularities, though it does not eliminate them (§10.2).

**Assessment: L2–L3 for coordination; the labour constraint is genuinely removed.**

### 6.2 Multilingual and localised messaging (L2–L3)

Machine translation and style transfer make operating in a target's own language and idiom cheap, including regional variation. The EEAS reports multilingual capacity as one of the ways AI is changing FIMI production.

**Assessment: L2–L3 for production. Effect on persuasion is untested at the population level.**

### 6.3 Continuous monitoring and adaptive narrative selection (L1–L2, contested)

**Monitoring** — social listening, sentiment tracking, narrative detection — is commercially available and cheap; this is uncontroversial and does not require AI agents specifically.

**Adaptation** is the contested element. The strongest claim is the commercial system described in §5.2 (real-time messaging adaptation against profiled targets). Beyond that, the evidence is largely **A/B-testing by other means**: selecting among pre-written variants based on engagement. That is a real capability but a much weaker one than adaptive framing.

**Assessment: monitoring L2; adaptive narrative selection L1–L2, single-source, contested.** This is precisely the element that would most increase effectiveness, and precisely the element with the weakest public evidence.

### 6.4 Long-running persona management (L1–L2)

Sustained, consistent persona maintenance over months is the capability that makes **persistence** (sub-claim H1d) plausible: it is what allows an operation to survive the natural decay of a news cycle.

Technically this is a natural strength of LLM-driven agents — consistency over long horizons is exactly what they are good at. But two constraints bite: platform identity verification (phone numbers, device fingerprints, behavioural analysis) and the accumulating risk of stylistic or factual inconsistency.

**Assessment: L1–L2. Feasible; the binding constraint is platform integrity and the cost of maintaining identities, not the model.**

### 6.5 Synthetic corroboration and synthetic eyewitness material (L2–L3)

The most consequential capability in the study, for the reason given in §5.3: it attacks **independence**, not content.

Documented supporting infrastructure includes the proliferation of **AI-generated news websites** (over 1,200 identified by 2024) and documented AI-persona networks. Generative image and audio make fabricated "eyewitness" material trivial to produce; the January 2024 New Hampshire robocall impersonating a candidate is the canonical recorded case of synthetic audio deployed in a political context.

**But note the countervailing finding from the EU's own assessment:** much AI-generated material in 2025 was **low-quality and readily identified as inauthentic by average viewers**, and organic engagement remained low.

**Assessment: L2–L3 for generation; L1–L2 for material that survives scrutiny. The gap between "can be generated" and "is believed" is where the entire threat assessment turns.**

### 6.6 Exploitation of emerging narratives (L1–L2)

"Newsjacking" at machine speed — identifying a breaking ambiguous story and attaching content to it within minutes — is technically straightforward and is the mechanism by which the hypothesised chain is entered at the optimal moment. It requires only monitoring plus generation, both of which are available.

**Assessment: L1–L2. Plausible and cheap; not separately documented as a distinct capability in the physical-threat domain.**

### 6.7 Automated rebuttal evasion (negative finding, L1)

The ability to detect that a claim is being fact-checked and adapt — pre-emptively reframing, shifting sub-claim, or abandoning an angle before it is discredited — would be a meaningful asymmetric advantage, converting fact-checking from a defence into a whack-a-mole problem.

**No documented case was located of this being used in a real information operation.** The closest thing found is *scaffolding*: agent harnesses with shared long-term memory files containing banned-word lists, approved-source lists, account-management rules and evasion rules, called in fixed batches. That configuration **permits** feedback-driven iteration; it does not demonstrate it. Closed-loop narrative adaptation was **not observed in any case**.

**Assessment: L1, speculative — and now a confirmed negative finding rather than merely an absence.** This is one of the few places where the evidence actively argues against the strongest form of the hypothesis.

### 6.8 Persuasion: what the controlled studies actually show (L1–L2)

The persuasion literature is the most quantitatively developed part of this field, and it is more deflating than headline coverage suggests.

- **Salvi et al. (2025), *Nature Human Behaviour*** — preregistered, N = 900. **The original headline — that personalised GPT-4 achieved an 81.2% relative increase in the odds of post-debate agreement — was corrected on 3 September 2026 and effectively retired.** The corrected personalisation result is **+48.7% (95% CI −2.9% to +127.6%, p = 0.068) — not statistically significant.** The study's own correction therefore removes its central claim. *(This report cited the uncorrected figure in an earlier draft; it is replaced here.)*
- **Costello, Pennycook & Rand (2024), *Science*** — N = 2,190 conspiracy believers; personalised dialogue with GPT-4 Turbo produced a **~20% reduction in conspiracy belief, durable at two months**. The strongest result in the literature — and it is a *relative reduction on a scale*, not a conversion.
- **The mechanism may not be AI at all.** A 2025 *PNAS Nexus* follow-up (N = 955) found the effect **held when the same dialogue was presented as coming from a human expert**. The active ingredient is **personalised, fact-dense, responsive dialogue** — agents scale that ingredient; they do not create it. This is the single most important deflationary finding in the persuasion literature.
- **Hackenburg et al. (2025), *Science*** — the largest study to date: N = 76,977, 19 LLMs, 707 issues. Post-training raised persuasiveness by up to **51%**, prompting by up to **27%**. Critically for ambiguous events: **where these methods increased persuasiveness, they systematically decreased factual accuracy.**
- **Bai et al. (2025), *Nature Communications*** — LLM-generated messages moved policy attitudes but were **"similarly effective" to messages written by lay humans.** The advantage was stylistic.
- **Microtargeting is the weakest link, not the strongest.** Hackenburg & Margetts (2024, *PNAS*) found message-quality effects of up to 12 percentage points — but **access to individual-level data did not increase persuasive influence.** The capability most feared is the one with the least support in the best-powered study.
- **The ambient channel is the most under-appreciated.** *Science Advances* (2026) found that a **biased AI writing assistant** shifted users' attitudes toward its own position, with a majority **unaware of the bias** — a per-user, deniable vector that is not a "campaign" at all and would not appear in any takedown statistic.

**The load-bearing gap.** There is **still no field experiment showing AI-generated persuasion shifting real-world behaviour at population scale.** Every headline result is a lab or survey experiment with self-selected participants, self-reported attitudes, and no competing counter-campaign. LLM persuasive superiority also **wanes over repeated interactions**, unlike human persuasiveness.

### 6.9 The binding constraint is distribution, not generation (L2, structural)

This is the most practically important finding in the study, and it is arithmetic rather than theoretical.

**Generating content became nearly free; distributing it did not.** The cost of generating a social-media post in 2026 is on the order of **$0.0001–$0.04** — a thousand posts for under $40. The cost of *distributing* one through platform APIs is around **$0.015 per post, rising to $0.200 when the post contains a URL**. That is a **roughly 524-fold asymmetry** between generation and distribution.

Three consequences follow:

1. **The labour constraint was removed; the access constraint was not.** An operator can now produce a fake newsroom, a fake expert institute, a fake analyst with a byline, and a persistent persona at near-zero marginal cost — in twenty languages. They cannot make people read it.
2. **Defensive leverage sits in identity and access, not content moderation.** The finding points to **account-acquisition cost, identity verification, platform integrity funding and speed-to-correction** as the places where the asymmetry can be attacked. Moderating text is expensive and endless; raising the cost of a credible account is neither.
3. **It explains the reach failure directly.** Every first-party 2026 disclosure puts AI-enabled networks at low breakout levels with little or no authentic engagement — **except where they ride existing state-media distribution.** The operators who succeed are the ones who already had an audience.

**The single most useful number in the entire corpus, for scale-versus-effect.** OpenAI's February 2026 disclosure describes a Chinese law-enforcement-linked unit that **"made over 50,000 posts across over 200 Western platforms. Of those, under 150 posts received over 300 shares or comments."**

That is a **hit rate of roughly 0.3%** — one post in three hundred achieving even modest distribution, from an operation with state resources across two hundred platforms. **It is the production-to-reach gap expressed as a single ratio, and it is the most compact answer available to the question of whether generating more content produces more effect.**

**This reframes the whole threat.** The question is not "can agents generate a persuasive campaign?" — they can. It is **"can agents get it in front of people who are not already looking?"** — and on the evidence, that remains the hard part.

**A cost reality check that punctures the "AI makes it free" narrative.** When agent labour is priced properly against its actual comparators, the picture is more modest than it appears:

- A cloned-voice agent running at **$3.00–$4.80 per hour** does beat US labour by **6–10×** — but **loses to Philippine offshore labour (under $2 per hour) by 1.5–2.4×**.
- **The comparator for influence labour is offshore outsourced labour, not a US salary.** The relevant saving is real but measured in multiples, not orders of magnitude — and for text production it is smaller still, because the output also has to be *distributed* (§6.9).

**And one defensive cost has conspicuously failed to fall.** Across sixteen years, the cost of defeating a **CAPTCHA** has improved by only about **1.8×** — the one defensive barrier that has not collapsed alongside everything else. Meanwhile SMS-verified account prices sit at **$0.08–$0.10** but **spike 12–15% before elections**, indicating that account acquisition is a genuine, priced constraint that responds to demand.

**And the cost finding is now confirmed by two independent derivations.** Metric Media's reported **1.3 million articles in 2025** would cost only **$1,300–$27,000** in inference against roughly **$10–14 million in funding** — a ratio of about **1,000:1**. That is derived from content volume; the 524:1 figure above is derived from platform API pricing. **Two unrelated methods, the same conclusion: generation is not the binding cost, and never was.**

**Both findings point the same way:** the durable defensive leverage is **account acquisition cost and identity verification**, precisely because those costs have *not* collapsed.
 This is the concrete version of the recommendation in §6.9 and §11.

### 6.10 The liar's dividend: the verification layer erodes in both directions (L2, genuinely new)

This is a qualitative change that this study initially under-weighted, and it deserves separate treatment because it is not about generating a campaign at all — it is about degrading the **evidentiary value of recorded reality**, which is a public good societies rely on.

Two findings establish it:

- **Synthetic faces are already judged indistinguishable from, and in some tests more trustworthy than, real faces** (Nightingale & Farid, *PNAS*, 2022).
- **Warnings do not fully repair the damage.** Clark, Lewandowsky and colleagues (January 2026) found that **45–54% of participants who *accepted* a deepfake warning still judged guilt from the video** — and, more strikingly, that **specific warnings led some people to call *real* video fake**.

**Three bounds on the effect, all of which matter for the assessment:**

1. **It works on text and largely fails against video.** Schiff et al. (*APSR*, 2024; >15,000 adults) is the best-powered test, and the asymmetry is the finding. The channel that matters most — video evidence of a physical incident — is the one most resistant to the dividend.
2. **Human detection is poor but not hopeless.** The pooled figure across **56 papers and 86,155 participants** is **55.5%** — barely above chance, and far below the "indistinguishable" framing this report initially used. *(That earlier figure is withdrawn.)*
3. **It is trainable against.** **Impression-based training lifts detection accuracy from 40% to 80%** (Dawel et al., *PNAS*, 2026). This is the largest defensive effect size on the synthetic-media side found anywhere in this study — considerably larger than the prebunking effects in §11.8 — and it is cheap.

The second finding is the important one, because it describes a two-sided effect:

| Direction | Effect | Beneficiary |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost of belief falls** | Fabricated evidence becomes usable — an ambiguous event can be "confirmed" cheaply | The attacker |
| **Cost of denial rises** | Genuine evidence becomes deniable — "that's AI" is now a universally available rebuttal | The attacker, and any guilty party |

**This is the "liar's dividend", and it is the most structurally significant change in the study.** Every prior category in this report is about *supplying* something — a claim, an ambiguity, a corroborating witness. This category is about *destroying* something: the baseline assumption that a recording of an event is evidence that the event occurred.

**Why it matters for the hypothesised chain specifically.** The chain in §4.1 requires an **informational vacuum** to run (§5.1). Historically that vacuum was created by *absence* — nobody knew what happened. The liar's dividend creates a vacuum by **abundance** — everybody can see what happened and nobody can agree whether it is real. That is a more durable and harder-to-fill condition, and it is the substrate on which every other category in this study becomes more effective.

**It also has a defensive cost that is easy to miss.** An institution that relies on video evidence to resolve an ambiguous incident — the mechanism that actually terminated the Boston 2013 cascade (§5.1c) and resolved the Vilnius and Polish bird cases (§5.1b) — is relying on a channel whose evidentiary value is declining. **The defence described in §11.3 depends on verification working, and this is the trend that undermines it.**

## 7. Credible evidence of deployment — and the epistemics problem

### 7.1 What exists at L3

| Claim | Evidence | Level | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI agents have been used in real influence operations | Sept 2026 reporting on Iranian, Chinese and commercial Israeli campaigns; hundreds of agents; multi-platform | **L3** | M–H |
| AI is increasingly used in state-linked FIMI | EEAS FIMI report, Mar 2026: 27% of detected incidents involved AI TTPs; 41→147 cases in 2024 (+259%) | **L3** | H (primary) |
| Synthetic persona networks exist and are detected | 230 AI-generated accounts in a national political network (Aug 2026) | **L3** | M |
| AI-generated *physical-event* content circulates at scale | Reporting on AI-generated fake natural-disaster videos during 2026 flooding in China | **L3 (content) / L1 (effect)** | M |
| Commercial influence-for-hire offers real-time adaptive targeting | "GoLaxy" dossier, via secondary legal analysis | **L2/L3, single-source** | **L** |
| **AI agents have driven an ambiguous physical-threat cascade** | **None located** | **—** | — |

### 7.2 The specific evidence that is missing

No case was located in which an AI-enabled actor was shown to have:

- seeded or amplified a **drone-sighting** episode;
- amplified a **sabotage or infrastructure** scare;
- manufactured and sustained an **unexplained-disruption** narrative;
- or been detected running synthetic **eyewitness** accounts around a physical incident.

This is a genuine absence after a deliberate search, and it should be stated plainly. But it must be interpreted correctly, because **three distinct explanations fit the same absence**, and they have opposite policy implications:

1. **It is not happening.** The capability exists but the cost-benefit does not favour this application — perhaps because influence operators get better returns elsewhere, or because physical-threat cascades are hard to steer (see §4.3).
2. **It is happening and has not been detected.** Successful operations would be, by design, indistinguishable from organic concern.
3. **It is happening at a scale too small to isolate** from the large volume of organic ambiguity-driven activity documented in §5.1.

**Explanation 3 is the most likely, and it is also the most analytically awkward.** It means the effect may be real but permanently unattributable — not because detection is failing, but because the phenomenon and its camouflage are the same thing.

### 7.3 The epistemics problem: success is indistinguishable from noise

This is the study's most important methodological finding, and it deserves to be stated at full strength.

**A perfectly executed amplification campaign against an ambiguous physical event produces exactly the observable signature of sincere public concern.** It generates reports, social sharing, media coverage, citizen vigilance and institutional response — which is what genuine concern generates. There is no residue that says "this was engineered" unless the engineering is *sloppy*.

The consequence is that the hypothesis is **partly unfalsifiable as usually stated**, and the standard falsification test in §2.1 cannot be applied cleanly. This has three implications:

1. **Detection effort must target the *means*, not the *effect*.** We cannot reliably infer intent from a cascade. We can detect persona networks, coordination patterns, provenance anomalies and infrastructure. **Attribution must be built on tradecraft signatures, not on outcome.**
2. **Absence of evidence is weak evidence here.** Unlike the autonomy question in the parent study — where a physical capability either exists or does not — this mechanism can operate invisibly by construction. "We found no case" is therefore a much weaker statement than it would be elsewhere, and **should not be reported as reassurance.**
3. **The base rate is the anchor.** Because organic cascades are well documented and frequent (§5.1), the correct prior is that most observed cascades are organic. An analyst should require **positive evidence of coordination** before attributing a cascade, and should expect that most attributions will be wrong.

**A practical corollary.** Since the effect is indistinguishable from noise, the useful question is not "was this cascade engineered?" — usually unanswerable — but **"does the presence of this cascade change the cost-benefit calculation for an adversary?"** That question is answerable, and it is what §9 and §12 address.

### 7.4 What *would* count as convincing evidence

Setting this out in advance makes the hypothesis testable rather than rhetorical. Convincing evidence would be **at least two of**:

- **Platform attribution** of a network whose content is specifically organised around a physical-ambiguity narrative, with the network's own operator identified.
- **Infrastructure correlation** — accounts sharing generation artefacts, hosting, or timing patterns, tied to the same incident.
- **A documented operational artefact** — internal material showing an actor selecting a physical-ambiguity narrative as a target.
- **Temporal anomaly** — a measurable divergence between synthetic activity and organic interest in a specific incident (the synthetic curve leading or decoupling from the organic one).
- **A prosecution, official attribution or court record** linking an actor to a physical-threat narrative campaign.
- **A controlled study** showing that injected agentic amplification measurably shifts institutional response to an ambiguous incident.

**And one claim this study must withdraw.** An earlier draft described synthetic corroboration as the genuinely dangerous new capability whose strong form was merely "untested." That is wrong: **the source-count mechanism has been directly tested and the result is a null** (§5.3 — Foster et al., 2012: "repetition, not number of sources"). What is real is **trust-laundering** — repetition making a *publisher* credible, and that credibility generalising to its other claims. A defender should therefore treat synthetic corroboration as a **supply-side attack on the citation layer**, not as a belief multiplier, and should verify **institutional standing** rather than merely counting accounts.

**The genuinely untested item** is narrower and is worth naming for a follow-up: Pillai & Fazio (*JARMAC*, 2025), "Repeated by many versus repeated by one," tests precisely this, but its effect size is unavailable through the publisher. **Reading that paper would settle the question.**


**The single most useful of these is the temporal-decoupling test.** Organic cascades have a characteristic signature: interest rises after an event, peaks, and decays. An engineered campaign would in principle show a different curve — activity preceding the triggering event, or a secondary rise after organic interest has decayed. **This is measurable, falsifiable, and has not, on the evidence located, been systematically applied.** It is the most concrete recommendation this study makes for making the hypothesis testable (§10.3).

---

### 7.5 The single most important constraint finding in the study

Six independent evidence streams converged on the same conclusion, and the strongest element of it deserves to be stated on its own.

> **Autonomous agent-to-agent propaganda coordination has been demonstrated only in simulation — never observed in the wild.**

The demonstration is a 2026 USC study presented at the Web Conference (arXiv:2510.25003). It shows what agents *could* do under controlled conditions. **No real-world case has been observed.**

**This matters more than any other negative finding in this report, because it removes the one element that would make the hypothesis genuinely novel.** Everything else in H1 can be assembled from components that already exist and are already documented — active measures, reflexive control, availability cascades, ordinary coordinated inauthentic behaviour. **"AI agents as the amplification engine" is the new part, and it is precisely the part with zero field evidence.**

It joins three confirmed non-findings:

| Non-finding | Status |
|---|---|
| AI generating or orchestrating **physical-incident** amplification | **No case found** (two independent searches) |
| A **fully autonomous** real-world agentic influence campaign | **No case found** |
| A **campaign-level causal estimate** that influence operations changed opinion or vote | **None exists** in the literature |

**And the anti-hype evidence is not only from critics — it is from the platforms and labs themselves.** OpenAI's own threat reporting states that AI-enabled influence operations **"do not appear to have meaningfully increased their audience engagement or reach"**, and that it has **"not observed any cases of election-related influence operations attracting viral engagement or building sustained audiences."** Every election-related influence operation it disrupted scored at **Breakout Category 2**.

**The cleanest natural experiment located.** In February 2026 OpenAI reported a case in which a single prompt produced one post with **150,000 views** and another with **57**. The difference tracked **follower count, not AI**. That is the entire distribution-versus-generation finding in one comparison: **the same tool, the same prompt, a factor of 2,600 in outcome — and the variable was the audience the account already had.**

**A caution that undermines the detection layer too.** The bot-detection literature is a warning about false positives: **Botometer achieved an AUC of 0.85, not the widely cited 0.94**, and roughly **76% of accounts labelled "bots" were human** (Rauchfleisch & Kaiser, 2020); a separate analysis (Gallwitz & Kreil, 2022) found **"not a single social bot"** in its sample. **Coordination-detection tools systematically over-detect, which means attributing a cascade to automation is more likely to be wrong than right.**

**A governance framing problem worth flagging.** Coordinated inauthentic behaviour is now properly defined — Meta formalised it on 21 October 2019, with **false identities central** and explicitly **"agnostic of content or ideology"**. It was never retired as policy; only the *reporting brand* changed. But **CIB targets the fake-account *network* and explicitly excludes authentic organic amplification — which is this hypothesis's entire payload.** The canonical critique (Starbird, Arif & Wilson, 2019) is titled, in its §5.2, "The Trouble with 'Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior'", and documents **"authentic accounts (real people, sincerely participating)"** that CIB policy "would have difficulty addressing."

**The governance implication is direct: CIB is the wrong frame for this threat.** A mechanism that works through *genuine* citizens amplifying an ambiguous event cannot be governed by a policy designed to catch fake ones — and applying that frame risks the failure mode of mislabelling real public concern as coordinated manipulation.

**Where this leaves the hypothesis, in the words all six strands converged on independently:** *plausible, novel, and currently unobserved* — **not** an extrapolation from demonstrated effects. The evidence base is strong on vocabulary and mechanism and weak on measured impact. **The decisive gap is measurement, not understanding.**

## 8. The disconfirming case

The hypothesis must be tested against the strongest available counter-evidence. This section states it at full strength, because the honest assessment is that **the disconfirming case is strong for the "AI persuades the masses" version of the hypothesis and weak for the "AI reaches decision-makers" version.**

### 8.1 The benchmark measurement — reach is extraordinarily concentrated

The best-powered real-world study of a major influence operation is **Eady et al. (2023, *Nature Communications*)**, which linked survey data to respondents' Twitter feeds during the 2016 Russian Internet Research Agency campaign:

- **1% of users accounted for 70% of all exposures** (10% accounted for 98%).
- Exposure was concentrated among **strong Republicans** — i.e. among people whose priors already aligned.
- **Median weekly exposure in the final month was zero.**
- Respondents saw roughly **4 IRA posts per day**, against **106 from national news media** and 35 from politicians — ratios of about 25:1 and 9:1.
- Across issue positions, polarisation and vote choice, the study found **no evidence of a meaningful relationship**.

Aggregate exposure was still large (roughly 32 million Americans), which is why this is a study about *concentration* rather than about nothing happening. But the mechanism it describes is one of reinforcement among the already-aligned, not conversion.

### 8.2 Official bodies now say the same thing

This is no longer a revisionist position; it is the assessment of the agencies that track these operations:

- The **EEAS** states that most operations **"fail to generate meaningful organic engagement"** and that **"an overwhelming majority of the interaction metrics remains inauthentically inflated."**
- **VIGINUM** (France) describes the real impact of Storm-1516 on public debate as **"difficult to estimate"**, and describes another operation as having **"very low online visibility… despite numerous attempts at artificial amplification."**
- **ISD's** measurement of the Matryoshka campaign found **110,200 views with 405 likes and zero comments** — posted by an account with one follower. A textbook illustration of a metric that is real as a number and empty as an audience.

### 8.3 Vendor measurements converge

- **OpenAI** assessed that disrupted operations saw **"no significant audience increase."**
- **Anthropic** reports that the overwhelming majority of discovered content **"drew little or no authentic engagement."**
- **Google's Threat Analysis Group** found that **58% of DRAGONBRIDGE channels had zero subscribers, and 42% of its videos had zero views** — "practically no organic engagement from real viewers."
- Breakout-scale assessments place AI-enabled networks at low levels — **except where they ride existing state-media distribution.**

**But one caution should govern all of these figures, and it cuts against complacency.** **No audited authentic-audience figure exists for any of these networks.** Every number is platform-side or actor-side and is best read as an **upper bound**. Reach figures should never be treated as verified audiences — in either direction.

**And a retraction worth generalising.** OpenAI initially rated Russia's "Stop News" operation at **Breakout Scale Category 3** (October 2024) and **downgraded it to Category 2** (October 2025), because the apparent "information partnerships" that justified the higher rating turned out to be **fictional — spoofed via flaws on the partners' own websites**.

The methodological lesson applies directly to §7.3: **apparent institutional uptake is not evidence of institutional uptake**, and any claim of Category 3 or above that rests on it should be discounted until independently verified. In a domain where the effect and its camouflage are identical, **the appearance of having reached an institution is exactly the kind of evidence most easily manufactured** — and, as this case shows, most easily mistaken.


### 8.4 Operations are frequently self-defeating

- Prism News shut down having built **no durable audience**.
- A Kenyan astroturfing campaign **reached no real people**.
- Matryoshka's localisation failed so badly that **only 7 of 269 posts were in German** — much of the target audience could not read the product.
- Several documented campaigns were **easily identified as inauthentic by average viewers** (EEAS, on Storm-1516 and Operation Overload).

### 8.5 The "backfire effect" is a weak explanation — debunking generally works

An appealing argument is that these operations fail because they backfire. The evidence does not support it. **Wood & Porter (2019)** tested corrections across 36 issues in four experiments and found only **one** instance of backfire. The defensible reading is the simpler one: **most operations fail because they never reach anyone**, not because they reach people and are rejected. This matters for defence, because it means correction works — and it means the constraint on the attacker is distribution, not credulity.

### 8.6 The capability-level negative findings

Three capabilities the hypothesis leans on were **not** found in the record:

- **Automated rebuttal evasion** — no case (§6.7).
- **Microtargeting uplift** — access to individual-level data produced **no** persuasive gain in the best-powered study; the feared capability is the least supported (§6.8).
- **AI as the active ingredient in persuasion** — the effect **persisted when the same dialogue was attributed to a human expert**, implying the mechanism is personalised responsive dialogue, not AI as such (§6.8).
- **Adaptive narrative selection in the field** — Meta's own half-year threat reporting states that feedback-adapting agents **"have not yet been deployed extensively at scale by threat actors."** The apparatus exists; the adaptation is unproven (§6.3).
- **Machine tempo in narrative exploitation** — the fastest documented false narrative around an ambiguous event was **human: roughly two hours, Southport 2024**. Machine speed is proven at *production* (a three-minute site-sync cycle), not at *reaction*. As one analyst put it: **hours for the attacker, days for the regulator** — but the attacker in the fastest case was a person.

**And the strongest negative finding of all: there is no legal deterrent.**

**No one anywhere has been convicted of a political deepfake as of 25 September 2026.** The canonical case — the January 2024 New Hampshire robocall impersonating a candidate — produced a **$6 million FCC order entered by default and left unpaid**, an **acquittal on all 22 counts in June 2025**, and the collection of only **$1 million from a telecom carrier**. Meanwhile the statutory picture moved *against* defenders: California's AB 2839 was struck down, and the EU AI Act's Article 50 marking code is voluntary.

This matters because every other constraint identified in this study — detection, correction, platform enforcement, attribution — is technical and probabilistic. **Criminal liability is the only mechanism that changes an operator's cost calculus directly, and it is absent.** An activity that is cheap, scalable, deniable and unpunished has no external deterrent; it is constrained only by ineffectiveness.


### 8.7 The counterexamples that keep the disconfirming case from being decisive

Three cases show that amplification *can* produce mass and physical effects when a genuine, high-salience trigger exists — and they are the reason the verdict is "moderate" rather than "strong":

- **Romania, December 2024.** A presidential election was **annulled** following a coordinated TikTok campaign — an outcome with no precedent in EU electoral history. Whatever the merits of the annulment, a platform campaign produced a state-level constitutional consequence.
- **United Kingdom, Southport, August 2024.** Rumour following a real and highly salient murder produced **riots** and physical disorder. (Note honestly: the rumour was **domestic**, concerned a real suspect, and was not about an ambiguous threat — so it is an analogue, not a case, for this hypothesis.)
- **Tenet Media.** Roughly **US$10 million** of Russian money purchased ordinary right-leaning commentary aimed at the already-convinced, followed by a US Department of Justice indictment. The *content* had little effect; the **funding relationship** was the crime and the consequence.

**The common feature is a real, high-salience trigger.** None of these is a manufactured panic; each is genuine public anxiety being steered. That is a materially different and more plausible threat model than the one usually described.

### 8.8 How small the effects actually are

Supporting figures that constrain the scale of the threat:

- **Fake news constituted 0.15% of the average US daily media diet** (Allen et al., *Science Advances*, 2020).
- **1% of users accounted for 80% of fake-news exposures, and 0.1% for roughly 80% of shares** (Grinberg et al., *Science*, 2019).
- **49 field experiments produced zero average effect** from campaign contact (Kalla & Broockman, *APSR*, 2018).
- **Corrections work modestly**: r = .35 overall but only r = .15 for politics and r = .14 for real-world outcomes (Walter & Murphy, 2018); fact-checking d = 0.29.
- **The backfire effect is largely retracted**: across **52 experiments**, "no correction backfired" (Wood & Porter, 2019).

**One further finding deserves emphasis because it inverts the usual framing:** public alarm about AI manipulation is itself partly a **third-person effect** — people believe *others* are more susceptible than they are. That has a defensive consequence: a population told it is being manipulated may respond with cynicism toward *all* information, including accurate official information. **Over-warning has costs.**



### 8.9 Two regimes, two treatments — why the null literature cannot carry the weight placed on it

**All of the resilience evidence above comes from a specific regime: national elections**, with saturated information environments, strong partisan priors, abundant competing messaging, and audiences who have seen it all before.

**Ambiguous local physical events are a different regime.** Priors are weak because the event is novel; verification is slow because it requires technical assessment; competing information is scarce because nobody else is covering it; and the first coherent account fills a vacuum rather than competing in a marketplace. As the computational-propaganda strand puts it: **"None of the resilience literature tests that regime directly."**

**And there is a second qualification, which is sharper still and cuts the nulls down to size.** The benchmark null results — **Bail (2020)** and **Eady (2023)** — measure **passive exposure** to broadcast-style content in a feed. The strongest positive results — **Costello (2024)** and **Salvi (2025)** — measure **interactive dialogue**. These are different treatments, and the difference is not incidental:

- The largest meta-analysis in the field (Hölbling, Maier & Feuerriegel, 2025; 7 studies, N = 17,422) reports a pooled effect of **g = 0.02 (−0.048 to 0.093)** — essentially zero — **but with I² = 76%**, meaning the average conceals real variation. The **moderators are the finding**:
  - **Model generation:** GPT-3.x was *weaker* than GPT-4.x (**b = −0.236**).
  - **Interactivity:** **one-shot messages were substantially weaker than interactive dialogue (b = −0.494)**.

**The consequence is direct: the pooled null is an average across weaker models and non-interactive treatments.** Citing it as evidence that "AI persuasion does not work" is unsafe, because averaging across generations and modalities **systematically understates the conversational agents this study is about**.

**So the correct boundary is not between *AI* and *no AI* — it is between *broadcast* and *conversation*:**

> The null literature is strong evidence that **broadcast-style AI content does not move populations**. It is **not** evidence that **conversational agents cannot move individuals**, because that treatment has rarely been tested at scale and is diluted in the pooled results.

This does not overturn the verdict. There is still **no field experiment showing AI persuasion changing real-world behaviour at population scale**, and **microtargeting is null across three high-powered studies**. But it narrows the safe ground considerably, and it is the strongest surviving argument for the hypothesis.

A further caution now applies with force: **the strongest positive result in this literature has been retracted by its own authors' correction.** Salvi et al.'s personalisation effect was corrected on 3 September 2026 to **+48.7% (p = 0.068, not significant)** — effectively retiring the headline. Matching that, the largest meta-analysis finds **no significant LLM-versus-human persuasion difference at all (g = 0.02, p = .530, N = 17,422)**. *(An earlier draft of this report cited the uncorrected figure; see §6.8.)*

**The hardest null in the corpus, and it is a strong one.** Allcott et al. (*Nature Human Behaviour*, 2026; **over 60,000 users**) tested removing **Facebook and Instagram political advertising entirely** — a far larger intervention than any covert campaign — and found **no detectable effect** on political knowledge, polarisation, turnout or participation. Coppock et al. separately estimate the **persuasion half-life at roughly four days**.

**The implication is uncomfortable for the entire influence-operations field, not just for AI:** if eliminating *all* paid political advertising on two major platforms moves nothing measurable, then the expected effect of a covert network reaching a few thousand people is, on priors, very close to zero. **That is the strongest quantitative statement available against the hypothesis, and it does not depend on AI at all.**


### 8.10 Verdict on the disconfirming case

| Version of the hypothesis | Disconfirming case | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "AI *broadcast content* can persuade mass publics at scale" | **Strong** — multiple null and near-null results, official assessments, concentration data | **Not supported** |
| "AI *conversational agents* can move individuals" | **Weak** — the nulls measure passive exposure; the interactivity moderator is large (b = −0.49) and under-tested | **Open, and under-tested** |
| "AI has industrialised the production of apparent corroboration" | **Weak** — this is demonstrated | **Supported** |
| "AI amplification can reach journalists, officials and institutions" | **Weak** — indirect effects of this kind *are* documented (§9.2) | **Plausible, underexamined** |
| "AI can drive an ambiguous physical-threat cascade" | **Not tested either way** — no case found, and the regime is untested | **Open** |

## 9. The amplifier is not the agent

### 9.1 Restating the structural finding

The single most consequential observation in this study is structural rather than evidentiary:

> **In the causal chain, the stages that generate material disruption are institutional, and they respond to perceived rather than verified threat. An agent that supplies only ambiguity is not competing with the state's information apparatus — it is feeding it.**

This is why the reflexive category (§4.2, category 5) carries the analytical weight. The adversary does not need to persuade anyone, because the target's own decision rules will convert uncertainty into cost: runways close, fighters scramble, inquiries open, legislation is drafted, budgets are committed. §5.4 documents all of this occurring repeatedly **with no adversary identified at all**.

### 9.2 But the effect still has to reach someone — and the evidence says who

Here the disconfirming evidence and the hypothesis meet productively. The computational-propaganda strand identifies **three documented pathways by which influence operations produce real effects without persuading anyone**:

1. **Elite and media agenda penetration.** Content is designed to be picked up by journalists and politicians; headline coverage, official denials and parliamentary questions are outcomes that **do not require mass belief**.
2. **Harassment and chilling.** The Metric Media/Oversight Project ecosystem produced faculty sanctions and terminations at US universities — around forty professors sanctioned after a single 2025 "watchlist" episode. The mechanism targets named individuals, not electorates.
3. **Workload and cost imposition.** Fact-checkers are deliberately flooded; one operation's method explicitly involved contacting fact-checkers to demand they investigate fabricated material.

**Pathway 1 is the crucial one for this study, because the physical-threat cascade runs through exactly that layer.** The chain in §4.1 is: citizen reports → **media attention** → citizen vigilance → **institutional response**. Stages 3 and 6 are populated by a few thousand people — reporters, editors, police commanders, airport operators, regulators, ministers — not by millions.

**This is the strongest version of the hypothesis, and it inverts the usual framing.** The target is not the public; the public is the *instrument*. The target is the small, busy, credibility-constrained set of people who must decide something by Friday about an incident nobody can explain. Reaching them is a much smaller distribution problem than reaching a population — which addresses the 524× distribution constraint in §6.9 — but they are also the most sceptical and best-resourced audience available.

### 9.3 The reflexivity trap, stated precisely

The trap is that every element of a rational defensive response is also an amplification. This is worth laying out because it is the mechanism the defender must design around:

| Rational defensive action | Amplification effect |
|---|---|
| Close the airport / suspend service | Publicly confirms the threat is serious |
| Deploy forces or scramble aircraft | Signals institutional alarm; visible to media |
| Issue a public alert | Raises salience, drives more reports |
| Open an investigation | Creates an official record that the event "exists" |
| Refuse to comment while investigating | Vacuum fills with speculation — the rumour driver |
| Announce new counter-measures or procurement | Validates the threat retrospectively |
| Attribute to a hostile state | Raises stakes, politically costly to walk back if wrong |
| Correct the record | Can spread the claim further (the "oxygen of publicity" problem) |

**Every row is defensible in isolation, and several are unavoidable.** That is precisely what makes the mechanism robust: it does not require the defender to make a mistake. It requires only that the defender behave rationally under uncertainty — which is the one thing a defender is guaranteed to do.

The trap has a second edge. **Not responding is also costly**: the same scepticism that resists manipulation also risks ignoring a real threat, and the failure mode of under-reaction (a genuine attack that was dismissed) is politically and morally far worse than the failure mode of over-reaction. **Any defensive doctrine that simply tells institutions to be less responsive is not implementable**, and this study does not propose it. The implementable version is in §11 — respond, but respond in ways that do not manufacture confirmation.

### 9.4 Reassessing "small input, large output"

The gain is real but the direction of causation is frequently misread. In the documented cases, the *output* was not produced by an adversary's skill; it was produced by the target's own institutional grammar operating on an unresolvable input. That has an uncomfortable implication:

**The vulnerability is not that societies are gullible. It is that they are correctly designed.** A society that closes a runway when it cannot rule out a threat, that investigates reports it cannot verify, and that legislates against a risk it cannot measure, is behaving as it should. The exploitable property is not credulity — it is **the rational asymmetry between the cost of precaution and the cost of catastrophe.**

### 9.5 The asymmetry that defines the real threat: institutions, not populations

Independent analysis of the risk-psychology literature converges on a sharper formulation than this study initially had, and it is worth stating as a headline finding:

> **Institutional cascades are engineerable even where public cascades are not. Elites and institutions are far more manipulable through ambiguity than populations are.**

The supporting evidence is specific. Measured attempts to move populations through covert influence come back at or near zero: **Eady et al.** find effects of roughly **0.05–0.06 standard deviations** with vote effects inside **±1 percentage point**; **Kalla & Broockman's** meta-analysis of **49 field experiments** finds **zero average persuasion**. Studies of shock advertising find attention without persuasion. Even the canonical *War of the Worlds* panic is substantially a media myth.

Meanwhile the **Havana syndrome** case shows what happens at the institutional layer: a decade of unresolved ambiguity — with contested evidence for any mass public panic — produced **task forces, legislation, compensation, an intelligence-community reorganisation and continuing procurement**, and it remained live in September 2026. The public did not panic. The institutions reorganised.

The mechanisms that make *population* amplification uncontrollable are held by actors the would-be engineer does not command: journalists competing for attention, partisans defending identity, well-meaning citizens doing crisis sense-making (Starbird's "digital vigilantes"), and platforms optimising engagement rather than message. **None of those intermediaries stands between an ambiguity and an institution in the same way.** An official does not need to be persuaded by a crowd; they need to be unable to rule something out.

**The practical formulation: emergent, with a controllable foothold.** Agents can plausibly influence *whether an ambiguity is noticed and sustained by institutions*. They cannot reliably determine *what a population does with it*.

### 9.6 What determines whether an ambiguity survives — six institutional variables

Cross-case analysis of six domains (drones, health scares, military alerts, balloons, UAP, industrial incidents) converges on a finding that reframes the whole study:

> **Durability is predicted by institutional variables, not by report volume or media attention.**

Six predictors, in descending order of explanatory power:

1. **An existing institutional owner for a *harm* question.** Havana syndrome had diplomats and intelligence officers — a victim class with institutional sponsors — and produced nine investigations, compensation legislation and a permanent Pentagon task force. 5G mast arson, by contrast, produced nothing durable, because **no institution owned mast safety as a health question**.

   **The sharpest evidence for this variable is an inversion.** **H5N1** was the *higher*-severity threat — a global case fatality rate of around **48%** (973 cases, 470 deaths) — and it produced durable **agricultural** machinery but **no public-health emergency**. **Mpox clade Ib** was the *lower*-severity event (case fatality roughly **0.2–0.5%**) and it produced a **WHO public health emergency declaration, Africa CDC's first-ever continental emergency, and commitments exceeding $1bn and 5 million doses**. **Severity did not determine durability. Institutional ownership did** — WHO and Africa CDC both had the mandate and the incentive to keep the category alive.

   Two corollaries are worth carrying. First, **a continental emergency can outlast a global one**: Africa CDC held its alert until 22 January 2026, roughly **four and a half months beyond** the end of WHO's global declaration. Second, **the machinery is often less solid than its existence implies**: Africa CDC's $245m request was about **10% funded**, and only around **724,000 of 2.9 million** doses were administered. **Institutions that persist are not necessarily institutions that work** — and an adversary seeking durable disruption needs the former, not the latter. Note also that ECDC rated EU/EEA risk "low" and **no Western border or travel measures were imposed**: the global framing outran the epidemiology without producing Western restrictions, which is what a *successful* ambiguity looks like when the institutional machinery is elsewhere.
2. **Availability of a decisive evidentiary test.** The 2023 balloon episode **collapsed within about ten days** once debris was recovered and identified as hobbyist or commercial. An H5N1 milk scare collapsed within a month when zero of sixty PCR-positive samples contained viable virus. Havana and UAP **cannot close, because nothing is recoverable** — there is no test that would end them.

   **The counter-intuitive corollary: a test that is actually run and returns a definitive answer kills the cascade, even when the underlying event is genuinely alarming.** The 2006 polonium poisoning of Alexander Litvinenko was a *validated* radiological assassination — sixty-plus scenes examined, 753 people urine-tested, 139 above the reporting level, and 664 people across 52 countries flagged for follow-up. It produced **no verified copycat wave and no sustained reporting cascade**. Meanwhile unvalidated and unresolvable incidents — Havana, UAP, the drone waves — persist for years. **Definitive bad news closes; ambiguous news does not.** That is the opposite of what threat intuition predicts, and it is the strongest argument for running the test and publishing the result even when the result is bad.

   **But there are two distinct routes to permanent non-closure, and they require different defences.** For **Havana and UAP, the decisive test is *unavailable*** — there is no specimen, no debris, nothing to analyse. For **COVID origins, a result exists and is *contested***: the ODNI position is that "all agencies continue to assess that both a natural and laboratory-associated origin remain plausible hypotheses", with agencies split across both conclusions and the CIA's lab-lean reached at low confidence using no new intelligence, while the WHO's SAGO panel still finds the weight of evidence suggests zoonosis. **A contested finding does not close ambiguity; it institutionalises it**, because each side can cite official expertise. An adversary seeking durable ambiguity therefore has two designs available: **remove the evidence**, or **ensure the evidence is disputed**.
3. **A named mechanism that cannot be refuted.** "Sonic weapon", "directed energy", "infrasound", "Disease X". A named mechanism converts an unexplained observation into a researchable hypothesis, and a researchable hypothesis generates institutions.
4. **An interested constituency for non-closure.** Havana is the proof: five of seven agencies assessed adversary involvement as "very unlikely", the NIH failed to replicate the brain-injury findings — and in 2026 the Pentagon rebranded its team *towards* directed energy while the outgoing DNI **rescinded the sceptical assessments**. The evidence moved one way; the institution moved the other.
5. **Whether the remedy is *material* or *legal*.** This is the sharpest and least obvious predictor, and it explains two Tylenol cases moving in opposite directions. The **1982** Tylenol poisonings had an unknown mechanism and a **material** fix — tamper-resistant packaging — which produced permanent regulation despite **no one ever being charged**. The **2025–26** acetaminophen–autism claim was *refuted*, and its remedy was **legal** (litigation): over 500 suits were dismissed in December 2024, then **revived by the Second Circuit on 13 July 2026**, with rehearing sought on 11 September 2026 — and the FDA label **still has not changed**. **A material remedy converts ambiguity into durable institutions; a legal remedy keeps it contested and alive.** For an adversary, this is an actionable design insight: ambiguity that can only be resolved by litigation and labelling does not close.
6. **Aggregation into a single category.** Havana absorbed Berlin, Vienna, Geneva, Bogotá, Guangzhou and Washington into **one 1,000+ case count**. The three 2023 balloon objects **fragmented** and were each resolved separately.

**The counterexample that disciplines this: New Jersey.** It had a real physical substrate, 5,000+ reports, maximum political attention, and four federal agencies engaged — and it still **burned out in about six weeks with no new agency, no compensation regime and no statute.** It lacked a named victim class, its reports were individually resolvable, and the FBI owned a *threat* question rather than a *harm* question: once that question was answered, the institution stopped.

**And a quantitative demonstration that ambiguity alone is *insufficient*.** The 5G/COVID conspiracy is the cleanest natural test in the entire dataset, because it had every input the hypothesis requires except one. There was a **real exposure** (a novel pandemic), an **unresolved official classification** (IARC Group 2B, which remains unchanged to this day), and genuine public anxiety. Yet the measured uptake was negligible: only about **0.05% of COVID-related tweets mentioned 5G**, and only **1.8% of a UK survey** agreed with the claim completely. Growth was platform-led, with traditional media playing essentially no role.

**The missing ingredient was an institution with a reason to act.** No agency owned "is 5G safe as a health question", so no audit, task force or compensation scheme followed, and the belief had nowhere to institutionalise. **This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the study that the substrate is necessary but not sufficient** — and it is why §9.6 leads with institutional ownership rather than with the ambiguity itself.

**This yields the narrowest credible agentic threat in the study.** An agent does not need to generate more reports — reports are abundant and cheap and mostly ignored. It needs to supply two specific narrative inputs:

- **a named mechanism** (which makes the phenomenon researchable), and
- **a victim class** (which gives an institution something to own).

**Both are text-generation problems. The conversion step — an audit, a compensation scheme, a task force, a statute — remains institutional, and cannot be automated.** That is the precise boundary of the agentic contribution, and it is a much better statement of the threat than "AI could cause panic."

**A scale inversion worth noting.** The first HAVANA Act disbursements totalled only around **$3 million** — while the durable institutional machinery built around the phenomenon costs far more to maintain. **The direct harm was small; the institutional cost was large.** For an adversary seeking to impose cost, the machinery is the target, not the victims.



### 9.7 The most plausible objective is not panic — it is sustained ambiguity

This reframing follows from §9.6 and is, in the author's assessment, the second most useful conclusion of the study.

If population panic is not reliably engineerable, then a rational adversary would not pursue it. The cheaper and more durable objective is to **maintain low-grade, open-ended ambiguity** — to keep the null hypothesis ("we cannot rule out a deliberate attack") permanently alive. The returns from that are:

- **Eroded institutional trust** — every unresolved incident is a small withdrawal.
- **Exhausted investigative capacity** — every report must be triaged, at real cost.
- **Shifted burden of proof** — agencies must disprove hostile action rather than have it demonstrated.
- **Sustained precautionary expenditure** — closures, deployments and procurement justified by what cannot be excluded.
- **Political pressure** — the appearance of a government that cannot protect its own infrastructure.

Two findings support the durability of this objective. First, **sustained low-grade ambiguity beats acute panic induction** precisely because it is unremarkable: it does not generate the backlash that a failed panic does, and it is never falsified because it never makes a specific claim. Second — and more striking — **once actors begin attributing an amplification dynamic to each other, perceived risk can polarise by orders of magnitude and then fail to decay**, leaving a durable residue of anxiety, distrust and depressed economic activity. Even with no adversary at all, the system can lock into a state that does not self-correct.

**This is a less dramatic threat than the one usually described, and a more difficult one.** A panic is an event: it peaks, it is investigated, it is often retracted, and it ends. A permanently unresolved ambiguity is a *condition*. You cannot fact-check it, because it does not assert anything false. You cannot wait it out, because it does not terminate. And you cannot ignore it, because occasionally it will be a real attack — which is exactly what makes it credible.

## 10. Detection and indicators

### 10.1 What can and cannot be detected

Given §7.3 — that a well-executed amplification is indistinguishable from organic concern — detection must target **means**, not **effects**. Four families of evidence are realistically available:

| Family | What it shows | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| **Network and behavioural analysis** | Accounts acting in concert: correlated timing, overlapping infrastructure, shared generation artefacts, anomalous activity windows | Moderate — erodes as automation improves, but has not disappeared |
| **Provenance and content forensics** | Whether material is synthetic or recycled; whether apparently independent sources share an origin | Improving (content credentials, watermarking), but defeated by re-encoding and by text-only material |
| **Temporal and structural anomaly** | Whether a narrative's activity curve diverges from the organic pattern of the underlying event | **Highest value, least used** — see §10.2 |
| **Tradecraft and infrastructure attribution** | Who is running it: hosting, tooling, payment, operators | High when available; slow and labour-intensive |

### 10.2 The temporal-decoupling test — the most useful unexploited method

Organic cascades have a signature: interest **rises after** a triggering event, peaks, and decays with a half-life measured in hours. An engineered campaign would in principle differ in one of three measurable ways:

1. **Anticipation** — activity *preceding* the triggering event, indicating foreknowledge or a manufactured substrate.
2. **Decoupling** — synthetic activity continuing after organic interest has decayed, or rising independently of any new stimulus.
3. **Second-wave structure** — a distinct secondary rise inconsistent with the event's own news cycle, particularly one timed to an institutional decision point.

**This test is falsifiable, cheap, and — on the evidence located — has not been systematically applied to the physical-ambiguity domain.** It is the single most concrete recommendation this study makes for converting an unfalsifiable hypothesis into a testable one. It also has the virtue of being **diagnostic rather than accusatory**: it identifies anomalies that warrant investigation without requiring a prior attribution.

### 10.3 Indicator framework

**Tier 1 — indicators that an incident is being amplified (not necessarily deliberately).**
- Reporting volume out of proportion to any verified physical event.
- Media coverage that treats uncertainty as evidence of concealment rather than absence of information.
- Official statements hedged in ways that sustain rather than resolve ambiguity.
- A rapid rise in *first-time* reports from people with no prior involvement in the domain.
- Narrative persistence beyond the normal news half-life.

**Tier 2 — indicators of deliberate amplification.**
- Multiple apparently independent accounts sharing generation artefacts, infrastructure or timing patterns.
- Narratives appearing *before* any physical trigger.
- A second wave of activity timed to an institutional decision point (a vote, a procurement, a summit).
- Systematic independence-failure: sources that present as diverse but trace to a common origin.
- Activity that continues after the underlying claim has been publicly knocked down.

**Tier 3 — indicators that the reflexive loop is closing.**
- Institutional responses cited by subsequent reporting as evidence for the original claim.
- Escalating precaution without escalating evidence.
- Political pressure calibrated to perceived rather than demonstrated risk.
- Mutual attribution between institutions ("the agency thinks..."), which the evidence suggests can lock in and fail to decay.

### 10.4 What detection realistically achieves

Two measured figures should temper expectations about detection-based defence:

- **Correction works when seen.** Platform community notes reduced reposts of corrected content by **61.2%** — but only **14.9% system-wide**, because notes arrived at a median lag of **62.9 hours** against a content half-life of **6.25 hours**.
- **The lag is the problem, not the mechanism.** The intervention is effective; it is simply too slow by an order of magnitude.

**The practical implication is that speed dominates accuracy in the first hours.** A fast, specific, honest "here is what we do not yet know" outperforms a slow, comprehensive correction — a conclusion that aligns with the rumour-theory finding that cascades are driven by ambiguity *in an informational vacuum* and terminate only when an authoritative source resolves them — not because the crowd corrects itself.

### 10.5 The indicator set worth actually building: monitor the response, not the reports

Two findings make the conventional indicator approach close to useless, and suggest a better one.

**First, report counts measure the observer, not the threat.** The FAA's own official UAS sighting series has **no disposition field**, and — decisively — it shows **no surge during the New Jersey panic**: November 2024 recorded 103 reports, the *lowest* of that winter. A cascade that generated 5,000 tips to the FBI, a federal interagency response and a legislative push produced **no signal at all** in the official aviation data. Similarly, the UK's Ministry of Defence attributes part of its own rising incident count to **better detection** — a count that measures the observer.

**Second, no institution anywhere publishes a confirmation rate.** Every headline national figure — 5,000 in the US, 558 in Belgium, 340 in the UK, thousands in Germany — is a **report count**. The single most decision-relevant number in the entire domain is systematically absent.

**Therefore the indicator set worth building is an *institutional-response* set.** These are published, countable, hard to conceal, and they measure the thing that actually imposes cost:

| Indicator | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Airport or transport suspensions on *unconfirmed* sightings** | Directly measures the ambiguity-to-cost conversion |
| **Emergency counter-UAS authorisations or accelerated procurement** | Shows the response outrunning the evidence |
| **New shoot-down or interdiction authorities granted** | A durable, legislative consequence |
| **Attributions issued ahead of technical confirmation** | Measures the confidence-evidence gap |
| **Restrictions imposed without a disposition in any case** | Distinguishes precaution from proof |
| **Audit or review findings on counter-drone spending** | **Currently nonexistent — no audit office has audited this spending, and no counter-UAS false-positive or effectiveness rate exists anywhere** |

**The virtue of this set is that it is self-critical.** It does not ask "is the threat growing?" — a question the available data cannot answer — but **"is our response growing faster than our evidence?"** That is answerable, attributable to our own records, and it is exactly the reflexivity trap in measurable form.

**One caution on detection, and it is important.** DFRLab examined **1,651 Storm-1516 amplifier accounts and found "no clear indicators of coordinated inauthentic behavior."** **Amplification overlap is not proof of coordination.** Genuine organic users frequently amplify the same content, so a network-detection method that treats shared amplification as evidence will generate false positives against the public. **No institution should attribute a cascade to coordination on that basis alone.**

## 11. Defensive implications

The governing principle, which follows directly from §9:

> **The defensive objective is not to prevent people from being frightened. It is to prevent the institution's own response from becoming the evidence.**

This is achievable, and it does not require anyone to be less careful about real threats.

### 11.1 Attribution discipline

The strongest available evidence is that **over-attribution is itself a vulnerability**. The Dutch AIVD's caution (a sighted drone "is not always a threat"; suspected Russian involvement "often cannot be confirmed") and Germany's BfV warning against *"Überattribution"* and overestimating Russian capability are the models. Three practical rules:

- **Attribute tradecraft, not outcomes.** Cascades are usually organic; only network evidence justifies an attribution claim (§7.3).
- **Reserve "hybrid attack" for cases with evidence.** Its repeated use on unverified sightings has already produced retractions, and each retraction costs credibility for the cases that are real.
- **Prefer "we do not know" to a hypothesis you may have to withdraw.** The Danish Justice Minister's "the adversary does not reveal itself" is unfalsifiable framing that survives any evidential outcome — and therefore tells the public nothing.

### 11.2 Communicating uncertainty without feeding fear

The rumour literature identifies the mechanism precisely: **ambiguity plus informational vacuum**. But this study's initial framing of the defence was wrong in one important respect, and the correction changes the advice.

**The amplifier is *inconsistency*, not uncertainty.** The instinctive conclusion — "admitting uncertainty will be exploited, so project confidence" — is contradicted by the evidence:

- Uncertainty disclosure does **not carry a measurable trust penalty**. A field experiment (van der Bles et al., *PNAS*, 2020; N = 5,780) found no such cost, and the World Health Organization issues uncertainty disclosure as a **strong recommendation on moderate-quality evidence**.
- What *does* amplify is **conflict between sources** — visible disagreement among authorities, or between authorities and experts (Gustafson & Rice, 2020).
- Accordingly, **the CDC's crisis-communication guidance explicitly retires the old "first 24 hours" rule**, and states that **"an over-reassured public isn't the goal."**

**Uncertainty is defensible; contradiction is not.** The practical rules that follow:

- **Say what is not known, in specific terms, early.** "We have found no evidence of a hostile drone and we are continuing to look" is better than silence, and better than confidence that may have to be withdrawn.
- **Coordinate before speaking, and speak with one voice.** Since inconsistency is the amplifier, an inter-agency disagreement is worse than a shared admission of ignorance.
- **Publish the *process* statistic, not just the conclusion.** The most effective documented move is procedural transparency: of 5,000 reports, roughly 100 generated leads, and the sightings resolved to aircraft, helicopters, lawful drones and stars. **This gives the public the disposition of the inquiry rather than asking them to trust a verdict.**
- **Resolve the base rate publicly.** Over a million lawfully registered drones in the US, thousands airborne at any moment. **Denominator information is the cheapest antidote to an availability cascade**, because it restores probability judgement where salience has displaced it.
- **Use an explicit "we do not know yet" script rather than improvisation.** The Dutch NCTV's formulation is the only published example located: **"informed waiting"**, the principle that **"process information is also information"**, and the guidance **not to rebut every disinformation claim**. This is the mature-practice template, and it is directly transferable.
- **Accept that completeness takes time.** Give the interim answer rather than waiting for the final one.


### 11.3 Incident verification and anomaly triage

The cases show that the real failure is not malice detection — it is **classification under workload**. Tower 22's air-defence cell dismissed a radar track as "birds or trash" while distracted; Vilnius scrambled a fighter at birds; Belgium's minister published a helicopter. Triage practice should therefore:

- **Fuse sensors rather than trust single reports.** The Vilnius and Polish bird cases were both resolved by *visual identification* after radar triggered a response — the verification layer worked, it was simply slower than the response layer.
- **Institutionalise the mundane explanation.** Require a documented assessment of non-hostile causes (aircraft, birds, celestial objects, lawful drones, hobbyist activity) before an incident escalates.
- **Track the report-to-verification ratio.** A rising ratio of reports to confirmed events is itself an indicator — the Netherlands' and Belgium's experience suggests the ratio moves with detection availability rather than with the threat.
- **Preserve evidence discipline.** In the Copenhagen case, the *absence* of hostile-drone detection at the airport was discovered only months later. Knowing what your sensors cannot see is part of the incident record.

### 11.4 Not amplifying — resolving the dilemma

The "oxygen of publicity" problem is real, and the honest position is that **neither publishing nor suppressing is uniformly correct**. The defensible decision rules are:

- **Publish when the public can act on it.** A specific, actionable warning is worth the amplification cost.
- **Suppress specifics, not existence.** Do not name a vulnerability, a location or a method; do confirm that an event is under investigation.
- **Never repeat a claim in order to deny it without also stating the correction in the same breath.** The correction should be the headline, not the trailer.
- **Do not let an attribution claim be the story.** Attribution disputes extend narrative life; the correction should be about the *event*, not about who did it.
- **Coordinate the message.** Visible inter-agency disagreement was a feature of several cascades, and it is itself an amplifier. One voice, even saying "we don't know yet".

### 11.5 Detection of synthetic corroboration

Practical, publicly documented approaches — none of which should be treated as reliable alone:

- **Independence testing.** Ask whether apparently separate sources share origin: identical phrasings, shared hosting, coordinated timing, common generation artefacts.
- **Provenance tooling.** Content Credentials and C2PA-style signing help where adopted, but re-encoding breaks them and none of it applies to text.
- **Behavioural signals.** Account age, posting rhythm, language-consistency errors, and whether an account has ever posted about anything else.
- **Human verification.** The cases in §5.1b were resolved by a person looking through a camera or at a video — not by an algorithm.

**Note the asymmetry the evidence supports**: detection of synthetic *personas* is feasible and improving; detection of synthetic *consensus* — deciding whether twenty accounts are one actor — is the harder problem and the one the defender actually needs solved.

### 11.6 Public reporting protocols

Reporting systems are double-edged: they generate the vigilance the chain depends on, and they generate the false positives that sustain it.

- **Ask for specific, falsifiable information** rather than general concern. The Dutch NCTV's guidance — date, time, route, estimated altitude, colour, payload, distance to buildings, photographs — is a good model because it captures *discriminating* detail.
- **Tell people what will not be investigated, and why.** Unbounded reporting without triage capacity produces exactly the workload-imposition effect documented in §9.2.
- **Set expectations about response volume.** Publicly explaining that most reports resolve to aircraft, birds or lawful drones reduces the sense that a non-response is a cover-up.
- **Do not ask for reports you cannot process.** A reporting channel that produces no visible outcome corrodes trust more than no channel.

### 11.7 The reflexivity trap: decision rules that do not self-validate

This is the hardest and most important area, and it follows from §9.3. Every rational defensive action doubles as amplification. There is no way to eliminate the trade-off, but there are design choices that reduce the feedback:

- **Separate *responding* from *announcing*.** A runway closure does not have to be framed as a security incident. The action can be taken while the public communication remains specific about what triggered it.
- **Pre-commit to thresholds.** Deciding in advance what level of evidence justifies what level of response makes the decision rule less vulnerable to salience and to blame-avoidance incentives.
- **Make proportionate response politically safe.** If an official's only safe option is over-reaction, the system will over-react. Explicit after-the-fact endorsement of a *proportionate* non-response is the cheapest available intervention.
- **Avoid mutual attribution spirals.** Because attributional dynamics can polarise and fail to decay, authorities should resist characterising each other's assessments as manipulation without evidence.
- **Budget for ambiguity as a standing condition.** Sustained low-grade ambiguity is the plausible adversary objective (§9.6), so the defensive posture should be one that can be maintained indefinitely without escalation — not a crisis mode that must either resolve or intensify.

### 11.8 Prebunking and inoculation — real, small, and decaying

The evidence is genuinely favourable but should not be overstated. Two different magnitudes need separating:

- **Lab-scale:** a **light-touch AI-literacy warning** — a brief note that an LLM may be prompted to persuade and may present information selectively — reduced belief change by roughly **half (−48.1%, 95% CI −59.5% to −36.8%)**, without significantly reducing general trust in AI.
- **Population-scale:** the largest real campaigns show much smaller effects. A twelve-country EU prebunking campaign reached **over 120 million** YouTube users with effect sizes around **ds ≈ 0.08–0.38**; a large Instagram field experiment (N = 375,597) produced about **+21 percentage points** of improvement persisting five months; a booster study (N = 11,759) shows effects **decaying over roughly one month** without reinforcement.

**The honest formulation:** prebunking is a real, cheap, repeatable measure with small per-exposure effects and meaningful decay. It is worth doing continuously as background resilience — **not** as a campaign that can be run once and won.

**And the at-scale evidence is now decisively negative, which lowers its ranking as a defensive priority.** A large platform-scale trial across **967,640 X users** found **"no meaningful changes in posting or retweeting."** The widely cited 2022 *Science Advances* field experiment **missed its own preregistered smallest effect size of interest**. Game-based inoculation **decays within roughly two weeks**. And where the two are compared directly, **debunking outperforms prebunking** — a conclusion the European Commission's own research service reaches, noting that "debunking is slightly more effective."

**So prebunking belongs in the portfolio, but not at the top of it.** The lab-scale −48% figure measures a controlled intervention on motivated participants; the population-scale results measure what happens in the wild, and they are close to null. **The honest ranking of defences in this study puts communication discipline (§11.2) and decision discipline (§11.7, §10.5) above inoculation.**

**A caution that cuts the other way.** Public alarm about AI manipulation is itself partly a **third-person effect**: people believe others are more susceptible than they are. Over-warning can therefore produce generalised cynicism toward *all* information — including accurate official information — which is precisely the opposite of what a defender facing an informational vacuum needs (§11.2).

### 11.9 What the evidence says *not* to do

- **Do not treat this as a content-moderation problem.** The binding constraint is distribution and identity, not text (§6.9). Moderating content at scale is expensive and endless; raising the cost of a credible account is neither.
- **Do not build for mass persuasion.** The mass-persuasion threat is the least-supported version of the hypothesis (§8).
- **Do not over-securitise the airspace.** The natural experiment in §5.1c is a warning: closing airspace and issuing alerts is itself an amplifier, and the European response produced at least one near-catastrophic friendly-fire incident. Restricting civil airspace imposes real costs on the legitimate drone economy and on civil liberties, and the threat model does not obviously justify it.
- **Do not assume that attribution will resolve the ambiguity.** In the cases examined, it frequently did not — and in several, the official position on what happened remains genuinely unknown.
- **Do not rely on mandatory AI-content labelling.** The measured effect is small and does not do the work: an "AI-generated" label shifted belief by **2.66 percentage points**, against **9.33 points** for a "False" label, and **labelling did not reduce persuasion at all** (N = 1,601). Labelling addresses provenance; the harm here is not about provenance.

### 11.10 The enforcement asymmetry — and why the opening is carriage, not content

One policy finding from this study is unusually concrete, and it follows from a clean comparison.

**Enforcement works where the speech is unprotected, and stalls where it is political.**

- **Where content is not politically protected, regulation is producing real consequences.** Non-consensual intimate imagery rules are viewpoint-neutral, and they now yield convictions, court orders and live platform duties — including a **conviction under Ohio's TAKE IT DOWN Act in April 2026** and a **Dutch court order against X/Grok in March 2026**.
- **Where the content is political, the same machinery stalls.** Political-deepfake regulation must adjudicate election speech, and it has produced **acquittals, uncollected fines, defied judgments and struck-down statutes** (§8.6).

**The actionable conclusion: the defensible opening is *carriage*, not *content*.** Carrier-level liability and call-integrity mechanisms are the only interventions in this domain with a **demonstrated financial consequence anywhere**:

- The **only money actually collected** in the New Hampshire robocall affair was **$1 million from the carrier, Lingo Telecom** — not from the operator, not from the platform.
- **Caller-ID and STIR/SHAKEN integrity** operate at the carriage layer, are viewpoint-neutral, and therefore avoid the First Amendment and Article 10 problems that defeat content rules.

**Why this matters for the physical-ambiguity case specifically.** A synthetic-voice or SMS-based scare — the most plausible agentic vector against responders (§12.3) — travels over carriage infrastructure. **Carriage-level integrity is the one lever that is both legally durable and technically upstream of the harm**, and it is currently the least discussed. Anyone proposing content moderation, labelling or platform takedown as the primary defence should note that the only mechanism to have extracted money from this ecosystem so far operates one layer below all of them.

## 12. Conclusions — the four questions

### 12.1 What is already established?

**The mechanism is real, pre-agentic, and documented in full — including its most dangerous failure mode.**

- Ambiguous physical events produce **institutional and social disruption grossly disproportionate to any verified physical event**, entirely without an adversary. The New Jersey episode (5,000+ reports, an interagency response, a legislative push, and "nothing anomalous") is the cleanest case; Copenhagen, Madrid, Vilnius, Norway and Belgium repeat the pattern, and in several the physical event was never evidenced at all.
- **The causal chain closes at the institutional stage.** Airports close, fighters scramble, readiness rises, legislation is drafted — in response to perceived rather than verified threat.
- **Societies self-amplify.** Media reporting becomes a source of new observations; public vigilance generates more reports; the reports generate more response. This is documented in the participants' own words.
- **The most dangerous outcome documented so far is not a panic but friendly fire.** Forty-three rounds fired at what was probably a passenger aircraft, in a cascade with no adversary.
- **The central negative finding stands.** No case was found of a deliberate AI-enabled amplification of a drone-sighting wave. Europe had real hostile flights, opportunistic propaganda *riding* the wave, and overwhelmingly organic misidentification amplified by media feedback. It did not have a manufactured panic.

### 12.2 What becomes newly possible because of agentic AI?

**A narrow, real, and specific set of changes — all of them on the production side, and one of them genuinely qualitative.**

- **Production is effectively free; distribution is not.** The cost asymmetry between generating a post ($0.0001–$0.04) and distributing one ($0.015, or $0.200 with a link) is roughly **524:1**. The labour constraint on covert influence has been removed; the access constraint has not.
- **Synthetic corroboration industrialises the manufacture of *sources* — but not of belief.** The escalation is documented: from 750+ fake outlets accumulated over fifteen years (Indian Chronicles) to 3,749 AI-generated news sites in under three, to a **fake think tank staffed with real researchers' names**. *(But the source-count mechanism is directly contradicted — the 2012 test found "repetition, not number of sources" — so the operative effect is **trust-laundering**, not a belief multiplier. §5.3, §7.4.)*
- **The liar's dividend degrades the evidentiary value of recorded reality — but it is bounded, and the bound matters.** Fabricated evidence becomes cheap to supply and genuine evidence cheap to deny. The effect is strongest on **text** and **largely fails against video** (Schiff et al., *APSR*, 2024; >15,000 adults). And human detection of synthetic media is not hopeless: the pooled figure across 56 papers and 86,155 participants is **55.5%** — barely above chance, but not zero, and **impression-based training lifts accuracy from 40% to 80%** (Dawel et al., *PNAS*, 2026). *(This replaces an earlier, unverifiable "more than half indistinguishable" claim in this report.)*
- **Reach is rented from the platform, not owned by the network.** Forty coordinated accounts produced over a billion views in a month — because a recommender amplified them. "Low organic reach" is therefore **not a stable property**, and defender leverage sits in recommendation and amplification policy more than in debunking.
- **AI is a coordination substrate.** Persistent-memory agent workspaces, shared doctrine files and batch pipelines let producers who never speak to each other behave as one organisation.
- **Multilingual and persona persistence are transformed.** Thousands of articles in twenty languages; impersonation sustained across thousands of posts.
- **Adaptive rebuttal evasion is still not observed.** Neither is microtargeting uplift, nor AI-specific persuasion beyond human dialogue.
- **Institutional cascades are the engineerable target — not public ones.** Measured attempts to move populations come back at or near zero (≈0.05–0.06 SD; zero average persuasion across 49 experiments). A decade of unresolved ambiguity in the Havana case reorganised institutions without producing a public panic.

**The synthesis:** an adversary does not need to persuade anyone. They need to reach the small number of officials, editors and operators who must decide something about an event nobody can explain — and then let the target's own rational procedures do the rest.

### 12.3 The residual risk, stated as narrowly as the evidence allows

Independent analysis of the counterexample literature converges on a specific conclusion: agentic amplification should be treated as a **second-order, local and time-critical risk**, not a mass-panic mechanism. **Six conditions must all hold simultaneously** for it to matter:

1. **Unresolved ambiguity** — the event cannot be immediately explained.
2. **Time-criticality** — a decision must be made in minutes to hours.
3. **Elite and responder penetration** — the narrative reaches officials, operators or editors, not just the public.
4. **Detection failure or delay** — the intervention is not identified in time to correct it.
5. **A small marginal physical event** — something real is happening, so the claim is anchored.
6. **Low inoculation coverage** — the relevant responders are not already primed to expect manipulation.

These conditions are rarely met together, which is why the effect is second-order. But when they are, the **strongest residual scenario is specific and worth naming**:

> **A genuine drone, chemical or infrastructure incident, during which an agent injects a *locally plausible false detail* — a second device, a casualty figure, a named school or hospital — inside a short decision window.**

Note what this scenario is and is not. It is **not** a manufactured panic. It is **not** mass persuasion. It does not require the public to believe anything. It requires one false detail, arriving fast, at the moment when officials are making an irreversible decision — and the detail is credible precisely because the underlying event is real.

**A second residual risk runs in the opposite direction, and is easier to overlook: under-reaction.** The same institutional scepticism that resists manipulation also produced the failures of 1973, 9/11 and early COVID. An adversary seeking to degrade a response does not need to convince anyone that a real attack is a hoax; they need only to make the *next* ambiguous incident look like the last six false alarms. **Habituation is an attack surface, and it requires no falsehood at all.**

Threat inflation is itself a documented institutional pathology, and it has a cost: every retracted scare spends credibility that the next genuine incident will need.


### 12.4 What evidence would convince us this is actually happening?

Because a well-executed amplification is **indistinguishable from genuine public concern** (§7.3), the standard of proof cannot be a cascade — cascades happen without adversaries. Convincing evidence would be **at least two** of:

1. **Network attribution** — a documented network organised specifically around a physical-ambiguity narrative, with an identified operator.
2. **Infrastructure correlation** — accounts sharing generation artefacts, hosting or timing, tied to one incident.
3. **An operational artefact** — internal material showing an actor selecting a physical-ambiguity narrative as a target.
4. **Temporal decoupling** — synthetic activity preceding the trigger, outlasting organic interest, or forming a second wave timed to an institutional decision point.
5. **A court record, prosecution or official attribution** linking an actor to a physical-threat narrative campaign.
6. **A controlled study** showing that injected agentic amplification measurably shifts institutional response to an ambiguous incident.

**The highest-value and most feasible of these is the temporal-decoupling test** (§10.2). It is falsifiable, cheap, and has not been systematically applied. Until it is, the honest position is not "this is not happening" but **"we have not built the instrument that would tell us."**

### 12.5 How could institutions avoid becoming part of the amplification loop?

Not by being less responsive — that is neither achievable nor desirable, since the same scepticism that resists manipulation also ignores real threats. The achievable version is **respond, but do not manufacture confirmation**:

1. **Attribute tradecraft, not outcomes.** Cascades are usually organic. Reserve "hybrid attack" for evidenced cases; over-attribution has already produced retractions, and each one costs credibility for the cases that are real.
2. **Fill the vacuum fast and specifically.** The vulnerability is not credulity, it is ambiguity in an informational vacuum. Say what is not known, early, and give the denominator — a million lawful drones and thousands airborne — because base rates restore the probability judgement that salience destroys.
3. **Separate responding from announcing.** Take the precautionary action; do not necessarily frame it as a security incident.
4. **Pre-commit to thresholds, and make proportionate non-response politically safe.** If over-reaction is the only politically survivable option, the system will over-react.
5. **Verify with a second modality, and institutionalise the mundane explanation.** Birds, aircraft, helicopters and stars resolved most of the cases examined.
6. **Put the correction in the headline.** Correction works when seen; the failure is the 62.9-hour lag against a 6.25-hour half-life. Speed dominates completeness in the first hours.
7. **Attack the distribution constraint, not the content.** Identity verification, account-acquisition cost, and platform integrity funding are where the 524× asymmetry can be narrowed.
8. **Prebunk cheaply and continuously.** A one-paragraph AI-literacy warning halved belief change. Nothing else identified in this study has that ratio of effect to cost.
9. **Plan for permanence, not for a panic.** The plausible adversary objective is sustained low-grade ambiguity — a condition, not an event. Defences should be sustainable indefinitely without escalation, and should not depend on the ambiguity ever being resolved.

**And one thing above all: do not treat the absence of an identified adversary as reassurance.** In this domain, that is the normal state of affairs — and, as §7.3 shows, it is also exactly what a successful operation would look like.

## Appendix C — Method, limitations and confidence

**Method.** Eight parallel research strands plus the author's own primary-source work: concept genealogy; risk psychology and sociology; computational propaganda; agentic capability; drone-sighting cases; health and military scares; counterexamples and resilience; defensive practice. Combined with direct retrieval of primary documents including the FAA/DHS/FBI/DoD joint statement, the EEAS FIMI report, Copenhagen Police and Danish government statements, Belgian Ministry of Defence clarifications, and the EU drone action plan.

**Principal limitations.**

1. **The primary web-search facility failed throughout.** Discovery relied on news-RSS search, DuckDuckGo via a text-extraction proxy, publisher site search and direct document retrieval. This biases coverage toward English-language and easily indexed sources.
2. **The central negative finding is an absence of evidence.** A deliberate search across platform, EU, think-tank and journalism sources found no documented case of AI-enabled amplification of a drone-sighting wave. Given §7.3, this absence is **weaker evidence than it would be elsewhere** and should not be reported as reassurance.
3. **The commercial adaptation claim ("GoLaxy") is single-source and unverified.**
4. **The best resilience evidence comes from a different regime** (national elections) than the one at issue (ambiguous local events). This asymmetry is stated wherever it matters.
5. **Two figures from early secondary sources have been withdrawn or bounded during the study** — an "indistinguishable in more than half of instances" synthetic-content claim (now replaced by the peer-reviewed pooled detection figure of **55.5%**, 56 papers, 86,155 participants) and a contested "25% of Twitter activity is bot-driven" claim, which is not used. Their removal is recorded rather than silently edited, because the direction of both errors was **toward overstating the threat**.
6. **No field experiment exists** measuring the real-world persuasive effect of a named, AI-enabled influence operation. This is the largest gap between the discourse and the evidence.
7. **This is a point-in-time assessment** at 25 September 2026; the capability landscape is moving quickly and the indicator framework in §10.3 is designed to be re-tested.

**Overall confidence.** *High* that ambiguity produces disproportionate disruption without an adversary, and that the institutional layer is where the effect is realised; *high* that AI has transformed influence-operation **production** and not **reach or persuasion**; *high* that no documented case of AI-enabled drone-panic amplification exists; *medium* that institutional cascades are more engineerable than public ones; *medium* that the plausible adversary objective is sustained ambiguity rather than panic; *low* that agentic amplification is currently producing material effects in NATO societies; and *low* on any specific attribution of a European drone cascade to a deliberate operation.
