# Military/Strategic Prior Art: "Make the Enemy Defeat Himself"

**Idea:** the attacker's action is small; the **target's own reaction** produces most of the damage — the target is the amplifier.

**Method.** Quotes are from primary full texts (Gutenberg, archive.org, DTIC items). Google Books API = HTTP 429; DTIC/CSBA direct = 403; archive.org search-inside on lending books = 403, so page-level quotes from restricted books were verified via **Open Library Search Inside**. Unconfirmed items are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 1. Sun Tzu (Giles 1910) — **H**
Giles trans., *The Art of War*, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/132 (text: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/132/132-0.txt). Ch. III §2: *"supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting."* Ch. III §6: *"the skilful leader subdues the enemy's troops without any fighting."* "Subdue the enemy without fighting" is that §6 wording, not a separate aphorism. Sun Tzu states the *goal*; he does not theorise the enemy's reaction as the amplifier.

## 2. Liddell Hart, indirect approach — **H (1929 wording) / M (popular variant)**
*The Decisive Wars of History* (1929), https://archive.org/details/decisivewarshistory (pages from that edition):
- p. 5–6: *"the attempt to throw the opponent without loosening his foothold and balance can only result in self-exhaustion… in all the decisive campaigns the dislocation of the enemy's psychological and physical balance has been the vital prelude to a successful attempt at his overthrow."*
- p. 150: *"his true aim is not so much to seek battle as to seek a strategic situation so advantageous that if it does not of itself produce the decision, its continuation by a battle is guaranteed to do so. In other words, dislocation is the aim of strategy."*
- p. 156: *"to move directly on an opponent is to consolidate his equilibrium, physical and psychological, and by consolidating it to augment his resisting power."*
- p. 230: *"the true aim in war is the mind of the hostile rulers, not the bodies of their troops; that the balance between victory and defeat turns on mental impressions and only indirectly on physical blows."*

**Apocrypha check:** the circulating *"the true aim in war is the mind of the enemy command, not the bodies of his troops"* is **not** his wording. Search Inside shows *"hostile rulers"* in his own *Strategy* (1954; 1967/1991 eds.); "the mind of the enemy command and government" appears in **Richard B. Gregg, *The Power of Nonviolence*** quoting him — a paraphrase. **H**/**M**

## 3. Mao / PLA — **H (formula) / M (doctrine)**
*Problems of Strategy in China's Revolutionary War* (Dec. 1936), https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-1/mswv1_12.htm: *"the sixteen-character formula: 'The enemy advances, we retreat; the enemy camps, we harass; the enemy tires, we attack; the enemy retreats, we pursue.'"* Same text: *"luring the enemy in deep"* — the enemy's own advance consumes him. **H**

Qiao Liang & Wang Xiangsui, *Unrestricted Warfare* (PLA Literature & Arts Publishing House, Feb. 1999); English = FBIS translation of **selections**, https://www.c4i.org/unrestricted.pdf. *"the first rule of unrestricted warfare is that there are no rules, with nothing forbidden"* is attributed by the FBIS editor to Qiao in a 28 June 1999 *Zhongguo Qingnian Bao* interview, **not** to the book text. On asymmetry (p. 211): *"asymmetrical measures which create power for oneself and make the situation develop as you want it to… It often makes an adversary… look like a big elephant charging into a china shop. It is at a loss as to what to do, and unable to make use of the power it has."* **M** for any explicit "his reaction destroys him" thesis.

PLA "Three Warfares" (public-opinion / psychological / legal warfare): Scobell, Lai & Kamphausen, *Chinese Lessons from Other Peoples' Wars* (US Army War College, 2011); Timothy Walton, *China's Three Warfares* (Delex Special Report 3, 2012). Founding regulation text **UNVERIFIED**.

## 4. Clausewitz / Jomini — **H / Jomini: absence (M)**
Clausewitz, *On War*, Graham trans., https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1946. Bk VI ch. VII ("Mutual Action and Reaction of Attack and Defence") is a direct hit: *"there is a double solution in the defensive, consequently a double kind of reaction, according as the aggressor is ruined by the sword of the defensive, or by his own efforts."* Also: *"the mere possibility of a reaction on our part may cause the retreat."* Friction (Bk I ch. VII): *"Everything is very simple in War, but the simplest thing is difficult. These difficulties accumulate and produce a friction…"* Escalation (Bk I ch. I §3–5): three *"reciprocal action[s]"* that *"logically must lead to an extreme."* **H**

Jomini, *The Art of War* (1838; https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13549): full-text search finds **no** doctrine that the defender's reaction is the primary source of his own loss — only incidental uses of "reaction." **No relevant named concept. M**

## 5. Cost imposition / offset / competitive strategies — **H (competitive strategies)**
The clearest quotable version of "make the adversary react and spend himself worse off" is Robert M. Davis, *Competitive Strategies* (DTIC ADA194850, 1988, https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA194850), quoting Weinberger's FY1988 Annual Report to Congress: *"By adopting competitive strategies we force the Soviets to perform less efficiently or effectively."* Davis adds that this *"will force Soviet diversion of resources into areas more defensive in nature and therefore less threatening, (a reactive vice proactive posture)."* **H**

Origin paper: Andrew W. Marshall & James G. Roche, *"Strategy for Competing with the Soviets in the Military Sector of the Continuing Political-Military Competition"* (OSD Net Assessment, 1976); title verified via Krepinevich, *The Last Warrior* (2015) and Rumelt, *Good Strategy/Bad Strategy* (2011). Direct quotes **UNVERIFIED** (not publicly retrievable). "Offset strategy" was termed by SecDef Harold Brown — Ashton B. Carter & John P. White, *Keeping the Edge* (2001): *"Brown called this new strategy the 'offset strategy,' because these new weapons would give…"* **The often-cited Krepinevich, "The Origins of Offset Strategy," could not be verified** (no CSBA page in the Wayback CDX index; no book index hit) — **UNVERIFIED**. "Cost imposition" has no single coinage; it is the punishment side of the denial-vs-punishment distinction (Glenn Snyder, *Deterrence and Defense*, 1961). **M**

## 6. Self-deterrence — **H (concept) / M (canonical definition)**
Keith B. Payne, *Nuclear Deterrence in U.S.-Soviet Relations* (1982): *"the problem of United States 'self-deterrence.' Self-deterrence is the paralyzing effect on…"* David A. Koplow, *Death by Moderation* (Cambridge UP, 2009), ch. 2, "Deterrence and Self-Deterrence," pp. 28–54, doi:10.1017/cbo9780511804816.003: *"The flip side of deterrence is self-deterrence, describing, in this context, a situation…"* Caveat: it arises from the actor's **own** expected costs, not attacker induction — adjacent to, not identical with, the claim. **H/M**

## 7. Escalation dominance / Kahn — **H**
Herman Kahn, *On Escalation: Metaphors and Scenarios* (Praeger, 1965): 44-rung ladder; rung 44, *"Spasm or Insensate War,"* verified via Search Inside. Robert Jervis, *The Illogic of American Nuclear Strategy* (Cornell UP, 1984), ch. 5, "Escalation Dominance and Competition in Risk-Taking," pp. 126–146, doi:10.7591/9781501738654-007. Applied in Robert S. Ross, "Navigating the Taiwan Strait," *International Security* 27(2), 2002, 48–85, doi:10.1162/016228802760987824. **H**

## 8. Schelling, Snyder, salami, fait accompli, provocation — **H (Schelling)**
Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (Harvard UP, 1960): ch. 8 "The Threat That Leaves Something to Chance," p. 187; **p. 193**: *"Brinkmanship is thus the deliberate creation of a recognizable risk of war, a risk that one does not completely control. It is the tactic of deliberately…"* Schelling, *Arms and Influence* (Yale UP, 1966): ch. 3, "The Manipulation of Risk"; *"The power to hurt is bargaining power. To exploit it is diplomacy—vicious diplomacy, but diplomacy."* (ch. 1; page not verified). Mechanism: the attacker deliberately cedes part of the outcome to the opponent's fear/reaction. **H**

Stability–instability paradox: attributed to Glenn H. Snyder (balance-of-terror work, 1961/1965); popularised in Krepon & Gagné, eds., *The Stability–Instability Paradox* (Stimson Center, 2003); exact 1965 page **UNVERIFIED**. Salami tactics: attributed to Mátyás Rákosi (*szalámitaktika*) — Crampton, *The Balkans since the Second World War* (2002); a verbatim Rákosi "slices of salami" quote is **UNVERIFIED**. Fait accompli: Ross 2002. Small provocation → disproportionate response *is* a named **"provocation strategy"**: Shiraev & Zubok, *Current Debates in International Relations* (2015) — *"A provocation strategy is an attempt to induce the enemy to respond…"* **H**/**M**

## 9. Reflexive control — **H (definition) / AI link UNVERIFIED**
Soviet theory; Vladimir A. Lefebvre is the key figure. Primary Western study: Diane Chotikul, *The Soviet Theory of Reflexive Control in Historical and Psychocultural Perspective* (Naval Postgraduate School, July 1986), https://archive.org/details/DTIC_ADA170613: reflexive control = *"conveying to a partner or an opponent specially prepared information to incline him to voluntarily make the predetermined decision"*; it is *"used to influence the actions of the opponent… by understanding his cognitive map thoroughly enough to shape his perceptions of the situation without evoking his awareness that his thoughts are not his own."* Standard citation: Timothy L. Thomas, "Russia's Reflexive Control Theory and the Military," *Journal of Slavic Military Studies* 17(2), 2004, 237–256, doi:10.1080/13518040490450529 (abstract elided/paywalled). **It does capture attacker-induced target-reaction**: the attacker supplies the premise and the target's own decision apparatus produces the damaging act. **No peer-reviewed "AI-driven reflexive control"/LLM work found — UNVERIFIED as a named school.**

## 10. Is there a named "the target is the weapon" concept? — **No canonical term (M)**
None exists in military/strategic vocabulary. Closest named concepts: **self-deterrence** (§6, target deters itself); **competitive strategies** (§5, force the adversary to react inefficiently and spend himself worse off); and **judo strategy** — Yoffie & Kwak, *Judo Strategy: Turning Your Competitors' Strength to Your Advantage* (Harvard Business School Press, 2001) — explicitly uses the opponent's strength/momentum, but business/IR, not military. Chinese maxims 借力打力 ("borrow force to strike force") and 四两拨千斤 ("four ounces deflect a thousand pounds") express the idea; no canonical military source verified — **UNVERIFIED**. "Autoimmune," "boomerang" and "own goal" are **not** established strategic terms; "blowback" (CIA; Chalmers Johnson, *Blowback*, 2000) is the inverse — the attacker's action harming **himself**.
