# 07 — The Disconfirming Evidence
### How strong is the case against the hypothesis that AI agents can amplify ambiguous physical threats so that target societies generate most of the disruption?

**Research cut-off: 25 September 2026.** Analytic/defensive research only; no operational guidance.

**Evidence grades** (consistent with the parent study): **A** primary/official · **B** peer-reviewed or authoritative analytic · **C** reputable secondary · **D** single-outlet/advocacy · **E** weak/unverified. **Confidence** in the inference drawn is rated **High / Medium / Low**.

---

## 0. Scope and framing

The hypothesis has two parts: (i) AI agents deliberately **amplify** an ambiguous physical threat, and (ii) the **target society itself generates most of the disruption**. The disconfirming case is strongest against mass, society-wide, attitude-driven panic, and weakest against small, local, time-critical disruption layered onto a **real** event. This brief is adversarial but reports where the counter-case fails.

---

## 1. Failed and counterproductive influence campaigns

**The Internet Research Agency (IRA) is the best-documented failure.** The largest peer-reviewed study linking individual exposure to behaviour found exposure was **heavily concentrated: 1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures**, disproportionately strong Republicans. Exposure was dwarfed by *domestic* media and politicians, and the authors found **"no evidence of a meaningful relationship"** between exposure and changes in attitudes, polarisation or voting. *(Eady et al., Nature Communications, 2023 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-35576-9

A longitudinal study merging survey data with non-public Twitter IRA data reached the same null across **six distinct measures** over one month (N=1,239), concluding the trolls "mostly interacted with those who were already highly polarized." *(Bail et al., PNAS, 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116 The repeated "126 million Americans reached" figure is **potential reach** on Facebook, not verified exposure; the platform's own data showed engagement concentrated among already-partisan audiences. *(US Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, 2018 — A; landing page blocked to automated fetch)*

**Doppelganger** (media-clone sites and paid ads impersonating *Le Monde*, *Le Parisien*, *DW*) was identified within weeks, attributed by France's VIGINUM in July 2023, and had its ad accounts blocked by Meta and Google; CORRECTIV's 2024 investigation is credited with helping to "bring the campaign to a halt." Its failure modes are structural: typosquatted domains are trivially discoverable, clone content is recycled rather than novel, and ad bans remove the only scalable channel. *(EU DisinfoLab; VIGINUM/SGDSN — A/B, Medium–High)* https://www.disinfo.eu/publications/ · https://www.sgdsn.gouv.fr/publications

**Storm-1516** produced viral fakes (the September 2024 "Kamala Harris hit-and-run" video; fabricated abuse claims against Tim Walz) but was **attributed by Microsoft within days** and debunked before reaching more than fringe audiences. Successor campaigns, including ISD's September 2026 "Matryoshka" operation against German regional elections, are assessed as recycling the same tactics with limited traction. *(Microsoft / ISD — B/C, Medium)* https://www.isdglobal.org/publications/

**Tenet Media is the clearest "counterproductive" case.** The US Department of Justice indicted a scheme in which Russian state funds covertly paid roughly **US$10 million** to a Tennessee media company for content that was, by prosecutors' account, **ordinary right-leaning commentary** rather than bespoke disinformation. The operation bought *reach among the already-convinced* and became a funding scandal, not an effective persuasion channel. *(US DOJ, Sept 2024 — A, High)* https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/justice-department-conducts-court-authorized-law-enforcement-actions-address-government

**Operations that reinforced the target's position.** Armenia's pro-Europe party won the June 2026 election despite a documented Russia-linked campaign, whose visible presence appears to have hardened pro-EU sentiment. *(Recorded Future / The Record, June 2026 — C, Medium)* This echoes Moldova and Poland, where heavy interference coincided with pro-Western or incumbent outcomes.**Honest counterexamples (these cut *against* the disconfirming case).** Romania's December 2024 first round was annulled after a coordinated TikTok campaign propelled a marginal candidate to first place — networked amplification *can* move a mass outcome. The UK's August 2024 Southport riots show online rumour producing physical disorder, including attacks on asylum-seeker hotels — but the rumour concerned a suspect's identity in an actual child murder, not an ambiguous threat, and was substantially **domestic**. *(C, High)* The null result is therefore not universal.

## 2. Measurement scepticism: how large is the real-world persuasive effect?

**The "minimal effects" tradition is empirically robust.** Lazarsfeld, Berelson and Gaudet's *The People's Choice* (1944) and Klapper (1960) established reinforcement rather than conversion as the norm, and modern meta-analysis confirms it: a synthesis of **49 field experiments** concluded the best estimate of campaign contact and advertising effects on general-election choice is **zero**. *(Kalla & Broockman, APSR, 2018 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055417000363

**Exposure is tiny.** Fake news comprised **0.15% of Americans' daily media diet**, and news of any kind at most 14.2%, in a nationally representative multi-mode panel. *(Allen et al., 2020 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aay3539 On Twitter in 2016, **1% of users accounted for 80% of fake-news source exposures, and 0.1% for nearly 80% of fake-news shares**; the heaviest sharers were older, conservative and highly engaged. *(Grinberg et al., 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau2706 Sharing was rare, and users over 65 shared roughly **seven times** as many fake-news articles as the youngest cohort. *(Guess, Nagler & Tucker, 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau4586 **The people most exposed are, systematically, the people already aligned.**

**Corrections work — modestly, and worse in politics and the real world.** Meta-analysis of 65 studies found an overall **r = .35**, falling to **r = .15 in politics** and **r = .14 for real-world misinformation** (versus r = .48 for constructed lab stimuli). *(Walter & Murphy, 2018 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1080/03637751.2018.1467564 Fact-checking of political claims yields **d = 0.29**, attenuated by prior beliefs and ideology. *(Walter et al., 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2019.1668894

**The "backfire effect" has been largely retracted.** The canonical claim that correcting misinformation strengthens false beliefs did not replicate: across **52 experiments and ~10,100 subjects**, corrections moved beliefs in the correct direction and **"no correction backfired."** *(Wood & Porter, Political Behavior, 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1007/s11109-018-9443-y The related "belief echoes" finding (persistence after correction) is real but contested and does not imply reversal. *(Thorson, 2015 — C, Medium)* https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2015.1102187

**AI-specific scepticism.** A preregistered experiment (**n = 8,587**) using GPT-4 found LLM messages broadly persuasive (up to **+12 percentage points**), but **microtargeting added nothing**: 4.83pp (microtargeted) vs 6.20pp (non-targeted), **p = 0.226**. *(Hackenburg & Margetts, PNAS, 2024 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2403116121 This directly undermines the "AI uniquely tailors persuasion" premise. But other work (n=1,788) finds personalized LLM messages *do* outperform generic ones *(Salvi et al., 2024)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-53755-0, and a GPT-4 dialogue intervention reduced conspiracy belief by **~20%**, persisting **two months** *(Costello, Pennycook & Rand, Science, 2024)* https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq1814. **The AI-persuasion literature is genuinely mixed: AI is neither magic nor inert.** Alarm about misinformation is itself driven by a **third-person effect** — people believe *others* are gullible — and because misinformation is so rare, interventions targeting it have **very small effects on the overall information environment**. *(Altay & Acerbi, 2023)* https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448231153379 · *(Acerbi, Altay & Mercier, 2022)* https://doi.org/10.37016/mr-2020-87

## 3. Prebunking and inoculation

**Measured effects are real but small.** The *Bad News* game improved manipulation detection in ~15,000 players irrespective of education, age, ideology or cognitive style. *(Roozenbeek & van der Linden, 2019 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-019-0279-9 Five inoculation videos improved technique recognition, discernment and sharing decisions across six RCTs (**n = 6,464**) and a YouTube field study (**n = 22,632**). *(Roozenbeek et al., Science Advances, 2022 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abo6254

**Scale has been achieved; effect size has not grown.** The largest prebunking campaign to date reached **120M+ YouTube users** before the 2024 EU elections; a 13-survey, 12-nation evaluation (**N = 19,735**, including 45+ populations) found improvements in discernment and sharing decisions — but effects were **small, ds ≈ 0.08–0.38**. *(Nature Communications Psychology, 2026 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00379-3 An Instagram field study (**N = 375,597**; quiz n = 806) found a **21-percentage-point** improvement **persisting five months** — the strongest durability result, but measured on a small subsample. *(HKS Misinformation Review, 2026 — B, Medium)* https://doi.org/10.37016/mr-2020-193

**Limits.** Text- and video-based inoculation decays within roughly **one month**; game-based effects decay faster; **"booster" reminders** restore but do not permanently fix resistance (N = 11,759). *(Nature Communications, 2025 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57205-x Inoculation works better for *technique* recognition than for deeply held conspiracy beliefs, does not reliably reach the least trusting audiences, and cannot be pre-positioned against a genuinely novel, locally verifiable claim (a specific hospital, flight number or casualty figure). *(van der Linden, 2022 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-01713-6

## 4. Platform and state countermeasures

**Coordinated inauthentic behaviour (CIB) enforcement is routine and fast.** Meta has published quarterly CIB takedowns since 2018, removing networks traced to Russia, China, Iran and commercial actors. *(Meta — A, High)* https://about.fb.com/news/tag/coordinated-inauthentic-behavior/ Doppelganger's ad accounts were blocked; Storm-1516 and successors were attributed within days. Detection favours defenders against **machine-like** behaviour: posting cadence, stylometric similarity, account-creation clustering and shared infrastructure are cheap signals. *(B/C, Medium)*

**EU DSA.** The Digital Services Act imposes systemic-risk assessment and transparency duties on very large platforms, with an **Article 36 crisis-response mechanism** for extraordinary threats. It has been used sparingly. During the August 2026 Ceuta migration crisis — accompanied by documented disinformation — **no member state triggered the crisis mechanism**; the Commission instead coordinated fact-checkers. *(European Commission; Euronews, Aug 2026 — A/C, Medium)* https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act · https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/crisis-response Enforcement has escalated to fines (Temu ~€200M, May 2026; an AliExpress record fine, July 2026). *(C, Medium)*

**UK model.** The Online Safety Act 2023 placed illegal-and-harmful-content duties on platforms, enforced by Ofcom. *(A, High)* https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-safety-act-explainer/online-safety-act-explainer · https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/ **But the UK's counter-disinformation architecture is contested**: the Counter Disinformation Unit / National Security Online Information Team has been criticised for opacity and for touching legitimate speech. *(C, Medium)*

**The erosion caveat.** Countermeasures are not monotonically improving: X/Twitter dismantled its election-integrity and trust-and-safety capacity after 2022 and removed state-affiliated media labels, scaling Community Notes as a partial substitute. Defence in 2026 is therefore **strong on large platforms under EU/UK jurisdiction, weaker on X and on encrypted or federated channels.** *(C, Medium)*

## 5. Cognitive and social resilience

**Habituation / "cry wolf."** Repeated false or unverifiable alarms reduce response to later warnings — long documented in disaster sociology and evident in clinical "alarm fatigue," where the large majority of alarms are non-actionable and clinicians desensitise. *(Joint Commission Sentinel Event Alert 50, 2013 — A, High)* https://www.jointcommission.org/resources/sentinel-event/sentinel-event-alert-newsletters/ Alarm fatigue is a *double-edged* finding: it blunts manufactured panics, but also genuine warnings. *(See §7.)*

**Community Notes.** The strongest causal estimate finds that once a note appears, subsequent spread of a misleading post falls **~61.2%**, and authors' odds of deleting it rise **~94.3%** — but notes arrive too late for the early viral stage, so the **system-wide reduction in engagement is only ~14.9%**, and effects are weaker for influential accounts and political content. *(Nature Communications, 2026 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72597-0 An earlier difference-in-differences study found **no significant system-wide reduction** because notes were "too slow" *(Chuai et al., 2024)* https://doi.org/10.1145/3686967, and a July 2026 review concluded **neither professional nor community fact-checking performs strongly across trust, scalability and impact.** https://communitynotes.x.com/guide/en/about/introduction

**Crowds often correct rather than amplify.** Aggregated lay judgments correlate strongly with fact-checker ratings across designs and pools, which is why Wikipedia and Community Notes work at all. *(Martel et al., 2023 — B, High)* https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916231190388 Wikipedia's verifiability policy, talk-page contestation and rapid reversion give it a genuine self-correction mechanism. *(A/B, Medium–High)* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability

**Rumour self-terminates.** Allport and Postman's law holds that rumour intensity varies with **importance × ambiguity**; when ambiguity is resolved or a trusted source refutes, the rumour loses its engine. *(Allport & Postman, 1947 — B, High)* https://archive.org/details/psychologyofrumo0000allp The 2019 "Momo challenge" panic — a supposed child-suicide game amplified by media coverage rather than evidence — collapsed within weeks once schools and platforms stated there was no evidence of harm. *(C, High)* This is the mechanism that most limits agentic amplification: **manufactured ambiguity is fragile, and resolution is cheap.**

## 6. Why panic is hard to sustain

**Satanic panic (c. 1980–1995).** A genuine moral panic that produced prosecutions and ruined lives, but it was **ended by evidence**: the FBI's 1992 Kenneth Lanning assessment found no substantiated case of organised ritual abuse, high-profile prosecutions collapsed (McMartin acquittals, 1990), and journalism exposed the recovered-memory techniques behind the accusations. Duration ~15 years, but **low intensity for most of it and confined to specific communities**. *(A/B, High)* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satanic_panic

**Video nasties (c. 1980–1985).** A UK panic over horror videotapes driven by elite campaigners and the tabloid press rather than mass public fear. It was **short** and ended when the **Video Recordings Act 1984** (in force 1985) converted the campaign's demands into a licensing regime — the panic subsided *because it won*. This is the honest caveat: some panics leave durable law. *(A/B, High)* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_nasty · https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/39/contents

**Knockout game (2013, recurring 2022).** Widely assessed as **largely media-manufactured**. St. Louis police estimated the activity involved **five to nine teenagers**, not a national trend; critics called it "an urban myth" and a "type of panic," and the Center on Juvenile and Criminal Justice described it as "a fake trend." It ended when journalists checked police data. *(B/C, High)* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_game

**Rainbow fentanyl (2022).** The DEA warned that brightly coloured fentanyl pills were being marketed to children; toxicologists called the Halloween-candy risk "absolutely ludicrous," noting no documented case of a child harmed by tampered Halloween candy. The panic **faded within weeks** and became a textbook case of official threat inflation outrunning evidence. *(C, High)*

**Pattern.** Panics are usually short, are ended by **verifiable evidence and institutional pushback**, and typically require a **pre-existing genuine anxiety** to ignite. Deliberate amplification rarely creates a panic from nothing; it rides one that already exists.

## 7. Institutional impermeability — and its honest cost

Intelligence and emergency-management practice is built to **discount ambiguous, uncorroborated reporting**. Warning analysis emphasises source reliability and avoiding single-source escalation; agencies routinely "hold" reports that look like activism or noise. The 1973 Yom Kippur failure, the 9/11 warnings and the early COVID-19 response are the canonical cases of the **opposite** failure: institutions *did* have ambiguous warning and discounted it, with catastrophic results. *(9/11 Commission Report, 2004 — A, High)* https://9-11commission.gov/report/ Missile-warning false alarms (1983; the 1995 Norwegian rocket) show the same tension: over-reaction is enormously costly, which is why warning systems tolerate false negatives.

**Threat inflation is also a documented pathology**: terrorist and security threats have been systematically overestimated by states and media, producing durable misallocation. *(Mueller, Overblown — B, High)* https://www.cato.org/books/overblown

**The trade-off, stated honestly:** the scepticism that makes societies resistant to manufactured panic **also** makes them slow to act on real, low-probability, high-consequence threats. A counter-case built on institutional impermeability is therefore not purely reassuring — it implies that if agentic amplification ever *did* coincide with a genuine ambiguous threat, the failure mode would be **under-reaction**, not panic.

---

## 8. The strongest version of the counter-case

> Covert influence campaigns almost never change mass attitudes, because exposure is minuscule, concentrated among the already-aligned, and overwhelmed by domestic media. Where campaigns do reach people, the effect is small, short-lived and correctable; the "backfire effect" did not replicate. AI does not change this: microtargeting adds little over generic messages, and AI content is detectable and checkable. Societies are structurally resistant to manufactured panic: rumour requires ambiguity, ambiguity resolves cheaply, panics are short, and crowds, Community Notes and Wikipedia self-correct. Platforms and states detect and remove inauthentic networks quickly, and the EU/UK regimes impose real penalties. **Agentic amplification is therefore a second-order concern**: if a physical threat causes disruption, that disruption is produced overwhelmingly by the physical event and by pre-existing domestic conditions, not by the agents.

**What would have to be true for agentic amplification to matter:** genuine, **unresolved ambiguity** at the moment of the incident (no authoritative account within the response window); **time-criticality** (decisions in minutes-to-hours, before debunking catches up); **elite/responder penetration** (the claim reaches officials, responders or journalists who *act*, not just partisans who consume); **detection failure or delay**; a **small, marginal physical event**, so incremental disruption is attributable to amplification rather than the event itself; and **low inoculation coverage** of the specific channel and audience.

All six are rarely met together. They are *plausibly* met in a narrow class of cases: a real drone incursion, chemical incident or infrastructure failure in which an agent injects a false but locally plausible detail (a second device, a casualty figure, a specific school or hospital) into a short decision window.

---

## 9. Verdict: strength of the disconfirming case

**Overall: MODERATE (strong against the mass-panic variant; weak against the local, time-critical variant).**

| Claim under test | Disconfirming strength | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Covert campaigns change mass attitudes/votes | **Strong** (multiple null replications, large N) | High |
| AI agents uniquely enable persuasion | **Moderate** (microtargeting null; mixed other results) | Medium |
| Prebunking/inoculation can blunt amplification | **Moderate** (real but small, decaying) | High |
| Platforms/states detect and remove fast | **Moderate** (strong on major platforms; eroding on X) | Medium |
| Crowds/communities self-correct | **Moderate** (Community Notes ~62% post-note, but ~15% system-wide) | High |
| Manufactured panics are short and fail | **Moderate–strong** (historical record) | High |
| Institutions resist ambiguous reports | **Strong** (but cuts both ways) | High |
| Agents can drive society-wide disruption from ambiguous physical threats | **Weak** (nulls dominate; Southport/Romania show real exceptions) | Medium |

**Why not "strong" overall.** Three findings keep the disconfirming case out of "strong" territory. First, the **exact hypothesis is almost untested**: the nulls are extrapolated from elections and health misinformation, not from AI agents manufacturing *physical-threat* ambiguity. Second, the **Romania 2024 and Southport 2024 cases** show networked amplification *can* produce mass and physical consequences when a real, high-salience trigger exists. Third, the **AI-persuasion literature is mixed**, with at least one high-quality study showing large, durable belief change from AI dialogue.

**Bottom line for the parent study.** The case against "society-wide panic manufactured by agents" is **strong and should be the default expectation**. The residual risk is **not** mass panic but **local, time-critical, decision-level disruption layered on a genuine physical event** — a narrow, low-probability, high-consequence pathway. Analytic effort is better spent on that pathway, and on the under-reaction failure mode, than on scenarios of society-wide agent-induced hysteria.
