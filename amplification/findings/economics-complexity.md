# Economics / complexity-science prior art for "attacker input small, target's own reaction does the damage"

**Idea under test:** a small attacker action triggers a public/market/institution to *amplify* its own damage
through endogenous feedback — "the target is the amplifier".

**Headline finding.** The closest existing **named** concept is **Robert K. Merton's "self-fulfilling prophecy"
(1948)**: a false definition of the situation evokes behaviour that makes the false definition true, and Merton's
canonical case is a **bank run** — a small trigger (a rumour) that the target's own reaction converts into ruin.
Its one critical divergence: **Merton requires no attacker**; the false definition can be wholly endogenous.

---

## 1. Merton, "The Self-Fulfilling Prophecy" — **H** (closest named concept)

- **Origin:** Robert K. Merton, *The Antioch Review* 8(2), Summer 1948, pp. 193–210.
- **URLs:** https://www.jstor.org/stable/4609267 · 2016 reprint
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7723/antiochreview.74.3.0504 · **free full text of the exact issue (verified
  directly):** https://archive.org/details/sim_antioch-review_summer-1948_8_2
- **Verbatim definition (p. 195):** "The self-fulfilling prophecy is, in the beginning, a false definition of the
  situation evoking a new behavior which makes the originally false conception come true. The specious validity of
  the self-fulfilling prophecy perpetuates a reign of error."
- **Mechanism (p. 193):** "Predictions of the return of Halley's comet do not influence its orbit. But the rumored
  insolvency of Millingville's bank did affect the actual outcome. The prophecy of collapse led to its own
  fulfillment."
- **Bank parable (pp. 193–194):** "It is the year 1932. The Last National Bank is a flourishing institution…
  Cartwright Millingville has ample reason to be proud… Until Black Wednesday… a rumor of insolvency, once believed
  by enough depositors, would result in the insolvency of the bank… the long lines of anxious depositors, each
  frantically seeking to salvage his own, grew to longer lines of even more anxious depositors." Verdict: "we know
  that Millingville's bank was solvent, that it would have survived for many years had not the misleading rumor
  created the very conditions of its own fulfillment."
- **Attacker required? No.** The trigger is a rumour; Merton generalises to war scares and racial prejudice where no
  one is "attacking". He supplies the *amplifier*, not the *attacker* — precisely the gap the user's concept fills.
- **Precursor:** Merton, "The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action", *ASR* 1(6), 1936, 894–904.

## 2. Self-defeating / "suicidal" prophecy — **H** (Merton's own term)

Merton's 1948 **footnote to p. 196** names the complement: "'Counterpart of the self-fulfilling prophecy is the
**'suicidal prophecy'** which so alters human behavior from what would have been its course had the prophecy not
been made, that it fails to be borne out. The prophecy destroys itself. This important type is not considered here."
It is the *opposite* (negative feedback): a prediction that prevents its own fulfilment.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defeating_prophecy. The coinage of "self-negating prophecy" is **UNVERIFIED**.

## 3. "Backfire" / "boomerang" effect — **H**, but a *different* phenomenon

- **Nyhan & Reifler 2010**, *Political Behavior* 32(2):303–330, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11109-010-9112-2. Verbatim
  abstract: "…corrections frequently fail to reduce misperceptions among the targeted ideological group. We also
  document several instances of a 'backfire effect' in which corrections actually increase misperceptions among the
  group in question." (https://sites.dartmouth.edu/nyhan/files/2021/03/nyhan-reifler.pdf)
- **Replication failure:** Wood & Porter, "The Elusive Backfire Effect", *Political Behavior* **41(1):135–163
  (2019)**, **https://doi.org/10.1007/s11109-018-9443-y** — the DOI in the brief (`…-018-9453-6`) is **incorrect
  (404)**. Verbatim preprint abstract (https://djflynn.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/elusive-backfire-effect-wood-porter.pdf):
  "we enrolled more than 8,100 subjects and tested 36 issues of potential backfire. Across all experiments, we found
  only one issue capable of triggering backfire… Evidence of factual backfire is far more tenuous than prior research
  suggests." Nyhan's PNAS 2021 retrospective agrees backfire is "extremely rare in practice".
- **Boomerang origin:** Hovland, Janis & Kelley, *Communication and Persuasion* (Yale UP, 1953), who "first recorded
  and named the boomerang effect" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang_effect_(psychology)). **Social norms:**
  Schultz et al., *Psychological Science* 18(5):429–434 (2007), https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01917.x —
  a descriptive norm produced "either desirable energy savings or the undesirable boomerang effect".
- **Disambiguation:** both terms mean **a message producing the opposite of its intended persuasive effect** — a
  sender-side failure, not "attacker pokes target; target overreacts and harms itself". Borrowing the name collides
  with ~70 years of persuasion research. **Avoid it.**

## 4. Soros, reflexivity — **H** for the definition; no security application found

Verbatim (*The Alchemy of Finance*, 1994 new introduction, p. 2): "In situations that have thinking participants,
there is a two-way interaction between the participants' thinking and the situation in which they participate… I
call the interference between the two functions 'reflexivity.' I envision reflexivity as a feedback loop between the
participants' understanding and the situation in which they participate… Reflexivity renders the participants'
understanding imperfect and ensures that their actions will have unintended consequences." (Also "fallibility" +
"reflexivity" as the two pillars; *The New Paradigm for Financial Markets*, 2008.) Verified at
https://archive.org/details/the-alchemy-of-finance-george-soros.
**Security/defence application: none found.** Crossref (`reflexivity deterrence military strategy`; `Soros
reflexivity security studies`) and Semantic Scholar (`reflexivity security studies war`) returned only
economics/philosophy hits (e.g. *Reflexivity and Economics*, Routledge 2018). **M / UNVERIFIED negative** — a
specialist security-studies search would be needed to call it definitively absent.

## 5. Self-fulfilling crises (bank / currency / debt) — **H**

- **Diamond & Dybvig 1983**, *JPE* 91(3):401–419, https://doi.org/10.1086/261155 (free:
  https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/records/3sa4m-hvw53). "Traditional demand deposit contracts… have multiple
  equilibria, one of which is a bank run." On the trigger: "This could be a bad earnings report, a commonly observed
  run at some other bank, a negative government forecast, or even sunspots. **It need not be anything fundamental
  about the bank's condition.** The problem is that once they have deposited, anything that causes them to
  anticipate a run will lead to a run… the good equilibrium is very fragile." The best formal statement of a
  disproportionate, self-amplifying overreaction to a small signal.
- **Obstfeld 1996**, *European Economic Review* 40(3–5):1037–1047,
  https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-2921(95)00111-5 (free NBER WP: https://www.nber.org/papers/w5285): "The discomfort a
  government suffers from speculation against its currency determines the strategic incentives of speculators and
  the scope for multiple currency-market equilibria."
- **Calvo 1988**, "Servicing the Public Debt: The Role of Expectations", *AER* 78(4):647–661,
  https://www.jstor.org/stable/1811166 — standard origin citation for **self-fulfilling debt crises**
  (**UNVERIFIED verbatim**; mechanism confidence M). Formalised by **Cole & Kehoe 2000**, *Review of Economic
  Studies* 67(1):91–116, https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-937X.00123.

## 6. Minsky — **H**

"The Financial Instability Hypothesis", Levy Economics Institute **WP 74** (May 1992),
https://www.levyinstitute.org/pubs/wp74.pdf. Verbatim: "The financial instability hypothesis is a model of a
capitalist economy which does not rely upon exogenous shocks to generate business cycles of varying severity." Also
the hedge/speculative/Ponzi taxonomy. "Stability is destabilizing" is **UNVERIFIED** in WP 74; "Minsky moment" is
later journalistic coinage.

## 7. Informational cascades — **H**

Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer & Welch, *JPE* 100(5):992–1026 (1992), https://doi.org/10.1086/261849 (free:
http://www.dklevine.com/archive/refs41193.pdf). "**DEFINITION.** An informational cascade occurs if an individual's
action does not depend on his private information signal… Consequently, a cascade once started will last forever,
even if it is wrong." On amplification: "small shocks lead to big shifts in mass behavior".

## 8. Crowd disaster / panic sociology — **H**

- **Helbing, Farkas & Vicsek 2000**, *Nature* 407:487–491, https://doi.org/10.1038/35035023 (free:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0009448): "crowd stampede induced by panic, often leading to fatalities as people
  are crushed or trampled"; the **"faster-is-slower effect"** — "trying to move faster… can cause a smaller average
  speed of leaving… fleeing people reduce their own chances of survival."
- **"Crowd turbulence":** Helbing, Johansson & Al-Abideen 2007, *Phys. Rev. E* 75:046109,
  https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.75.046109 (free: https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0701203): "…transition to
  turbulent flow… is responsible for sudden eruptions of pressure release comparable to earthquakes, which cause
  sudden displacements and the falling and trampling of people." The phrase **"crowd quake"** is **UNVERIFIED** as a
  formal coinage.
- **Debunking "mass panic":** Drury, Cocking & Reicher 2009, *IJMED* 27(1):66–95,
  https://doi.org/10.1177/028072700902700104: "'Selfish' behaviors were found to be rare; mutual helping was more
  common." See also **Quarantelli, "The Nature and Conditions of Panic", *AJS* 60(3):267–275 (1954)**,
  https://doi.org/10.1086/221536 (closed; **UNVERIFIED verbatim**).

## 9. Named concepts for "small trigger → large self-amplifying response"

| Concept | Origin | URL | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-fulfilling prophecy** | Merton 1948 | https://www.jstor.org/stable/4609267 | **closest**; no attacker needed |
| Information cascade | Bikhchandani et al. 1992 | https://doi.org/10.1086/261849 | conformity amplifies a small signal |
| Self-organised criticality | Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld 1987 | https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.381 | small perturbation → avalanche |
| Tipping point | Grodzins c.1960 / Schelling 1971 / Gladwell 2000 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipping_point_(sociology) | "how little things can make a big difference" |
| Threshold model | Granovetter, *AJS* 83(6) (1978) | https://doi.org/10.1086/226707 | heterogeneous thresholds flip aggregates |
| Cascading failure | engineering/systems | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascading_failure | one failure triggers others |
| Reflexivity | Soros 1987/1994/2008 | https://archive.org/details/the-alchemy-of-finance-george-soros | perception↔fundamentals loop |
| Moral panic | Cohen, *Folk Devils and Moral Panics* (1972) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_panic | control agents **amplify** deviance |
| Deviancy amplification spiral | **Wilkins, *Social Deviance* (1964)**; Young 1971; Cohen 1972 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deviancy_amplification_spiral | closest named *reaction-amplification* mechanism, but the amplifier is society/media, not a target under attack |

**Bottom line.** Merton (1948) is the only established named concept matching "small trigger → target's own reaction
→ large self-inflicted damage", and the bank parable matches the mechanism almost exactly — but it is a
social-epistemic process, not an attack, and the trigger is a belief rather than an adversary. Viable labels:
**"self-fulfilling prophecy"** with an adversarial trigger grafted on, or a new coinage such as **"self-amplifying
overreaction" / "reflexive blowback"**. Backfire/boomerang vocabulary must be avoided: it already means "persuasion
produced the opposite of its intended effect".
