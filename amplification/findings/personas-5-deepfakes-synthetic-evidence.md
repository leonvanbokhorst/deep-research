# Personas-5 — AI-Generated Synthetic Evidence in Influence Operations

**Area:** deepfake audio/video, AI fake "eyewitness" imagery, AI-generated fake documents, synthetic personas, the "liar's dividend". **Cut-off:** 25 September 2026. Defensive capability assessment only. **Grades:** A = primary/official · B = peer-reviewed/investigative · C = reputable news · D = think-tank/vendor · E = unverified.

## 1. Established picture

1. **Audio deepfakes are the cheapest and most consequential synthetic-evidence vector in electoral politics** — instantly deniable and typically released inside the media blackout window, when fact-checking is slowest (Slovakia 2023).
2. **True deepfake video is rarer than headlines imply. The dominant real technique is the "cheap fake"** — re-cut, re-voiced or mislabelled *real* footage. Indonesia's 2024 "Suharto" video and much 2025–26 Iran/Israel material are mixtures; several "deepfake" claims are cheap fakes.
3. **AI imagery's demonstrated damage is market impact, erosion of genuine atrocity evidence, and harassment — not voter persuasion.**
4. **The liar's dividend is no longer theory:** reproduced in preregistered experiments and observed in state practice.
5. **Not demonstrated:** that a synthetic artefact alone flipped an election, or that a state-grade audiovisual deepfake caused a documented military or diplomatic decision.

## 2. Deepfake audio of politicians

**Slovakia, 28 September 2023 (reference case).** Two days before the 30 September election, a 48-second clip spread on Facebook/Telegram purporting to be Progressive Slovakia leader **Michal Šimečka** and a *Denník N* journalist plotting to rig the vote by buying Roma votes. The impersonated outlet filed a police complaint; the file was quickly shown fabricated. Reported giveaways: flat cadence, audible artefacts, no corroborating recording or chain of custody (C, medium). Police reopened the investigation at least three times and were still investigating in November 2024 (C, medium). The significance is *timing*: released inside the moratorium, when institutional response was slowest. CEDMO and the HKS *Misinformation Review* (22 Aug 2024) treat it as paradigmatic; HKS is explicitly sceptical of the "deepfake decided the election" narrative (B, medium-high).

**US "Biden robocall", 21–23 January 2024.** Ahead of the New Hampshire primary, voters received a robocall in a cloned Biden voice telling them to "save your vote" for November. The FCC issued a **unanimous declaratory ruling on 8 February 2024** confirming AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act — regulating the *technology*, not merely the act (A, high). On **23 May 2024** the FCC proposed a **$6 million** forfeiture against consultant **Steve Kramer** and New Hampshire indicted him on voter-suppression charges; carrier **Lingo Telecom** settled for **$1 million** (Aug 2024); the $6m forfeiture was finalised in late September 2024 (A/C, high). **Corrective:** in **June 2025 a New Hampshire jury acquitted Kramer on all counts** (C, high). Enforcement is not proof of guilt.

**Others.** A cloned **Keir Starmer** clip circulated in the UK in **October 2023** (C, high). In **Pakistan (February 2024)** Imran Khan's party used voice cloning so the imprisoned ex-PM could deliver a "victory speech" — the first widely documented case of a party *openly and attributively* using a synthetic voice (C, high). India 2024 saw many cloned leader voices alongside its video cases (C, medium). In the **India–Pakistan crisis (Operation Sindoor, April–May 2025)**, deepfake audio mimicking senior Pakistani commanders issuing **nuclear-alert and stand-down orders** circulated to civilians and lower ranks — the clearest reported case of synthetic audio targeting command-and-control ambiguity (D, low-medium; reported, not independently verified).

## 3. Deepfake video as fabricated evidence

The **Zelensky "surrender" video** (16 March 2022) — a low-quality face-swap on a hacked Ukrainian news site, pushed to Telegram, removed within hours after unnatural head/neck geometry was documented — is the origin case (C, high). Since then: the **Gabon video** (Aug 2023) on President Ali Bongo; fabricated **Burkina Faso** material celebrating Capt. Ibrahim Traoré's imaginary achievements, catalogued by **Dubawa** (Jun 2025, B/C); fabricated **Hamas/Israel** clips, including AI "babies" (AP, Nov 2023) and AI crowds cheering Israeli soldiers (AFP, Aug 2025); **DR Congo–Rwanda**, where Kinshasa accused Kigali of **faking video of captured FDLR fighters** (BBC, 2 Mar 2025, C); and **Sudan**, where Misbar and DW documented a fabricated image of an RSF fighter threatening a mother and child (Oct 2025) and a fabricated "buried alive" video (Jun 2026) around the RSF capture of **el-Fasher** (B/C).

The pattern is consistent: fabricated video is inserted into contested atrocity disputes where verification is impossible, and *genuine* evidence is dismissed by association.

## 4. AI images as false eyewitness evidence

**Pentagon "explosion", 22 May 2023.** A synthetic image of smoke near the Pentagon was posted by verified-badge accounts and amplified by state media, then debunked by US officials in roughly 20 minutes. The **S&P 500 dipped ≈0.3% intraday** before recovering (AP 23 May 2023; Bloomberg; NYT — A/C, high on direction and rough magnitude). The widely repeated **~$500 bn "wiped"** figure is *not* well corroborated (contested, low). Still the clearest case of a synthetic image moving a major market.

**Hurricane Helene, October 2024.** The AI image of a crying girl in a boat with a puppy became the emblem of the disaster misinformation wave, amplified by prominent political accounts; Snopes, The Dispatch and AFP confirmed it was AI-generated and it caused documented distress to real victims' families (C, high). Milton produced a parallel wave. **August 2024:** Trump reposted AI images falsely suggesting **Taylor Swift** and a "Swifties for Trump" movement had endorsed him — significant because a candidate personally distributed synthetic evidence (C, high).

**Kolkata (Aug 2024), Gaza (2023–25), LA fires (Jan 2025), Mamdani (2025–26).** The LA fires produced forged images of the **Hollywood Sign burning** (Reuters/NBC, C). For Gaza, AFP and DW documented AI-generated children and flood scenes — while France 24 (29 May 2025) documented the inverse failure: **real** images of Gazans queueing for aid were falsely accused of being AI-generated. The Mamdani case is the sharpest US example: AI images depicting him with Jeffrey Epstein circulated alongside the Epstein files release, which he called "incredibly difficult"; a Cuomo-linked AI attack ad with racist imagery was condemned across the spectrum (Oct 2025) (C).

## 5. AI-generated fake documents

Document fabrication is the least technically demanding branch and hardest to police, because a synthetic PDF carries no provenance signal by default. Documented patterns: fabricated "leaked" intelligence and law-enforcement documents (including fake FBI-style material), fabricated Ukraine/Biden material, and 2025–26 Epstein-files forgeries. **NewsGuard Reality Check** documented a "'leaked' Trump–Epstein audio clip" that was an AI deepfake (21 Nov 2025) and shocking images falsely presented as being in the Epstein files (3 Feb 2026), then reported that commercial image tools **generated fake Epstein images on request** (5 Feb 2026) and that **AI image-detection tools performed unreliably** (May 2026) (D, medium).

Detection levers remain weak in combination: **C2PA/Content Credentials** (present only if written at capture/generation, routinely stripped by re-encoding or screenshots), container/generator metadata, font-kerning and layout forensics, and template/numbering inconsistencies. **Absence of C2PA is not evidence of forgery** — most genuine material also lacks it.

## 6. Synthetic people: GAN faces, avatars, AI personas

The earliest documented GAN-face influence operation is the **December 2019 Facebook/Graphika takedown** of accounts using **thispersondoesnotexist/generated.photos** profile pictures (WIRED, NYT — C, high). The technique has industrialised: **"Patriot Bait"** (Trend Micro, May 2026) is a five-year, one-operator influence-and-fraud operation built on a single AI persona (D, medium). Meta disrupted an **Iranian fake-persona network** in March 2026; OpenAI has disclosed Russian content farms pairing LLMs with image/video generators to mass-produce human-seeming personas, and the 2024 Israeli firm **STOIC** campaign it flagged targeted US, Canadian and Indian audiences (D, medium). Synthetic presenters have moved from novelty to infrastructure — AI "news anchor" formats, AI avatars as "eyewitnesses" in the Gaza information war, political AI influencers; commercial prototypes (**Lilmiquela**, **Shudu**) established the format. **Scale:** a UK poll reported 30 May 2026 found roughly **one in three voters (≈16 million)** had seen AI deepfake content of candidates ahead of local elections (C, medium).

## 7. The "liar's dividend"

**Origin.** Coined by **Bobby Chesney and Danielle Citron**, "Deep Fakes: A Looming Challenge for Privacy, Democracy, and National Security", *California Law Review* 107 (2019) (A/B): as the public learns deepfakes are realistic, *false* claims that real evidence is synthetic become more credible.

**Empirical tests.**

- **Ternovski, Kalla & Aronow**, *Journal of Online Trust and Safety* 1(2) — informing voters about deepfakes reduced belief in **both** fake and real footage (B, high).
- **Vaccari & Chadwick** (2020), *Social Media + Society* — synthetic political video produced "generalized uncertainty" and reduced trust in news generally (B, high).
- **Schiff, Schiff & Bueno**, *American Political Science Review* (2024) — false misinformation claims yield measurable reputational benefit to politicians (B, high).
- **Grohmann, Halle & Appel**, *Psychology of Popular Media* (accepted 2026; doi 10.1037/ppm0000665), N = 182. Politicians who falsely called genuine footage a deepfake were rated **significantly higher on leadership ability** (M ≈ 4.39/4.07) than those who apologised (M ≈ 2.97/3.01), F(1,178) = 50.01, p < .001, η²p = .22; recipients were significantly more likely to misidentify the **authentic** video as a deepfake (F(1,178) = 21.36, p < .001, η²p = .11). Direct peer-reviewed confirmation (B, high).
- **Clark & Lewandowsky et al.**, "The continued influence of AI-generated deepfake videos despite transparency warnings", *Communications Psychology* (Nature), **2 January 2026**, three preregistered experiments (N = 175, 275, 223). Most participants **still relied on a deepfake video's content after being explicitly warned it was fake** — including those who said they believed the warning: 53.8%, 44.7% and 50.0% across the three experiments still judged the person guilty from the video. A specific warning also caused participants to misclassify a **real** video as a deepfake (Exp 2: M = 0.97 vs −0.18), while a generic warning lowered guilt perception **without** convincing anyone the video was fake — diffuse cynicism. The authors invoke the liar's dividend and advise against generic warnings (B, high).

**Observed in state practice.** Iran's January 2026 crackdown produced a documented inversion: a **genuine** viral photograph of a lone man confronting security forces was discredited by regime outlets circulating *altered copies of the same photo* to argue the original was fake — an "epistemic fog" (D/C, medium). India's 2023 leaked-audio case and the Réffitt and Tesla "deepfake defence" courtroom attempts are earlier instances (C/B, medium).

## 8. Responses and defences

- **Provenance, not detection.** C2PA/Content Credentials plus capture-time signing is the only approach that scales; NewsGuard's May 2026 finding of unreliable AI-image detectors undercuts detection-first strategy.
- **Targeted, not generic, warnings** — generic warnings backfire by producing blanket cynicism (Clark & Lewandowsky).
- **Regulation arriving unevenly:** FCC's February 2024 ruling; EU AI Act Article 50 deepfake-disclosure duty; spreading US state deepfake-election laws before the 2026 midterms; India's AI-video labelling mandate; Japan amending two election laws (July 2026); the NO FAKES Act advancing in the US Senate (June 2026). Enforcement lags generation — the Kramer acquittal shows the regulatory/criminal gap.
- **Speed and chain of custody beat cleverness**: the Pentagon image was neutralised in ~20 minutes; the Slovak clip's damage concentrated in the hours before any institution responded.

## Gaps and cautions

- **Cheap fake vs deepfake.** Several cases popularly labelled deepfakes are re-cut or re-voiced real footage; where sources disagree this brief marks the technique contested.
- **Not corroborated / recall gaps:** the "$500 bn wiped" figure; named 2025–26 AI influencer personas ("Ayleen", "Mia Zelu"); and the **Bangladesh and Senegal** deepfake cases — extensive searching produced nothing above grade C with specific verified detail. These are gaps, not negative findings.
- **Attribution caution:** the Slovak clip's sponsor has never been established to grade A; pro-Kremlin amplification is reported (VSquare, Bloomberg) but not proven.
- **English-language bias:** Misbar (Arabic) and Dubawa (West African) were reachable; much Global South fact-checking is not English-indexed and is under-represented.
