## 2. Pravda network / Portal Kombat: mass-produced pseudo-media

Viginum's February 2024 investigation identified **at least 193** "information portals" producing **no original content**, republishing pro-Russian Telegram channels, state agencies and official sites, with automation and search-engine optimisation carrying distribution. A second report (14 Feb 2024) attributed site creation to the Crimean firm **TigerWeb** and its founder Evgueni Chevtchenko, and noted **RRN/Doppelgänger** accounts artificially amplifying Portal Kombat. A third (29 Apr 2024) raised the count to **224** and documented an "information grid" across nearly every EU member state weeks before the June 2024 European elections.

**Correction to a common premise:** as of 25 September 2026 the SGDSN/Viginum index contains **no separate February 2025 "Pravda" technical report** carrying domain/article counts. Viginum's February 2025 output is the three-year synthesis (24 Feb 2025), which says only "over 200 websites". The granular counts come from **DFRLab/Check First** and **ISD**. (Negative finding, medium-high confidence.) Similarly, the "**33% of AI answers**" figure traces to an American Sunlight Project/NewsGuard-linked study cited by ISD; ISD's own testing found popular LLMs **rarely cite** Pravda links — a *claimed* effect that did not replicate cleanly.

The network's innovation is **volume as credibility**. ISD recorded **919 third-party sites** linking to Pravda over twelve months; **81%** treated it as credible, many with >1m monthly visits. Because search engines and LLMs read links as trust signals, even refutations amplify the source. DFRLab separately found Pravda links used heavily in **Wikipedia** citations and **X Community Notes** — trusted surfaces repurposed as laundering channels.

EU restrictive measures cover information manipulation (Council hybrid-threats packages of 21 May 2025, 16 Mar and 21 Apr 2026), but this research **could not verify** that Portal Kombat, TigerWeb or Chevtchenko are named in them; the Council pages returned 403 to automated retrieval. Treat such claims as **unverified** pending manual reading of the legal acts.

## 3. Indian Chronicles: the canonical fake-expert architecture

- **2019 ("265"):** **265+ fake local news sites in 65+ countries**, mostly named after defunct local papers or spoofing real outlets. EU DisinfoLab named the mechanism directly: layering outlets that "quote and republish one another" to make the manipulation untraceable, offering a "**mirage** of international support" and "multiplying iterations of the same content available on search engines". That is the operational definition of false amplification.
- **2020 ("Indian Chronicles"):** a **15-year** operation since **2005** using **10+ UN Human Rights Council-accredited NGOs** — mostly real NGOs *resurrected* after dissolution, including the Canners International Permanent Committee. The network also **resurrected a person**: Prof. Louis B. Sohn, who died in 2006, was listed as attending a UN HRC meeting in 2007 and a 2011 Washington event. Identity thefts included former European Parliament President Martin Schulz's name and former UK minister James Purnell's photograph. Scale: **750+ fake outlets in 119 countries**, **550+ domains**, syndication in **at least 97 countries**; the associated Big News Network / World News Network comprised **500+ fake local media in 95 countries**.
- **Institutional capture:** 27 MEPs were taken on a Kashmir trip presented as an official EU delegation; **11 MEPs** published op-eds in the fake outlet *EU Chronicle* within six months of launch. India's ANI repackaged the Brussels/Geneva output as the work of "independent media".

This is the template later operations copy: fabricate the **institution** — NGO, think tank, journal, fact-checker — not merely the claim.

## 4. Fake experts, fake think tanks, fake fact-checkers

- **Fake credentialed scientists.** EU DisinfoLab's study of **1,168** fact-checked Italian COVID-19 items found **74%** of political disinformation came from populist politicians (**54%** one party) and **42%** of items were wholly fabricated; 779 sources accumulated **7.4 million** reactions. A Disinfo2024 presentation documented "**Doctor Lysov**", a fabricated scientific authority invented to lend a book on LGBT rhetoric "the light of scientific facts".
- **Real-fake think tanks.** On **25 August 2026** OpenAI reported disrupting a covert Russian operation whose distinctive element was a **fake Israeli think tank staffed with real researchers' names**, whose "research" recycled **stolen academic papers** and was distributed via ChatGPT accounts. This defeats simple "does this person exist?" verification: the named staff are real and findable.
- **Fake fact-checkers.** Viginum's *Matriochka* (June 2024) documented fabricated reports, graffiti and memes impersonating North American and European media and personalities, pushed directly **at journalists and fact-checking units in 60+ countries** with demands they investigate. Russian-language "fake fact-checks" — content formatted as debunks that propagate the false claim — were documented by ProPublica (8 Mar 2022) and The Moscow Times (21 Feb 2023); the pseudo-fact-check channel **"War on Fakes" (Война с фейками)** was profiled by Business Insider (11 Mar 2022) and Time (15 Mar 2023), and the wider "assault on fact-checkers" by CFR (4 Nov 2024). The **"Лапша"/"Noodle"** project and the **FactCheckArmenia** case are named in the brief but **could not be verified from primary sources in this pass** — flagged as open tasks, not asserted (confidence: low).
- **Industrial pseudo-news.** NewsGuard with Pangram Labs reported **3,006 AI content farms** (March 2026), **more than doubled in a year**, growing **300–500 sites/month**; **358** tied to pro-Russian **Storm-1516**. Sites mimic local newspapers and seed stories later amplified by Russian state media. False amplification is now automated: the "many outlets" need no human operators.

## 5. Fabricated science and paper mills

- **Record retractions.** **>10,000** papers retracted in 2023 (*Nature*, 12 Dec 2023). Hindawi alone retracted **8,000+** in 2023 — more than all publishers combined in any prior year — from an initial **500** in Sept 2022, banning "several hundred" guest editors and costing Wiley **$35–40m**; the brand was retired. Springer Nature retracted **2,923** in 2024. Retraction Watch reported (14 Mar 2024) that up to **one in seven** submissions to hundreds of Wiley journals were flagged by a paper-mill detection tool.
- **Detection science.** Guillaume Cabanac's "**tortured phrases**" work and the **Problematic Paper Screener** industrialised detection; a "tortured acronyms" detector followed (*Science*, 31 May 2024) and Springer Nature adopted tortured-phrase tooling in July 2025.
- **The AI flood.** *Science* (4 Aug 2025) reported **one-fifth** of computer-science papers may include AI content; arXiv moved to block AI-generated survey papers after a "flood" of low-quality submissions (Nov 2025); *Nature* described an "AI slop" crisis in computer science (13 Feb 2026). The consequence is that fabricated expert reports no longer need a paper mill's infrastructure, and that genuine peer-reviewed venues can be cited as cover for content that never passed review.

## 6. Assessment

Three shifts matter. Fabrication has moved **up the trust hierarchy**: fake news sites (2019) → fake UN-accredited NGOs (2020) → fake experts with fake books (2024) → fake think tanks staffed with real people's names (2026). Second, **the amplifier is now often legitimate** — 81% of ISD's sampled sites cited the Pravda network credulously, and linking confers credibility even when refuting. Third, **verification heuristics that work on individuals fail on institutions**: name, affiliation and even publication checks can be satisfied by a fabricated body whose members are real. Durable defences are registry verification (ECOSOC/UN accreditation history, DOI and indexing status, domain-registration timelines), link-provenance analysis, and treating backlinks and citation counts as compromised signals.
