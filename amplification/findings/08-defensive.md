# Defensive practice: how institutions avoid becoming part of an amplification loop

**Study strand:** final section of *Agentic amplification of ambiguous physical threats*
**Research cut-off:** 25 September 2026 · **Scope:** defensive and analytic only
**Grades:** A official/primary · B peer-reviewed · C news · D expert/NGO/think-tank · E vendor/advocacy

---

## Summary

The evidence converges on one finding: **the documented amplification mechanism in ambiguous physical threats is institutional, not informational.** The best-evidenced links are the defender's own responses — closing an airport on unverified sightings, emergency counter-drone procurement, premature attribution — while evidence that an adversary engineers that loop remains thin and prebunking decays within weeks. Mature practice therefore looks like **process discipline**: a published confirmation standard, a decision gate before public naming, separation of observation from confirmed drone, and monitoring one's own response. No institution has assembled that package, and several disagree with themselves.

---

## 1. Attribution discipline

**Mature practice.** Public attribution as a *decisional, decision-gated, probabilistic* act: separate likelihood from evidential confidence, publish the probability lexicon, record who approved the naming, and accept that naming is optional even when guilt is clear.

**Evidence.** The Dutch model is best documented: AIVD/MIVD named LAUNDRY BEAR as "highly probably Russian state-supported" (27 May 2025), with a released *Beslisnota* showing ministerial approval ([AIVD/MIVD](https://www.aivd.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2025/05/27/aivd-en-mivd-onderkennen-nieuwe-russische-cyberactor/Advisory+AIVD+en+MIVD+Public+report+on+new+cyber+actor.pdf), [Kamerbrief](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2025/05/27/kamerbrief-inzake-attributie-nieuwe-russische-cyberactor)) (A). The UK publishes the clearest yardstick — seven probability bands (Remote chance >0–5% … Almost certain 95–<100%) plus separate High/Moderate/Low confidence ([PHIA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment/explaining-uncertainty-in-uk-intelligence-assessment)) (A). Germany's BaKS *Arbeitspapier* 3/2023 supplies the argument for silence — *"even if authorship for an incident is unequivocally established, there may be political circumstances under which it appears opportune not to attribute"* — and warns that misattributions *"weaken the credibility of the attribution process"*, concluding that *"attribution is at most part of deterrence, but not the best-suited instrument for it either"* ([BaKS](https://www.baks.bund.de/de/arbeitspapiere/2023/cyberangreifer-benennen-globale-normen-staerken-erfahrungen-mit-dem)) (A; author flags personal opinion). The EU supplies the escape clause — attribution "remains a sovereign political decision" and *"not all measures … require attribution"* ([Council 9916/17](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-9916-2017-INIT/en/pdf)); NATO keeps it permissive, with Allies "may consider, **when appropriate**, attributing hybrid activities" ([Vilnius 2023](https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/official_texts_217320.htm)) (A).

**Costs.** Over-attribution damages credibility (BaKS); fragmented attribution weakens EU cyber diplomacy (SWP); unverifiable claims are *"politically manipulable"* (ETH CSS 244). Naming does not reliably change behaviour — AIVD's 2025 report records the actor *"has not ceased its activities"* afterwards (A).

**Negative findings.** *Überattribution* could **not** be sourced to any BfV or official document — do not attribute it. No official document states criteria for **when not** to attribute, and all retrieved doctrine is cyber-centric: the bridge to physical events exists only in NATO's "hybrid activities" language.

**Failure mode.** Premature naming ahead of technical confirmation; conflating likelihood with evidential confidence.

## 2. Crisis communication about uncertainty

**Mature practice.** Acknowledge uncertainty *and* show the process that will reduce it; never over-reassure; fixed cadence even with no news; one consistent voice.

**Evidence.** WHO makes uncertainty disclosure a **strong recommendation on moderate-quality evidence**: *"include explicit information about uncertainties … and indicate what is known and not known at a given time"* ([WHO 2018](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550208)) (A). CDC's CERC manual — which declares the "first 24 hours" rule obsolete — states *"an over-reassured public isn't the goal"* and tells communicators to *"acknowledge uncertainty … share with them the process you are using to get more information"* ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/index.html)) (A). The peer-reviewed evidence says the amplifier is **conflict, not uncertainty**: negative effects come from studies operationalising uncertainty as disagreement (Gustafson & Rice 2020, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963662520942122)) (B). The clearest unknown-cause script is Dutch: NCTV directs agencies not to explain cause prematurely, to practise "informed waiting" (*"process information is also information"*), and not to rebut every false claim ([NCTV 2024](https://www.nctv.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2024/07/26/koepelnotitie-risico--en-crisiscommunicatie-bij-extreem-geweld-en-terrorisme/250604_Koepelnotitie+EGT.pdf)) (A). When findings cannot be published, publish the *process statistic*: the 17 December 2024 US statement offered "more than 5,000 reported … approximately 100 leads generated" ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/dhs-fbi-faa-dod-joint-statement-ongoing-response-reported-drone-sightings)) (A).

**Failure mode.** Institutional inconsistency — agencies, or a minister, contradicting each other — rather than uncertainty itself.

## 3. Incident verification and anomaly triage

**Mature practice.** Verify before declaring; keep observation, confirmed drone and hostile drone as three separate findings; rule out conventional traffic via ADS-B; treat "unknown object" as a legitimate published category.

**Evidence.** EASA's *Drone Incident Management at Aerodromes* (2021) requires pre-agreed verification methods *"to establish if the information on the drone is credible enough to declare a drone incident"*, while conceding it is *"often difficult to confirm reliably a sighting"* ([EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/drone_incident_management_at_aerodromes_part1_website_suitable.pdf)) (A). The UK Airprox Board publishes an explicit triage rule — a positively described drone-like object counts as a drone, a vague "object" as unknown ([UKAB](https://www.airproxboard.org.uk/topical-issues-and-themes/drones/)) (A). The FAA's quarterly *Reported UAS Sightings* series has **no disposition field** and shows no surge during the New Jersey panic — November 2024 (103) was the lowest month that winter ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/uas/resources/public_records/uas_sightings_report)) (A). US findings attributed most sightings to lawful and hobbyist drones, law-enforcement drones, and *"manned fixed-wing aircraft, helicopters, and stars"*. Germany's BKA logged roughly 1,300 drone case files in 2025, locating pilots only "rarely"; Denmark is the sharpest contradiction: Defence concluded drones were present on ~200 "special reports" resting mainly on soldiers' observations, while five police districts found no evidence and police retracted their earlier "verified drone" claim — *"an observation alone is not sufficient to confirm a drone flight that poses a security risk."*

**Failure modes.** No institution publishes a confirmation rate — national headlines are all *report* counts. Multi-source convergence is treated as self-validating, though UK JDP 2-00 §3.28 sets a two-source rule *and* warns adversaries "will plan multiple-source deception".

## 4. Detecting synthetic corroboration

**Mature practice.** Detection as probabilistic triage, never proof; provenance as one input; verify the referent; publish each technique's limits.

**Evidence.** Meta publishes the most concrete coordinated-inauthentic-behaviour indicators: reused proxy infrastructure, recycled analytics tags, personas reused years apart, identical ad copy within two days ([Meta H2 2025](https://github.com/facebook/threat-research/blob/main/indicators/meta-h2-2025-russia-based-cib-network-2.md)) (A/E). C2PA/Content Credentials — governed by Adobe, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Sony, TikTok and the BBC — defines a "Durable Content Credential" meant to survive removal ([spec](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.1/specs/C2PA_Specification.html), [members](https://c2pa.org/membership/)) (A). EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties took effect 2 August 2026 ([EC FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)) (A). Manual OSINT practice — geolocation, chronolocation, reverse-image search, ADS-B cross-reference, as documented by Bellingcat — remains the workhorse method.

**Limits — severe.** C2PA is opt-in and capture-side: **absence of credentials does not mean fake**, post-transcoding recovery is unsolved, and C2PA's own *Security Considerations* lists specVersion downgrade, UI and homoglyph spoofing and trust-list phishing ([C2PA](https://c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/security/Security_Considerations.html)) (A). Detector false positives are structural: GPT detectors flag non-native English as AI ([Patterns 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)) (B). The decisive negative result: DFRLab examined 1,651 accounts amplifying Storm-1516 and found *"no conclusive evidence about their authenticity and … no clear indicators of coordinated inauthentic behavior"* ([DFRLab](https://dfrlab.org/2026/09/17/storm-1516-operation-targets-the-baltic-states/)) (D) — **amplification overlap is not proof of coordination.**

**Failure mode.** Treating absent provenance as evidence of fabrication, and letting detection claims become an amplification vector — NewsGuard's 2026 tracker records a rise in accusations that *authentic* reporting is AI-generated.

## 5. Not amplifying — the central dilemma

**Mature practice (contested).** No single rule. The defensible position is a *triage*: when a narrative is not yet widespread, pair national-scale silence with targeted engagement where the narrative lives; attack amplification mechanics rather than repeating the claim; and coordinate so multiple official voices do not each add reach.

**Evidence.** The cleanest official "do not amplify" statement is EU, not UK. The EEAS *2nd FIMI Threat Report* lists **"Ignore"** as a countermeasure: *"While it may be tempting to directly confront manipulated information, doing so can inadvertently increase the damage … any reactive response should be avoided"* ([EEAS, Jan 2024](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/2024/EEAS-2nd-Report%20on%20FIMI%20Threats-January-2024_0.pdf)) (A). UK Cabinet Office guidance says it operationally: *"Think before you respond to any reports of disinformation. This may inadvertently amplify the suspected disinformation and could make the matter worse. If an official response is required, use official channels and avoid referencing the disinformation"* ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/security-guidance-for-may-2021-elections/online-disinformation-and-ai-threat-guidance-for-electoral-candidates-and-officials)) (A). The counter-argument is bluntest from NATO's StratCom COE: *"A further risk is amplifying ineffective FIMI campaigns unnecessarily. Given the limited engagement achieved by Doppelgänger and Spamouflage, arguably they received far more media and political attention than their impact warranted"*; *"Decisions on attribution should factor in that public exposure could amplify the FIMI campaign"*; *"we know remarkably little about what counter-FIMI interventions are effective"* ([Colley, Jul 2026](https://stratcomcoe.org/publications/download/Counter-FIMI_The-Case-for-Disrupting-Hostile-Actors-FINAL-5d539.pdf)) (A; disclaims representing NATO policy). Carnegie's review synthesises it: pair silence at national scale with targeted messaging where the narrative lives, and beware "perception hacking" ([Carnegie 2024](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2024/01/countering-disinformation-effectively-an-evidence-based-policy-guide)) (D). The scholarly anchor is Donovan & boyd's *"Stop the Presses? Moving From Strategic Silence to Strategic Amplification"* ([Am Behav Sci](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0002764219878229)) (B, paywalled).

**Institutions disagree with themselves.** The EEAS publishes both the "Ignore" doctrine and named attribution reports; its 4th report (March 2026) records 540 FIMI incidents in 2025 — 29% attributed to Russia, 6% to China, **65% unattributed** — and concedes *"Exposure alone does not stop operations"* ([EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en)) (A). That report links FIMI directly to physical incursions: drone incidents *"were accompanied by FIMI designed to manage public perception and test responses."* In the UK, Cabinet Office caution coexists with GCS *RESIST 2* (2021), which prescribes the "truth sandwich" and asserts repetition risk is "generally lower than of not responding at all" — uncited, and contradicted by its first direct test (Swire-Thompson et al. 2025, N=1,046) (B).


**Failure mode.** The core hypothesis — that publishing a hoax attribution increases its reach — **is untested by any study found**, while the cost of silence is unquantified.

## 6. Public reporting protocols

**Mature practice.** Ask for observations, not interpretations; template them as what/where/when; publish pre-authorised flight registers so legitimate activity can be discounted; route reports to a body that can act.

**Evidence.** The Dutch NCTV template is the best designed found: record date and time; the drone's flight route, estimated height, colour, payload and distance to buildings and people; and collect photos, videos and witness statements for police or Marechaussee — while conceding *"it is difficult to distinguish a drone that poses a threat from a harmless one"* ([NCTV](https://www.nctv.nl/onderwerpen/drones)) (A). UK Counter-Terrorism Policing publishes the only clean yield figure found: *"Around a fifth of reports from the public provide information useful to our investigators"* ([ACT](https://act.campaign.gov.uk/reports-save-lives/)) (A) — and gives **no** reporter feedback by design. DHS's template includes a subjective "why it's suspicious" field with no confidence field ([DHS](https://www.dhs.gov/publication/if-you-see-something-say-something%E2%84%A2-campaign-overview)) (A).

**False-positive evidence.** The best available — journalistic, not official — is the *Trouw*/Dronewatch analysis of **61 alleged drone incidents in 11 countries (9 Sep–27 Nov 2025)**: 14 were not drones, 41 unknown, 3 confirmed Russian (all bordering Ukraine), 3 hobby/tourist — **55 of 61 unconfirmed** ([Dronewatch](https://www.dronewatch.eu/61-european-drone-sightings-analysed-heres-what-we-know/)) (C). Misidentifications included a police helicopter circulated as a "huge drone" by Belgium's defence minister, and bright stars. The cheapest missing filter: **no jurisdiction publishes a "was this authorised?" disposition**, nor joins its pre-authorisation register to its sighting-report stream.

**Failure mode.** Asking for interpretations rather than observations multiplies false positives; withholding feedback removes any learning loop; no channel publishes its own false-positive rate.

## 7. Prebunking as institutional defence

**Mature practice.** One layer, not a shield: targeted, boosted, paired with debunks, with realistic expectations about decay.

**Evidence.** The only pooled estimate retrieved is pre-digital and modest (Banas & Rains 2010, *d*=0.43, with no significant effect of the theory's own threat component; [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637751003758193)) (B). Modern field effects are small: the 2022 *Science Advances* RCT reported Cohen's *h*=0.09 and **missed its own preregistered smallest effect size of interest** ([Roozenbeek & van der Linden](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abo6254)) (B). The largest campaign run — 12 EU nations, N=19,735, 120M+ YouTube users reached — reported *d*≈0.08–0.38 with Google's own brand-lift at 1.53% ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00379-3)) (B). A 2025 *Royal Society Open Science* study across 967,640 X users found *"no meaningful changes in posting or retweeting"*, with only ~7.5% exposed ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.251377)) (B). Decay is fast: text and video inoculation survived about a month, game-based inoculation lost significance within roughly two weeks, while a *memory* booster retained 100% of effect at 30 days ([Maertens et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57205-x), N=11,759) (B). The active ingredient is contested: prebunking worked *"only when the forewarning was omitted"*, and Bruns et al. (2024, N=5,228) found **debunks beat prebunks**. Institutional uptake is weaker than assumed: the Commission's own JRC states that *"debunking is slightly more effective"*, WHO never uses the term, and CISA's and Google/Jigsaw's prebunking pages now 404 (A/D).

**Failure mode.** Treating prebunking as population-scale immunity and substituting it for verification, decision gates and non-amplification discipline.

## 8. The reflexivity trap

**Mature practice.** Treat the response as an observable and a variable: pre-commit a confirmation threshold before closure, deployment or procurement; log the operational cause of every reported sighting (including your own assets); publish effectiveness data; keep an independent verification channel (ADS-B, OSINT) able to contradict the official reading.

**Evidence — the sequence is documented at every link.** Gatwick, 19–21 December 2018: an unresolved sighting event closed a runway and affected ~120,000 (DfT) to >140,000 (Sussex Police) passengers; military counter-UAS capability deployed; the DfT Cross-Whitehall report (Feb 2019) states *"Gatwick is now urgently procuring additional commercial capabilities"*; the UK Counter-Unmanned Aircraft Strategy (CP 187, Oct 2019) enshrined Gatwick as its threat exemplar; a £142m UK programme (MoD, Dec 2025) was justified by *"Russian-linked drone incursions across Europe"* (A). The self-generated-sighting mechanism is documented: Sussex Police's chief constable conceded later sightings may have been of a **police** drone, and the National Police Air Service recorded no drone sightings at Gatwick that month. In 2025, 55 of 61 European incidents went unconfirmed — yet Belgium bought Senhive detection and DroneShield jammers, the Netherlands ordered 100 IRIS radars and SkyRanger cannon, and Belgian parliamentarians questioned both the communication and the procurement.

**Official acknowledgements exist but stop short of the full proposition.** The Commission's *Action Plan on Drone and Counter-Drone Security* (COM(2026) 81, 11 Feb 2026) says unidentified overflights *"increase security tension and act as a signalling tool, testing [the] Union's preparedness"* ([EC](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d2161887-6c06-424c-a8e6-efd46f50e4e8_en?filename=Action_Plan_on_Drone_and_Counter_Drone_Security.pdf)). The FAA warns that detection deployment *"may provoke response actions that disrupt air traffic operations … second-order effects"*, and that *"detection systems do not have the ability to determine intent or the level of threat"* ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/airports/new_entrants/uas_detection_mitigation_response)) (A). Kasperson's framework treats institutions as *"amplification stations"* (B); Dronewatch describes *"a self-reinforcing mechanism"* in which *"the media became part of the observation chain"* (C).

**Failure mode — and the honest limit.** **No document states the full proposition** that closing an airport "proves" the threat and rewards the attacker; the trap is our inference from verified sequencing plus documented mechanism, not an official finding. No false-positive, false-negative or effectiveness rate exists for any national counter-UAS system, and the Netherlands Court of Audit (2026) criticised the *legal basis* of accelerated procurement while accepting the minister's urgency (A).

## 9. Indicators and warnings

**Mature practice.** Use published taxonomies as shared vocabulary, not detectors; expect indicators to be probabilistic and staleable; monitor the defender's own response alongside the adversary's.

**Evidence.** The EEAS FIMI analytical model (ABCDE framework, TTP catalogue, STIX sharing) is the best-documented open taxonomy, alongside the DISARM/AMITT technique catalogue as applied in Hybrid CoE Research Report 7 (A). The EU's own numbers undercut confident attribution: only ~7% of the mapped network is Russian-attributed and ~2% Chinese-attributed against **~91% unattributed-but-aligned**, and the EEAS itself reports AI-generated FIMI had "limited overall impact" (A). The sharpest published limit is Hybrid CoE's (Rietjens 2020): Cold War indicators were "predictive, diagnostic, unambiguous and collectible", whereas hybrid indicators often are not (B/D).

**An addition this study can offer.** Every indicator set located is **actor-side**. An institutional-response set — published, countable, hard to conceal — would track airport suspensions attributed to unconfirmed sightings; emergency counter-UAS procurement authorisations; and attribution statements issued ahead of technical confirmation. Offered as framing, not a validated detector.

**Failure mode.** Indicator lists are unvalidated as detectors; no false-positive or attribution-error rate has been published for influence-operation detection; no study quantifies temporal synchronisation between narrative spikes and physical sightings; and because indicators are public they are staleable.

## Where institutions disagree

- **Denmark (2026):** Defence concluded drones were present on soldiers' observations; five police districts found no evidence, and police retracted their own "verified drone" claim.
- **The UK and the EU, each with itself:** Cabinet Office "think before you respond" versus GCS *RESIST 2*'s truth sandwich; the EEAS lists "Ignore" as a countermeasure in one report series while publishing named attributions in the same series.
- **Germany:** BaKS argues it may be "opportune not to attribute"; Germany publicly attributed the August 2026 Leipzig drone to Russia.
- **The US (2024):** DHS stated there were *"no electronic detections to confirm the visual reports"* while the episode was politically amplified as a threat.

## What is not known

1. **Whether publishing a hoax attribution increases its reach** — the study's core hypothesis is untested.
2. **Any confirmation rate.** No institution publishes reported-versus-confirmed figures; national headline numbers are *report* counts.
3. **Any counter-UAS performance data** — no official false-alarm, false-negative or detection-probability figures exist.
4. **Whether uncertainty-transparent messaging reduces amplification** — no study measures amplification as an outcome of an uncertainty manipulation in an ambiguous incident.
5. **Whether public feedback increases or decreases reporting volume** — unanswered, though UK ACT deliberately withholds feedback.
6. **No published criteria for when *not* to attribute**, no physical-domain attribution doctrine beyond NATO's "hybrid activities" language, and no doctrine contemplating AI/agentic amplification — current doctrine assumes hours-long incidents, not multi-week ambient ambiguity.
7. **Citation hygiene:** the widely repeated "2023 *Science Advances* cross-cultural prebunking RCT" does not exist as described, and *Überattribution* is not an official BfV usage. Drop both.
8. **Method limitation:** `web_search` was inoperable throughout; discovery relied on news RSS, site search, sitemaps and direct retrieval, biasing coverage toward English-language material. Some absences are "not found", not "does not exist".

## Confidence

**High (A/B, corroborated):** attribution as decision-gated and probabilistic; uncertainty disclosure as official doctrine; conflict rather than uncertainty as the amplifier; EASA's verify-before-declaring framework; the absence of any published confirmation rate; C2PA's structural limits; the Gatwick closure→procurement→strategy sequence.

**Medium:** the 55-of-61 figure (rigorous but journalistic); institutional overreaction as the dominant amplification mechanism (well-supported sequencing, no doctrinal statement).

**Low / contested:** whether publicising a coordinated campaign helps or harms; the real-world effect of alert fatigue; any claim about adversary intent behind the 2024–2026 sighting waves.
