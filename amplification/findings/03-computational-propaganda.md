# 03 — Covert influence operations and coordinated inauthentic behaviour, 2023–2026

**Question:** has AI changed the *mechanism* of covert influence, or only the *volume*?

**Research cut-off:** 25 September 2026. **Scope:** defensive/analytic; methods are described only as needed to judge capability and defence, with no operational guidance.

**Grades:** **A** primary/official · **B** peer-reviewed/high-quality investigative · **C** reputable news · **D** vendor/think-tank · **E** anonymous/unverified. Claims are tagged **DEMONSTRATED**, **CLAIMED** (asserted by an authoritative actor, not independently measured) or **INFERRED** (analyst judgement).

---

## Headline judgements

1. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** AI has decisively changed the *production and concealment* layer: volume, multilingual localisation, persona construction, attribution laundering, and internal "programme-office" work (doctrine, target databases, staff scoring). The EEAS recorded a **259% rise in AI-related FIMI incidents (41 → 147 cases)** in 2025 — **27% of all detected incidents** ([EEAS, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en)).
2. **DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high).** AI has *not* demonstrably changed *reach or persuasion*. The same report states "much of the AI-generated material in 2025 remains low-quality… resulting in limited overall impact as organic engagement remains low", naming **Storm-1516 and Operation Overload** as producing videos "easily identifiable as inauthentic by average viewers". Anthropic's cases converge: most content "drew little or no authentic engagement". **One important exception (see §4):** when a platform's recommender rewards engagement-shaped content, a network of only ~40 accounts can reach hundreds of millions — reach is then *rented from the platform*, not manufactured by the personas.
3. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** The clearest *qualitative* change is organisational, not rhetorical: persistent-memory "agent" workspaces, doctrine files reused across hundreds of sessions, shared approved-source and banned-word lists, and semi-autonomous batch pipelines. AI acts as a **coordination substrate** letting producers who never communicate behave as one network.
4. **CLAIMED, not measured (D/C, low–medium).** That AI makes influence *more persuasive*; and that high-volume networks deliberately "groom" LLMs so assistants reproduce their narratives. Both are plausible and partly evidenced; neither has a real-world effect size.
5. **INFERRED (medium).** The measurable harms sit **upstream of the public**: newsroom and fact-checker workload, contamination of the search/Wikipedia/LLM *citation layer*, harassment of named individuals, and diplomatic embarrassment — not mass attitude change.

Three propositions must be separated: **volume/velocity** (demonstrated), **verisimilitude** (contested), and **coordination/agency** (demonstrated at case level, unquantified in aggregate). The defences differ accordingly: volume is a platform-integrity and cost problem, verisimilitude a provenance problem, agency a detection and attribution problem.

## 2. Persona management and synthetic corroboration

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — AI removed the human bottleneck.** Anthropic documents full personas with AI-generated profile photos and invented biographies for fake reporters, fabricated political spokespeople, impersonation of a real state spokesperson and a real human-rights organisation, and forged government documents. One commercial network paired each of ~70 fake outlets with a matching social account and staffed its "newsroom" with bylines for journalists who **did not exist**.

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — the architecture is industrialised.** The recurring pattern is persistent memory files holding doctrine, banned-word lists, approved sources and evasion rules; prompts demanding fixed output schemas; and batch tooling calling the model without a human per item. One operator was building a **training course** to teach the workflow. That is the difference between a hundred people writing a hundred posts and one person running a content factory.

**DEMONSTRATED (B/A, high) — "false amplification" is a named, long-standing technique that AI has now automated.** EU DisinfoLab's *Indian Chronicles* remains the canonical case: a **15-year** operation (from 2005) using **10+ UN Human Rights Council-accredited NGOs** — mostly real NGOs *resurrected* after dissolution — **750+ fake outlets in 119 countries** and 550+ domains, with outlets quoting and republishing one another to create a "**mirage of international support**". It even resurrected a person: Prof. Louis B. Sohn, dead since 2006, was listed as attending UN and Washington events. What is new is cost: NewsGuard's AI-content-farm series runs from **49 sites (May 2023) to 3,749 (June 2026)**, a ~**76-fold** rise — though NewsGuard itself calls the count a sample, not a census, defined by "no significant human oversight". Relatedly, NewsGuard counted **1,265 pink-slime outlets against 1,213 US daily newspapers** (June 2024) and attributed a **167-site pro-Russia network, 64 of them posing as local news, to John Mark Dougan** — the first documented crossover of pink slime, AI production and Russian disinformation in one network.

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — laundering one source into apparent consensus.** Documented techniques: **cross-border laundering** (rewriting one story in opposite ideological directions for different national audiences; stripping context); **state-attribution stripping** (Sputnik/RIA/CGTN content republished under fabricated bylines as independent reporting); **false verification loops** (one story echoed across nominally separate outlets "so it appeared to be independently confirmed", with the model's own "unverified" caveats removed); and **institutional impersonation** (a front NGO copying a real Swiss organisation's identity, plus ghost-written testimony delivered at the **62nd UN Human Rights Council session**, engineered so neither speech named the sponsoring state).

**DEMONSTRATED (B/C, medium-high) — synthetic media: cheap fakes dominate, and the liar's dividend is the durable harm.** True deepfake *video* is rarer than headlines imply; the dominant technique remains the **"cheap fake"** — re-cut, re-voiced or mislabelled *real* footage. Where synthetic media has mattered, the mechanism is timing and ambiguity, not persuasiveness: the Slovak Šimečka audio (Sep 2023) was released inside the election moratorium, when institutional response is slowest; the fake Pentagon-explosion image (May 2023) moved the S&P 500 ≈0.3% intraday before being debunked in ~20 minutes (the "$500bn wiped" figure is not corroborated). The durable harm is the **liar's dividend**: AI faces are already judged indistinguishable from — and more trustworthy than — real faces ([Nightingale & Farid, *PNAS* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2120481119)), and a 2026 preregistered study found **45–54% of participants who accepted an explicit deepfake warning still judged guilt from the video**, while specific warnings also led people to call *real* video fake ([Clark & Lewandowsky et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00381-9)). **Not demonstrated:** that any synthetic artefact alone flipped an election or caused a documented military or diplomatic decision.

**DEMONSTRATED (B/A, medium-high) — fabrication has moved *up* the trust hierarchy.** The progression runs fake news sites (2019) → fake UN-accredited NGOs (2020) → a fabricated scientific authority (2024) → in August 2026, an OpenAI-disrupted Russian operation running a **fake Israeli think tank staffed with real researchers' names** and recycling **stolen academic papers**. The last case defeats ordinary verification: the named people exist and are findable. Verification heuristics that work on individuals fail on institutions.

**DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high) — one model can replace one trusted person.** The MEK/NCRI case is the purest example: an agent ingested ~8,400 Telegram posts to clone an activist's style, then held live political conversations with his contacts, who are not known to have realised.

**CLAIMED/INFERRED — the citation layer as the real target.** ISD found 919 sites linking Pravda material over a year; of 303 English-language sites reviewed, **81% treated Pravda as credible**, only **3% flagged it as an information operation**, and **34 sites above 1M monthly visits** cited it as reliable; DFRLab found Pravda links in Wikipedia and X Community Notes. The inference that this contaminates retrieval and training is reasonable but untested: the claim that ten AI tools repeated Pravda claims **33% of the time** comes from a **NewsGuard audit (2025)** and **did not replicate** in ISD testing. What is established is the substrate: Pew Research found **~10% of a random July 2026 English web sample showed significant AI authorship**. **Treat "LLM poisoning is working" as unproven.**

**DEMONSTRATED (B) — a commercial sector sells the whole chain.** Team Jorge (Israel, exposed 2023) ran an avatar-creation interface using photos stolen from real people; its headline claims ("33 presidential campaigns, 27 successful") are its **own sales pitch, not verified** ([Guardian, 15 Feb 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/15/revealed-disinformation-team-jorge-claim-meddling-elections-tal-hanan)). Its successors are documented above: LKM Company ran ~70 fabricated newsrooms as an advertising-agency sideline; BBS Bilisim sold a "military-grade, AI-driven" platform with a tunable parameter for artificial views.

**DEMONSTRATED (C, medium) — the target now includes the assistants.** European officials have warned that pro-Russian groups are bombarding AI systems with propaganda to make chatbots reproduce Kremlin talking points ([NBC News, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/europe-warns-russian-groups-seed-ai-chatbots-propaganda-rcna351233)); Politico reported on 25 Sep 2026 that AI chatbots "are quietly spewing partisan talking points". No independent measurement of changed model behaviour attributable to a named operation was available.

## 3. Platform and lab reporting: capability versus outcome

- **Anthropic (Sep 2026, A)** is the most informative record because it sits *upstream* of distribution: nine influence cases spanning six continents, with explicit **Breakout Scale** ratings and a candid limit — "Our visibility into these operations ends once it's live… Most of the content we discovered drew little or no authentic engagement." Its widest authentic reach came where **state media was the distribution mechanism** (radio, satellite, shortwave, TV), not where AI was used; matched output ran from a Telegram post with **~2,000 views** to aired broadcast copy.
- **OpenAI (A)** lists roughly **20 distinct influence-operation disruptions between May 2024 and August 2026** (including Spamouflage, Doppelganger, "Data Center Bandwagon" and an August 2026 covert Russian campaign), emphasising persona creation and detection evasion rather than reach. OpenAI's own investigator assessed the June 2026 "Data Center Bandwagon" case as Breakout Scale 1–2 and said: "We didn't see any signs that it succeeded."
- **Meta (A/D)** publishes recurring network-level takedown data, though its per-quarter CIB counts could **not** be verified from primary sources here; treat precise figures as **unconfirmed**. **Google/YouTube TAG (A)** publishes the most consistent series: >**50,000** Dragonbridge instances disrupted in 2022 and >**65,000** in 2023 (of >57,000 channels disabled, **80% had zero subscribers**), lifetime terminations past **175,000** by early 2024 — and TAG's central finding that the network "achieves practically no organic engagement from real viewers", engagement being "almost entirely inauthentic".
- **X/Twitter (E, low).** Post-2022 research transparency is markedly thinner; no comparable recurring, network-attributed CIB reporting series was retrievable. A **material evidence gap**, not evidence that enforcement stopped. **EEAS (Mar 2026, A)** gives the best aggregate: 540 incidents, ~10,000 channels, 43,000 observables on 19 platforms, **88% of instances on X**; **35% attributed (29% Russia, 6% China), 65% unattributed**; **90.5% of mapped channels covert** versus 7% state-controlled and 4.5% state-linked.

**Agentic behaviour — what is actually documented.** Operators run **persistent, memory-bearing, scheduled, semi-autonomous pipelines with humans setting targets and reviewing output**. Anthropic's Bangladesh case (one actor, 29 rotated accounts, a custom script producing fixed batches of headlines, narratives and image prompts, videos queued to YouTube months ahead) is the clearest; the MEK case adds a **shared** agent platform with per-workspace memory. What is *not* documented in influence is target selection or escalation without human direction.

## 4. Effectiveness: what is known

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — the benchmark measurement.** [Eady et al. (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9) linked a three-wave panel of 1,496 US Twitter users to their timelines: **1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures** (10% for 98%; 1% of Russian accounts supplied 89% of IRA content); median weekly exposure in the final month was **zero**; ~4 IRA posts/user/day versus **106 from national news media** and 35 from politicians; ~**32 million Americans** potentially exposed; estimated effect on vote choice **−0.18 pp for Trump (90% CI −1.15, 0.78)**, with no meaningful relationship to issue positions or polarisation.

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — independent nulls and the right benchmark.** [Bail et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116) matched non-public IRA data to 1,239 partisan Twitter users: only **76** had interacted with an IRA account (44 treated vs 1,106 control); **none of six outcomes** moved, and IRA interactions were **0.1%** of those users' activity. [Coppock, Hill & Vavreck (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046) pooled **59 experiments and ~34,000 people** on real campaign ads: effects are small regardless of context — the crucial benchmark, since a covert campaign has a fraction of that dosage.

**One nuance and one real effect.** [Freelon & Lokot (2023)](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/russian-disinformation-campaigns-on-twitter/) analysed **2,057,747 authentic replies** to IRA accounts across ten communities: reach extended into separate ideological silos, qualifying "preaching to the converted" without overturning the null. [Kim et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2519944123) found geo-racially targeted **voter-suppression ads produced small turnout decreases** — via paid targeting, not organic operations.

**DEMONSTRATED (B) — AI persuasion in the lab is real but narrow.** [Salvi et al. (2024/25)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14380), N=820: debaters facing *personalised* GPT-4 had **81.7% higher odds** of increased agreement than those facing a human (p<0.01), but **without personalisation the advantage was non-significant (p=0.31)**. [Liu, Salvi et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09662) show the advantage is context-dependent and **wanes over repeated interactions**; [Orchinik et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16432) found a brief warning cut belief change by **~48.1%**. These are short-horizon, self-reported, low-dosage effects in volunteers — not votes.

**THE COUNTERWEIGHT (B, high) — small inauthentic networks can rent mass reach from the platform.** ISD measured two pro-Iran X networks of **~40 accounts total** generating **>1 billion views, ~16 million likes and 3.5 million reposts in a single month** during the 2025–26 Iran war. ISD attributes that reach not to the personas but to **X's "For You" recommendation algorithm plus reposts by accounts with >500k followers (≈100 times) and >50k followers (>2,000 times)**. This is the most important corrective in this brief: persona networks cannot manufacture reach, but they can **borrow** it when a platform's recommender rewards engagement-shaped content. It also relocates the defender's leverage — recommendation policy and large-account amplification matter more than debunking individual posts.

**INFERRED (medium) — where effects are real, they are indirect.** (i) **Elite/media agenda penetration**: coverage, official denials and parliamentary questions need no mass belief. (ii) **Harassment and chilling**: the Metric Media ecosystem produced faculty sanctions and terminations — ~40 professors were sanctioned after a single 2025 "watchlist" episode. (iii) **Workload and cost imposition**: fact-checkers are deliberately flooded — Matryoshka demanded they investigate fabricated material. Operations are also frequently self-defeating: Prism News shut down, the Kenyan campaign reached no real people, and Matryoshka published only 7 of 269 posts in German. The classic **backfire effect** explains neither failure nor success — Wood & Porter found one instance across 36 issues ([*Political Behavior*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2018.1493009)) — and the stronger caution is the opposite, the **implied truth effect**: tagging some false content can make untagged false content look truer.

## 5. Detection and takedown

**How networks are found.** *Infrastructure*: registration bursts, shared deployment identifiers, one deployment behind ~70 "independent" sites, prompter residue in favicon filenames. *Behaviour*: strict posting windows, near-identical articles across sites within three minutes, seeder–amplifier splits, shared account-creation timestamps, warm-up logic rotating cookies and IPs. *Content*: translation artefacts, residual source-language text, template reuse, stylometric cloning. *Engagement shape*: high views with disproportionate likes and zero comments. **Frameworks:** Meta's CIB triggers on **behaviour, not falsity**; Google TAG uses audience metrics as the impact test; the **Breakout Scale** measures breakout, not persuasion, and holds that journalists, celebrities and policymakers — not operators — do most of the amplifying.

**Lag and asymmetry.** Public lag figures are **exposure lag, not detection lag**: Matryoshka operated from at least September 2023 and was named in June 2024; Rokh Solis was detected in March 2026 and published in June; Portal Kombat kept growing after exposure. Conversely, **upstream disruption is fast**, so a rising count of lab disruptions measures *production capacity denied*, not audience harm averted. Summing lab disruptions with platform takedowns double-counts the same operations at two stages.

**Limits.** AI-text detection has large, measured false-positive rates — Liang et al. (*Patterns*, 2023) found an **average 61.22% false-positive rate** across seven detectors on 91 human TOEFL essays, with **97.8%** flagged by at least one. Sadasivan et al. (*TMLR*) show recursive paraphrasing defeats watermarking and neural detectors while barely degrading quality, with a theoretical ceiling on any detector's AUROC. Detection therefore migrates to infrastructure and account-level invariants, and independent audit of platform enforcement is essentially absent.

## 6. Where the evidence is thin (explicit flags)

1. **No effect size for AI-enabled influence.** Authoritative sources measure *output* and *inflated reach*; none measures attitude, behaviour or vote change for a 2023–2026 AI-enabled campaign. "AI has qualitatively changed influence effects" is **claimed, not demonstrated**.
2. **The instruments are bounded, not precise.** No field experiment has randomly assigned exposure to foreign influence operations; "exposure" means potential exposure, not attention. Eady's interval (±~1 pp) excludes large effects but not small ones.
3. **Attitudinal effects are asserted far more than measured**, and **the strong form of the synthetic-corroboration hypothesis is untested**: no located study isolates whether a claim repeated by *N* fabricated sources is more believed than the same claim from one source.
4. **"LLM grooming works" is unproven**, self-reported adversary reach circulates uncorrected, and **platform statistics are not comparable or audited** — Meta's per-quarter CIB counts and X's post-2022 reporting could not be verified from primary sources.
5. **Vendor and official naming is inconsistent** (ISD's ~90 Pravda sites vs VIGINUM's 224 portals), **domestic influence-for-hire is systematically under-covered** (the Metric Media ecosystem appears in no FIMI statistic despite larger demonstrated effects), and **attribution confidence varies enormously** — GRU 29155 attribution rests on official assessment, while several Israeli-nexus claims rest on technical indicators at **medium** confidence.

**Citation-integrity note.** The 2023 *Nature Communications* IRA paper is by **Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker**; "Poteat" and "Trexler" do not appear on it. A work titled **"Like, Post, and Mistrust"** attributed to Guess and Nyhan could not be located in Crossref, arXiv, Europe PMC, OpenAlex or the HKS index; do not cite it without a DOI.

## 7. Bottom line

**DEMONSTRATED:** AI has industrialised the supply side of covert influence — production, localisation, persona manufacture, attribution laundering and internal coordination — making one-operator, multi-persona, persistent networks cheap and durable. The frontier of harm has moved toward the **citation layer** (search, Wikipedia, AI assistants), toward **targeted harassment**, and toward **renting reach from recommenders**, not toward mass persuasion.

**NOT DEMONSTRATED:** that AI has made influence operations more persuasive or given them organic reach. Exposure is concentrated, engagement largely synthetic, quality often poor, and official bodies describe reach as limited — with the important exception of algorithmic amplification, where reach is rented rather than owned.

**INFERRED:** the correct framing for 2026 is **cheap, fast, deniable noise plus targeted reputational damage**, not a new era of mass cognitive capture. Three indicators would change this judgement: (a) a documented operation achieving non-rented, organic engagement at scale; (b) measured downstream effects in AI assistant outputs traceable to a named campaign; (c) field-experimental evidence that AI-generated covert content shifts attitudes more than human-written content.

### Method limits

`web_search` was unavailable session-wide; discovery used news-RSS and Bing-News RSS search, publisher site search, sitemaps and direct retrieval. Several sources returned 403/bot-blocks to every method. Non-English material and paywalled journalism are the main recall gaps.
