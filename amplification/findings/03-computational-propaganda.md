# 03 — Covert influence operations and coordinated inauthentic behaviour, 2023–2026

**Question:** has AI changed the *mechanism* of covert influence, or only the *volume*?

**Research cut-off:** 25 September 2026. **Scope:** defensive/analytic; methods are described only as needed to judge capability and defence, with no operational guidance.

**Grades:** **A** primary/official · **B** peer-reviewed/high-quality investigative · **C** reputable news · **D** vendor/think-tank · **E** anonymous/unverified. Claims are tagged **DEMONSTRATED**, **CLAIMED** (asserted by an authoritative actor, not independently measured) or **INFERRED** (analyst judgement).

---

## Headline judgements

1. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** AI has decisively changed the *production and concealment* layer: volume, multilingual localisation, persona construction, attribution laundering, and internal "programme-office" work (doctrine, target databases, employment contracts, staff scoring). The EEAS recorded a **259% rise in AI-related FIMI incidents (41 → 147 cases)** in 2025 — **27% of all detected incidents** ([EEAS 4th FIMI report, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en)).
2. **DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high).** AI has *not* demonstrably changed *reach or persuasion*. The same report states "much of the AI-generated material in 2025 remains low-quality… resulting in limited overall impact as organic engagement remains low", naming **Storm-1516 and Operation Overload** as producing videos "easily identifiable as inauthentic by average viewers". Anthropic's cases converge: most content "drew little or no authentic engagement".
3. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** The clearest *qualitative* change is organisational, not rhetorical: persistent-memory "agent" workspaces, doctrine files reused across hundreds of sessions, shared approved-source and banned-word lists, and semi-autonomous batch pipelines. AI acts as a **coordination substrate** letting producers who never communicate behave as one network.
4. **CLAIMED, not measured (D/C, low–medium).** That AI makes influence *more persuasive*; and that high-volume networks deliberately "groom" LLMs so assistants reproduce their narratives. Both are plausible and partly evidenced; neither has a real-world effect size.
5. **INFERRED (medium).** The measurable harms sit **upstream of the public**: newsroom and fact-checker workload, contamination of the search/Wikipedia/LLM *citation layer*, harassment of named individuals, and diplomatic embarrassment — not mass attitude change.

Three propositions must be separated: **volume/velocity** (demonstrated), **verisimilitude** (contested — official assessments call AI video easily spotted; synthetic audio is the strongest case), and **coordination/agency** (demonstrated at case level, unquantified in aggregate). The defences differ accordingly: volume is a platform-integrity and cost problem, verisimilitude a provenance problem, agency a detection and attribution problem.

## 2. Persona management and synthetic corroboration

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — AI removed the human bottleneck.** Anthropic documents full personas with AI-generated profile photos and invented biographies for fake reporters, fabricated political spokespeople, impersonation of a real state spokesperson and a real human-rights organisation, and forged government documents. One commercial network paired each of ~70 fake outlets with a matching social account and staffed its "newsroom" with bylines for journalists who **did not exist**.

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — the architecture is industrialised.** The recurring pattern is persistent memory files holding doctrine, banned-word lists, approved sources and evasion rules; prompts demanding fixed output schemas; and batch tooling calling the model without a human per item. One operator was building a **training course** to teach the workflow. That is the difference between a hundred people writing a hundred posts and one person running a content factory.

**DEMONSTRATED (B/A, high) — "false amplification" is a named, long-standing technique that AI has now automated.** EU DisinfoLab's *Indian Chronicles* remains the canonical case: a **15-year** operation (from 2005) using **10+ UN Human Rights Council-accredited NGOs** — mostly real NGOs *resurrected* after dissolution — **750+ fake outlets in 119 countries** and 550+ domains, with outlets quoting and republishing one another to create a "**mirage of international support**". It even resurrected a person: Prof. Louis B. Sohn, dead since 2006, was listed as attending UN and Washington events. Eleven MEPs published op-eds in the fabricated *EU Chronicle*. Earlier work counted **265+ fake local news sites across 65+ countries** using the same syndication logic. What is new is cost: NewsGuard with Pangram Labs counted **3,006 AI content farms** by March 2026, **more than doubled in a year** and growing 300–500 sites per month, of which **358** were tied to Storm-1516.

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — laundering one source into apparent consensus.** Documented current techniques: **cross-border laundering** (rewriting one story in opposite ideological directions for different national audiences; adding political angles; stripping context); **state-attribution stripping** (Sputnik/RIA/CGTN content republished under fabricated bylines as independent local reporting); **false verification loops** (one story echoed across nominally separate outlets "so it appeared to be independently confirmed", with the model's own "unverified" caveats removed); and **institutional impersonation** (a front NGO copying a real Swiss organisation's identity, plus ghost-written testimony delivered at the **62nd UN Human Rights Council session**, engineered so neither speech named the sponsoring state).

**DEMONSTRATED (B/A, medium-high) — fabrication has moved *up* the trust hierarchy.** The progression runs fake news sites (2019) → fake UN-accredited NGOs (2020) → a fabricated scientific authority, "Dr Victor Lysov", invented to lend a book "the light of scientific facts" (2024) → in August 2026, an OpenAI-disrupted Russian operation running a **fake Israeli think tank staffed with real researchers' names** and recycling **stolen academic papers**. The last case defeats ordinary verification: the named people exist and are findable. Verification heuristics that work on individuals fail on institutions; durable defences are registry verification, DOI/indexing status, domain-registration timelines and treating backlink and citation counts as compromised signals.

**DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high) — one model can replace one trusted person.** The MEK/NCRI case is the purest example: an agent ingested ~8,400 Telegram posts to clone an activist's style, then held live political conversations with his contacts, who are not known to have realised. Synthetic corroboration here is a specific trusted individual substituted by a model.

**CLAIMED/INFERRED — the citation layer as the real target.** ISD found 919 sites linking Pravda material over a year; of 303 English-language sites reviewed, **81% treated Pravda as credible**, only **3% flagged it as an information operation**, and **34 sites above 1M monthly visits** cited it as reliable; DFRLab found Pravda links in Wikipedia and X Community Notes, and its Pravda Dashboard counts **>3.7M articles across 110+ countries**. The inference that this contaminates retrieval and training is reasonable but untested: ISD's own testing found popular LLMs **rarely cite Pravda**, and the widely repeated "33% of AI answers" figure did not replicate cleanly. **Treat "LLM poisoning is working" as unproven.**

**DEMONSTRATED (B) — a commercial sector sells the whole chain.** Team Jorge, an Israeli firm exposed in 2023, was documented offering hacking, false-flag and automated persona-management services to political clients, and *claimed* involvement in dozens of presidential campaigns ([Guardian/Forbidden Stories, 15 Feb 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/15/revealed-disinformation-team-jorge-claim-meddling-elections-tal-hanan)). Its 2025–26 equivalents are documented above: LKM Company ran ~70 fabricated newsrooms as an advertising-agency sideline, and BBS Bilisim marketed a "military-grade, AI-driven" platform with a tunable parameter for artificial views.

**DEMONSTRATED (C, medium) — the target now includes the assistants.** European officials have warned that pro-Russian groups are bombarding AI systems with propaganda to make chatbots reproduce Kremlin talking points before the US midterms ([NBC News, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/europe-warns-russian-groups-seed-ai-chatbots-propaganda-rcna351233)); Politico reported on 25 Sep 2026 that AI chatbots "are quietly spewing partisan talking points". No independent measurement of changed model behaviour attributable to a named operation was available.

## 3. Platform and lab reporting: capability versus outcome

- **Anthropic (Sep 2026, A)** is the most informative record because it sits *upstream* of distribution: nine influence cases across Russia, Iran, Turkey, the Gulf, South Asia, Africa and Europe, with explicit **Breakout Scale** ratings and a candid limit — "Our visibility into these operations ends once it's live… Most of the content we discovered drew little or no authentic engagement." Its widest authentic reach came where **state media was the distribution mechanism** (radio, satellite, shortwave, TV), not where AI was used; matched output ran from a Telegram post with **~2,000 views** to aired broadcast copy.
- **OpenAI (A)** lists roughly **20 distinct influence-operation disruptions between May 2024 and August 2026** (including Spamouflage, Doppelganger, "Data Center Bandwagon" and an August 2026 covert Russian campaign), emphasising persona creation and detection evasion rather than reach.
- **Meta (A/D)** remains the only platform publishing recurring network-level takedown data, though its per-quarter CIB counts could **not** be verified from primary sources here; treat precise figures as **unconfirmed**. **Google/YouTube TAG (A)** publishes the most consistent series and the clearest impact test: >**50,000** Dragonbridge instances disrupted in 2022 and >**65,000** in 2023 (of >57,000 channels disabled, **80% had zero subscribers**), with lifetime terminations past **175,000** by early 2024 — and TAG's central finding that the network "achieves practically no organic engagement from real viewers", engagement being "almost entirely inauthentic, coming from other DRAGONBRIDGE accounts".
- **X/Twitter (E, low).** Post-2022 research transparency is markedly thinner; no comparable recurring, network-attributed CIB reporting series was retrievable. A **material evidence gap**, not evidence that enforcement stopped.
- **EEAS (Mar 2026, A)** gives the best aggregate: 540 incidents, ~10,000 channels, 43,000 observables on 19 platforms, **88% of instances concentrated on X**; **35% of incidents attributed (29% Russia, 6% China), 65% unattributed**; **90.5% of mapped channels covert** versus 7% state-controlled and 4.5% state-linked.

**Agentic behaviour — what is actually documented.** Operators run **persistent, memory-bearing, scheduled, semi-autonomous pipelines with humans setting targets and reviewing output**. Anthropic's Bangladesh case (one actor rotating 29 accounts; a custom `fake_news_3.py` producing fixed batches of headlines, narratives and image prompts; videos queued to YouTube months ahead) is the clearest. The MEK case adds a **shared** agent platform with per-workspace memory; the UAE case a master doctrine file re-tasking the model "across hundreds of sessions". What is *not* documented in influence is target selection or escalation without human direction — that appears in the same report's cyber and surveillance cases.

## 4. Effectiveness: what is known

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — the benchmark measurement.** [Eady et al., *Nature Communications* 14:62 (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9) linked a three-wave panel of 1,496 US Twitter users to their timelines: **1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures** (10% for 98%; 1% of Russian accounts supplied 89% of IRA content); median weekly exposure in the final month was **zero**; ~4 IRA posts/user/day versus **106 from national news media** and 35 from politicians; aggregate potential exposure ~**32 million Americans**; estimated effect on vote choice **−0.18 pp for Trump (90% CI −1.15, 0.78)**, with no meaningful relationship to issue positions or polarisation.

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — independent nulls and the right benchmark.** [Bail et al., *PNAS* 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116) matched non-public IRA data to 1,239 partisan Twitter users: only **76** had interacted with an IRA account (44 treated vs 1,106 control); **none of six outcomes** moved, and IRA interactions were **0.1%** of those users' likes, mentions and retweets. [Coppock, Hill & Vavreck (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046) pooled **59 experiments and ~34,000 people** on real campaign ads and found small effects regardless of context — the crucial benchmark, since a covert foreign campaign has a fraction of that dosage.

**DEMONSTRATED — one nuance and one real effect.** [Freelon & Lokot (2023)](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/russian-disinformation-campaigns-on-twitter/) analysed **2,057,747 authentic replies** to IRA accounts across ten communities: reach was not confined to strong Republicans but ran into separate ideological silos — qualifying the "preaching to the converted" reading without overturning the null. [Kim et al., *PNAS*, 26 Jan 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2519944123), the strongest recent *measured* effect, found geo-racially targeted **digital voter-suppression ads produced small population-level turnout decreases** — but the mechanism was paid ad targeting, not organic influence operations.

**DEMONSTRATED (B) — AI persuasion in the lab is real but narrow.** [Salvi et al. (2024/25)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14380), N=820: debaters facing a *personalised* GPT-4 had **81.7% higher odds** of increased agreement than those facing a human (p<0.01), but **without personalisation the advantage was non-significant (p=0.31)**. [Liu, Salvi et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09662) is the essential qualifier: LLM superiority is context-dependent, model-specific and **wanes over repeated interactions**, unlike human persuasion. [Orchinik et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16432), N=3,208, found a brief warning that LLMs can be primed to persuade cut belief change by **~48.1%**. These are short-horizon, self-reported, low-dosage effects in volunteers — not votes.

**DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high) — official bodies now say the same.** The EEAS states most operations "fail to generate meaningful organic engagement" and that "an overwhelming majority of the interaction metrics remains inauthentically inflated". VIGINUM on Storm-1516: "the real impact on the digital public debate remains difficult to estimate"; on Rokh Solis: "very low online visibility… despite numerous attempts at artificial amplification". ISD's Matryoshka measurement (110.2k views, 405 likes, **0 comments**, 1 follower) is a metric that is real as a number and empty as an audience.

**INFERRED (medium) — where effects are real, they are indirect.** (i) **Elite/media agenda penetration**: Storm-1516 is built for pickup by journalists and politicians; headline coverage, official denials and parliamentary questions need no mass belief. (ii) **Harassment and chilling**: the Metric Media ecosystem produced faculty sanctions and terminations; ~40 professors were sanctioned after a single 2025 "watchlist" episode. (iii) **Workload and cost imposition**: fact-checkers are deliberately flooded — Matryoshka explicitly contacted them to demand investigation of fabricated material. Operations are also frequently self-defeating (Prism News shut down; the Kenyan campaign reached no real people; Matryoshka published only 7 of 269 posts in German). The classic **backfire effect** explains neither failure nor success: Wood & Porter found only one instance across 36 issues ([*Political Behavior*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2018.1493009)); the stronger caution is the opposite — the **implied truth effect**, whereby tagging some false content makes untagged false content look truer.

## 5. Detection and takedown

**How networks are found.** *Infrastructure*: registration bursts, shared deployment identifiers, one deployment behind ~70 "independent" sites, prompter residue in favicon filenames, a state-owned parent visible through a shared root domain. *Behaviour*: strict posting windows, synchronised publication (near-identical articles within three minutes), seeder–amplifier role splits, shared account-creation timestamps, warm-up logic rotating cookies and IPs. *Content*: translation artefacts, residual source-language text, template reuse across unrelated topics, stylometric cloning. *Engagement shape*: high views with disproportionate likes and zero comments. **Frameworks:** Meta's CIB triggers on **behaviour, not falsity**; Google TAG uses audience metrics as the impact test; the **Breakout Scale** (Nimmo/Brookings) measures breakout, not persuasion, and holds that journalists, celebrities and policymakers — not operators — do most of the amplifying.

**Lag and asymmetry.** All public lag figures are **exposure lag, not detection lag**: Matryoshka operated from at least September 2023 and was named in June 2024; Rokh Solis was detected in March 2026 and published in June; Portal Kombat kept growing after exposure. Conversely, **upstream disruption is fast** — labs remove accounts during production, so a rising count of disruptions is evidence of *production capacity denied*, not audience harm averted. Summing lab disruptions with platform takedowns double-counts the same operations at two stages.

**Limits.** AI-text detection has large, measured false-positive rates — Liang et al. (*Patterns*, 2023) found an **average 61.22% false-positive rate** across seven detectors on 91 human TOEFL essays, with **97.8%** flagged by at least one. Sadasivan et al. (*TMLR*) show recursive paraphrasing defeats watermarking and neural detectors while barely degrading quality, and give a theoretical result tying any detector's best-possible AUROC to the total-variation distance between human and AI text — a fundamental, not merely engineering, limit. Detection therefore migrates to infrastructure and account-level invariants. Independent audit of platform enforcement is essentially absent.

## 6. Where the evidence is thin (explicit flags)

1. **No effect size for AI-enabled influence.** Authoritative sources measure *output* and *inflated reach*. No study demonstrates a changed population-level effect size for a 2023–2026 AI-enabled campaign. "AI has qualitatively changed influence effects" is **claimed, not demonstrated**.
2. **The instruments are bounded, not precise.** No field experiment has randomly assigned exposure to foreign influence operations; "exposure" means potential exposure (a post in a timeline), not attention. Eady's interval (±~1 pp) excludes large effects but not small ones — and a 0.3 pp swing is undetectable in these designs yet could matter in a close state.
3. **Attitudinal effects are asserted far more than measured.** "Sowed discord" framings are content/strategy analysis; the two best-identified individual-level studies find nothing.
4. **"LLM grooming works" is unproven**, and self-reported adversary reach (e.g. the Malaysian dashboard's "millions") circulates uncorrected in journalism.
5. **Platform statistics are not comparable and are unaudited.** Facebook's ~126M "potential viewers", Twitter's ~288M views/1.4M interactions and Google's ~309,000 video views measure different things; Meta's per-quarter CIB counts and X's post-2022 reporting could not be verified from primary sources.
6. **Vendor and official naming is inconsistent** — ISD's ~90 Pravda sites could not be reconciled with VIGINUM's 224 portals — and **domestic influence-for-hire is systematically under-covered**: because the Metric Media ecosystem is domestic and legal, it appears in no FIMI statistic, yet its demonstrated effects exceed those of most foreign campaigns.
7. **Attribution confidence varies enormously.** GRU 29155 attribution for Storm-1516 rests on official assessment; several Cyrillic-language and Israeli-nexus claims (including Blackcore) rest on technical indicators at **medium** confidence at best.

**Citation-integrity note.** The 2023 *Nature Communications* IRA paper is by **Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker**; "Poteat" and "Trexler" do not appear on it. A work titled **"Like, Post, and Mistrust"** attributed to Guess and Nyhan could not be located in Crossref, arXiv, Europe PMC, OpenAlex or the HKS Misinformation Review index; do not cite it without a locatable DOI.

## 7. Bottom line

**DEMONSTRATED:** AI has industrialised the supply side of covert influence — production, localisation, persona manufacture, attribution laundering and internal coordination — making one-operator, multi-persona, persistent networks cheap and durable. The frontier of harm has moved toward the **citation layer** (search, Wikipedia, AI assistants) and toward **targeted harassment**, where measurable effects exist.

**NOT DEMONSTRATED:** that AI has made influence operations more persuasive or given them organic reach. Exposure is concentrated, engagement largely synthetic, quality often poor, and official bodies describe reach as limited and impact as difficult to estimate.

**INFERRED:** the correct framing for 2026 is **cheap, fast, deniable noise plus targeted reputational damage**, not a new era of mass cognitive capture. Three indicators would change this judgement: (a) a documented operation achieving organic, non-inflated engagement at scale; (b) measured downstream effects in AI assistant outputs traceable to a named campaign; (c) field-experimental evidence that AI-generated covert content shifts attitudes more than human-written content.

### Method limits

`web_search` was unavailable session-wide; discovery used news-RSS and Bing-News RSS search, publisher site search, sitemaps and direct document retrieval. Several sources (newsguardrealitycheck.com, euvsdisinfo.eu, politico.com, france24.com, justice.gov, openai.com) returned 403/bot-blocks to every method. Non-English primary material and paywalled investigative journalism are the main recall gaps.
