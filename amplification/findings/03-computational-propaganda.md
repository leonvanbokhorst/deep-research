# 03 — Covert influence operations and coordinated inauthentic behaviour, 2023–2026

**Question:** has AI changed the *mechanism* of covert influence, or only the *volume*?

**Research cut-off:** 25 September 2026. **Scope:** defensive/analytic; methods described only as needed to judge capability and defence, with no operational guidance.

**Grades:** **A** primary/official · **B** peer-reviewed/high-quality investigative · **C** reputable news · **D** vendor/think-tank · **E** anonymous/unverified. Claims are tagged **DEMONSTRATED**, **CLAIMED** (asserted by an authoritative actor, not independently measured) or **INFERRED** (analyst judgement).

---

## Headline judgements

1. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** AI has decisively changed the *production and concealment* layer: volume, multilingual localisation, persona construction, attribution laundering and internal "programme-office" work (doctrine, target databases, staff scoring). The EEAS recorded a **259% rise in AI-related FIMI incidents (41 → 147)** in 2025 — **27% of all detected incidents** ([EEAS, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eeas/4th-eeas-report-foreign-information-manipulation-and-interference-threats_en)).
2. **DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high).** AI has *not* demonstrably changed *reach or persuasion*. The same report finds AI material "remains low-quality… organic engagement remains low", naming **Storm-1516 and Operation Overload** as producing video "easily identifiable as inauthentic by average viewers". Anthropic's cases converge: most content "drew little or no authentic engagement". **Exception (see §4):** where a recommender rewards engagement-shaped content, ~40 accounts can reach hundreds of millions — reach is *rented from the platform*, not manufactured by personas.
3. **DEMONSTRATED (A, high).** The clearest *qualitative* change is organisational: persistent-memory "agent" workspaces, doctrine files reused across hundreds of sessions, shared approved-source and banned-word lists, and semi-autonomous batch pipelines. AI acts as a **coordination substrate**, letting producers who never communicate behave as one network.
4. **CLAIMED, not measured (D/C, low–medium).** That AI makes influence *more persuasive*; and that networks deliberately "groom" LLMs so assistants reproduce their narratives. Both are plausible and partly evidenced; neither has a real-world effect size.
5. **INFERRED (medium).** The measurable harms sit **upstream of the public**: newsroom and fact-checker workload, contamination of the search/Wikipedia/LLM *citation layer*, harassment of named individuals, and diplomatic embarrassment — not mass attitude change.

Three propositions must be separated: **volume/velocity** (demonstrated), **verisimilitude** (contested), and **coordination/agency** (demonstrated at case level, unquantified in aggregate) — each with a different defence: platform integrity, provenance, detection. A fourth distinction carries the argument: the payload of "synthetic corroboration" is **not** a belief multiplier (the best evidence finds repetition, not source count, drives belief) but the **manufacture of institutional standing** for the outlet, which then lends credibility to everything else it publishes.

## 2. Persona management and synthetic corroboration

**DEMONSTRATED (A, high) — AI removed the human bottleneck and industrialised the architecture.** Anthropic documents full personas with AI-generated photos and invented biographies, fabricated spokespeople, impersonation of a real state spokesperson and a real human-rights organisation, and forged government documents. One network gave each of ~70 fake outlets a matching social account and bylines for journalists who **did not exist**. The recurring pattern is persistent memory files holding doctrine, banned-word lists, approved sources and evasion rules; prompts demanding fixed output schemas; and batch tooling calling the model without a human per item. One operator was building a **training course** to teach the workflow.

**DEMONSTRATED (B/A, high) — "false amplification" is a named, long-standing technique that AI has now automated.** EU DisinfoLab's *Indian Chronicles* remains the canonical case: a **15-year** operation (from 2005) using **10+ UN Human Rights Council-accredited NGOs** — mostly real NGOs *resurrected* after dissolution — **750+ fake outlets in 119 countries** and 550+ domains, with outlets quoting one another to create a "**mirage of international support**". It even resurrected a person: Prof. Louis B. Sohn, dead since 2006, listed as attending UN and Washington events. What is new is cost: NewsGuard's AI-content-farm series runs **49 sites (May 2023) → 3,749 (June 2026)**, a ~**76-fold** rise (a vendor sample, not a census). It counted **1,265 pink-slime outlets against 1,213 US daily newspapers** (June 2024) and attributed a **167-site pro-Russia network, 64 posing as local news, to John Mark Dougan** — the first documented crossover of pink slime, AI production and Russian disinformation in one network. VIGINUM attributed **≥293 domains** to that network and recorded **84 registered in a single day** (10 May 2024) across a ~**9-year** span; Dougan was EU-designated on **15 December 2025**, the first American sanctioned for pro-Kremlin disinformation.

**DEMONSTRATED (A/B, high) — laundering one source into apparent consensus, and what that actually buys.** Techniques: **cross-border laundering** (one story rewritten in opposite ideological directions for different audiences); **state-attribution stripping** (Sputnik/RIA/CGTN content republished under fabricated bylines); **false verification loops** ("so it appeared to be independently confirmed", with the model's own "unverified" caveats removed); and **institutional impersonation** (a front NGO copying a real Swiss organisation, plus ghost-written testimony delivered at the **62nd UN Human Rights Council session**). Fabrication has moved *up* the trust hierarchy, culminating in an OpenAI-disrupted August 2026 Russian operation running a **fake Israeli think tank staffed with real researchers' names** and recycling stolen papers — defeating verification that works on individuals but not institutions. **The mechanism is not a belief multiplier:** the best direct test finds **repetition, not the number of sources, drives susceptibility and confidence** ([Foster et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2011.12.004)), and source count works through subjective familiarity rather than perceived independence. What repetition does produce is **credibility for the outlet itself**, generalising to its other claims ([Mattavelli et al., *PSPB* 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672251320704)).

**DEMONSTRATED (A/D, high) — one model can replace one trusted person.** Anthropic's MEK/NCRI case: an agent ingested ~8,400 Telegram posts to clone an activist's style, then held live political conversations with his contacts, who are not known to have realised.

**DEMONSTRATED (B/C, medium-high) — synthetic media: cheap fakes dominate; degraded detection and the liar's dividend are the harms.** True deepfake *video* is rarer than headlines imply; the dominant technique remains the **"cheap fake"** — re-cut or mislabelled *real* footage. Where synthetic media has mattered, the mechanism is timing: the Slovak Šimečka audio (Sep 2023) was released inside the election moratorium, when institutional response is slowest; the fake Pentagon-explosion image (May 2023) moved the S&P 500 **≈0.3% intraday** before being debunked in ~20 minutes (the "$500bn wiped" figure is not corroborated). Pooled across **56 studies and 86,155 participants**, human detection of synthetic media averages **55.5%** ([Diel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2024.100538)); AI faces are judged human **~66% of the time — more often than real faces**, with the worst performers most confident; and **45–54% of participants who accepted an explicit deepfake warning still judged guilt from the video**, while specific warnings led people to call *real* video fake ([Clark & Lewandowsky et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00381-9)). The liar's dividend is nevertheless bounded: across >15,000 adults it operates on **text** but largely fails against **video** ([Schiff et al., *APSR* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055423001454)). Impression-based training nearly doubled detection accuracy (40% → 80%) in a 2026 PNAS experiment — the strongest known countermeasure. **Not demonstrated:** that a synthetic artefact alone flipped an election or caused a documented military or diplomatic decision.

**CLAIMED/INFERRED — the citation layer as the real target.** ISD found 919 sites linking Pravda material over a year; of 303 English-language sites, **81% treated Pravda as credible** and only **3% flagged it as an information operation**, with **34 sites above 1M monthly visits** citing it as reliable. The inference that this contaminates retrieval and training is reasonable but untested: the claim that ten AI tools repeated Pravda claims **33% of the time** comes from a **NewsGuard audit (2025)** and **did not replicate** in ISD testing. What is established is the substrate: Pew Research found **~10% of a random July 2026 English web sample showed significant AI authorship**. **Treat "LLM poisoning is working" as unproven.**

**DEMONSTRATED (B, medium) — a commercial sector sells the whole chain.** Team Jorge (Israel, exposed 2023) ran an avatar-creation interface using photos stolen from real people; its headline claims ("33 presidential campaigns, 27 successful") are its **own sales pitch, not verified** ([Guardian, 15 Feb 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/15/revealed-disinformation-team-jorge-claim-meddling-elections-tal-hanan)). LKM Company ran ~70 fabricated newsrooms as an advertising-agency sideline; BBS Bilisim sold a "military-grade, AI-driven" platform with a tunable parameter for artificial views.

**DEMONSTRATED (C, medium) — the target now includes the assistants.** European officials warn that pro-Russian groups are bombarding AI systems to make chatbots reproduce Kremlin talking points ([NBC News, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/europe-warns-russian-groups-seed-ai-chatbots-propaganda-rcna351233)); Politico reported on 25 Sep 2026 that AI chatbots "are quietly spewing partisan talking points". No independent measurement of changed model behaviour attributable to a named operation was available.

## 3. Platform and lab reporting: capability versus outcome

- **Anthropic (Sep 2026, A)** is the most informative record because it sits *upstream*: nine cases spanning six continents, with explicit **Breakout Scale** ratings and a candid limit — "Our visibility into these operations ends once it's live… Most of the content we discovered drew little or no authentic engagement." Its widest authentic reach came where **state media was the distribution mechanism** (FM/satellite radio, TV); matched output ran from a Telegram post with **~2,000 views** to aired broadcast copy.
- **OpenAI (A)** lists ~**20 influence-operation disruptions, May 2024–August 2026**. Its sharpest scale-versus-effect datum: a Chinese law-enforcement-linked unit "made over **50,000 posts across over 200 Western platforms**. Of those, **under 150 posts received over 300 shares or comments**." Its investigator rated the June 2026 "Data Center Bandwagon" case Breakout Scale 1–2: "We didn't see any signs that it succeeded."
- **Meta (A/D)** publishes recurring network-level data, though its per-quarter CIB counts could **not** be verified from primary sources here (transparency pages are not machine-readable); treat precise figures as **unconfirmed**. **Google/YouTube TAG (A)** gives the strongest reach data: of >**57,000** Dragonbridge channels disabled, **80% had zero subscribers**; of >**900,000** videos suspended, **>65% had under 100 views**; lifetime disruptions exceed **175,000** — and TAG's finding that the network "achieves practically no organic engagement from real viewers", engagement being "almost entirely inauthentic".
- **X/Twitter (E, low).** Archived voluntary platform-manipulation disclosures **end at H2 2021** and `transparency.x.com` now carries only regulatory filings: a **material evidence gap**, not evidence that enforcement stopped. (The EU's first DSA penalty against X — €120m, 5 Dec 2025 — concerned blue-check and ad transparency, not CIB.)
- **EEAS (Mar 2026, A)** gives the best aggregate: 540 incidents, ~10,000 channels, 43,000 observables on 19 platforms, **88% of instances on X**; **35% attributed (29% Russia, 6% China), 65% unattributed**; **90.5% of mapped channels covert**.

**Agentic behaviour.** Operators run **persistent, memory-bearing, scheduled, semi-autonomous pipelines with humans setting targets and reviewing output**. Anthropic alone gives explicit evidence: its Bangladesh operation "ran semi-autonomously with minimal human oversight", and MEK's "Viktor" platform produced content "without a human user directing each session". OpenAI pushes back: actors "bolt AI onto old playbooks to move faster", with "no evidence of new tactics". **No lab documents a fully self-directed campaign agent, and none reports a measured audience, electoral or policy effect for any case.**

## 4. Effectiveness: what is known

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — the benchmark measurement.** [Eady et al. (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-35576-9) linked a three-wave panel of 1,496 US Twitter users to their timelines: **1% of users accounted for 70% of exposures** (10% for 98%); median weekly exposure in the final month was **zero**; ~4 IRA posts/user/day versus **106 from national news media** and 35 from politicians; ~**32 million Americans** potentially exposed; estimated vote-choice effect **−0.18 pp for Trump (90% CI −1.15, 0.78)**, with no meaningful relationship to issue positions or polarisation.

**DEMONSTRATED (B, high) — independent nulls and the right benchmark.** [Bail et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1906420116) matched non-public IRA data to 1,239 partisan users: only **76** had interacted with an IRA account (44 treated vs 1,106 control); **none of six outcomes** moved. [Coppock, Hill & Vavreck (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc4046) pooled **59 experiments and ~34,000 people** on real campaign ads: effects are small regardless of context — the crucial benchmark, since a covert campaign has a fraction of that dosage. [Freelon & Lokot (2023)](https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/russian-disinformation-campaigns-on-twitter/) add the nuance that reach extended into separate ideological silos, and [Kim et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2519944123) the one real recent effect: geo-racially targeted **voter-suppression ads produced small turnout decreases** — via paid targeting, not organic operations.

**DEMONSTRATED (B) — AI persuasion in the lab is real but narrow.** [Salvi et al. (2024/25)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14380), N=820: debaters facing *personalised* GPT-4 had **81.7% higher odds** of increased agreement than those facing a human (p<0.01), but **without personalisation the advantage was non-significant (p=0.31)**. [Liu, Salvi et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09662) show the advantage is context-dependent and **wanes over repeated interactions**; [Orchinik et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16432) found a brief warning cut belief change by **~48.1%**. These are short-horizon, self-reported, low-dosage effects in volunteers — not votes.

**THE COUNTERWEIGHT (B, high) — small networks can rent mass reach.** ISD measured two pro-Iran X networks of **~40 accounts** generating **>1 billion views, ~16m likes and 3.5m reposts in one month**, attributed to **X's "For You" algorithm plus reposts by accounts with >500k followers (≈100 times) and >50k followers (>2,000 times)**. Meta's H2 2026 report described an Iran-linked network of **4 Facebook and 31 Instagram accounts with ~79,400 followers** whose reach Meta called "moderate" (secondary reporting only). Persona networks cannot manufacture reach, but they can **borrow** it — which relocates the defender's leverage to recommendation policy and large-account amplification rather than post-level debunking.

**INFERRED (medium) — where effects are real, they are indirect.** (i) **Elite/media agenda penetration**: coverage, official denials and parliamentary questions need no mass belief. (ii) **Harassment and chilling**: the Metric Media ecosystem produced faculty sanctions and terminations. (iii) **Workload and cost imposition**: fact-checkers are deliberately flooded — Matryoshka demanded they investigate fabricated material. Operations are also frequently self-defeating: Prism News shut down, the Kenyan campaign reached no real people, and Matryoshka published only 7 of 269 posts in German. The classic **backfire effect** explains neither failure nor success (Wood & Porter found one instance across 36 issues); the stronger caution is the **implied truth effect**, whereby tagging some false content makes untagged false content look truer.

## 5. Detection and takedown

**How networks are found.** *Infrastructure*: registration bursts, shared deployment identifiers, one deployment behind ~70 "independent" sites, prompter residue in favicon filenames. *Behaviour*: strict posting windows, near-identical articles across sites within three minutes, seeder–amplifier splits, shared account-creation timestamps, warm-up logic rotating cookies and IPs. *Content*: translation artefacts, residual source-language text, template reuse, stylometric cloning. *Engagement shape*: high views with disproportionate likes and zero comments. **Frameworks:** Meta's CIB triggers on **behaviour, not falsity**; Google TAG uses audience metrics as the impact test; the **Breakout Scale** measures breakout, not persuasion, and holds that journalists, celebrities and policymakers — not operators — do most of the amplifying.

**Lag and asymmetry.** Public lag figures are **exposure lag, not detection lag**: Matryoshka operated from at least September 2023 and was named in June 2024; Rokh Solis was detected in March 2026 and published in June; Portal Kombat kept growing after exposure. Conversely, **upstream disruption is fast**, so a rising count of lab disruptions measures *production capacity denied*, not audience harm averted, and summing lab disruptions with platform takedowns double-counts the same operations. Note the reciprocal bias: because labs see operations *before* distribution, their "little authentic engagement" findings may understate reach that would have occurred had the operation run — the nulls in §4, not lab reach figures, carry the effect argument.

**Limits.** AI-text detection has large, measured false-positive rates — Liang et al. (*Patterns*, 2023) found an **average 61.22% false-positive rate** across seven detectors on 91 human TOEFL essays, with **97.8%** flagged by at least one. Sadasivan et al. (*TMLR*) show recursive paraphrasing defeats watermarking and neural detectors while barely degrading quality. Detection therefore migrates to infrastructure and account-level invariants, and independent audit of platform enforcement is essentially absent.

## 6. Where the evidence is thin (explicit flags)

1. **No effect size for AI-enabled influence.** Sources measure *output* and *inflated reach*; none measures attitude, behaviour or vote change for a 2023–2026 AI-enabled campaign. "AI has qualitatively changed influence effects" is **claimed, not demonstrated**.
2. **The instruments are bounded, not precise.** No field experiment has randomly assigned exposure to foreign influence operations; "exposure" means potential exposure, not attention. Eady's interval (±~1 pp) excludes large effects but not small ones.
3. **The multi-source "synthetic corroboration" hypothesis is contradicted, not merely unproven.** Repetition, not number of sources, drives susceptibility; the corrected illusory-truth meta-analysis across **182 studies and 31,184 participants** gives only **g = 0.37** and is **negligible for socio-political opinion** (Ye et al., 2026). The one paper framed exactly as this mechanism (Pillai & Fazio, *JARMAC* 2025) exists but its effect size could not be retrieved — the highest-value manual read outstanding. What survives is **trust-laundering**, not a belief multiplier.
4. **"LLM grooming works" is unproven**; self-reported adversary reach circulates uncorrected; and **platform statistics are not comparable or audited** — Meta's per-quarter CIB counts and X's post-2022 reporting could not be verified from primary sources.
5. **Vendor naming is inconsistent** (ISD's ~90 Pravda sites vs VIGINUM's 224 portals), **domestic influence-for-hire is under-covered** (the Metric Media ecosystem appears in no FIMI statistic despite larger demonstrated effects), and **attribution confidence varies** — GRU 29155 rests on official assessment, while several Israeli-nexus claims rest on technical indicators at **medium** confidence.

**Citation-integrity note.** The 2023 *Nature Communications* IRA paper is by **Eady, Paskhalis, Zilinsky, Bonneau, Nagler & Tucker**; "Poteat" and "Trexler" do not appear on it. **"Like, Post, and Mistrust"** (attributed to Guess and Nyhan) could not be located in Crossref, arXiv, Europe PMC, OpenAlex or the HKS index. Also unsound and not to be cited: "All the News That's Fit to Print?" (Clark et al. 2023 — no record exists); a *Nature Communications* liar's-dividend paper by Simchon, Edwards and Lewandowsky (their verified work is on microtargeting); and "AI Can Persuade Humans on Political Issues" (Bai et al.) — a preprint widely miscited as published. Vaccari & Chadwick 2020 appeared in *Social Media + Society*, not the *HKS Misinformation Review*.

## 7. Bottom line

**DEMONSTRATED:** AI has industrialised the supply side of covert influence — production, localisation, persona manufacture, attribution laundering and internal coordination — making one-operator, multi-persona, persistent networks cheap and durable. The frontier of harm has moved toward the **citation layer** (search, Wikipedia, AI assistants), toward **targeted harassment**, and toward **renting reach from recommenders**, not toward mass persuasion.

**NOT DEMONSTRATED:** that AI has made influence operations more persuasive or given them organic reach. Exposure is concentrated, engagement largely synthetic, quality often poor, and official bodies describe reach as limited — with the important exception of algorithmic amplification.

**INFERRED:** the correct framing for 2026 is **cheap, fast, deniable noise plus targeted reputational damage**, not a new era of mass cognitive capture. Three indicators would change this judgement: (a) a documented operation achieving non-rented, organic engagement at scale; (b) measured downstream effects in AI assistant outputs traceable to a named campaign; (c) field-experimental evidence that AI-generated covert content shifts attitudes more than human-written content.

### Method limits

`web_search` was unavailable session-wide; discovery used news-RSS and Bing-News RSS search, publisher site search, sitemaps, the Wayback Machine and direct retrieval. Several sources returned 403/bot-blocks to every method. Non-English material and paywalled journalism are the main recall gaps.
