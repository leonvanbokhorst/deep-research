# Security / Terrorism / Influence Prior Art: "Attacker Acts Small, Target's Reaction Does the Damage"

**Idea:** the attacker's action is small; the target's *own reaction* causes most of the damage — an engineered ambiguous incident provoking disproportionate state overreaction and public panic.

**Bottom line:** no single term means exactly this. Closest named prior art: Italy's **"strategy of tension"**. Closest named doctrine: Soviet/Russian **"reflexive control"**. Closest quantified statement: Mueller's **"costs of reaction."**

> Full verbatim quote set, the Turkey/Latin-America separations and method notes are in `security-terrorism-annex-quotes.md`.

## 1. Strategy of tension (*strategia della tensione*) — HIGHEST PRIORITY

**(1) Origin.** Coined in *The Observer*, 14 Dec 1969, reporting the Piazza Fontana bombing; attributed via Franco Ferraresi, *Threats to Democracy* (Princeton UP, 1997), p. 87. Bound to Italy's *anni di piombo*, 1968–82 (Piazza Fontana 12 Dec 1969 → Bologna 2 Aug 1980).

**(2) URLs.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy_of_tension · https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategia_della_tensione · Anna Cento Bull, *Italian Neofascism: The Strategy of Tension and the Politics of Nonreconciliation* (Berghahn, 2007), doi:10.2307/j.ctt1btbzwf · Philip Jenkins, *Studies in Conflict & Terrorism* 13(4–5), 1990, doi:10.1080/10576109008435838 · Commissione Stragi: http://www.parlamento.it/parlam/bicam/terror/home.htm

**(3) Claim (verbatim).** *"A strategy of tension (Italian: strategia della tensione) is a political policy where violent struggle is encouraged rather than suppressed. The purpose is to create a general feeling of insecurity in the population and make people seek security in a strong government."* Closer to our idea, it.wikipedia: *"La strategia si basa su una serie di atti terroristici volti a diffondere nella popolazione uno stato di insicurezza e di paura, tali da far giustificare, richiedere o auspicare svolte politiche di stampo autoritario; può anche essere attuata sotto forma di tattica militare che consiste nel commettere attentati dinamitardi e attribuirne la paternità ad altri"* (…commit bombings and **attribute them to others**). The **2000 parliamentary report** found a strategy of tension was supported by the US to *"stop the PCI, and to a certain degree also the PSI, from reaching executive power"*; *"Those massacres, those bombs, those military actions had been organized or promoted or supported by men inside Italian state institutions and, as has been discovered more recently, by men linked to the structures of United States intelligence."*

**(4) Confidence.** **H** that the term, its 1969 coinage and its attributed purpose exist. **M** that a centrally directed state/NATO strategy explains the massacres. **L** for any single massacre attribution.

**(5) Documented vs alleged.** *Documented:* Gladio's existence (Andreotti to the Chamber of Deputies, 24 Oct 1990); arms caches; judicial findings of intelligence interference and *depistaggio*; convictions only of material perpetrators, never *mandanti*. *Alleged/disputed:* direct CIA/NATO sponsorship — Ganser's *NATO's Secret Armies* (2005) leans on **US Army Field Manual 30-31B**, which the State Department calls a **1976 Soviet forgery**; Hansen (2005) and Davies (2005) reject his conclusions. Turkey's Counter-Guerrilla and the 1996 Susurluk scandal are documented as structures; the *strategy-of-tension* framing for Turkey and for Latin America (Operation Condor, Argentina's *Proceso*) is **alleged or analogical, not proven** — see annex.

## 2. Propaganda of the deed

**(1)** Pisacane 1857 (*propaganda dei fatti*); Bakunin/Nechayev 1869; Malatesta & Cafiero, Bern 1876; Brousse 1877; Most, US 1880s. **(2)** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_of_the_deed · Chaliand & Blin (eds.), *The History of Terrorism* (2007): https://archive.org/details/historyofterrori00grar

## 4. False flag operations

**(2)** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_flag
**(3)** *"A false flag operation is an act committed with the intent of disguising the actual source of responsibility and pinning blame on another party"* — extended to *"countries that organize attacks on themselves… thus giving the nation that attacked itself a pretext for domestic repression or foreign military aggression."*
**(4)** Definition **H**. **Mukden, 18 Sep 1931 — H:** Japanese officers blew up the South Manchurian Railway; *"the explosion was too weak to disrupt operations on the rail line"*, yet it was used to seize Manchuria — the purest small-incident → disproportionate-state-action case. **Gleiwitz 1939 — H.** **Reichstag fire 1933 — M.** **Operation Northwoods 1962 — H as a proposal only:** declassified Joint Chiefs plan to stage attacks on US citizens and blame Cuba; rejected by JFK, never executed. **(5)** Heavily contaminated by conspiracy usage; absent a declassified document or judicial finding, treat as **UNVERIFIED**.

## 7. Security theatre and the policy ratchet

Schneier, *Beyond Fear* (2003); "Beyond Security Theater", *New Internationalist*, Nov 2009: https://www.schneier.com/essays/archives/2009/11/beyond_security_thea.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_theatre
*"Security theater refers to security measures that make people feel more secure without doing anything to actually improve their security."* Core mechanism: *"A terrorist attack cannot possibly destroy a country's way of life; it's only our reaction to that attack that can do that kind of damage… the more we're doing the terrorists' job for them."* **H.**
Ratchet: Peacock & Wiseman (1961) found *"public spending increases like a ratchet following periods of crisis"*; Higgs, *Crisis and Leviathan* (1987). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_effect — **H** as a concept, but a *description* of the ratchet, not a doctrine to induce it.

## 8. Manufactured panic / moral panic

**Cohen**, *Folk Devils and Moral Panics* (1972): a panic occurs when *"a condition, episode, person or group of persons emerges to become defined as a threat to societal values and interests."* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_panic · **Hall et al.**, *Policing the Crisis* (1978): panics *"could thereby be ignited to create public support for the need to 'police the crisis'."*
**H.** **Key gap:** neither requires an attacker who engineers the triggering incident — exactly the space this idea occupies.

## 10. "Provocation" as a named security concept

**Agent provocateur:** *"a person who actively entices, encourages and incites another person to commit a crime that would not otherwise have been committed and then reports the person to the authorities."* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_provocateur — **H**. **Okhrana / *provokatsiya*:** *"The Okhrana became notorious for its agents provocateurs, including… Yevno Azef (1869–1918), Roman Malinovsky (1876–1918), and Dmitry Bogrov (1887–1911)."* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhrana · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evno_Azef — **H**. **UNVERIFIED:** no citable scholarly definition of *provokatsiya* as a doctrine. **L.**

## 11. Named term for exactly this idea

**None exists.** Nearest: (1) **"Strategy of tension"** — closest named prior art, but its purpose is a domestic authoritarian turn, typically attributed to state actors, not a small external attacker. (2) **"Reflexive control"** (*рефлексивное управление*) — closest named *doctrine* for "provoke the enemy into defeating himself": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_control — *"control someone has over their opponent's decisions by imposing on them assumptions that change the way they act"*; techniques include *"Exhaustion, forcing the adversary to expend resources to perform unproductive activities"* and *"Provocation, through the imposition of an unfavorable scenario of action."* **M.** (3) **"Propaganda of the deed"** — reaction logic, but the reactor is the public. (4) **bin Laden's "provoke and bait" / "bleeding America to the point of bankruptcy"** — an actor's own framing; no canonical label. (5) **"Amplification attack"** (DDoS reflection) — a technical analogue: a small request makes a third party emit a large response at the victim.

**Verdict: a genuine terminological gap.** "Strategy of tension" names the political version; "reflexive control" the military-deception version; Mueller quantifies the cost asymmetry but offers no doctrine label.
