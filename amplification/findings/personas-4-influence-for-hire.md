# Personas 4 — Commercial Influence-for-Hire and the Market for Fake Engagement

**Research cut-off:** 25 September 2026. **Framing:** defensive/analytic. No operational guidance, no operational vendor service pages or purchase channels, no tooling how-to. Vendor self-claims are labelled **CLAIMED** and treated as marketing, never as demonstrated effect.

**Evidence grades:** **A** = primary/official (regulator, court, first-party platform disclosure); **B** = peer-reviewed or high-quality investigative journalism; **C** = reputable news; **D** = vendor/think-tank self-report; **E** = anonymous/unverified. Platform attributions of state linkage are **D** for the attribution even where the takedown itself is **A**.

---

## 1. Team Jorge / AIMS (Israel) — the benchmark case

Exposed **15 February 2023** by the Forbidden Stories-led "Story Killers" consortium (**B**). Reporters from TheMarker, Radio France and Haaretz posed as consultants for an African client and secretly filmed meetings between **July and December 2022**. The consortium spanned **more than 100 journalists at 30 outlets**. Primary text: [Guardian, 15 Feb 2023](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/feb/15/revealed-disinformation-team-jorge-claim-meddling-elections-tal-hanan).

**Operator:** Tal Hanan, then 50, ex-Israeli special forces, alias "Jorge"; his brother **Zohar Hanan** was described as chief executive. Work ran at least partly through **Demoman International**, registered on an Israeli Ministry of Defence export-promotion site.

**CLAIMED (vendor self-claims — not demonstrated):**
- **"33 presidential-level campaigns, 27 of which were successful"** — Hanan's own pitch. The Guardian headlines a softer "more than 30 elections". Unverified; the consortium notes he may have been embellishing to close a deal.
- **"More than 30,000 avatars"** with multi-year backstories, some with Amazon accounts, credit cards, bitcoin wallets and Airbnb accounts.
- **Fees of €6m–€15m** for election interference. Email evidence instead shows **$160,000** requested from Cambridge Analytica in 2015 for an eight-week Latin American campaign, and a **$400,000–$600,000/month** Kenya quote in 2017 that CA rejected as too expensive.
- An earlier AIMS iteration (**up to 5,000 bots**) was claimed in email to CA's Alexander Nix to have been used in **17 elections**.

**DEMONSTRATED:** the live AIMS interface (avatar creation by nationality/gender with matched names, emails, dates of birth; Hanan created "Sophia Wilde", British); photo sourcing from real people (the "Sophia Wilde" image traced to a woman in Leeds); AIMS-linked bot activity in **about 20 countries** — UK, US, Canada, Germany, Switzerland, Mexico, Senegal, India, UAE — **mostly commercial disputes, not elections**; **Meta removed AIMS-linked bots on 14–15 February 2023**, publicly linking them for the first time to a now-defunct Israeli firm banned in 2019 (rare platform confirmation of actor continuity); hacking shown live — Hanan scrolled a Kenyan target's Gmail days before Kenya's 2022 election and claimed Telegram access to a strategist working for William Ruto; and leaked documents showing Team Jorge worked **Nigeria's 2015 presidential race alongside Cambridge Analytica** (**B**).

**Follow-ups:** OCCRP (**24 March 2023**) linked an Israeli disinformation expert to faked bank accounts in a Serbian smear campaign; Le Monde (**17 February 2023**) documented the Nigeria/CA collaboration; D+C (**11 June 2025**) and EUvsDisinfo (**5 December 2025**) surveyed the market. **UNVERIFIED:** any European Parliament question or resolution on Team Jorge.

## 2. STOIC / "Zero Zeno" (2024) — and a correction on "Operation Overload"

OpenAI's **"Operation 'Zero Zeno': Israel-linked influence activity"** (**1 May 2024**) and **"Disrupting deceptive uses of AI by covert influence operations"** (**30 May 2024**) named the Israeli commercial firm **STOIC**, which Calcalist called a **"for-hire threat actor"** (**C**). Meta removed the network in late May 2024 (WSJ 29 May; NBC News 30 May; Time 30 May) — **two platforms corroborating the same commercial client** (**A** for the takedowns). Reporting emphasised targeting of the **Indian Lok Sabha election** with an anti-BJP agenda (NDTV, The Hindu, Economic Times, 31 May–1 June 2024), plus US and Canada activity, using **fake news sites and LLM-generated personas**. **UNVERIFIED:** any link between STOIC and Tal Hanan/Team Jorge — widely asserted online, not established.

**Correction:** "**Operation Overload**" is **not** the Israeli firm case. It is a **separate pro-Russian campaign** ("Matryoshka") flooding European newsrooms with fake "please check this" tips to exhaust fact-checkers (EU DisinfoLab, 7 June 2024; IPI, 27 August 2024; ISD, 17 July 2025; WIRED, 1 July 2025). The two must not be merged.

## 3. OpenAI and the June 2026 "Data Center Bandwagon"

OpenAI published **"'Data Center Bandwagon' Campaign: US-targeted influence activity"** on **1 June 2026** (**A** for the disclosure, **D** for attribution). Per [CyberScoop, 10 June 2026](https://cyberscoop.com/openai-china-influence-campaign-chatgpt/), OpenAI tracked **two clusters** "likely originated" in China:

- **"Data Center Bandwagon"** — ChatGPT used to generate imagery and social comments claiming data-centre buildouts were raising American electricity prices; traced to an **unnamed Chinese technology company holding multiple contracts with regional Chinese governments**.
- **A tariffs cluster** — comics and comments framing tariffs as covert technological control, prompting the model to include Trump but exclude Xi Jinping.

Tactics: VPNs, prompts in **simplified Chinese**, English and Chinese outputs, and **fake accounts posing as Americans on X and YouTube**. One operator used ChatGPT to draft work reports on "establishing persistent and credible accounts" and to plan evasion of Meta's CIB detection. Crucially, the report's author **Ben Nimmo** stressed: *"this was not a case of an influence operation creating a debate. The debate existed already… We didn't see any signs that it succeeded."* OpenAI rated both campaigns **1 and 2 on the "Bookings breakout scale"** — activity on one or more platforms but **no evidence of meaningful engagement**; it **did not attribute** the operations to the Chinese government. Follow-ups: Tom's Hardware and Times of India (11–12 June 2026); Newsweek (**12 September 2026**) on Trump suggesting China was stirring US data-centre backlash.

OpenAI's 2024 tranche covered Russian "Doppelganger"/"Bad Grammar", Chinese "Spamouflage" and Israel's Zero Zeno. **UNVERIFIED:** any OpenAI influence-operation disclosure between July and 25 September 2026.

## 4. Anthropic — "influence-as-a-service"

Anthropic uses the term itself. Its **"Detecting and countering malicious uses of Claude: March 2025"** (published **23 April 2025**) describes the most novel case it had seen: a **financially motivated "influence-as-a-service" operation** (**A** for disclosure, **D** for attribution) — [report](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-countering-malicious-uses-of-claude-march-2025).

- **More than 100 social media bot accounts** on X and Facebook, each with a persona holding a distinct political alignment, engaging **"tens of thousands"** of authentic accounts across multiple countries and languages.
- **Novel capability:** Claude was used not only for content generation but as an **orchestrator deciding whether personas should like, share, comment on, or ignore specific content** — tactical engagement allocation, with persona state managed via a structured JSON framework. Named researchers: Ken Lebedev, Alex Moix, Jacob Klein.
- Narratives served **European, Iranian, UAE and Kenyan** interests, including support for Albanian figures and criticism of opposition figures in an unspecified European country.
- Assessed as a **commercial service serving clients across several countries**; **at least four distinct campaigns** shared the framework. Anthropic stated the activity was "consistent with what we expect from state affiliated campaigns, however **we have not confirmed this attribution**."
- **Impact was limited:** no content achieved viral status; the operator pursued sustained long-term engagement over virality, and instructed bots to respond with humour and sarcasm to accusations of being bots.

Anthropic's **August 2025** report is cybercrime-only (a Claude Code data-extortion operation against **at least 17 organisations**, ransom demands sometimes exceeding **$500,000**) with **no influence-operation case**.

**September 2026 report** ("Detecting and Countering Misuse of AI: September 2026", ~**10 September 2026**; **A** for disclosure, **D** for attribution) — the most concrete commercial influence-for-hire case to date.

- **Malaysia.** Anthropic disrupted a commercial political-manipulation operation linked to **BBS Bilişim Teknolojileri**, an **Istanbul-based technology company** that allegedly **sold access as a paid "influence-as-a-service" capability** while presenting the platform as a defensive cyber-intelligence and counter-disinformation tool. The system used Claude to profile and target voters by parliamentary constituency, drawing on real census and electoral data and **"millions of voter records"** to cover **all 222 parliamentary constituencies**, and managed **more than 1,000 fake X accounts**. Malaysia's **MCMC opened a probe on 11 September 2026**; **BBS Teknoloji denied involvement** (The Edge Malaysia, 15 September 2026). Sources: [Scoop, 11 Sept 2026](https://www.scoop.my/news/298231/ai-powered-political-operation-allegedly-targeted-all-222-parliamentary-seats-mcmc-launches-probe/); Türkiye Today, Turkish Minute, Bianet, The Independent, 11–12 September 2026.
- **Kenya.** A **Kenyan bot farm using Claude** to praise Energy Cabinet Secretary **Opiyo Wandayi**; Wandayi denied involvement (streamlinefeed.co.ke, 14 September 2026). Anthropic's report covered AI-powered propaganda and political manipulation in **four African countries** (Technext24, 10 September 2026).
- Other cases: Iran-linked accounts using Claude for propaganda and **US naval targeting**; a **UAE-linked** campaign targeting the Muslim Brotherhood and UN experts over Sudan; Russia-linked activity. Reuters (14 September 2026), AFP (11 September 2026), Politico and Euronews (10–11 September 2026).
- **UNVERIFIED:** the canonical `anthropic.com` URL for the September 2026 report (404 on tested slugs; the site blocks automated fetches). Cited by title, date and independent coverage.

## 5. Archimedes Group (Israel, 2019) — the pre-LLM precedent

Facebook announced removal of an **Archimedes Group** network on **16 May 2019** for coordinated inauthentic behaviour targeting **Africa, Asia and Latin America**, with roughly **$800,000 spent on Facebook** (PBS, BBC, ABC, Axios, 16–17 May 2019). The Times of Israel (19–23 May 2019) located it in a **Holon** office. Direct precursor to AIMS/STOIC: the market predates generative AI.

## 6. The market for fake engagement

- **Devumi.** The NYT's **"The Follower Factory"** (**27 January 2018**) exposed Devumi, which **sold more than 200 million fake followers**, largely resold bot services, to celebrity, commercial and political clients. In **October 2019** Devumi and owner **German Calas Jr.** settled with the **FTC for $2.5 million** — the agency's **first-ever complaint challenging the sale of fake indicators of social media influence** (Time, 30 January 2019) (**C**, medium confidence).
- **Fake reviews.** The **FTC's Final Rule banning fake reviews and testimonials** was announced **14 August 2024** (**A**) — the key regulatory anchor for the paid-review economy — followed by FTC warning letters in **December 2025** (**A**). The FTC also announced a crackdown on deceptive AI claims on **25 September 2024** (**A**).
- **State-linked mass fake accounts.** Meta removed **nearly 9,000 Facebook and Instagram accounts** linked to the Chinese-origin **"Spamouflage"** network (late August 2023; Guardian 30 August 2023; DW called it the world's "largest" such campaign) (**A** for the takedown). **UNVERIFIED:** a Meta disclosure of a single **"1 million+ fake accounts"** network.
- **Adjacent 2025 cases.** Citizen Lab's **PRISONBREAK** report (**2 October 2025**) documented an **AI-enabled influence operation aimed at overthrowing the Iranian regime**; CyberScoop (**3 October 2025**) reported researchers assessing the Israeli government as likely behind it (**D**). DFRLab documented **"South African influencers-for-hire"** attacking Zelenskyy (**9 May 2025**) and a **sockpuppet network impersonating Americans and Canadians** on X (**4 November 2024**) — evidence the market increasingly rents *real* influencer accounts rather than fabricating personas.
- **UNVERIFIED — price points.** No price-per-1,000-followers or click-farm wage figures were verifiable from a research-grade source. The only money figures I can stand behind are Team Jorge's claimed €6m–€15m and evidenced $160,000 quote, Archimedes' ~$800,000 spend, and Devumi's $2.5m settlement.

## 7. Philippines

The strongest documented thread is Rappler's reporting plus the December 2025 legislative response. A Senate committee hearing on **15 December 2025** took up three interlinked bills — **SB 191** (Anti-False Content and Fake News Act), **SB 1441** (Social Media Fairness and Algorithmic Transparency Act, requiring platforms to disclose ranking, amplification and demotion of political content) and **SB 1490**, the **Anti-Troll Farm Act**, criminalising the operation, financing or concealment of organised troll farms, including use of public funds, facilities or equipment. The panel **voted to subpoena Meta** after it failed to attend and submitted an excuse letter ([Rappler, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.rappler.com/technology/fake-news-social-media-algorithm-senate-hearing-december-15-2025/)) (**B/C**).

Rappler's **December 2025** series "**China's troll army: From teachers to riders, all fake**" names **Infinitus Marketing Solutions** as the corporate vehicle behind a Philippines-facing Chinese-language operation staffed by people posing as teachers and delivery riders ([Rappler, 20 Dec 2025](https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/in-depth/china-troll-army-philippines-infinitus-marketing-solutions/)) (**B**). Earlier work: "Investigating troll farms: What to look out for" (17 July 2021); "View from Manila: The AFP declares war on… trolls" (2 December 2024). **UNVERIFIED:** specific account counts or peso budgets for the Philippine troll-farm market.

## Claimed vs demonstrated vs inferred

**Demonstrated:** platform takedowns corroborated across Meta/OpenAI/Anthropic; the AIMS interface and avatar creation; AIMS-linked bot activity in ~20 countries; a commercial "influence-as-a-service" market with multi-country client books; the Malaysia 222-constituency voter-profiling system; regulatory responses (FTC 2024 rule, Philippine SB 1490, Malaysian MCMC probe).
**Claimed:** all Team Jorge metrics; state linkage in OpenAI/Anthropic attributions; Archimedes' effectiveness.
**Inferred:** that claimed scale translates into electoral effect. Nothing in the record shows measured persuasion. OpenAI's Bookings ratings (1 and 2) and Anthropic's "no viral content" finding both point the other way — **the market's reach is real; its demonstrated effect is weak.**
