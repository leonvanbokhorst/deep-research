# AI-Generated News Sites, Fake Local News, and "Pink Slime" Networks

**Research area:** AI-generated news sites; fake local news / "pink slime" networks
**Cut-off:** 25 September 2026 · **Framing:** Defensive/analytic
**Evidence grades:** A = primary/official · B = peer-reviewed / high-quality investigative · C = reputable news · D = vendor/think-tank · E = anonymous/unverified
**Status keys:** **[D]** demonstrated · **[C]** claimed · **[I]** inferred

---

## 1. Scope and the definitional problem

Two converging phenomena are tracked here: (1) **AI "content farms"** — domains largely machine-generated with little human oversight, dressed as news and monetised via programmatic ads (NewsGuard is the dominant tracker); and (2) **"pink slime" local news** — outlets mimicking local newspapers but funded by partisan actors, PACs or hostile states, often filled with algorithmic or repurposed copy (Tow Center/CJR are the primary trackers). The categories now overlap: NewsGuard's June 2024 finding was the first documented crossover of pink slime, AI and Russian disinformation in one network. **[D]**

**Critical caveat:** every headline count below is a **vendor or research sample, not a census.** NewsGuard releases the full domain list only on request; Tow Center counts are bounded by its DNS/forensic tooling. Counts are **lower bounds on a moving, definitionally contested population**. **[D]** NewsGuard's own definition (below) is a judgement call.

## 2. NewsGuard's counts over time

NewsGuard's original study — "Rise of the Newsbots," Sadeghi & Arvanitis, **1 May 2023** — found **49 sites across seven languages** "entirely or mostly generated by artificial intelligence." **[A/D]** NewsGuard emailed the 29 with contact details: 2 confirmed AI, 8 addresses were invalid, 17 never replied; many articles were bylined "Admin"/"Editor", and reverse image searches showed some "authors" were not real people. **[A/D]** This became the standing **AI Tracking Center**.

The **3,749** figure (last updated 23 June 2026) covers Arabic, Chinese, Czech, Dutch, English, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Tagalog, Thai and Turkish — roughly a **76-fold rise in three years**. **[A/D]**

## 3. NewsGuard's methodology, and why it is contested

Four cumulative criteria define an "AI Content Farm" **[A/D]**: (1) clear evidence a substantial portion is AI-produced; (2) **strong evidence of no significant human oversight** — e.g. articles retaining chatbot artefacts such as "I am not capable of producing 1500 words… However, I can provide you with a summary"; (3) presentation as news that a typical reader could mistake for human journalism; and (4) no clear AI disclosure. Crucially, sites using AI **with effective human oversight are explicitly excluded** — a subjective threshold making the count a lower bound that can fall as well as rise.

**Engagement and revenue:** NewsGuard's consistent finding is that individual sites get **little to no engagement**, and their economics rest on **programmatic advertising** — ad tech delivering ads "without regard to the nature or quality of the website," so top brands unintentionally fund them. **[A/D]** **Caution:** the widely repeated **~$50/month** ad-revenue figure is a vendor-derived estimate for a low-traffic exemplar, not an audited median — treat as illustrative. **[I, low confidence]**

## 4. Pink slime: the Tow Center / CJR record

Priyanjana Bengani's Tow Center (Columbia) work is canonical. **[B]**

- **Dec 2019** — **450** sites "purporting to be local or business news publications" in one linked network. **[B]**
- **Aug 2020** — **nearly tripled to 1,200+**, after 700 new titles in eight months. **Metric Media alone: 960+ sites (80%)**; Franklin Archer ~179. Sites covered 49 states; **over 90% of stories were algorithmically generated** or repurposed from legitimate sources. **[B]**
- **Oct 2022** — at least **$1.6m from three conservative PACs** in the 2022 pre-midterm cycle, backed by oil-and-gas and shipping magnates. **[B]**
- **2024 consolidated report** — "the vast majority of pink slime sites are part of a single entity: the extended Metric Media network, which **encompasses more than 1,100 sites**." **[B]**
- **11 Jun 2024 — NewsGuard: fake local news sites now outnumber real US daily newspapers.** **1,265 pink-slime outlets** vs **1,213 dailies** (papers closing ~2.5/week, Northwestern). The tipping point was **167 pro-Russia sites, 64 posing as local news**, attributed to **John Mark Dougan**, a former Florida deputy sheriff in Moscow. NewsGuard named Metric Media, LGIS, Courier Newsroom, The American Independent and States Newsroom. **[A/D]**

Scholars cited in 2022 estimated roughly **1,200 right-wing to fewer than 70 left-leaning** local pink-slime sites. **[B/C]**

**2025–26:** CJR "Pink Slime Never Went Away" (Sep 2025); LA Times/CalMatters on a network potentially influencing California's 2026 election (Jan 2026); CJR on pink slime **weaponising FOIA** (Feb 2026); Israeli start-up **Prism News launched ~200 US local news sites** (May 2026), called "a new flavor of classic pink slime journalism"; **Poynter reported more than 200 AI-generated "local news" sites suddenly went dark** (18 Aug 2026), indicating fragility and platform enforcement. **[B/C]**

## 5. Audience: small — but politically consequential

The strongest audience evidence is Tow's reader study plus a companion survey **[B]**: a nationally representative survey estimated **~1 in 25 Americans (≈4%) were exposed to pink-slime content** between 24 Aug and 7 Dec 2020. Living in a **news desert did not raise exposure likelihood**, and neither older adults nor conservatives were more likely to visit — undercutting the intuitive "news desert" mechanism. Readers were **more likely to recognise the political slant than the algorithmic nature** of content, and Tow's panelists often viewed sites **positively on first contact**, needing multiple visits to doubt them.

Tow's key analytic claim: **"large audiences are not necessarily required — or, indeed, desired — for such material to achieve its goals."** **[B/I]**

**Demonstrated secondary effects [D]:** misinformation about race-based grading in Illinois, first published on a Local Government Information Services site, **ricocheted across multiple conservative outlets and into candidate campaign outreach**. This is the **"synthetic corroboration"** mechanism in its pre-generative form. Note also that Franklin Archer and Metric Media published **identical claims of 3 million monthly page views and 600 stories/day** while never disclosing their relationship. **[B, claimed figures]**

## 6. Specific viral AI-generated cases

- **Pentagon "explosion," 22 May 2023.** A fabricated AI image spread on X and briefly moved US markets. Covered by **AP** ("FACT FOCUS… briefly sends jitters through stock market," 23 May), **NPR**, **Bloomberg**, **NYT**, **CNN**, **Barron's**, **TechCrunch** — the last framing it as a peril of X's **paid verification**. Reporting described a **brief, shallow intraday dip that reversed quickly**; treat any precise percentage as low-confidence. The reliable claim is *a brief market jitter*, not a quantified crash. **[D event; C magnitude]**
- **"The People's Voice" / "Your News Wire" (Sean Adl-Tabatabai).** Identified by NewsGuard as among the most-read AI-generated pseudo-news sites and a major false-claim source; metrics remain vendor figures. **[D/C]**
- **"SpaceXMania" and "Esspots."** AI-generated **satire** sites whose stories were reported as real: fake "Billy Joel death," "Biden dead," George Clooney leaving the US, Beyoncé refusing red-state shows, the Jon Voight/Angelina Jolie studio claim. **Lead Stories** and **Snopes** debunked multiple instances (2024). **[D debunks; C pattern]**
- **Celebrity death-hoax farms.** NewsGuard's original 49 included generic "CelebritiesDeath"-type domains publishing AI obituaries of living people. **[A/D]**
- **2024 election:** the Dougan network's 64 fake local outlets pushed Russia-aligned narratives pre-election. **[A/D]**
- **2026:** NewsGuard tracked fake Starmer TikTok videos (Dec 2025), a fake UK "curfew" TikTok (NYT, 30 Dec 2025), a college paper fighting an AI-slop site that stole its identity (Washington Post, 13 Jan 2026), and pro-Russian contamination of chatbot answers (France 24/NewsGuard, 3 Jun 2026). **[C/D]**

## 7. Google News, Discover and AI Overviews

**Google News and Discover do not ban AI-generated content.** Nieman Lab's Oct 2025 investigation found AI slop farms in **Google Discover**, quoting a content-tool entrepreneur: "If you can appear in Google Discover or Google News, you've hit the jackpot… 10,000, 50,000, 100,000 visitors in a single day." **[B]** The **expired-domain abuse** pattern is central: dormant legitimate local mastheads (Farmingdale Observer, Carroll County Observer, WECB.fm, Paris2018.com, Glass Almanac) were repurposed into AI farms, lending residual trust and SEO equity. **[B/D]** Google says its spam policies prohibit expired-domain abuse and deceptive practices and that it acts on violations, but it declined to detail how it evaluates AI news. **[A/C]** Separately, NewsGuard found **AI chatbots are blocked by 67% of top news sites**, pushing them toward lower-quality sources — a structural AI-Overviews risk. **[D]**

## 8. Synthetic corroboration and web-scale contamination

- **Nieman Lab (16 Oct 2025)** documented the full chain: one Guardian column (Iceland's 36-hour week) was **stripped, repackaged and republished at least a dozen times** by AI "news outlets," including with a **fabricated quote** attributed to the real author, and surfaced in Google Discover. A firm (Tremplin-Numerique, Estonia) claimed **1,500 sites created and €2m revenue**; sponsored posts priced **€10–€2,000** by traffic; a claimed exemplar drew **4m visitors in May** and was listed in Google News. **[B]**
- **Pew Research Center, "How Much of the Internet Is Written With AI?" (20 Aug 2026)** is the strongest web-scale measurement: **~490,000 English pages over five years** from Common Crawl, analysed with Open Pangram. **10% of a random 10,000-page July 2026 sample shows significant AI authorship**; among pages published **after ChatGPT's release, more than one-third**. Domain skew: **~10% of .com**, 4.6% .org, ~1% .edu/.gov. The .com AI-authored share rose from ~1.1% (Jan 2021) to **9.35% (Jan 2026)**. AI "tells" rose web-wide: em dashes ~2×, Oxford commas +63%, AI-favoured words ("delve," "interplay," "testament") more than doubled, negative parallelism nearly tripled. **[A/B]**
- **AI-generated "news" feeds AI models:** a March 2025 NewsGuard audit found the 10 leading generative AI tools **repeated pro-Kremlin Pravda network false claims 33% of the time**. **[D]**
- **Platform slop shares:** a Guardian-cited study found **>20% of videos shown to new YouTube users** were AI slop (27 Dec 2025); another found **nearly 60% of TikTok videos shown to new users** were AI slop (Jun 2026). Third-party, methodology varies. **[C/D]**
- **Politico, 25 Sep 2026:** "AI Chatbots Are Quietly Spewing Partisan Talking Points" — same-day evidence the loop is live. **[C]**

## 9. Assessment

- **Demonstrated:** AI news sites exist at scale and are growing (49 → 3,749, May 2023 → Jun 2026); pink slime outnumbered US dailies by Jun 2024 (1,265 vs 1,213); one AI image briefly moved markets (22 May 2023); AI text is measurably widespread (Pew); AI farms colonise expired domains and appear in Google News/Discover; AI output contaminates chatbots.
- **Claimed (vendor):** interval totals; the ~$50/month figure; "little to no engagement"; specific ownership attribution.
- **Inferred:** that low direct traffic implies low political effect. Tow's evidence runs the other way — effect flows through **secondary circulation and candidate/outlet pickup**.
- **Contested:** NewsGuard's "effective human oversight" test; the boundaries of "pink slime" itself.
- **Defensive implication:** the binding constraint is not audience size but **trust laundering** — a credible masthead, a repurposed or lapsed domain, and search/discover distribution. Verification should weight **provenance and ownership disclosure** far above content plausibility, and treat near-identical syndication across unrelated outlets as a corroboration *warning*.
