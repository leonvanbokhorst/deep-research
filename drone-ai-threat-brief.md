# Drones, Uncrewed Systems, AI and Autonomy as an Internal Security Threat in Europe
### Institutional evidence base and official threat picture, 2023 – September 2026

**Scope and method.** What NATO, EU and national institutions have *formally said* about drones, uncrewed systems, AI and autonomous capabilities as a threat inside Europe. Claims are tagged **[PRIMARY]** (agency document/official statement read directly) or **[SECONDARY]** (journalism/analysis). A three-way distinction is kept: **official assessment** (stated as current fact), **official warning about the future**, and **journalistic inference**. Disagreements and thin evidence are flagged. No operational or attack guidance.

## 1. Europol

### 1.1 EU-SOCTA 2025 **[PRIMARY]** — *The changing DNA of serious and organised crime*, March 2025
Europol's flagship crime threat assessment names AI as a structural driver: crime is "progressively destabilising, increasingly nurtured online, and strongly accelerated by Artificial Intelligence (AI) and other new technologies." AI and technologies such as blockchain and quantum computing "are a catalyst for crime, and drive criminal operations' efficiency by amplifying their speed, reach, and sophistication." Criminal networks "turn AI and technology into tools for their criminal use" and "find opportunities to operate as proxies for hybrid threat actors." **The executive summary contains no drone or uncrewed-systems content at all** — a notable gap in Europol's flagship strategic product.
https://www.europol.europa.eu/cms/sites/default/files/documents/EU-SOCTA%202025%20-%20Executive%20Summary.pdf

### 1.2 IOCTA 2025 / 2026 **[PRIMARY 2025]**
*IOCTA 2025 — "Steal, deal and repeat"* is cybercrime-only: LLMs and generative AI "are improving the efficacy of social engineering techniques by tailoring communication with the victims and automating criminal processes," and AI is "accelerating the dark side of the digital revolution." **No drone content.** IOCTA 2026 (28 April 2026) is reported to flag "industrialised cybercrime powered by AI, ransomware and data theft" **[SECONDARY for 2026]**.
https://www.europol.europa.eu/cms/sites/default/files/documents/Steal-deal-repeat-IOCTA_2025.pdf

### 1.3 *The Unmanned Future(s)*, 8 December 2025 **[PRIMARY]** — Europol's dedicated drone assessment
- Criminal and terrorist groups "are often early adopters of technology"; the commercial rise of hobby drones meets the criteria of good, cheap, easy and freely obtainable.
- Organised crime "actors are now capable of producing professional and advanced drones in an artisanal way"; 2022 marked the turning point, with the first known malicious **unmanned underwater** drones.
- The **Hamas attacks of 7 October 2023** "involved extensive use of UAS," demonstrating "the potential and relevance of these systems for criminal and terrorist purposes."
- A new **"crime-at-a-distance"** model: drone pilots selling services online to conduct reconnaissance or attack rivals for criminal networks.
- On attribution: unmanned systems "can offer state actors certain plausible deniability. This can complicate attribution and lead to a (mis-)categorisation of these activities as mere criminal acts."
- **Capability gap, verbatim:** "the capability to counter them at scale is limited. The relative distance between the threat and the capability to mitigate and protect has grown into a substantial gap. This gap is not only technological but also relates to regulations, training, data sharing and infrastructure."
- It cites the **September/October 2025 Danish and German airport drone sightings** as underlining "capability gaps within both military and law enforcement."
https://www.europol.europa.eu/cms/sites/default/files/documents/The-Unmanned-Future-Report.pdf

### 1.4 EU TE-SAT 2026 **[PRIMARY]** — the clearest official "lowered threshold" statement
Europol's Terrorism Situation and Trend Report 2026 (July 2026): terrorist and violent extremist actors "continued to show an interest in innovative attack methods. These included 3D-printed weapons, bladed weapons designed to evade metal detection and drones. **Wider access to the required materials and technical guidance is deemed to have lowered the threshold for would-be perpetrators to explore these options**, especially when reinforced by propaganda promoting their use. In 2025, terrorist and violent extremist actors regularly featured unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV) systems in their propaganda." On AI: "AI was abused by terrorists and violent extremists in several ways," including deepfake propaganda, bots/chatbots used to "boost recruitment," and "AI-driven applications... to conduct preliminary research to prepare attacks." Right-wing actors showed "fascination in more innovative attack methods such as 3D-printed weapons or drones." The report records **five terrorist attacks in the EU in 2025** (IEDs, firearms, bladed weapons) and a 12 September 2025 Belgian case of two IS-linked men manufacturing explosives to attack a prominent public figure.
https://www.europol.europa.eu/cms/sites/default/files/documents/EU-TE-SAT-2026.pdf

## 2. EU bodies

### 2.1 Commission Action Plan on Drone and Counter Drone Security, COM(2026) 81 final, 11 February 2026 **[PRIMARY]** — the central EU statement
"Recent incidents involving malicious or irresponsible use of drones have exposed significant and growing security challenges for the Union": airspace violations, disrupted airport operations, near-misses with civil aircraft, and effects on critical infrastructure, borders, ports, transport hubs and public spaces. In energy, "drones have been used to disrupt the operations of power plants, wind and solar installations, district heating systems and energy transport vessels." Unidentified overflights "act as a signalling tool, testing Union's preparedness."

The actor set is named explicitly: "**state and state-linked hostile actors, terrorist organisations, organised crime networks and individuals**. They span different levels of intensity, from criminal or negligent behaviour to hybrid operations and military-type activities. They deliberately blur the boundaries between civilian and military domains."

It also identifies a **new officially recognised vector**: "several hundreds of meteorological balloons have been launched from outside the EU into the airspace of some Member States for different illicit purposes including smuggling... heavy unmanned balloons represent a strategic and largely uncontrollable threat." The Plan states it **"replaces… the drone strategy 2.0"** and the Commission's mid-term review — it is now the keystone EU drone document. Mitigation and deliverables: a **"Drone Security Package" Q3 2026** extending registration and identification to all drones above 100 g; an **EU counter-drone centre of excellence Q1 2027**; an **"EU trusted" drone label Q4 2026**; geofencing technical requirements **by 2027**; **"Rapid Counter-drone emergency teams" explored by Q4 2026**; a counter-drone expert group extended to Frontex, Europol, EASA and EDA; and proposed **National Drone Security Coordinators**.
**Official EU funding figures [PRIMARY]:** a BMVI **EUR 150 m** uncrewed-surveillance call; a **EUR 250 m** external-border call published December 2025 "including against drones"; **EUR 1 bn** of EDF and precursor funding already spent on drone-related R&D plus **EUR 200 m** foreseen over two years; and **EDIP EUR 240 m** for common procurement "such as counter drone, air and missile defence," with grants **up to EUR 20 m per project**. The **EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance** was launched 17 July 2026 with 18 founding members, implementing the Drone Deal of 15 July 2026. The relevant internal-security strategies are the EU Security Union Strategy 2020–2025 (COM(2020) 605) and its successor **ProtectEU** (1 April 2025).
https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/document/download/d2161887-6c06-424c-a8e6-efd46f50e4e8_en · https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/commission-publishes-action-plan-drone-and-counter-drone-security-2026-02-11_en

### 2.2 Drone Strategy 2.0, EASA and U-space **[PRIMARY]**
**Citation correction: the Drone Strategy 2.0 is COM(2022) 652 final, adopted 29 November 2022 — not COM(2023) 658.** Its §5 ("Increasing counter-drone capabilities and system resilience") states: "Drones are a highly innovative tool that can be used for legitimate but also malicious purposes, including organised criminal activities (e.g. smuggling of goods and migrants) as well as attacks on public spaces, individuals and critical infrastructure… there are no specific EU rules and guidelines on countering their unauthorised or even criminal use." It judges "the threat is likely to grow," and notes Member States face "challenges with liberating the necessary budgets." Flagship actions included a counter-drone (C-UAS) package and amending aviation security rules because EASA's non-binding drone-incident guidance was "insufficient." A follow-up Communication of 18 October 2023 addressed "unlawful and dangerous use of drones that are designed for civil use," built on two JRC handbooks.
The EASA framework — Regulations **(EU) 2019/947** and **2019/945** (Open / Specific / Certified) — is the civil layer; EASA states explicitly that **"Neutralisation is NOT in the remit of EASA."** U-space is Regulations **(EU) 2021/664/665/666**, applicable from 26 January 2023, with four mandatory services (flight authorisation, geo-awareness, network identification, traffic information). EASA's 2019 Counter-UAS Action Plan was folded into the European Plan for Aviation Safety; parts of its drone-incident-management material are marked "For Official Use Only." The 2026 Action Plan records U-space "is in force" but "its deployment across Member States remains uneven."
**Official cost figures [PRIMARY]:** Gatwick 2018 cost "up to EUR 64 million"; for the ten largest European airports, "the delay cost of a 30 minutes runway closure is estimated to range from EUR 325,000 to EUR 514,000."
https://transport.ec.europa.eu/document/download/1cb5fb4f-4252-4f97-abf4-c4a167b1c7d2_en?filename=COM_2022_652_drone_strategy_2.0.pdf · https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones

### 2.2a EUROCONTROL **[PRIMARY, thin]**
EUROCONTROL reported on 7 October 2019 that drone incursions at/around airports were "increasing almost exponentially, with no reversal trend in sight." A November 2024 high-level C-UAS workshop (160+ experts) noted "the serious challenge posed by careless or malicious users" and that "legislation is struggling to keep pace." Its most recent European Aviation Overview (23 January 2026) says only that "some major European airports were disrupted (drone incidents, cyberattacks)" — **no airport, date, count or attribution, with drones bundled together with cyberattacks.** Evidence strength: thin.

### 2.3 EU INTCEN — **[NOT PUBLIC]**
There is **no public INTCEN product.** INTCEN is the EU's civilian intelligence unit, a directorate of the EEAS attached to the High Representative, with an Intelligence Analysis Division and an open-source division, under the SIAC arrangement with EUMS INT; every post requires clearance to EU Secret. Across 108,108 EEAS sitemap URLs, "intcen" appears only in vacancy notices. **INTCEN drone/hybrid assessments are EU-classified and not public; no inference about their content is made here.** Public substitutes are other producers: the **EU Hybrid Toolbox** and Hybrid Rapid Response Teams (Council framework, 22 May 2024 — first mobilised for Moldova before the September 2025 elections) and EEAS StratCom **FIMI** annual reports (4th edition, 12 March 2026).

### 2.4 Eurojust **[PRIMARY press release; negative finding on case law]**
On 2–3 June 2026 Eurojust convened a two-day expert meeting on "the judicial response to threats caused by drones," "in response to the growing threat posed by malicious use of drones across Europe," including a session on interagency and international collaboration. https://www.eurojust.europa.eu/news/expert-meeting-judicial-response-threats-caused-drones
**However, an exhaustive check is a negative finding:** all 899 Eurojust press items across 90 listing pages were enumerated and **no drone-specific case press release exists**; the Annual Reports 2023, 2024 and 2025 contain **no drone/UAS content**; and **no Eurojust–Europol joint drone case was found.** The only drone mention in Eurojust primary material is as OSINT evidence in core international crimes (geolocation via "drones, 3D modelling, satellite imagery"). The EU's judicial-cooperation evidence base for drone crime is therefore **assertion-heavy and case-poor.**

### 2.5 Frontex **[PRIMARY, with corrections]**
**Correction:** Frontex publishes a **two-year** series ("Risk Analysis for YYYY/YYYY"); there is no standalone annual edition. The latest is **Risk Analysis for 2026/2027, 17 June 2026**, which states that "the use of semi-submersibles, autonomous vessels and **fixed-wing drones** flying at low altitude from the Moroccan coast toward the Cadiz area to evade radar **is rising sharply and is expected to intensify**" — **without incident counts, seizures or case numbers.** It also reports that "in the air domain, the hostile use of drones and **weather balloons** are causing airport and airspace closures" (a Russian/Belarusian hybrid stream, distinct from smuggling), and that East Shield "foresees… anti-drone deployments." Earlier editions: RA 2025/2026 — smugglers "will continue to adapt… using innovative methods such as drones, GPS-tracked balloons and rafts"; SRA 2024 — "The use of drones and other unmanned craft is already a reality, but a lot of 'criminal potential' can still be discovered in the use of advanced AI devices."
**Frontex's own capability is small:** its 2025 consolidated annual activity report records only **2 unmanned aerial vehicles** deployed (against 52 fixed-wing aircraft and 13 helicopters). It runs counter-UAS activities including a Counter-UAS Prize Contest (Portugal) and is establishing "a UAS and C-UAS cluster in the EU Innovation Hub." **No Frontex drone cost figure exists in the RA/SRA/CAAR set — treat any such figure as unverified.**
https://www.frontex.europa.eu/

### 2.6 European Parliament (EPRS) **[PRIMARY]** — records institutional disagreement
EPRS briefing PE 777.962 (October 2025): at least ten European countries (Belgium, Lithuania, Latvia, Denmark, Norway, Romania, Poland, Estonia, Germany, France) "have reported drone sightings over airports and military installations"; "many of the devices recovered so far were unarmed and some merely decoys"; governments regard the intrusions as "a form of hybrid threat designed to test air-defence readiness and impose psychological and economic costs." On the Drone Wall, the initiative "has split EU capitals": frontline states see it as urgent, while France, Germany, Italy and Greece "question its cost, technical feasibility and the risk of Brussels encroaching on national defence prerogatives." It quotes an expert that the Drone Wall "does not constitute a strategic response to Russian hybrid warfare... The main purpose of the Drone Wall is to reassure worried populations," and an IISS view that using fighters and expensive missiles against drones "is operationally possible but fiscally unsustainable."
https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/777962/EPRS_ATA(2025)777962_EN.pdf

## 3. NATO

### 3.1 September 2025 Polish incursions and Operation Eastern Sentry **[PRIMARY]**
NATO's own account (12 Sept 2025): the North Atlantic Council met on 10 September 2025 "in light of Poland's request for Article 4 consultations"; Rutte and SACEUR held a joint press conference on the violation of Polish airspace by Russian drones. Rutte: "what happened on Wednesday was not an isolated incident. **Russia's recklessness in the air along our eastern flank is increasing in frequency**," describing it as "the largest concentration of violations of NATO airspace." **Eastern Sentry** was launched, involving "novel technologies, including elements designed to address challenges associated with drones." Follow-on: Defence Ministers (15 Oct 2025) agreed "additional counter-drone measures… including testing integrated counter-drone systems under Eastern Sentry," and "nine Allies came together, along with Ukraine, to support Denmark's ability to counter potential drone threats." **Baltic Sentry** (14 Jan 2025) protects undersea infrastructure using "a small fleet of naval drones" — NATO *operating* drones, not countering them. **Arctic Sentry** (11 Feb 2026) extended the series to the High North. A **NATO Innovation Range for uncrewed systems** ran in Latvia, 9–13 March 2026.
EPRS adds that "six European NATO allies have declared that they will use force to defend their airspace, with Lithuania authorising peacetime drone shoot-downs."
https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/articles/news/2025/09/12/nato-launches-eastern-sentry-to-bolster-posture-along-eastern-flank

### 3.2 "Drone wall" and the counter-drone posture
The European Drone Wall and Eastern Flank Watch were proposed in von der Leyen's 2025 State of the Union address and endorsed in principle in Copenhagen (Oct 2025). **JOIN(2025) 27 final, the Defence Readiness Roadmap 2030 (16 Oct 2025)** states: "Recent repeated violations of the airspace of EU Member States have shown the urgency of creating a flexible, agile and state of the art European capability to counter unmanned aerial vehicles." It renamed the effort the **European Drone Defence Initiative** (detection, tracking and neutralisation, 360° multi-layered) and designated both it and Eastern Flank Watch as **European Projects of Common Interest** — launched Q1 2026, initial capacity end-2026, Drone Defence fully functional end-2027, Eastern Flank Watch end-2028.
**The single most important NATO text located: "NATO's Drone Edge," official NATO text, 7 July 2026 [PRIMARY]** — "Drones have fundamentally altered the character of modern warfare… Drone incidents affecting Allies, in particular on the Eastern Flank, have increased in recent months." Commitments: "**Investing over USD 40 Billion in counter-drone capabilities over the next five years**," a **NATO counter-drone marketplace**, and "Training **five times as many drone operators** in our Armed Forces by the end of 2027." Rutte, same day: "Allies themselves have seen repeated drone incursions… we are building robust counter-drone defences to detect, identify, and neutralise drones." This corrects the earlier secondary "$40 bn" figure: it is an **aggregate Allied investment pledge over five years, not a NATO contract value.**
**A notable silence:** the **Washington Summit Declaration (10 July 2024)** mentions only UAVs supplied by DPRK/Iran; the **Hague Summit Declaration (25 June 2025) contains zero occurrences of "drone", "unmanned", "uncrewed" or "UAS"** (verified by string count on the official text). The counter-drone push is therefore a **post-September-2025 development, not summit-driven.**

### 3.3 NATO DIANA, the NATO Innovation Fund, exercises and doctrine
DIANA's **2026 challenge areas include "Autonomy and Unmanned Systems"** and "Contested Electromagnetic Environments"; 150 companies from 24 NATO nations were selected (10 Dec 2025), with 16 accelerator sites and 200+ test centres. The **NATO Innovation Fund** is "a standalone venture capital fund backed by 24 NATO allies deploying €1 billion+ in deep tech"; published drone/counter-drone portfolio companies include **Tytan** ("AI-guided, low-cost interceptor drones"), **Tekever**, **STARK**, **ARX Robotics** and **Kraken**. **NIF does not disclose per-company amounts.**
NATO's **C-UAS Technical Interoperability Exercise (TIE)**, 10–20 Sept 2024 in the Netherlands, involved 450+ participants, 19 Allies and 3 partners and **60+ counter-drone systems** ("sensors, drone-on-drone systems, jammers and cyber interceptors") tested live, with **Ukraine participating for the first time**. Further trials: **Project Flytrap 5.0** (May 2026). Procurement: NSPA will acquire **700 PAC-2 and 200 PAC-3 missiles** (7 July 2026); Rutte stated NSPA contracts are "worth hundreds of millions of euros to procure surveillance drones for Allies." **No per-unit interceptor or counter-drone system cost has been published by NATO 2024–2026.**
**Doctrine — a clear gap:** **no publicly available NATO STANAG or Allied Joint Publication on counter-UAS was located**, and **no published NATO C-UAS policy document.** The only public title-level evidence is an archived June 2021 NATO Standardization Office list (STANAG 3700 → AJP-3.3 Air and Space Operations; STANAG 3880 → AJP-3.3.1(B) Counter-Air Operations; STANAG 4670 → ATP-3.3.8.1 UAS operator training; STANAG 7232 → ATP-3.3.8.2 UAS TTPs) — **no C-UAS-specific standard title appears**, though a newer unlisted standard cannot be excluded. NATO's public framing remains air defence and capability development, not internal-security threat assessment.
**The only concrete per-unit counter-drone cost found [PRIMARY verbatim, attribution weak]:** the **Merops** interceptor, demonstrated at Nowa Dęba (filmed 18 Nov 2025), where NATO text states "each drone costs roughly **USD 14,500** — just about one tenth of the cost of the Shahed drones," and that it had taken down "more than 1,000 Russian drones… inflicting more than USD 200 million in losses with only USD 15 million worth of interceptors." **These figures are attributed to "US officials", are not independently verifiable, and the source contains a textual error.** The **USD 40 bn** pledge carries **no baseline year, national breakdown or methodology**. **NATO's own documents conflict on currency**: the news article says the NSPA surveillance-drone contract is "in the hundreds of millions of **dollars**"; Rutte's same-day transcript says "hundreds of millions of **euros**." No NSPA tender value could be verified.
https://www.nato.int/cps/en/natohq/topics_216199.htm · https://www.nif.fund/

## 4. National assessments

### 4.1 United Kingdom
**CONTEST 2023** (22 Sept 2023) remains current — **no 2024/25/26 refresh exists as of 25 Sept 2026.** It frames both technologies as hedged *future* risk: AI has "the potential for terrorist activity to become more sophisticated with less effort"; on drones it records "the first UK police counter-drone capability to address the increasing potential for small drones to be used as weapons." A promised "refreshed UK Drone Security Strategy" was **never located — NOT VERIFIED**. https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/650b1b8d52e73c000d54dc82/CONTEST_2023_English_updated.pdf

**MI5 DG Ken McCallum, annual threat update, 16 Oct 2025 [PRIMARY] — the most explicit UK AI statement, and self-limiting:** "Would-be terrorists already try to harness AI for their propaganda, their weapons research, their target reconnaissance." On frontier autonomy: "potential future risks from non-human, autonomous AI systems which may evade human oversight and control." Then the cap: "I am not forecasting Hollywood movie scenarios… Artificial intelligence may never 'mean' us harm. But it would be reckless to ignore the potential for it to cause harm." **Drones appear only as a protective-security advisory topic; MI5 makes no claim that drones are used in UK terrorist plots.** JTAC raised the UK threat level to **SEVERE on 1 May 2026** citing Islamist and Extreme Right-Wing threat — **no drone or AI nexus.** https://www.mi5.gov.uk/director-general-sir-ken-mccallum-gives-threat-update

**Independent Reviewer of Terrorism Legislation, Jonathan Hall KC [PRIMARY]** — the most substantive UK official voice. Speech "Generative AI, Drones, and Terrorism," 22 Oct 2024: "Drones have been adopted by criminals — for example, to fly contraband into prisons — and **will no doubt be adopted further by terrorists**." An explicit **official warning about the future**, not a current-use assessment. He also warns against over-reaction: "in the absence of laws, then terrorists will be emboldened to use drones; and society may end up overreacting, and miss out on their benefits." https://terrorismlegislationreviewer.independent.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/IRTL-GenAI-Drones-Oct-24-1.pdf

**NCA National Strategic Assessment 2026 [PRIMARY]:** "Technology is no longer simply a tool that criminals use. It is reshaping crime itself: accelerating it, globalising it, and making it more harmful." Drones are named as a prison drug-smuggling method; on AI, "AI adds complexity and volume." https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/news/nca-launches-the-national-strategic-assessment-2026

**Prisons — the strongest UK quantified series [PRIMARY, MoJ/HMPPS]:** **1,712 drone incidents between April 2024 and March 2025** in England and Wales, **+43% year on year**; **+770% between 2019 and 2023**; **over 200 arrests**. Expenditure includes **£35 m** (June 2026) for grilles on up to **13,000 cell windows** at 17 high-risk prisons and £6.5 m (Jan 2026) for anti-drone R&D to "learn from Ukraine's expertise"; a gang received a combined 22 years in March 2026. Drone-restricted airspace around closed prisons came via **SI 2023/1101** (in force 25 Jan 2024). The 2019 strategy recorded 284 incidents (2016), 319 (2017), 168 (2018), with Gatwick Dec 2018 as "a wake-up call."

**Military sites — MOD, 2 Feb 2026 [PRIMARY]:** "In 2025, there were **266 reported Uncrewed Aerial Vehicle incidents near Defence sites**, a rise from the **126 incidents reported in 2024**." Counter-UAS spending "**quadrupled**," "**over £200 million this year alone**"; restricted airspace at **40 defence sites**; the **Armed Forces Bill** (introduced 15 Jan 2026) will let Defence personnel defeat aerial, land and maritime drones without police assistance. Healey: "The doubling of rogue drones near military sites in the UK in the last year underlines the increasing and changing nature of the threats we face." An official assessment of **incident volume**, not responsibility. **Counterweights:** BBC (3 Feb 2026) — a UK airbase drone inquiry "ends without any suspects"; September 2026 reporting of "over 340" incidents is **NOT VERIFIED**. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-powers-for-defence-personnel-to-defeat-drones-following-doubling-of-incidents-near-bases

### 4.2 Germany
**Verfassungsschutzbericht 2025** (published 30 June 2026) [PRIMARY] is markedly **more cautious than political rhetoric**. The BfV refers to "Meldungen über mutmaßliche Drohnenüberflüge" (*reports of **suspected** drone overflights*), saying these "underline the need for a precise examination of all incidents by the security authorities in order to classify the current hybrid threat situation realistically." It **warns explicitly against "Überattribution" and an "Überschätzung der Fähigkeiten des russischen Staates"** (over-attribution and over-estimation of the Russian state's capabilities), and states outright: "**there is so far no evidence** that Russian state bodies have instrumentalised extremists in Germany and used them for sabotage." Drone-specific hostile activity appears in the **Islamism** chapter: a Lebanese national accused of procuring "products for manufacturing drones" for Hizb Allah, arrested in Salzgitter 14 July 2024, indicted at the OLG Celle 2 Sept 2025. AI has a dedicated section, "KI-Nutzung durch Islamisten." The **2024** report (10 June 2025) contains **no** substantive treatment of drone overflights. https://www.verfassungsschutz.de/SharedDocs/publikationen/DE/verfassungsschutzberichte/2026-06-30-verfassungsschutzbericht-2025.pdf

**BKA — the clearest official de-escalation of AI claims.** *Bundeslagebild Organisierte Kriminalität 2025* [PRIMARY]: a new structured survey of offence methods detected **AI in exactly one case (1 EV)** and 3D printers in one, against **43 cases** using vehicles with professionally built smuggling compartments. The BKA still assesses AI's *potential* ("can be used, for example, for planning and executing offences") — a **capability assessment, not an observed-prevalence claim**. The *Bundeslagebild Cybercrime 2025* goes further: "**Bislang liegen keine polizeilichen Kennzahlen zum konkreten KI-Einsatz… vor**" (to date no police metrics on concrete AI use exist). Drones appear as a **skills-transfer risk** from veterans. https://www.bka.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Publikationen/JahresberichteUndLagebilder/OrganisierteKriminalitaet/organisierteKriminalitaetBundeslagebild2025.pdf

**Official government framing — a key internal disagreement.** The **Bundesregierung FAQ (2 September 2026)** states that illegal drone flights over critical infrastructure are rising and are "mutmaßlich" (presumed) steered by foreign state actors — but adds that "**viele Drohnenflüge zielen nicht primär auf direkte Angriffe, sondern auf Verunsicherung**" (many drone flights are aimed not primarily at direct attacks but at creating uncertainty). Separately, **Bundestag Drucksache 21/7725 (25 August 2026)** classified comparable 2026 airspace intrusions as "**technische Fehlfunktionen**" from GPS jamming — **not an armed attack** — putting parliament's written record at odds with the Chancellor's public "presumption" of Russian responsibility.

**Law and procurement [PRIMARY/SECONDARY]:** the **Zweites Gesetz zur Änderung des Luftsicherheitsgesetzes** entered into force **17 March 2026**, allowing the Bundeswehr to use armed force "als letztes Mittel" (as a last resort) in administrative assistance. The **Bundespolizeigesetz** reform adds drone detection and defence powers plus "automatisierte Erkennung von Gefahren." The **GDAZ** (joint drone-defence centre) opened 17 December 2025; the Bundespolizei's drone-defence unit was expanded from 130 to 300 staff with €100 m. Opposition Greens (Drucksache 21/7669) call the GDAZ "Symbolpolitik" and doubt its operational readiness.

**Legal limits acknowledged by the German government [PRIMARY]:** the BMVg stated (12 Sept 2025) that "für den Bereich von zum Beispiel Kleinstdrohnen… hat die Bundeswehr in diesem Höhenband nicht die Befugnis und die Wirkungshoheit," and set the constitutional limit (6 Oct 2025): "Eine kinetische Wirkung ist bei diesen Vorfällen im Innern durch das Grundgesetz aber eben nicht vorgesehen." The Chancellor's attribution was explicitly presumptive: "**Unsere Vermutung ist, dass Russland hinter den meisten dieser Drohnenflüge steckt**" (our presumption is that Russia is behind most of these drone flights). After an explosive-laden drone was found near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at **Leipzig/Halle (4–5 Aug 2026)**, the government **attributed it to Russia on 1–4 September 2026** — the first hard German attribution. Countervailing, Antwort **21/7725 (25 August 2026)** classified comparable 2026 airspace intrusions as "**technische Fehlfunktionen**" from GPS jamming — **not an "bewaffneter Angriff"** under Art. 5 NATO / Art. 42(7) TEU.

**German incident numbers are of secondary provenance** — treat with care: "over 1,000 suspicious drone flights in 2025" (attributed to BKA President Münch, but the Bundestag paper citing it footnotes *tagesschau*, so not directly verifiable; Münch also cautioned "**Nicht jede Drohne ist ein 'Liebesgruß aus Moskau'**"); 144 airport incidents to August 2025; 166 in H1 2026 (+64%); NRW 232 → 351; 321 sabotage-suspicion cases; Bundeswehr incidents since Feb 2022 "mehrere Hundert". **DFS per-year figures and German drone arrest/prosecution statistics: NOT VERIFIED.** The most current German government statement, **BT-Drucksache 21/8140 (22 September 2026, "Aktueller Stand der Drohnenabwehr"), was unreachable (HTTP 404 on dserver.bundestag.de)** — worth re-attempting.

### 4.3 France, Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Belgium — no consensus, and strong de-escalation evidence
**The single most important cross-cutting finding: no institution in any of these five states claims that AI lowers the barriers to terrorism, sabotage or assassination.** Across VIGIPIRATE (zero AI mentions in the whole plan), Denmark's PET ("for tidligt at vurdere de mere specifikke effekter" — too early to assess the more specific effects), Norway's PST (AI framed as a cyber and information-gathering enabler), Sweden's Säpo (no AI mention retrieved), the Swedish SOU (propaganda), Belgium's OCAM (extremist propaganda) and France's SGDSN/INESIA (systemic model risk), **AI is framed as a reach, cyber, reconnaissance or defensive multiplier only.** The one official-record barrier-lowering claim concerns **online propaganda** lowering the threshold for violence (Säpo) — **not AI.**

**There is no French–Nordic–Belgian consensus on drones.** France (dedicated VIGIPIRATE section) and Denmark (dedicated PET section) treat drones as a rising threat; **Norway explicitly down-assesses** — PST NTV 2026 states "Droner har til nå vært lite benyttet" (drones have so far been little used); **OCAM never writes the word "drone"**; Säpo reports drone flights as anomalies while stating that **no physical sabotage has yet been tied to a foreign power.**

**Strong official de-escalation evidence — weight this equally with the warnings:**
- **Denmark:** the National Operative Staff (NOST), 25 September 2025, said the Danish drone flights were intended "primært … at skabe utryghed og forstyrre vores infrastruktur" (primarily to create insecurity and disrupt infrastructure), concluding **no public risk**.
- **Norway:** **three police districts publicly retracted autumn-2025 drone sightings** — Ørland ("stjerner og planeter" — stars and planets), Sleipner (ship traffic), Gardermoen (case closed, insufficient evidence).
- **Belgium:** OCAM's 6 February 2026 analysis reports **157 threat reports in 2025, down 26%** from 213, with **5% at level 3 and ZERO at level 4**; the cited modus operandi is "les explosifs, la violence physique et les armes à feu" — **drones are not cited**. Plan SHIELD (29 June 2026), Belgium's first National Defence Plan since the Cold War, describes itself as "mesure préventive, et non une réaction à une menace imminente."
- **Sweden:** Säpo's 2025–2026 lägesbild states "Sabotagehotet från Ryssland är reellt" **but** "Inget fysiskt sabotage har än så länge kunnat knytas till främmande makt" and "Sverige är i dagsläget inte det primära målet." Sweden **lowered** its terror threat level from 4 to 3 on 23 May 2025.
- **France:** Sénat report n° 861 (7 July 2026) contains the strongest barrier-lowering wording — "La barrière à l'entrée pour l'attaquant est très faible… vers des groupes terroristes" — **but this is expert testimony (Thierry Berthier, 9 June 2026) in a report that expressly excludes internal-security counter-UAS.** **The strongest wording rests on the weakest evidence.** VIGIPIRATE (June 2026) is the strongest genuine agency doctrine: drones "constituent une menace sérieuse et en plein essor," can be a weapon via payload "(grenade, arme chimique ou biologique)," and drone photography of targets is listed as a reconnaissance mode.

**Hard numbers [PRIMARY]:** France's MoD reported (19 and 27 Aug 2024) "plus de 2400 aviateurs… 88 interceptions d'aéronefs et 64 télé-pilotes de drones interpellés" for the Paris Olympics, with the ORCUS system detecting 9 drones near an Olympic site. Sénat n° 812 (30 June 2026): gendarmerie operates **51 counter-drone systems** (9 jammer vehicles + 42 kits, target 71 kits by end-2026); police OSLAD has 10 vehicles + 52 kits; **police counter-drone missions rose 96 (2023) → 1,434 (2024) → 866 (2025)**. Denmark: Rigspolitiet (26 Sept 2025) logged "mere end 500 anmeldelser … på tværs af alle politikredse"; Copenhagen Police (24 Sept 2025) "mere end 100 anmeldelser." Belgium: mil.be (16 Apr 2026) admitted **Elsenborn military-site overflights in October 2025** and "Les capacités très limitées en matière de moyens anti-drones," calling it a "**menace hybride aiguë**" — a sharp divergence from OCAM's assessment. Sweden: Dir. 2026:45 (12 June 2026) launched a hostile-drone inquiry; SEK 8.7 bn GUTE II (2 Apr 2026) and >SEK 4 bn for unmanned systems (12 Jan 2026); SOU 2025:57 (19 May 2025) would let the military support police to "oskadliggöra obemannade farkoster (drönare m.m.)" under direct police command. Norway: Politidirektoratet created a national competence centre against illegal drone flying at Taraldrud on 17 December 2025, operational "i løpet av 2026."

**Corrections that must be applied (these were errors in the original tasking, restated here so they are not repeated):**
1. **The French legal premise is wrong.** CSI art. **L. 114-2** governs *enquêtes administratives* (background checks for transport-security staff), **not** counter-drone powers. Loi n° 2021-998 du 3 août 2021 contains **no drone article** (verified against the full code and Conseil constitutionnel n° 2021-822 DC). The real basis is **CSI art. L. 213-2, Ch. III bis** (version LOI n° 2026-791 du 16 août 2026): devices "destinés à rendre inopérant ou à neutraliser un aéronef circulant sans personne à bord, en cas de menace imminente…". **The statute authorises only neutralise/disable — "detect/identify/jam" is not statutory language but doctrine.** Authorisation: décret n° 2024-221 du 12 mars 2024; jamming requires an ANFR impact study; the regime has no sunset.
2. **The DGSI publishes no public threat assessment or activity report.** Its public navigation is limited to descriptive pages and podcasts; any citation of a "DGSI Rapport d'activité" should be treated as **unconfirmed or false**.
3. **"SNRP" could not be verified as any French strategy document** — do not expand the acronym.
4. **A delegated researcher reported a PET 2026 terror assessment containing new drone text, but its media URL returned HTTP 302 → 404 on three separate re-tests on 25 September 2026. That quote is flagged UNCONFIRMED and should not be built upon without independent retrieval.**

**NOT VERIFIED — do not pad:** Säpo årsbok 2024–2026; DGSI activity report; SNRP; French prison, airport or current nuclear drone incident counts (the only substantive French nuclear-drone evidence, OPECST n° 267, is from **2015**); Danish counter-drone legislation, procurement and the PET espionage assessment 2024–26; Belgian incidents at Kleine-Brogel, Brussels Airport and Peutie, and Belgian counter-drone law; aggregate Norwegian and Swedish drone-incident counts; NSM drone posture. **No primary document in any of the five states establishes state attribution for the autumn-2025 drone wave.**

### 5.1 NCTV — Dreigingsbeeld Terrorisme Nederland (DTN)
**DTN December 2025 [PRIMARY]** records that ISIS propaganda increasingly calls for attacks "met messen en voertuigen, maar steeds vaker ook tot brandstichting en het gebruik van explosieven en drones" (with knives and vehicles, but increasingly also arson, explosives and drones), and that ISKP's media arm may use AI "om in een hoog tempo zoveel mogelijk materiaal te produceren" (to produce as much material as possible at high speed). It documents an **October 2025 Belgian case**: three men arrested over a plot against, among others, Belgian PM Bart De Wever; searches found homemade explosives, a 3D printer and a target list — "Ze wilden met de 3D-printer een drone maken om hier explosieven aan te monteren" (they wanted to make a drone with the 3D printer to attach explosives to it).
https://www.nctv.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2025/12/09/dtn-december-2025/dtn-december-2025.pdf

**DTN June 2026 (published 30 June 2026) [PRIMARY]** contains the most carefully calibrated official Dutch statement, in a box titled *"Dreiging van drones gaat mogelijk toenemen"* (threat from drones may increase):
- **Official assessment — the barrier remains:** "In West-Europa is voor zover bekend nog nooit een aanslag succesvol uitgevoerd met een drone" (in Western Europe, to the best of current knowledge, no attack has ever been successfully carried out with a drone). A recent research report shows a drone attack "uitgebreide kennis van explosieven, aflever- en ontstekingsmechanismen en planning vereist" (requires extensive knowledge of explosives, delivery and detonation mechanisms, and planning), and "toegang tot explosieven of precursoren waarschijnlijk een belemmering" (access to explosives or precursors is probably an obstacle); therefore "Een aanslag met een eenvoudig aanslagmiddel zal daarom, ook voor jihadisten, waarschijnlijker blijven" (an attack with a simple means will remain more likely, also for jihadists).
- **Official warning about the future:** the threat "zal mogelijk toenemen" because the Russia–Ukraine war has accelerated drone development, conversion manuals are easy to find, and both ISIS and al-Qa'ida distribute them and urge drone attacks in propaganda.
- It cites the **Antwerp 9 October 2025** plot as the concrete illustration.
https://www.nctv.nl/site/binaries/site-content/collections/documents/2026/06/30/dreigingsbeeld-terrorisme-nederland-juni-2026/dreigingsbeeld-terrorisme-nederland-juni-2026-def.pdf

### 5.2 AIVD — Jaarverslag 2025 (23 April 2026) **[PRIMARY]** — the counterweight to alarmism

De AIVD en MIVD hebben in 2025 inderdaad een toenemend aantal meldingen ontvangen over waarnemingen van drones in de buurt van vitale infrastructuur, luchthavens en bijvoorbeeld militaire faciliteiten. ### 5.5 Dutch prosecution and pending legislation
**Dordrecht court, 22 April 2026 [SECONDARY, court/OM reported]:** a 34-year-old man was convicted of smuggling drugs and phones into prisons by drone — approximately **70 drone flights between April 2021 and February 2024** across the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg, earning about €30,000 — receiving a four-month suspended sentence, 120 hours community service and a €10,000 (half-suspended) fine. ## 6. Where institutions disagree, and where evidence is thin

1. **Russian attribution vs. German, Dutch and Nordic caution — the largest gap.** Media and some political statements assume Russian responsibility for the 2025 drone waves. **Germany's BfV Verfassungsschutzbericht 2025 states flatly: "there is so far no evidence that Russian state bodies have instrumentalised extremists in Germany and used them for sabotage,"** and refers only to "**suspected** drone overflights." The **AIVD** likewise declines to confirm suspected Russian involvement and warns "a sighted drone is not always a threat." Sweden's Säpo states "Inget fysiskt sabotage har än så länge kunnat knytas till främmande makt"; Denmark's NOST concluded the September 2025 flights created **no public risk**; and **no primary document in France, Sweden, Denmark, Norway or Belgium establishes state attribution at all.** EPRS notes many recovered devices "were unarmed and some merely decoys." The BBC reports UK airbase investigations closing "without any suspects."
2. **Retracted sightings — the hardest de-escalation evidence anywhere.** **Three Norwegian police districts publicly retracted autumn-2025 drone reports**: Ørland ("stjerner og planeter" — stars and planets), Sleipner (ship traffic) and Gardermoen (insufficient evidence). This is a direct, official demonstration that a measurable share of the 2025 "drone wave" was misidentification, and it is the single best-evidenced caution against treating incident counts as threat counts.
3. **Official AI claims vs. BKA's own count, and a five-country silence.** Europol TE-SAT 2026 and the Commission treat AI as an accelerator; the **German BKA's 2025 organised-crime survey found AI in exactly one case**, against 43 using concealed vehicle compartments, and BKA Cybercrime 2025 admits **no police metrics on concrete AI use exist**. **No institution in France, Sweden, Denmark, Norway or Belgium claims AI lowers barriers to terrorism, sabotage or assassination** — AI appears only as a reach, cyber, recon or defensive multiplier. The one barrier-lowering claim on the official record concerns **online propaganda**, not AI.
4. **Allies disagree with each other on the same events.** Belgium's **OCAM** reports threat reports **down 26%** with zero level-4 threats and does not mention drones, while **mil.be** calls Elsenborn's October 2025 overflights an "**menace hybride aiguë**" and concedes "very limited anti-drone capabilities." Norway **down-assesses** ("drones have so far been little used") while France and Denmark treat drones as rising.
5. **Strongest wording, weakest evidence.** The most quoted barrier-lowering statement in the French record — "La barrière à l'entrée pour l'attaquant est très faible" — is **expert testimony in a Senate report that expressly excludes internal-security counter-UAS**, not an agency assessment.
6. **Drone-wall utility.** EPRS records open member-state disagreement on cost, feasibility and strategic value, and quotes an expert that the Drone Wall is primarily about **reassurance**.
7. **NATO vs. EU/internal-security framing.** NATO frames counter-drone work as air defence and capability development; EU agencies and interior ministries frame it as internal security, terrorism and organised crime. The two are not reconciled in public documents.
8. **Threat trajectory vs. current barriers — not actually contradictory.** Europol TE-SAT 2026 says drones have "lowered the threshold"; the Dutch NCTV simultaneously documents that **no drone attack has succeeded in Western Europe**, that technical and precursor barriers are real, and that simple weapons remain more likely. MI5's McCallum ("I am not forecasting Hollywood movie scenarios") and the Independent Reviewer ("will no doubt be adopted further by terrorists") occupy the same hedged middle ground. This is direction of travel vs. current state, frequently misreported as conflict; the NCTV statement is the more precise.
9. **Balloons.** The Commission's recognition of "several hundreds of meteorological balloons" as a smuggling and hybrid vector is new and largely unexamined in national threat assessments.
10. **NATO's internal inconsistencies and poor cost transparency.** NATO documents conflict on dollars vs euros for the same NSPA contract; the Merops video dates the Polish incursion to 9 September with "more than 20 drones" while NATO's own article says 10 September with no count. The largest figures (USD 40 bn; EUR 240 m EDIP; EUR 250 m border call) are aggregate pledges; only EASA and EUROCONTROL publish per-incident estimates, and EASA's "up to EUR 64 million" methodology is undisclosed.
11. **Legally unsettled neutralisation authority.** Frontex's own 2025 activity report concedes "more work needed to address how to deal with neutralisation in terms of competences, both at national and European level," while EASA states "**Neutralisation is NOT in the remit of EASA**." This is a **legal, not merely technical, gap** — and it is why national counter-drone powers remain contested (the Dutch OM's pending request for legislative change, §5.5; France's statute authorising only neutralise/disable, §4.3).
12. **Data gaps.** No consistent Europe-wide series of drone incidents reported to police exists; Europol and Eurojust publish no central counts of drone arrests or prosecutions. The two hard national series are the UK's (military sites 126→266; prisons 1,712 in 2024/25) and France's police counter-drone missions (96 → 1,434 → 866 across 2023–25); the Netherlands and most other states publish none.
13. **Access limitation.** `eur-lex.europa.eu` returned HTTP 202 with an empty body throughout this research, and `consilium.europa.eu`, `diana.nato.int`, `nspa.nato.int`, `act.nato.int`, `nso.nato.int`, SHAPE, `legifrance` and `dgsi.interieur.gouv.fr` returned 403/blocked. EU legal texts were read from the publishing DGs' own PDF mirrors and French codes from `codes.droit.org`; re-verify those citations against working official routes before publication.
